\begin{abstract}
We give the final proof composition establishing dimension at most58 for
finite-sided, finite-volume real hyperbolic Coxeter polytopes. The argument
combines a simultaneous local corner-payment theorem, an exact upper
certificate for all finite Coxeter forests of rank59, and a positive
shape-curvature surplus at every ideal vertex. Nonsimple cusps are treated
through their products of simplex links. The finite upper equals the
curvature coefficient, so the strictly positive global correction already
gives a contradiction. We specify the computational certificate boundary
and distinguish exact arithmetic checking, mathematical review and the
ongoing proof-assistant translation. The local geometric atlas and the
previously completed dimension59 tail are internal parts of the proof,
not additional published assumptions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and scope}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Every finite-sided, finite-volume real hyperbolic Coxeter polytope has
dimension at most58.
\end{theorem}

No arithmeticity, compactness, simple-cusp or facet-count hypothesis is
imposed. The result is an internally checked computer-assisted proof.
This manuscript does not assert external refereeing, novelty, optimality,
or completed end-to-end Lean verification. The accompanying geometric
atlas consists of the frozen local theorem reports and their exact
certificates. The endpoint and its recursive dependency manifest are
\cert{fresh_a15_d15_dimension58_20261018}.

The proof has two logically distinct dimension steps. The completed
internal bound59 excludes all dimensions at least60. We exclude
dimension59 here. It would not suffice to exclude59 alone: no unproved
claim about induced Coxeter faces is used to supply the higher tail.
The later ambient58 search for a bound57 is independent of this proof.

\section{Actual corners and cusp multiplicities}

We use the norm-two convention for outward roots: diagonal Gram entries
are2 and off-diagonal entries are nonpositive. At an actual two-face, the
supporting white roots form an elliptic set of size $n-2$. Adjoining either
incident side root gives an elliptic edge principal. At a finite vertex,
the incident diagram is a finite Coxeter forest of rank$n$.

At an ideal vertex$v$, write its irreducible affine factor ranks as
$r_1,\ldots,r_s$, with $r_i\geq1$ and $\sum_i r_i=n-1$. Its Euclidean
link is
\[
 L_v=\prod_{i=1}^s\Delta^{r_i},\qquad
 V_v=\prod_{i=1}^s(r_i+1).
\]
The standard finite-volume link and simple-at-edges facts are the
geometric input to the incidence reduction. They do not assert that an
ideal vertex itself is simple.

\begin{lemma}[Factor-fiber multiplicity]\label{lem:fiber}
At an actual ideal two-face corner, its marked pair belongs to the one
varying affine factor. A fixed pair in factor$i$ occurs in exactly
$V_v/(r_i+1)$ actual corner fibers at$v$. Consequently, if the total pair
weight of any rank$r$ affine factor is at most $(r+1)c(r)$, then the
actual cusp charge satisfies
\[
 C_{\actual}(v)\leq V_v\sum_i c(r_i).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An edge of the product link varies in exactly one simplex factor.
The supporting white diagram retains $r_i-1$ nodes of that factor and
one-node-deleted elliptic pieces from the other factors. The two marked
sides restore the whole varying factor. The other factors admit
$\prod_{j\ne i}(r_j+1)$ choices, proving the multiplicity.

The use of a whole factor is justified geometrically. A positive kernel
combination of an affine subdiagram is a nonzero feasible null vector:
it is nonzero because its product with a strict chamber center is
negative, and is feasible by acuteness. Its projective point is an ideal
vertex, by strict convexity of the closed Klein ball. Within the ideal
diagram it is a complete irreducible affine component, because a proper
principal subdiagram of such a component is positive definite. The
internal factor-fiber report records this argument and the corresponding
published parabolic-containment input.
\end{proof}

\section{The deficit and its shape-curvature lower bound}

For a two-face$F$, let$s(F)$ be its number of sides and put
\[
 \Dfive(P)=\sum_F(5-s(F)).
\]
For a vertex figure of dimension$d=n-1$, define
\[
 H(t)=\sum_{j=0}^d h_jt^j,\quad
 V=H(1),\quad G=\sum_{j=0}^d h_j\min(j,d-j),\quad
 \kappa=\frac{5G-dV}{2}.
\]
For an ideal vertex, $H(t)=\prod_i(1+t+\cdots+t^{r_i})$.
For a finite vertex, the figure is a simplex, so $h_j=1$.

\begin{lemma}[Shape-curvature inequality]\label{lem:curvature}
In dimension59,
\[
 \Dfive(P)\geq\frac{783}{2}v_f+\sum_{v\text{ ideal}}\kappa(L_v)+4350.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a generic linear function on the bounded projective closure.
For each vertex figure let$E$ be its number of edges and$E^0$ the number
not cut by the induced level section. Khovanskii's exact reduction gives
the two-face incidence sum $I=\sum E$ and $2f_2=\sum E^0$. Hence
\[
 \Dfive=\sum_v\left(\frac52E_v^0-E_v\right).
\]
Orient each simple vertex figure generically. A vertex of index$j$ has
$j$ descending and$d-j$ ascending edges, and the index distribution is
$h_j$. Assign an uncut edge below the section to its upper endpoint and
one above to its lower endpoint. Each vertex receives either$j$ or$d-j$
such contributions, giving $E^0\geq G$.

The global minimum and maximum of the original polytope are distinct;
their level sections miss the entire vertex figures. At either one,
symmetry and the index formula give
\[
 E-G=\sum_{j>d/2}h_j(2j-d).
\]
All the vertex figures in question are simplices or products of
simplices, so every$h_j\geq1$. At$d=58$ each extremum contributes an
extra at least
$\sum_{j=30}^{58}(2j-58)=870$ to$E^0-G$.
Multiplication by$5/2$ and addition of the two extrema gives4350,
counted once.

For a finite vertex $G=\sum_{j=0}^{58}\min(j,58-j)=841$ and
$E=58\cdot59/2$. Thus its curvature is
\[
 \frac{5\cdot841-58\cdot59}{2}=\frac{783}{2}.
\]
The ideal term is the stated$\kappa$. This proves the inequality.
\end{proof}

The exact incidence and index identities are published inputs.
The retained shape term and the quantitative two-extrema strengthening
above are internal deductions, not assumptions of the dimension bound.

\section{Exact finite-forest upper bound}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:finite}
Every actual finite Coxeter forest of total rank59 has final charge
at most$783/2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The final ordinary costs are A15=D15=100 and B15=97. The four added
component-pair costs are respectively$1/2,1/2,2,2$. The representative
A1/D13 allowance becomes$33/16$, and A4/D13 becomes8. The A1/A12 and
A4/A11 charges fit their unchanged allowances$5/2$ and8. The exact
certificate checks all180 actual component types through rank59,
their bases and all32,400 ordered pair comparisons simultaneously.
The arbitrary-label rank-two family and exceptional finite types are
included by the stated representative classification.

Fix all multiplicities of ranks9 through16 and of the separate A17
representative. There are961 possible large-component vectors.
Fixing the rank5 multiplicity as well gives2574 expanded vectors and
interaction keys. Residual ranks are1--4,6--8,17--59; residual rank17
is D17, whereas A17 has already been fixed. Residual pairwise upper
interactions vanish. Each residual item consists of its base plus
all interactions with the fixed factors;128,700 exact identities
check this statement for the entire domain.

For positive-rank items$(r,w_r)$, a table$V(0)=0$ satisfying
\[
 V(k)\geq V(k-r)+w_r\qquad(r\leq k)
\]
dominates every additive residual forest by induction after removing
one item. An attaining predecessor proves equality when required.
The5148 normal/no-A2 tables satisfy6,723,288 such inequalities and
303,732 attaining-predecessor checks. The verifier checks inequalities
and exact equality rather than trusting an optimizer's status.

There are two contextual charges. The old A2/A2 charge is
$a_2(a_2-1)/4$ when a whole A10 and at least two A1 factors occur.
The other charge is$a_2d_5$ when a whole A9 and at least one A1 occur.
The representative guards may allow any rank10/rank9 representative
and replace D5 by A5. All A2 multiplicities are fixed explicitly.
The no-context, one-reserved-A1 and two-reserved-A1 branches, using
no-A2 residual tables, cover every forest. The last branch includes
both charges if both guards hold. The relaxed one-A1 branch permits
extra A1 only as an upper relaxation; the reconstructed winning forest
has its full contextual cost checked. There are9191 options in total.

Every expanded upper is at most6264 sixteenths, namely$783/2$.
This is a representative upper, not a claim of actual attainment.
Its representative maximizer $8A_1+3A_5+3D_{12}$ has actual literal
cost355.5. Actual sharpness is unnecessary for the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\section{The ideal envelope and complete terminal domain}

The normalized affine cost$c(r)$ is the total factor-pair allowance
divided by$r+1$. For the completed58 proof it is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
r&10&11&12&13&14&15&16\\\hline
c(r)&53/11&31/6&11/2&40/7&367/60&411/64&227/34
\end{array}
\]
with$c(r)=r/2$ for$1\leq r\leq9$. For$r\geq17$ the exact all-family
envelope through58 is in the exported certificate; it follows the
proved classical graph-count formulas, not a sampled large-rank cutoff.
The factor-fiber lemma gives $C_{\actual}\leq C_{\upper}=V\sum c(r_i)$.

\begin{lemma}[Low-nine concentration]\label{lem:concentration}
Every positive rank partition of58 can be reduced to one with at most
one part below9, such that its normalized curvature-minus-envelope
margin and its product$V$ do not increase.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For$1\leq a\leq b<9$, replace$(a,b)$ by$(a-1,b+1)$, omitting zero.
Let$U_r$ be uniform on$\{0,\ldots,r\}$. Before centering, the probability
of$U_a+U_b=j$ is
\[
 \frac{\min(j+1,a+1,b+1,a+b-j+1)}{(a+1)(b+1)}.
\]
After the replacement the denominator is$a(b+2)$, smaller by$b-a+1$.
The new probability is larger on the tails$j<a$ or$j>b$ and smaller
on$a\leq j\leq b$. Both distributions are symmetric about the same
mean. Symmetrizing a convex test function makes it nondecreasing with
distance from that mean; subtracting its value at the crossing radius
proves the convex-order inequality. Adding the other independent
uniforms preserves it.

For$d=58$, the normalized curvature is
\[
 \frac{\kappa}{V}=\frac{3d}{4}
   -\frac52\mathbb E\left|\sum_iU_{r_i}-\frac d2\right|.
\]
It therefore does not increase. The normalized cost is unchanged,
because both affected ranks stay at most9 and$c(r)=r/2$ there.
The product factor decreases from$(a+1)(b+1)$ to$a(b+2)$.
Each move increases the bounded integer$\sum r_i^2$, so iteration
terminates. No concentration of the ranks at least9 is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ideal}
Every actual ideal vertex in dimension59 has
$\kappa-C_{\actual}\geq91/2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Enumerate every multiset of ranks at least9 summing to$58-\ell$, for
$0\leq\ell\leq8$, adjoining$\ell$ if it is positive. This gives exactly
1890 terminal profiles. For every profile the exact certificate checks
the product polynomial, its integer coefficients and moments
\[
 V=\sum_jh_j=\prod_i(r_i+1),\qquad
 G=\sum_j\min(j,58-j)h_j,
\]
and the rational normalized margin
\[
 m=\frac{5G-58V}{2V}-\sum_i c(r_i).
\]
All are positive. The minimum normalized margin is$1/340$, attained
at$(9,9,9,15,16)$; the minimum absolute surplus$Vm$ is$91/2$, attained
at the singleton profile$(58)$.

Under Lemma~\ref{lem:concentration}, the original profile has
$V\geq V'$ and$m\geq m'>0$. Hence$Vm\geq V'm'\geq91/2$.
This multiplication uses positivity; it would not follow from negative
terminal margins. Finally $C_{\actual}\leq C_{\upper}$ gives the same
lower surplus for the actual cusp charge, with its correct factor
multiplicities. Nonsimple cusps have not been discarded.
\end{proof}

\section{The contradiction}

For any polygon with at least five sides the deficit$5-s$ is nonpositive.
Proposition~\ref{prop:local} therefore gives, by summing nonnegative
corner weights and then regrouping by vertices,
\[
 \Dfive\leq\frac{783}{2}v_f+\sum_{v\text{ ideal}}C_{\actual}(v).
\]
Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:curvature} and Proposition~\ref{prop:ideal},
this implies
\[
 0\geq4350+\sum_{v\text{ ideal}}
       \bigl(\kappa(L_v)-C_{\actual}(v)\bigr)
   \geq4350+\frac{91}{2}v_i>0.
\]
Thus dimension59 is impossible, including the compact case$v_i=0$.
The completed internal dimension59 theorem excludes all dimensions
at least60. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
