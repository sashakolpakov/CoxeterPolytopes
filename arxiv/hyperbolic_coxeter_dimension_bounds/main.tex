\begin{abstract}
We prove that a finite-volume hyperbolic Coxeter polytope has dimension
at most $57$, that a compact one has dimension at most $17$, and that a
compact arithmetic one has dimension at most $15$. The finite-volume
argument compares corner weights with the geometry of vertex links.
The compact arguments use projections of one vector associated with an
original farthest vertex; arithmeticity further restricts the ground
field and integral wall parameters. This concise edition presents the
main geometric arguments and their exact local interfaces. The complete
case proofs, computational proofs and reproduction instructions are supplied
as versioned addenda in the accompanying repository.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
All polytopes in this article are convex, finite-sided and
full-dimensional. A hyperbolic Coxeter polytope has dihedral angles
that are integer submultiples of $\pi$; reflection in its facets
generates a discrete group with the polytope as fundamental domain.
Hyperbolic space has curvature $-1$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $P\subset\HH^n$ be a finite-volume hyperbolic Coxeter polytope.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},nosep]
\item $n\le57$;
\item if $P$ is compact, then $n\le17$;
\item if $P$ is compact and its reflection group is arithmetic, then
 $n\le15$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The classical finite-volume ceiling is $995$, due to
Prokhorov~\cite{Prokhorov}, using the combinatorial estimates of
Khovanskii~\cite{Khovanskii}. Vinberg obtained the compact ceiling
$29$~\cite{Vin84,Vin85}. The largest known finite-volume examples
have dimension $21$~\cite{Borcherds}; the bounds here are upper bounds
and make no new existence assertion. Compactness is essential in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(iii).

Guo and Zheng recently obtained the general finite-volume bound
$81$~\cite{GuoZheng2026}. An earlier version of our bound $57$,
including its corner-weight method and higher-dimensional argument,
was publicly posted on 21 October 2026~\cite{FirstDisclosure}.
The present article condenses the three proof families assembled in
the repository edition of 30 October~\cite{Addenda}.

The methods separate after the common local geometry. In the
finite-volume case, triangles and quadrilaterals require corner
payments that high-dimensional vertex diagrams cannot accommodate.
Nonsimple ideal vertices are treated through the product structure
of their links. In the compact case, local walls force quadratic
lower bounds for components of a single vector of norm less than one.
Arithmeticity enters only in the third argument, through integral
cycles and positivity at the other real embeddings.

\section{Actual walls and vertex diagrams}\label{sec:geometry}
We use a Lorentz space of signature $(-,+,\ldots,+)$ and the future
unit hyperboloid model of $\HH^n$. Outward wall normals are spacelike;
the chamber inequalities are $\ip{y}{e_i}\le0$. Distinct wall normals
have nonpositive products. Finite vertex diagrams are spherical.
A horospherical link at an ideal vertex is a product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:link}
 L_v=\prod_i\Delta^{r_i},\qquad \sum_i r_i=n-1,
\end{equation}
whose factors correspond to irreducible affine diagrams of orders
$r_i+1$. These standard face and link facts are used in their
actual-wall form~\cite{Vin85,FeliksonTumarkin}.

Two elementary observations account for much of the local geometry.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positive}
A positive-definite Gram matrix with nonpositive off-diagonal entries
has entrywise nonnegative inverse. A positive-definite principal set
of actual chamber walls has a feasible finite common point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $B$ is the matrix, choose $s$ larger than its largest eigenvalue.
Then $M=sI-B$ is entrywise nonnegative and has spectral radius less
than $s$, so $B^{-1}=s^{-1}\sum_{j\ge0}(M/s)^j$.

Take a strict interior future timelike point $o$ and put
$b_i=-\ip{o}{e_i}>0$ for the selected walls. The vector
\[
 q=o+\sum_i(B^{-1}b)_i e_i
\]
is orthogonal to the selected normals and has square
$q^2=o^2-b^TB^{-1}b<0$. Its product with $o$ is negative, so it
is future pointing. Every other chamber inequality is strict,
because the coefficients are nonnegative and all other wall
products are nonpositive. Normalize $q$ to the hyperboloid.
\end{proof}

An irreducible affine principal set has a positive kernel vector
$(u_i)$. The root combination $\nu=\sum_i u_i e_i$ is null,
nonzero since $\ip{o}{\nu}<0$, and feasible against every wall.
It represents an actual ideal point. Irreducibility and the positive
definiteness of proper deletions identify its whole affine factor.
In contrast, a kernel vector in an indefinite core represents the
zero vector: that vector is null and orthogonal to a timelike
vector in the core span, whose perpendicular space is positive
definite. Both interpretations are needed when controlling extensions.

At an actual two-face, the supporting walls form a positive-definite
diagram of rank $n-2$. Adding one side gives an edge diagram, and
adding two adjacent sides gives a positive-semidefinite corner
diagram. For opposite sides of a quadrilateral, the corresponding
principal diagram is indefinite. A positive or affine alternative
would give a feasible common finite or ideal point of those sides.
Minimal indefinite subdiagrams, inverse Gram matrices and the
classification of finite and affine diagrams consequently reduce
the local payment questions to explicit marked configurations.

\section{The finite-volume bound}\label{sec:fv}
For a two-face $F$, let $s(F)$ be its number of sides and set
\[
 \Dfive(P)=\sum_{\dim F=2}(5-s(F)).
\]
A corner rule assigns a nonnegative weight $w(v,F)$ to every
incidence of a vertex with a two-face. Write
$C(v)=\sum_{F\ni v}w(v,F)$.

\begin{lemma}[Payment comparison]\label{lem:payment}
If every triangle receives total weight at least $2$ and every
quadrilateral at least $1$, then $\Dfive(P)\le\sum_v C(v)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each face receives at least $5-s(F)$: this is the hypothesis for
three and four sides, and follows from nonnegativity for at least
five sides. Sum over all faces and interchange the two sums.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The local rule and its finite maximum}
The explicit rule is defined on whole marked vertex diagrams in
Addendum FV, under ``The intrinsic corner weights.'' It combines
same-component pair weights, cross-component roles and contextual
terms, with a prescribed priority among overlapping roles.
Finite and affine components have separate tables. Whole-component
conditions and all compensating payments are included in this one
rule.

\begin{proposition}[Local payment and finite charge]\label{prop:local}
In ambient dimension $58$, this simultaneous rule pays at least
$2$ on every triangular two-face and at least $1$ on every
quadrilateral. At every finite vertex its charge is at most $377$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Construction and proof location]
The geometric construction uses minimal indefinite diagrams for
opposite pairs and for the three sides of a triangle. The bounded
core algorithms include all permitted overlaps, cross bonds and
additional supporting walls. Singular cores are controlled by
their actual root relations, while nonsingular cores give inverse
and Schur-complement bounds on the entire attached forest.
The complete construction and extension proofs are Addendum FV's
``Geometric coverage and exact local certificates'' and
``The dimension-specific final local rule.''

The finite arithmetic maximizes an explicit function over all
spherical forests of rank $58$. If $m_T$ is the multiplicity of
type $T$, its charge, scaled by $64$, has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forest}
 \sum_T b(T)m_T+
 \sum_T j(T,T)\binom{m_T}{2}+
 \sum_{T<U}j(T,U)m_Tm_U+\Psi(m).
\end{equation}
The alphabet contains $A_r,B_r,D_r$ at their allowed ranks through
$58$, the exceptional finite types, and a symbolic rank-two type
for the remaining unbounded dihedral labels. Their common cost
is established before that symbolic encoding is used.
The tables and the complete context function are printed in
Addendum FV. Catalogue item FV2 states the exact formal maximum $377$ over this
complete domain, including all contextual corrections. Its proof and
finite certificates are in the repository. No realization of a
maximizing forest is required.
\end{proof}

The geometric coverage theorem identifies every actual vertex with
a member of this necessary finite domain. The catalogue supplies the
whole-component tables, context states, complete maximization and
its verification records.

\subsection{The shape term}
Suppose now that $n=58$. For any vertex figure define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shape}
 H(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{57}h_jt^j,\quad V=H(1),\quad
 G=\sum_{j=0}^{57}h_j\min(j,57-j),\quad
 \kappa=\frac{5G-57V}{2}.
\end{equation}
For an ideal vertex, $H(t)=\prod_i(1+t+\cdots+t^{r_i})$
by~\eqref{eq:link}. For a finite vertex, $H(t)=1+t+\cdots+t^{57}$.

\begin{lemma}[Shape curvature]\label{lem:shape}
For a proposed $58$-dimensional polytope,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower}
 \Dfive(P)\ge377v_f+\sum_{v\text{ ideal}}\kappa(v)+4205,
\end{equation}
where $v_f$ denotes the number of finite vertices.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a generic linear function on the bounded Klein closure.
Let $E_v$ count the edges of the simple vertex figure, and let
$E_v^0$ count those not cut by the induced level section. The
incidence identities of Khovanskii~\cite[Theorem 9]{Khovanskii} give
\[
 \Dfive(P)=\sum_v(5E_v^0/2-E_v).
\]
The index distribution of an oriented simple polytope is its
$h$-vector. Assign each uncut edge below the section to its upper
endpoint, and each above it to its lower endpoint. An endpoint of
index $j$ receives at least $\min(j,57-j)$, so $E_v^0\ge G_v$.
Since each figure is simple of dimension $57$, $E_v=57V_v/2$.

At the global minimum and maximum the whole figure is uncut.
Symmetry and $h_j\ge1$ give, at each of these two vertices,
\[
 E_v-G_v=\sum_{j=29}^{57}h_j(2j-57)\ge841.
\]
The resulting correction is $2(5/2)841=4205$.
At a finite vertex, $G=812$ and $V=58$, hence $\kappa=377$.
Substitution gives~\eqref{eq:lower}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Positive ideal surplus]\label{prop:surplus}
Every ideal vertex in dimension $58$ satisfies $\kappa(v)-C(v)\ge36$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For independent uniform variables $U_r$ on $\{0,\ldots,r\}$, put
$S=\sum_iU_{r_i}$. Then
\[
 \frac GV=\frac{57-\mathbb E|2S-57|}{2}.
\]
For $1\le a\le b$, $U_{a-1}+U_{b+1}$ dominates
$U_a+U_b$ in convex order. This follows by comparing the
stop-loss functions at integer thresholds; the full elementary
calculation is the ``Concentrating two simplex ranks'' lemma in
Addendum FV. Conditioning proves preservation under adding a
common independent summand.

Apply this transfer while two positive ranks are below eight.
The sum of $c$-costs is unchanged, the normalized curvature cannot
increase, and the product of factor orders decreases. The integer
potential $\sum_i r_i^2$ strictly increases and is bounded by $57^2$,
so the process terminates. Its final domain consists of partitions
of $57$ with all parts at least eight, except possibly one part
in $\{1,\ldots,7\}$.

Generate sorted partitions recursively by their first part and
multiply $1+t+\cdots+t^r$ by exact convolution. This finite
calculation has $2552$ terminal profiles and gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal}
 \min\left(\frac\kappa V-\sum_i c(r_i)\right)
 =\frac{87}{10240}>0,\qquad
 \min\left(\kappa-V\sum_i c(r_i)\right)=36.
\end{equation}
The complete domain, coefficients and exact comparisons are supplied
with the ``Complete terminal calculation'' in Addendum FV.
The recursion terminates because the remaining sum decreases;
the unique first part proves that each partition is generated once.

Write the original normalized margin as $m$ and the terminal one
as $m'$, with products $V,V'$. The transfer gives $m\ge m'>0$
and $V\ge V'$. Thus $Vm\ge V'm'\ge36$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:envelope} proves the assertion for actual charges.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The contradiction and the higher-dimensional tail}
Proposition~\ref{prop:local} and Lemma~\ref{lem:payment} give
\[
 \Dfive(P)\le377v_f+\sum_{v\text{ ideal}}C(v).
\]
Subtracting~\eqref{eq:lower} and using
Proposition~\ref{prop:surplus} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fvcontradiction}
 0\ge4205+\sum_{v\text{ ideal}}(\kappa(v)-C(v))
   \ge4205+36v_i>0.
\end{equation}
Hence dimension $58$ is impossible, including the case $v_i=0$.

\begin{proposition}[Independent higher-dimensional exclusion]
\label{prop:fvtail}
There is no finite-volume hyperbolic Coxeter polytope in any
dimension $n\ge59$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof and addendum reference]
The range $59\le n\le163$ is treated by the dimension-specific
payment families in Addendum FV's ``The bounded higher-dimensional
tail.'' Each family has its own finite maximum, affine envelopes
and complete terminal-profile comparison. The ranges are
$59$--$65$, $66$--$97$ and $98$--$163$; all local replacement
conditions are included before the charge comparison is applied.

For $n\ge164$, the ``Unbounded tail: actual framing walls and two
budgets'' uses Nikulin's narrow-facet theorem~\cite{NikulinNarrow}.
The proof constructs actual framing walls and proves a Lorentz
inequality with the two original endpoint budgets. Its point
potentials, full-budget classical estimate and ordered packing
control the complete unbounded range. The alternative in which
the chosen narrow facet is compact has its own dimension estimate.
These arguments concern the original chamber walls and do not
require that an arbitrary facet be a Coxeter polytope. The full
calculations, including their uniform rank reductions, are printed
in the cited addendum.
\end{proof}
Combining Proposition~\ref{prop:fvtail} with
\eqref{eq:fvcontradiction} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(i).

\section{The compact bound}\label{sec:compact}
\subsection{One original vector and legal reference vectors}
Normalize wall normals to square one. The positive projections
of Lemma~\ref{lem:positive} check every original wall.
For compact $P$, fix $z$ strict interior and a vertex $p$ farthest
from $z$. Retain this original choice throughout. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:original}
 z=E(p-x),\quad E=-\ip zp>0,\quad x\in p^\perp,
 \quad \norm x^2=1-E^{-2}<1.
\end{equation}
The incident diagram has exactly $n$ nodes. Its whole irreducible
components $T$ give orthogonal projections with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \sum_T e_T=\norm{x}^2<1,\qquad e_T=\norm{x_T}^2.
\end{equation}
Every term uses this same original vector. On a geodesic the positive
function $-\ip zr$ has positive second derivative, so its maximum
on a compact polytope occurs at a vertex. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:allpoint}
 -\ip zr\le E\quad(r\in P).
\end{equation}
If $q$ is another vertex and $z=E_q(q-y)$, comparisons there use
$\kappa=E/E_q$; $q$ is not assumed farthest.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pointpressure}
If $\xi=Ap-U$ is future timelike, $U\in p^\perp$, $\xi^2=-k<0$,
and $\xi/\sqrt k\in P$, then for every Euclidean subspace $S\subset p^\perp$
containing $U$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pointenergy}
 \ip xU\ge A-\sqrt k,\qquad
 \norm{x_S}^2\ge\frac{A-\sqrt k}{A+\sqrt k}.
\end{equation}
At $q$ the first bound is $\ip yU\ge A-\kappa\sqrt k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Here $x_S$ is the orthogonal projection of $x$ to $S$. Substitute $\xi/\sqrt k$ into \eqref{eq:allpoint} and use
$\norm U^2=A^2-k$ and Cauchy's inequality. The zero-displacement
case follows directly.
\end{proof}

Let $B$ be the incident Gram matrix, $C=B^{-1}$, and
$\omega_i=\sum_jC_{ij}n_j$, $d_i=C_{ii}$, $u_i=\omega_i/\sqrt{d_i}$.
Every actual wall not through $p$ has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actualinterior}
 c=Ap-v,\quad v=\sum_i\beta_i\omega_i,\quad
 \beta_i=-\ip c{n_i}\ge0,\quad A^2=\beta^TC\beta-1,
 \quad \ip xv<A.
\end{equation}
For an original edge of length $L_i$ in direction $-u_i$, comparison
at its endpoint gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge}
 \ip x{u_i}\ge\tanh(L_i/2).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Reciprocal shadows]\label{prop:shadows}
Put $T_i=(C\beta)_i/(A\sqrt{d_i})$. If $c$ closes edge $i$,
then $\tanh L_i=1/T_i$; otherwise $\tanh L_i\ge T_i$.
Thus $T_i>1$ forces closure and $T_i=1$ is impossible. Distinct
opposites of $i,j$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shadow}
 \tanh L_i\tanh L_j\ge K_{ij},\qquad
 K_{ij}=C_{ij}/\sqrt{d_i d_j}.
\end{equation}
Opposites of orthogonal original directions are distinct.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the actual opposite $d=Dp-\sum\gamma_j\omega_j$ of $i$,
the endpoint equation gives $\tanh L_i=D\sqrt{d_i}/(C\gamma)_i$.
If $c\ne d$, acuteness and the nonnegative Schur inverse give
\[
 AD\ge\beta^TC\gamma\ge
 (C\beta)_i(C\gamma)_i/d_i.
\]
Here $C-C_{\cdot i}C_{i\cdot}/d_i$ is zero on row and column $i$
and elsewhere is the inverse of the retained Gram matrix. This proves
the alternative. For the opposite of $i$, the same Schur identity
gives $T_j\ge K_{ij}/\tanh L_i$; distinctness at $j$ gives
the pair inequality.
If one wall closed orthogonal $i,j$, clearing the other old walls
by positive coefficients would give
$c_F=Ap-Au_i/r_i-Au_j/r_j$, $r_i=\tanh L_i$.
Positive clearing keeps $\ip z{c_F}<0$, so strict interiority requires $\ip x{u_i}/r_i+\ip x{u_j}/r_j<1$.
By \eqref{eq:edge} the sum is at least
$1+(\tau_i^2+\tau_j^2)/2>1$, where $\tau_i=\tanh(L_i/2)$.
\end{proof}

An opposite wall contacts each retained foreign component at most
once, since its finite replacement has no cycle. A positive foreign
cost is $q=\beta_j^2C_{jj}\ge1/4$; the only cost below $1/3$ is
a simple $3$-contact on $A_1$, of cost $1/4$. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:foreigncost}
 q<A^2:
\end{equation}
otherwise the reciprocal test closes that orthogonal direction too
(or gives a null endpoint at equality). For unit directions with
correlation $k$ in a component of energy at most $e$, put
$F(t)=2t/(1+t^2)$. Cauchy and concavity of $\log F$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairupper}
 \tau_i+\tau_j\le\sqrt{2e(1+k)},\qquad
 F(\tau_i)F(\tau_j)\le\frac{4s}{(1+s)^2},\quad s=e(1+k)/2.
\end{equation}
If this upper bound is below $K_{ij}$, the opposites coincide.

\paragraph{Actual points and simultaneous references.}
For an actual cap $c=Ap-v$, clear an old positive block $J$ by
$w=\sum_{i,j\in J}(B_J^{-1})_{ij}\beta_j n_i$. Set
$\eta=c+w$, $\delta=\eta^2=1-\beta^TB_J^{-1}\beta$.
If $\delta<0$, normalize $\eta$; if $\delta>0$, use
$p+(A/\delta)\eta$, of square $-1-A^2/\delta$.
Once selected products are nonpositive, all unselected products
follow from the positive normal coefficients. The respective
pressures are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corepressure}
 \ip x{v-w}\ge A-\sqrt{-\delta},\qquad
 \ip x{v-w}\ge\frac A{1+\sqrt{\delta/(A^2+\delta)}}.
\end{equation}
A nonzero feasible future null vector contradicts compactness.

For any Euclidean reference $W$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square}
 \norm x^2\ge2\ip xW-\norm W^2.
\end{equation}
Suppose $w=\sum b_iP_i$, $b_i\ge0$, and actual pressures give
$\ip x{P_i}>a_i$. A correction $Q=Z-\sum t_iP_i$ with
$\ip xZ\ge h$ is legal at weight $\mu\ge0$ only if every
$b_i-\mu t_i\ge0$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:payment}
 \ip x{(w+\mu Q)}\ge\sum b_i a_i+\mu(h-\sum t_i a_i),\qquad
 \norm{w+\mu Q}^2=\norm w^2+2\mu\ip wQ+\mu^2\norm Q^2.
\end{equation}
For several corrections retain their total debit at each anchor and
every mutual cross term. Strictness survives a positive remaining
strict anchor or a positively weighted strict new pressure. All
component sums below concern projections of the same original $x$.

For $P_i=e_1+\cdots+e_i$, increasing concave interpolation through
$(0,0)$ and actual floors $\ip x{P_i}>f_i$ gives a reference with
successive slopes as its coordinates. Its coefficients in the $P_i$ are nonnegative slope
decreases, and its demand equals its square. Deleting a knot between intervals of lengths $a,b$ and slopes
$s_1,s_2$ loses $ab(s_1-s_2)^2/(a+b)$. For unit adjacent
spacings this is half the squared slope difference. For the
$\sqrt i-\mathrm{constant}$ floors at $i\ge5$, such a unit-neighbor
loss is $<1/3200$; nonadjacent omissions have disjoint intervals
and their losses add. End omissions use their exact chord costs.
An omitted floor has coefficient zero.

\section{The compact arithmetic bound}\label{sec:arithmetic}
\subsection{The actual arithmetic field}
Arithmeticity supplies an admissible quadratic-form model over
the actual totally real field $K$~\cite[Lemma 5.2]{ABSW}.
Root lines are $K$-defined, invariant cyclic products are algebraic
integers, and the nonphysical real forms are positive definite.
For roots $r_i$ put $a_{ij}=2\ip{r_i}{r_j}/\ip{r_i}{r_i}$.
The cycles in the $a_{ij}$ are used without assuming that physical
unit roots are themselves $K$-valued or that disconnected root
lines have the same squareclass.

If an actually permitted rescaling defines an indefinite principal
Gram over $k\subset K$, an additional embedding fixing $k$ would
preserve its indefiniteness. Nonphysical positivity therefore forces
$K=k$. Norms of nonzero algebraic integers, together with bounds at
every nonphysical embedding, give the degree reductions. The
golden-field branch uses Bugaenko's published lattice
theorem~\cite{BugaenkoGolden1984} after the invariant-lattice
identification proved in Addendum A. The rational field is
excluded by anisotropy and Meyer's theorem in the dimensions at
issue~\cite{Belolipetsky}.


\subsection{Paying all isolated constraints together}
The arithmetic component estimates use a common analytic step. It
explains why overlapping local pressures can be combined without
optimizing different vectors independently.
\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous absorption]\label{lem:absorption}
Let $n_i$ be incident unit normals, $\omega_i$ their duals, and
$w=\sum_i\beta_i\omega_i$ with $\beta_i=\ip{w}{n_i}\ge0$.
Suppose $E_w=\norm{w}^2=\sum_i\beta_iH_i$. Outside a set $J$,
assume $\ip{x}{\omega_i}\ge H_i$. For $i\in J$ suppose
$\beta_i>0$, $0<u_i<1$, and
\[
 \ip{x}{\omega_i-u_i n_i}\ge H_i-\delta_i,\qquad
 2\delta_i<\beta_i(2u_i-u_i^2).
\]
Then $\norm{x}^2\ge E_w$, strictly if $J$ is nonempty or some
positively weighted retained pressure is strict.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the single vector $Z=w-\sum_{i\in J}\beta_i u_i n_i$.
The pressures and $\ip{n_i}{n_j}\le0$ for $i\ne j$ give
\[
 \ip{x}{Z}\ge E_w-\sum_{i\in J}\beta_i \delta_i,\qquad
 \norm{Z}^2\le E_w-\sum_{i\in J}\beta_i^2(2u_i-u_i^2).
\]
Substitution in~\eqref{eq:square} proves the assertion, including
its strictness. In particular adjacent isolated sites are allowed.
\end{proof}

For an actual cap isolated within its component in the retained
range $0<u<1$, retain its full foreign projection and write
\[
 c=Ap-\gamma\omega_i-v_{\rm for},\quad Q=\norm{v_{\rm for}}^2,
 \quad u=(1-Q)/\gamma^2\in(0,1),\quad D_i=\norm{\omega_i}^2.
\]
Positive clearing gives the actual vector
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \eta=c+v_{\rm for}+\gamma u n_i
 =\gamma\{\sqrt{D_i-u}\,p-(\omega_i-un_i)\},\\
 \eta^2=-\gamma^2u(1-u).
\end{gathered}
\]
It is future pointing, zero against the contacted foreign block
and $c$, negative against $n_i$, and nonpositive against every
other wall. Lemma~\ref{lem:pointpressure} therefore gives
\[
 \ip{x}{\omega_i-un_i}\ge\sqrt{D_i-u}-\sqrt{u(1-u)}.
\]
The loss from $\sqrt{D_i}$ is at most
$a_i u+\sqrt{u(1-u)}$, where
$a_i=(\sqrt{D_i}+\sqrt{D_i-1})^{-1}$ in the chain ranges used.
Thus a reference with $H_i+\ell<\sqrt{D_i}$, positive prices
$\beta_i\ge b$, and $a_i\le a$ absorbs every such foot whenever
\[
 au+\sqrt{u(1-u)}-\frac b2(2u-u^2)<\ell
 \qquad(0<u<1).
\]
The actual cap inventories, golden-helper exclusions and numerical
reference coefficients are catalogue item A0. Their complete
proofs are in Addendum A; the lemma proves the shared analytic
combination used by those component estimates.

mean is

Put $N_H=\N U$ and $A=-\N(U-2)=2\Tr U-4-N_H$.

At a prime $\mathfrak p$ above two let

 r+k,r+l,k+l\ge2e,\qquad
 r+s+e+k\equiv r+s+e+l\equiv0\pmod2.
\end{equation}
Also $s=r$ for $r<e$, $s=e$ for $r>e$, and $s\ge e$ for $r=e$.

\begin{proposition}[Dyadic terminal alternatives]\label{prop:dyadic}
The necessary integral conditions reduce any actual remaining
configuration to one of the following two possibilities:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},nosep]
\item $K=\Q(\sqrt6)$, $U=5+2\sqrt6$, and $F=G=4U$;
\item $K=\Q(\sqrt{57})$, $U=(9+\sqrt{57})/2$, and

\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

and the norm bounds leave bounded valuation possibilities.

discriminant bound give the complete unit-fork list.
All other branches are treated by the full integer-order strips

norm with ramified two'' and ``The complete split branch.''
The split head list has seventeen rows. Its valuation and full

Both possibilities contradict the norm bound for the original
vector. In (a) the actual head and fork edge cosines satisfy
\[

 C_+=C_-=2(2+\sqrt6)>44/5.

Equation~\eqref{eq:edge} gives $x_1>.61$ and, for both signs,
\[

\]
Averaging gives $S_{15}>3.56$. Interpolating at indices $1,6,15$
with $S_6>2.01815$ yields
\[
 \norm{x}^2>.61^2+\frac{1.40815^2}{5}
                   +\frac{1.54185^2}{9}
 =1.032821887>1.
\]

In (b), $U>8$ gives $x_1>1/\sqrt3>.577$. Rebuilding the
first-five block of the reference in Proposition~\ref{prop:d16reduce},
with its sum fixed, gives gain greater than
\[
 E_{14}+\frac54(.577-.361)^2>.96672.
\]
If either actual fork had $\cosh L\ge9$, its signed pressure

the first-block correction, would then give
\[
 \norm{x}^2>.96672+.26(3.577-3.311)-.0338=1.00208>1.
\]
Thus both fork cosines are below nine. In the isolated fork
normalization $C_F^2=2F$, so $F<81/2$, whereas
$F=23+3\sqrt{57}>44$. This excludes (b).

There is no whole $D_{16}$ configuration. Together with
Proposition~\ref{prop:arithreduce}, this excludes ranks $16$ and
