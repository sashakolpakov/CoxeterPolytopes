\begin{abstract}
We prove that a finite-volume hyperbolic Coxeter polytope has dimension
at most $57$, that a compact one has dimension at most $17$, and that a
compact arithmetic one has dimension at most $15$. The finite-volume
argument compares corner weights with the geometry of vertex links.
The compact arguments use projections of one vector associated with an
original farthest vertex; arithmeticity further restricts the ground
field and integral wall parameters. We explain the three proof strategies,
prove their central geometric and arithmetic steps, and give precise
technical propositions from which the global bounds follow. Complete
technical proofs, finite inventories and computational certificates are
provided in a versioned GitHub supplement, with a reproduction guide and
selected conditional Lean kernels.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
All polytopes in this article are convex, finite-sided and
full-dimensional. A hyperbolic Coxeter polytope has dihedral angles
that are integer submultiples of $\pi$; reflection in its facets
generates a discrete group with the polytope as fundamental domain.
Hyperbolic space has curvature $-1$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $P\subset\HH^n$ be a finite-volume hyperbolic Coxeter polytope.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},nosep]
\item $n\le57$;
\item if $P$ is compact, then $n\le17$;
\item if $P$ is compact and its reflection group is arithmetic, then
 $n\le15$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The classical finite-volume ceiling is $995$, due to
Prokhorov~\cite{Prokhorov}, using the combinatorial estimates of
Khovanskii~\cite{Khovanskii}. Vinberg obtained the compact ceiling
$29$~\cite{Vin84,Vin85}. The largest known finite-volume examples
have dimension $21$~\cite{Borcherds}; the bounds here are upper bounds
and make no new existence assertion. Compactness is essential in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(iii).

Guo and Zheng recently obtained the general finite-volume bound
$81$~\cite{GuoZheng2026}. An earlier version of our bound $57$,
including its corner-weight method and higher-dimensional argument,
was publicly posted on 21 October 2026~\cite{FirstDisclosure}.
The present article condenses the three proof families assembled in
the repository edition of 30 October~\cite{Addenda}.

The methods separate after the common local geometry. In the
finite-volume case, triangles and quadrilaterals require corner
payments that high-dimensional vertex diagrams cannot accommodate.
Nonsimple ideal vertices are treated through the product structure
of their links. In the compact case, local walls force quadratic
lower bounds for components of a single vector of norm less than one.
Arithmeticity enters only in the third argument, through integral
cycles and positivity at the other real embeddings.

\section{Actual walls and vertex diagrams}\label{sec:geometry}
We use a Lorentz space of signature $(-,+,\ldots,+)$ and the future
unit hyperboloid model of $\HH^n$. Outward wall normals are spacelike;
the chamber inequalities are $\ip{y}{e_i}\le0$. Distinct wall normals
have nonpositive products. Finite vertex diagrams are spherical.
A horospherical link at an ideal vertex is a product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:link}
 L_v=\prod_i\Delta^{r_i},\qquad \sum_i r_i=n-1,
\end{equation}
whose factors correspond to irreducible affine diagrams of orders
$r_i+1$. These standard face and link facts are used in their
actual-wall form~\cite{Vin85,FeliksonTumarkin}.

Two elementary observations account for much of the local geometry.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positive}
A positive-definite Gram matrix with nonpositive off-diagonal entries
has entrywise nonnegative inverse. A positive-definite principal set
of actual chamber walls has a feasible finite common point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $B$ is the matrix, choose $s$ larger than its largest eigenvalue.
Then $M=sI-B$ is entrywise nonnegative and has spectral radius less
than $s$, so $B^{-1}=s^{-1}\sum_{j\ge0}(M/s)^j$.

Take a strict interior future timelike point $o$ and put
$b_i=-\ip{o}{e_i}>0$ for the selected walls. The vector
\[
 q=o+\sum_i(B^{-1}b)_i e_i
\]
is orthogonal to the selected normals and has square
$q^2=o^2-b^TB^{-1}b<0$. Its product with $o$ is negative, so it
is future pointing. Every other chamber inequality is strict,
because the coefficients are nonnegative and all other wall
products are nonpositive. Normalize $q$ to the hyperboloid.
\end{proof}

An irreducible affine principal set has a positive kernel vector
$(u_i)$. The root combination $\nu=\sum_i u_i e_i$ is null,
nonzero since $\ip{o}{\nu}<0$, and feasible against every wall.
It represents an actual ideal point. Irreducibility and the positive
definiteness of proper deletions identify its whole affine factor.
In contrast, a kernel vector in an indefinite core represents the
zero vector: that vector is null and orthogonal to a timelike
vector in the core span, whose perpendicular space is positive
definite. Both interpretations are needed when controlling extensions.

At an actual two-face, the supporting walls form a positive-definite
diagram of rank $n-2$. Adding one side gives an edge diagram, and
adding two adjacent sides gives a positive-semidefinite corner
diagram. For opposite sides of a quadrilateral, the corresponding
principal diagram is indefinite. A positive or affine alternative
would give a feasible common finite or ideal point of those sides.
Minimal indefinite subdiagrams, inverse Gram matrices and the
classification of finite and affine diagrams consequently reduce
the local payment questions to explicit marked configurations.


\section{The finite-volume bound}\label{sec:fv}
In dimension 58 the proof compares corner payments with the
curvature of vertex links. Its improvement comes from transferring
one unit away from eight extremal finite forests while preserving
the required face payments. Nonsimple cusps contribute a strictly
positive surplus. Separate dimension-specific rules, followed by
an all-rank narrow-facet argument, exclude the higher dimensions;
we do not assume that arbitrary faces inherit Coxeter angles.

We develop these mechanisms and their final deductions below.
Catalogue FV1--FV4 in the pinned Addendum FV supplies the exhaustive
local classifications, numerical tables and computational proofs
at the interfaces where they are used.

For each two-face $F$, let $s(F)$ be its number of sides, and put
\[
 \Dfive(P)=\sum_{\dim F=2}(5-s(F)).
\]
A nonnegative corner rule assigns $w(v,F)$ to an actual incidence
$(v,F)$; its charge at $v$ is $C(v)=\sum_{F\ni v}w(v,F)$.

\begin{lemma}[Payment comparison]\label{lem:payment}Suppose every triangular face receives total payment at least 2
and every quadrilateral receives at least 1. Then
 \[
 \Dfive(P)\le\sum_v C(v).
\]
More generally, suppose an incidence argument gives
 \[
 \Dfive(P)\ge Bv_f+\sum_{v\text{ ideal}}\kappa(v)+\Delta,
 \qquad\Delta>0,
\]
while a simultaneous payment rule has $C(v)\le B$ at every finite
vertex and $C(v)\le\kappa(v)-\varepsilon$ at every ideal vertex,
where $\varepsilon\ge0$. These hypotheses are inconsistent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A face with $s$ sides needs payment at least $5-s$. This is 2
for a triangle and 1 for a quadrilateral. For $s\ge5$ it follows
from nonnegativity. Sum over faces and then over their corners.
Combining the resulting upper bound with the proposed lower bound
gives $0\ge\Delta+\varepsilon v_i>0$.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Actual cores, extensions and whole components}
\label{fv:sec:finite-interface}
We use outward norm-two roots, with diagonal Gram entries 2 and
nonpositive off-diagonal entries. At an actual two-face in dimension $n$,
the supporting white roots form a positive-definite rank $n-2$ diagram.
White roots together with one black side give a strictly positive-definite
edge diagram; adjacent corners are positive semidefinite. A finite corner
has an elliptic forest of rank $n$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:positive} makes these diagrams geometric: a positive
principal set has an actual common finite point, strict against
all other walls. An irreducible affine set has a positive relation
giving a nonzero feasible null vector, hence an actual ideal point.
It is a whole cusp factor, since each proper principal subdiagram
is positive definite; see also~\cite[Section~1]{FeliksonTumarkin}.

\begin{lemma}[Opposite sides and minimal cores]\label{fv:lem:opposite-cores}
For either opposite pair of sides of an actual quadrilateral, the
supporting whites and that pair form an indefinite principal set.
An inclusion-minimal indefinite subset contains both marked sides.
Its order is at most ten; if all proper principal subsets are
elliptic, its order is at most five.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A positive set would have an actual common finite point by the
projection argument. A positive-semidefinite singular set would
have a common ideal point by its affine kernel. Opposite closed
sides of a nondegenerate quadrilateral are disjoint, so neither is
possible. Deleting either marked side leaves a subset of an actual
finite edge diagram. Hence both marks lie in every minimal
indefinite subset. The published Lann\'er/quasi-Lann\'er order
bounds now apply; the classification through order ten
is~\cite{Chein}.
\end{proof}

Two opposite cores cannot be orthogonal: their negative vectors
would give two negative directions. Their overlap or a joining
path therefore supplies an adjacent-side route. The local theorem
must restore every other supporting white. All presence conditions
refer to connected components of the \emph{whole actual corner
diagram}, not merely to the displayed core.

\begin{lemma}[Two ways to seal a component]\label{fv:lem:seals}
Let $G$ be the Gram matrix of an actual indefinite core.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A Gram-kernel vector $z$ of this core gives the actual zero
root relation $\sum_i z_i e_i=0$. Every further wall therefore
satisfies $\sum_i z_i(e,e_i)=0$.
\item Suppose $G$ is nonsingular, an added forest contacts it only
at a position $p$, and $(G^{-1})_{pp}=r>1$. If every connected
component of the added forest contacts $p$, its total rank is at
most $1/(r-1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The span of an indefinite core contains a timelike vector. The root
combination associated with a Gram-kernel vector is null and
orthogonal to that timelike vector. The perpendicular space is
positive definite, so the combination is zero. This differs from
the nonzero feasible null vector of an affine block.

For the second statement let $B$ be the positive Gram matrix of all
added supporting whites, including indirect tails, and let $u$ be
their contact column at $p$. Schur elimination and the one-negative-
direction condition give $B-r uu^T\succeq0$, whence
 $u^TB^{-1}u\le1/r$.

For a connected square-two finite Coxeter Gram of rank $k$, every
inverse diagonal is at least $k/(k+1)$. This follows by induction:
delete the chosen node, let the remaining component ranks be $k_j$,
and use a contact of magnitude at least one into each component.
Inverse positivity gives Schur cost at least
 $\sum_j k_j/(k_j+1)\ge(k-1)/k$. The resulting inverse diagonal
is at least $1/(2-(k-1)/k)=k/(k+1)$; the rank-one base is $1/2$.
For an added forest of total rank $M$ contacted in each component,
the same bound gives $u^TB^{-1}u\ge M/(M+1)$.
Thus $M/(M+1)\le1/r$, proving $M\le1/(r-1)$.
\end{proof}

The Schur argument includes every indirect tail. A radical seal
instead pairs its zero relation with every further root: the
contact signs force either an already paid shortcut or orthogonality.
An indirect extension has a first contacting root, so these tests
really control whole components.

At an ideal vertex the link is $\prod_i\Delta^{r_i}$, with
$\sum r_i=n-1$. A marked pair in factor $i$ occurs in
$V/(r_i+1)$ actual corner fibers, where $V=\prod_i(r_i+1)$:
an edge varies in that factor and chooses an arbitrary vertex
in every other factor. Thus an affine factor envelope $(r+1)c(r)$
gives the actual charge bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope}
 C_{\mathrm{act}}\le C_{\mathrm{up}}=V\sum_i c(r_i).
\end{equation}
The marked pair restores the varying affine factor; other factors
appear with one node deleted. Omitting these fiber multiplicities
would not count a nonsimple cusp correctly.

The intrinsic tables, including role priorities, are fixed in
Addendum FV, ``The intrinsic corner weights'' (FV1). They specify
within-component, cross-component and contextual finite weights
and the varying affine-factor weights. The preliminary rule below
restores the $B_{13}$ endpoint unit and omits the three rank-15
changes. All later modifications are to this one simultaneous rule.

\subsection{Why the final unit matters}

The finite calculation ranges over spherical forests of total rank 58.
Its alphabet consists of the classical components through that rank,
$E_6,E_7,E_8,F_4,H_3,H_4$, and a single cost symbol $I_2^*$ for the
remaining dihedral components. The last symbol is justified by the
all-label local theorem; it imposes no cutoff on the dihedral angle.
Every actual finite vertex lies in this domain. Its formal members
need not all be realized by polytopes.

After multiplying charges by 64, the complete cost has the form
\begin{align}\label{eq:forest}
C_{64}(m)&=\sum_T b(T)m_T+\sum_Tj(T,T)\binom{m_T}{2}\notag\\
&\quad+\sum_{T<U}j(T,U)m_Tm_U+\Psi(m).
\end{align}
The tables $b,j$ give component and cross-component costs; $\Psi$
contains the contextual additions. FV2 fixes context-sensitive
counts before an additive recurrence treats the residual rank.
Every last-component transition, not just a maximizing path,
is checked. The exact tables, complete domains and computational
proof are in Addendum FV, ``Contexts in the finite-forest
calculation.'' Their geometric identification with actual charges
is the separate local theorem FV1.

\begin{proposition}[Computed finite interface]\label{fv:prop:oldcount}
The preceding simultaneous rule in ambient 58 pays every triangle at
least 2 and every quadrilateral at least 1. Every actual rank 58 finite
forest has charge at most 377 except precisely the following eight formal
forests: one $B_{13}$ or $D_{13}$ together with any multiset of three
 $A_{15}$ or $D_{15}$ components. Each of those eight has charge $378$.
All ideal weights admit the envelope of the ideal-vertex discussion below.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Computed interface FV2]
The complete context cover and residual recurrence in FV2 dominate
every actual finite vertex. Reconstructing each full contextual
cost, including interactions, leaves only the stated eight formal
forests above 377; each has cost $78+3\cdot100=378$.
The local geometric coverage and triangle proof are supplied by
FV1. No realization of these extremal formal forests is asserted.
\end{proof}

Number $A_r$ by its path $0,\ldots,r-1$ and $B_r$ by the same
path with its last bond doubled. Number $D_r$ by the spine
$0,\ldots,r-3$ with leaves $r-2,r-1$ at $r-3$; its long endpoint
is $0$. These conventions describe actual whole components. The
intrinsic weight tables are invariant under their automorphisms.

The separate $B_{13}$ endpoint theorem removes pair $(0,12)$.
Two older affine-$B_{13}$ singleton routes genuinely used that
unit; their whole cores instead give distinct already-unit
$A_1/D_{12}$ short-leaf contributions. Pair $(1,12)$, needed
by the $D_{12}$ theorem, is retained. This repair, proved in FV1,
lowers the four $B_{13}$ extremal forests to 377.

\subsection{The dimension-specific final local rule}
\begin{theorem}[Three-rank 15 transfer]\label{fv:thm:local}
In ambient dimension 58, make these changes to the simultaneous rule:
\begin{enumerate}
\item In a whole finite $D_{13}$, give pairs $(0,11),(0,12)$ weight $1/2$
instead of 1 only if its actual corner has three distinct whole unmarked
 $A_{15}$ or $D_{15}$ components.
\item Add $1/2$ to whole $D_{28}$ pair $(0,12)$ only if its corner has two
such whole unmarked rank 15 components.
\item Add $1/2$ between an endpoint of whole $A_{12}$ and the long endpoint
of whole $D_{16}$ under the same two-component presence guard.
\end{enumerate}
All ideal weights and other finite weights remain. Every triangle still
pays at least 2 and every quadrilateral at least 1.
\end{theorem}
Witnesses are not counted with multiplicity. The negative guard uses
 $13+3\cdot15=58$ ranks, so the corner has exactly those four components.
The two positive guards similarly force exactly $D_{28}+2\cdot15$ or
 $A_{12}+D_{16}+2\cdot15$. The theorem is not asserted in larger ambient
dimensions.

\paragraph{Why the guard survives additional walls.}
FV1 classifies every affected core and route after restoring all
supporting whites. A new white either supplies an already paid
short path, violates a projection bound, or is restricted by a
zero root relation as in Lemma~\ref{fv:lem:seals}. Pairing such
a relation with every further wall either supplies a shortcut
or seals a small factor incompatible with three rank-15 witnesses.

The remaining growing-factor cases illustrate why a bound on
immediate neighbors is insufficient. Their old small factor $K$
has rank $s=1,2,3$, and all additional contacts, after excluding
paid shortcuts, meet one old position with inverse square
$r=4/3,3/2,2$, respectively. Include \emph{all} added whites in
a forest $Y$, of total rank $M$, with contact column $u$. The
Lorentz condition gives
\[
 G_Y-r uu^t\succeq0,\qquad
 \frac{M}{M+1}\le u^tG_Y^{-1}u\le\frac1r.
\]
Thus $M\le3,2,1$ and the whole component has rank at most four.
This proves the guard impossible in each such case, however long
an indirect extension was initially allowed to be. FV1 supplies
the finite classification placing every affected route in one of
these cases, including the proper completions and all contact signs.

\paragraph{Why an affine route needs a new payment.}
An older affine-$D_{12}$ route really uses the lowered $D_{13}$
unit, so it requires a repair rather than a retention argument.
This case explains the two new half-payments. Number the affine
factor by spine $0,\ldots,8$, leaves $9,10$ at 0 and $11,12$ at 8.
Take opposite-end marks $a=9,b=11$, with the other two black
roots $c,d$. Let $Z$ be the orthogonal elliptic spectator forest,
and $x,y$ their nonnegative contact rows on $Z$.

An adjacent-black bond or shared spectator neighbor is an
independently paid short path. Otherwise $x,y$ have disjoint
supports. The all-label end-profile reduction in FV1 gives costs
$g_c,g_d\le8$, with a nonextreme end costing at most four, and
the exact Schur identity
\[
 \frac{\det S}{h^2k^2}
 =1-\frac{g_c+g_d}{12}
  +\frac1{12}(x/h-y/k)^tG_Z^{-1}(x/h-y/k),
\]
where $h,k>0$ are its height scales. The actual four-black star
has determinant zero. If an end is nonextreme, equality forces
costs eight and four and $x=y=0$. The complete equality cases
give a retained short-leaf unit, an independent affine-$B_{13}$
priority, or a second guarded $D_{13}$ half at another corner.
Those cases, including the earlier $B_{13}$ endpoint repair, are
proved separately in FV1; the latter does not depend on the
newly financed $D_{13}$ route.

When both ends are extreme, $h=k=1/2$ and the identity becomes
\begin{equation}\label{fv:eq:energyone}
 (x-y)^tG_Z^{-1}(x-y)=1.
\end{equation}
The affected output lies in the one-sided branch, say $y=0$;
the two-sided branch keeps its short paths or sealed alternatives.
If the quiet corner has the new guard, $Z$ consists of exactly
three whole $A_{15}/D_{15}$ factors. The following local consequence
makes the compensation transparent.

\begin{lemma}\label{fv:lem:rank 15}
Under the strict edge conditions and disjoint-support hypothesis,
energy one on three whole $A_{15}/D_{15}$ factors forces a single
unit contact at the long endpoint of one $D_{15}$, by one black
root. Both black roots are orthogonal to the other two factors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Reduction to the profiles in FV1]
The inverse diagonals of $A_{15}$ below two are
$15/16,\allowbreak 7/4,\allowbreak 7/4,\allowbreak 15/16$ at nodes $0,1,13,14$. In $D_{15}$ only
the long endpoint has inverse diagonal below two, namely one.
Strict edge positivity and the native contact gap leave the
simple contacts at those $A_{15}$ nodes, doubled contacts at its
endpoints, and the single unit $D_{15}$ contact. The complete
inverse-profile check in FV1 also treats disjoint two-sided
differences: every nonzero component energy is at least $15/16$,
and no $A_{15}$ profile has energy one. Two active components
would cost at least $15/8>1$. Equation~\eqref{fv:eq:energyone}
therefore leaves just the stated contact.
\end{proof}

The two inactive factors remain whole: they are orthogonal to
the entire star and its active factor. Attaching $c$ to the
selected leaf yields, at the opposite corner, whole $A_{12}$
at an endpoint and whole $D_{16}$ at its long endpoint.
Attaching it to the mate yields whole $D_{28}$ at $(0,12)$.
The new half in either case is guarded by the two inactive
rank-15 factors and lies at a different corner from the quiet
$D_{13}$ half. The required unit is therefore restored.

\paragraph{Retention is part of the simultaneous rule.}
FV1 proves that every older selected output is retained or receives
one of these replacements. In particular the changed distance is
eleven, while the triangle proof uses distinct short paths of
length at most five, paths of length at most seven, or protected
$E_8$ outputs. Those protections and the affine triangle exclusions
are part of the complete actual-face proof. Pairwise tests in an
abstract corner diagram would not suffice. This finite retention
classification, the whole-component seals, and the repaired
affine-$D_{12}$ route together prove Theorem~\ref{fv:thm:local}.

\subsection{The finite maximum after the transfer}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:local}Every actual finite rank 58 forest under the final simultaneous rule has
charge at most 377. The exact formal maximum is 377.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The $B_{13}$ theorem lowers four forests in Proposition~\ref{fv:prop:oldcount}
from $378$ to $377$. The new $D_{13}$ guard lowers the other four by 1. Every
other forest was already at most 377, except that the two new positive
coupon families must be checked.

Rank capacity makes those families exact. They have respectively
 \[
 170+2(100)+\tfrac12=370.5,
 \qquad66+108.5+2(100)+1=375.5.
\]
The second total counts both $A_{12}$ endpoint coupons. The previous
 $A_{12}/D_{16}$ cross cost is zero. Each family has three possible
multisets of two $A_{15}/D_{15}$ factors, so all six actual forests are
covered. No additional context witness can fit. Outside them the change
is pointwise nonincreasing. Thus the bound 377 holds everywhere, and the
eight changed forests attain it formally. A geometric realization of an
attainer is neither required nor asserted.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Ideal vertices and positive surplus}\label{fv:sec:ideal}
For affine ranks $r_i$ summing to 57 set
 \[
 \begin{gathered}
 H(t)=\prod_i(1+t+\cdots+t^{r_i})=\sum_{j=0}^{57}h_jt^j,
 \qquad V=\prod_i(r_i+1),\\
 G=\sum_jh_j\min(j,57-j),\qquad\kappa=\frac{5G-57V}{2}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The geometric all-family envelope is unchanged by the finite
transfer. Its function $c$ is fixed in FV3 (Addendum FV,
``Positive ideal surplus,'' \texttt{fv:sec:ideal}); in particular
\[
 c(r)=r/2\quad(1\le r\le8),\qquad
 c(r)=\frac{5r+56}{r+1}\quad(24\le r\le57).
\]
The remaining fifteen rational entries and their factor-charge
proofs are supplied there. We need the linear part for a
concentration argument, and the full table for one finite
calculation. The geometric premise is
$C_{\rm act}\le V\sum_i c(r_i)$ from~\eqref{eq:envelope};
evaluating a polynomial alone would not establish it.

Let $\mathcal P(n,8)$ denote the nondecreasing partitions of $n$
with parts at least eight, including the empty partition when
$n=0$. Define the terminal profiles
\begin{equation}\label{fv:eq:terminal-domain}
 \mathcal T_{57}=\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{7}
 \{(\ell,p):p\in\mathcal P(57-\ell,8)\},
\end{equation}
omitting a zero part. For any profile set
$m=\kappa/V-\sum_i c(r_i)$.

\begin{proposition}[Complete terminal calculation]\label{fv:prop:terminal}
The complete domain~\eqref{fv:eq:terminal-domain} has 2552 profiles.
Exact polynomial convolution and rational evaluation give
\[
 \min_{\mathcal T_{57}}m=\frac{87}{10240}>0,
 \qquad \min_{\mathcal T_{57}}Vm=36.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Computational proof in FV3]
Sorted-partition recursion lowers the remaining rank at each step;
the unique first part and its sorted tail establish completeness
and absence of duplicates. Repeated convolution with
$1+t+\cdots+t^r$ gives every coefficient of $H$, and hence $V,G$
and each rational margin. The certificate and its checker cover
every profile and coefficient list before taking the two minima.
The full algorithms, correctness proofs and recorded outputs are
in Addendum FV, \texttt{fv:prop:terminal}, identified by FV3.
The normalized and absolute minima are distinct assertions;
they need not have the same attainer.
\end{proof}

The reason this finite domain controls arbitrarily many small
simplex factors is convex order. Moving one rank from a smaller
factor to a larger one spreads the link's index distribution,
which reduces the curvature available to pay for corners.

\begin{lemma}[Concentrating two simplex ranks]\label{fv:lem:concentration}
Let $1\le a\le b$ be integers, and let $U_r$ be uniform on
 $\{0,\ldots,r\}$. With independent summands,
$U_{a-1}+U_{b+1}$ dominates $U_a+U_b$ in convex order.
Adding any common independent summand preserves this order.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both sums have mean $(a+b)/2$ and are symmetric about that mean.
For an integer threshold $t$, let $\Delta_t$ be the difference
of their expectations of $(X-t)_+$. Equal means allow us to use
 $(t-X)_+$ instead, and symmetry gives
 $\Delta_t=\Delta_{a+b-t}$. It suffices to take
 $0\le t\le(a+b)/2$. Put $D=(a+1)(b+1)$ and $D'=a(b+2)$.
For $0\le t\le a$, direct summation of the increasing side of
the two convolution triangles gives
 \[
 \Delta_t=\binom{t+2}{3}\left(\frac1{D'}-\frac1D\right)\ge0,
 \qquad D-D'=b-a+1>0.
\]
For $a\le t\le(a+b)/2\le b$, summing over the first uniform
instead gives
 \[
 \Delta_t=
 \frac{t(a+b-t)-(a-1)(a+2b+2)/3}{2(b+1)(b+2)}.
\]
The numerator is increasing up to $(a+b)/2$. At $t=a$ it is
 $(a+2)(b-a+1)/3>0$. Outside the common support the difference
is zero; between integer thresholds it is linear. Thus all
stop-loss differences are nonnegative. On the integer support the
values of a convex function are an affine function plus a
nonnegative sum of these stop-loss functions, proving convex order. Conditioning
on a common independent summand proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Concentration below rank eight]\label{prop:surplus}Every actual ideal vertex has $\kappa-C_{\mathrm{act}}\ge36$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $1\le a\le b\le7$, replacing $(a,b)$ by $(a-1,b+1)$ preserves
normalized envelope cost, since all these ranks stay at most eight.
Lemma~\ref{fv:lem:concentration}, including its independent-summand
assertion, gives the required convex order. Since
 \[
 \frac GV=\frac{57-\mathbb E|2S-57|}{2},
\]
the transfer does not increase normalized curvature or normalized
margin. Its product decreases from $(a+1)(b+1)$ to $a(b+2)$. Iteration
terminates at ranks at least 8 and at most one positive rank below 8.
Indeed the integer potential $\sum_i r_i^2$ increases by
 $2(b-a+1)\ge2$ at every step and is bounded above by $57^2$.
Its initial value is at least 57, so at most 1596 steps occur.
Rank zero is omitted. No transfer passes through the changed rank 9
envelope. The stopping domain is exactly~\eqref{fv:eq:terminal-domain}.

Proposition~\ref{fv:prop:terminal} gives positive normalized terminal
margin and absolute envelope surplus at least 36. If the original and terminal quantities
are $(V,m)$ and $(V',m')$, then $V\ge V'$ and $m\ge m'>0$. Thus
 $Vm\ge V'm'\ge36$. This multiplication uses positivity. Finally
\eqref{eq:envelope} transfers the same lower bound to actual cusp charge.
\end{proof}


\subsection[Shape curvature and the contradiction in dimension 58]{Shape curvature and the contradiction in dimension $58$}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:shape}For a proposed ambient 58 polytope,
\[
 \Dfive\ge377v_f+\sum_{v\text{ ideal}}\kappa(v)+4205.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a generic linear function on the bounded Klein closure of
the polytope. Let $E_v$ be the number of edges of its simple vertex
figure and $E_v^0$ the number not cut by the induced level section.
If $I$ counts all polygon vertices with multiplicity, the published
incidence identities give $I=\sum_vE_v$ and
 $2f_2=\sum_vE_v^0$ \cite[Theorem~9]{Khovanskii}. Hence
 $\Dfive=\sum_v(5E_v^0/2-E_v)$.

Orient each vertex figure by its induced generic linear function.
Assign an uncut edge below the section to its upper endpoint and
one above the section to its lower endpoint. A vertex of index $j$
receives $j$ or $57-j$ contributions, at least their minimum.
The index distribution is its $h$-vector
\cite[Theorems~2 and~6]{Khovanskii}, so $E_v^0\ge G_v$.
At the distinct global minimum and maximum the whole
vertex figure is uncut, and symmetry gives
 \[
 E_v-G_v=\sum_{j>57/2}h_j(2j-57)\ge\sum_{j=29}^{57}(2j-57)=841.
\]
Every coefficient of a simplex or product-of-simplices $h$-polynomial is
at least 1. Adding the two extrema and multiplying by $5/2$ gives 4205,
counted once. For a finite vertex $h_j=1$, so
 \[
 G_0=\sum_{j=0}^{57}\min(j,57-j)=812,
 \qquad\frac{5G_0-57\cdot58}{2}=377.
\]
The remaining ideal terms are precisely $\kappa$.
\end{proof}

For faces with at least five sides,$5-s(F)\le0$. The nonnegative local
rule and Proposition~\ref{prop:local} therefore give
 \[
 \Dfive\le377v_f+\sum C_{\mathrm{act}}.
\]
Subtract Lemma~\ref{lem:shape} and use Lemma~\ref{prop:surplus}:
\[
 0\ge4205+\sum(\kappa-C_{\mathrm{act}})\ge4205+36v_i>0.
\]
This also excludes the all-finite case. Thus dimension 58 is impossible.

It remains to exclude dimensions at least $59$. We now give the separate
arguments used for that purpose.

\subsection{The unbounded tail: actual framing walls and two budgets}
\label{fv:app:narrow-tail}

The range $n\ge164$ requires a proof at all ranks, not a finite list
of partitions. We use Nikulin's narrow-facet theorem
\cite[pp.~654--655]{Nikulin}; its unrestricted statement is also given
in \cite[Section~4, p.~10]{NikulinNarrow}. A finite-volume chamber has a facet $F$
whose actual framing hyperplanes have pairwise distance strictly
less than $\operatorname{arccosh}7$. With outward normals of square 2
this implies
 \begin{equation}\label{fv:eq:narrow}
 -(x,y)<14
\end{equation}
for any two framing walls. This is the external narrowness input.
We prove the incidence, endpoint and scale statements needed to
apply it; they are not additional hypotheses on the cusp.

\subsubsection{Outgoing edges give actual framing walls}

Suppose first that $F$ contains a cusp $v$. Its horospherical link is
a product of affine Coxeter simplices. Write their ranks $r_a$,
with $\sum_ar_a=N=n-1$, and normalize every old root to square 2.
In each factor choose two omitted nodes in advance. In the factor
containing the normal $\alpha_s$ of $F$, both choices avoid $s$.
The two choices need not be distinct. If that factor has affine rank
one, use its other node twice; its modified target $2(r_a-1)/9$ is zero.
Deleting one node per factor gives a positive-definite support $S$
of $N$ roots and a vertex of the horospherical link. It therefore
defines an actual outgoing edge in $F$. Finite volume gives a
finite or ideal second endpoint.

At a finite endpoint choose any new wall $x$ terminating that edge.
It and $\alpha_s$ belong to a spherical diagram, so their rank-two
Gram is positive definite. At an ideal endpoint let $T_b$ be its
affine factors. The positive-definite support $S$ contains at most
 $\operatorname{rank}T_b$ nodes from each. The total rank $N$ forces
equality in every factor: exactly one node of each $T_b$ is missing.
If the factor containing $\alpha_s$ has rank at least 2, choose its
missing node as $x$; every pair in such a factor has finite angle.
If it is affine $A_1$, choose the missing node of another factor,
which is orthogonal to $\alpha_s$. Another factor exists in the
range at issue. Thus again the pair is positive definite.

To see that this is an actual ridge, start with a strict interior
timelike point $z$. Project it off the span of $x,\alpha_s$.
The projection correction is a nonnegative combination of those
normals, because their Gram is a positive-definite matrix with
nonpositive off-diagonal entries and nonnegative inverse.
Every other wall inequality stays strict, and the projected square
is even more negative. It gives a point of precisely their common
face. Hence $x$ is in the framing set controlled by
\eqref{fv:eq:narrow}. Apply the same construction to the second
omission tuple and call its terminating wall $y$.

Neither $x$ nor $y$ is an old deleted cusp wall: that wall is strict
along the outgoing edge away from $v$. Their old contact rows
 $p_j=-(x,\alpha_j)$ and $q_j=-(y,\alpha_j)$ are nonnegative,
and every nonzero entry is at least 1. For each affine factor let
 $c_a=\sum_j\delta_{aj}\alpha_j$, with $\delta_{aj}>0$ normalized
by $\min_j\delta_{aj}=1$. These are nonzero future null vectors,
since their pairing with an interior point is negative. They are
all positive multiples $k_ac$ of one cusp vector $c$. Therefore
 \[
 \sigma_a=\delta_a\cdot p=-(x,c_a),\quad
 \tau_a=\delta_a\cdot q=-(y,c_a),\quad
 q_0=\sigma_a/\tau_a
\]
have a common positive ratio. Indeed, if a row height vanished,
all its nonnegative contacts with that factor would vanish;
proportionality of the $c_a$ would force every factor height and
every old contact to vanish. A square-two normal would then lie
in the null orthogonal complement of the full cusp span, impossible.

For each endpoint the principal Gram $S+x$ or $S+y$ is positive
semidefinite. Its Schur complement gives the separate budgets
 \begin{equation}\label{fv:eq:two-budgets}
 \sum_aC_{0a}\le2,\qquad\sum_aC_{1a}\le2,\qquad
 C_{\ell a}=p_{\ell,S_a}^{\,t}G(S_a)^{-1}p_{\ell,S_a}.
\end{equation}
The inverse is entrywise nonnegative. No bound on a deleted-node
contact or on its Coxeter label has been used.

\subsubsection{The common-height Lorentz identity}

Choose a null partner $d_0$ with $(c,d_0)=-1$, and write
 $x=a c+b d_0+u$, $y=a'c+b'd_0+u'$ in the orthogonal Euclidean
quotient. Then $b=-(x,c)>0$, $b'=-(y,c)>0$. Since
 $(x/b-y/b')\perp c$ and $x^2=y^2=2$,
\[
 \left\|u/b-u'/b'\right\|^2
   =2/b^2+2/(b')^2-2(x,y)/(bb').
\]
The quotient is the orthogonal sum of the affine factor spaces.
In factor $a$ put
 $E_a=\|u_a/\sigma_a-u'_a/\tau_a\|^2$.
Since $\sigma_a=k_ab$ and $\tau_a=k_ab'$, the $k_a^2$ cancels and
the preceding identity becomes
 \begin{equation}\label{fv:eq:lorentz-product}
 -(x,y)=\frac12\sum_a\sigma_a\tau_aE_a-q_0-q_0^{-1}.
\end{equation}
This proves the scale cancellation for a product cusp; no
commensurability or chosen horosphere is required.
Let $M=\max(q_0,q_0^{-1})$ and
 $W_a=\min(\sigma_a,\tau_a)^2E_a$. Then
 $\sigma_a\tau_aE_a=MW_a$ and $q_0+q_0^{-1}\le2M$.

The prescribed omission choices have targets
 \[
 T_a=2r_a/9\quad\hbox{ordinarily},\qquad
 T_a=2(r_a-1)/9\quad\hbox{in the sole avoid-node factor}.
\]
Thus $\sum T_a=(2N-2)/9$. We next prove
 \begin{equation}\label{fv:eq:two-range}
 \sum_aW_a\ge
 \begin{cases}(2N-2)/9-4&1\le M\le21/20,\\
 (2N-2)/9-16/3&M\ge21/20.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
It follows from\eqref{fv:eq:lorentz-product} that
 $-(x,y)\ge M(N-37)/9$ in the first range and
 $-(x,y)\ge M(N-43)/9$ in the second. At $N=163+t$ these are at
least $14+t/9$ and $14+7t/60$, respectively. They contradict
\eqref{fv:eq:narrow} for every $t\ge0$.


\subsubsection{Local distances and the original endpoint budgets}

Each affine diagram has Euclidean point potentials $v_j$:
their squared distances $R(i,j)$ are the corresponding grounded
inverse-Gram quantities. Normalizing a contact row by its affine
height gives nonnegative weights of sum one on these potentials.
Thus the local energy $E$ is the squared distance between two
convex means. An omitted-node contact is included in the mean;
there is no bound on its magnitude.

For illustration, an $A$ cycle of order $m$ has
\[
 \delta_j=1,\qquad
 R(i,j)=h(m-h)/m,\qquad h=(i-j)\bmod m.
\]
The ordinary omissions are $0,\lfloor m/2\rfloor$. Rotation
avoids a prescribed node without weakening the estimate; at
$m=2$ the repeated allowed omission has modified target zero.
For $B,C,D$, partial sums of orthonormal coordinates give the
potentials and all-rank inequalities. Their complete formulas,
the exceptional grounded matrices, and the prescribed ordinary
and avoid-node choices are fixed in Addendum FV, ``Point potentials
and the complete local domain.'' Those specific choices are used
throughout the following interfaces.

\begin{lemma}[Full-budget classical unit charge]
\label{fv:lem:full-budget-classical-unit}
For the prescribed ordinary or avoid-node omissions of a classical
affine factor, let $C_0,C_1\le2$ be its actual retained costs and
$W=\min(\sigma,\tau)^2E$. If the common height ratio is at most
$21/20$, then
\[
 W+C_0+C_1\ge T,
 \qquad T=2r/9\ \hbox{or}\ 2(r-1)/9,
\]
with the second target in the avoid-node factor. This includes
arbitrary real contacts whose nonzero magnitudes are at least one,
and both possibilities for each omitted-node contact.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Geometric reduction and the local certificates in FV4]
For omissions $i,j$, write the two point means as $v_i+a,v_j+b$,
with contact weights $w_k,z_l$. Polarization gives
\begin{align}\label{fv:eq:full-budget-polarization}
E={}&R(i,j)-\sum_k w_k\{R(i,j)+R(i,k)-R(k,j)\}\notag\\
&-\sum_l z_l\{R(i,j)+R(j,l)-R(l,i)\}+\|a-b\|^2.
\end{align}
The omitted-node displacement is zero. Every full row height is
at least one, so $W\ge E$. If
$c_{ik}=\delta_k^2R(i,k)$, inverse positivity and the contact gap give
\[
 C_0\ge\sum_{k\text{ retained, contacted}}c_{ik},\qquad
 C_1\ge\sum_{l\text{ retained, contacted}}c_{jl}.
\]
These unit-contact costs therefore control the linear losses
without a bound on the full contact magnitudes. For $B,C,D$ the
coordinate proof partitions contacts into those whose cost already
exceeds $T$, and those whose linear loss is at most their unit cost.
The complete partition, including the modified $B_2$ case, is in
the full-budget lemma of FV4.

Here is the all-rank part for the $A$ cycles. If $m\ge18$, a
retained contact at cyclic distance at least three costs more than
two, since $3(m-3)/m>2$ and resistance increases to the antipode.
The remaining distances one and two have linear-loss to cost ratio
at most $(m+\varepsilon)/(m-2)$, where $\varepsilon=m\bmod2$.
Using the two original budgets in the polarization identity leaves
\[
 W+C_0+C_1-T\ge
 R(0,\lfloor m/2\rfloor)-T-\frac{4(2+\varepsilon)}{m-2}.
\]
For $m=18+2u$ its right side is
$(128+84u+4u^2)/(36(m-2))$; for $m=19+2u$ it is
$(360+1708u+252u^2+8u^3)/(36m(m-2))$. Both are positive
for every $u\ge0$. The remaining orders $2\le m\le17$
are the finite convex-quadratic domain of FV4. Its certificates
cover every retained support, both omitted-node states and the
common-height ratio constraint. In particular this interface holds
at the original budgets two, before selecting any failing factor.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why two ordered budgets must remain separate.}
A factor can fail the coefficient-one estimate only in a small
exceptional domain. Removing it changes the two endpoint budgets
by different amounts. Replacing them by their sum would lose the
restriction that makes the remaining loss affordable. The exact
local support classification and residual calculation used here
are the following two finite interfaces from FV4.

\begin{proposition}[Exceptional losses at the original budgets]
For the prescribed omissions and $1\le M\le21/20$, a factor with
$T-W>C_0+C_1$ is either an ordinary $E_8$, with cost floors
$(8/9,2/3)$ and target $16/9$, or an ordinary $E_7$/modified
$E_8$, with floors $(1/2,1)$ or $(1,1/2)$ and target $14/9$.
After such a factor is removed, the maximum residual loss is at
most $20/9$ under ordered budgets $(10/9,4/3)$, and at most
$22/9$ under $(1,3/2)$ or $(3/2,1)$. The residual bounds permit
at most one modified factor.
\end{proposition}

The classification is applied at the original budgets two:
Lemma~\ref{fv:lem:full-budget-classical-unit} first excludes
classical failures, and the exceptional proof checks every cheap
grounded support, with all inverse cross terms and both
omitted-node states. In particular its ordinary $E_7$ two-contact
case requires four distinct ordered full-support keys. Only after
this classification may the smaller residual budgets be used.

The residual proof is an ordered packing calculation. Every local
domain is dominated by one of 28 records with no larger ordered
cost floors, no smaller loss bound and the same modified flag.
A recurrence maximizes the sum of recorded losses subject to
each remaining endpoint budget and at most one modified record.
Each record consumes at least $1/2$ of total budget, so this
recursion has finite depth. FV4 supplies the domination proof,
continuous support certificates and every recurrence inequality
giving the stated maxima. The records are necessary envelopes,
not asserted simultaneous geometric realizations.

If no factor fails, summing its coefficient-one inequalities
bounds the total loss by four. Otherwise an $E_8$ failure leaves
$(10/9,4/3)$, so its own loss at most $16/9$ and residual loss at
most $20/9$ again sum to four. The other failures give
$14/9+22/9=4$. All further failures belong to the residual
calculation. Even if the removed factor was modified, permitting
one modified residual factor only increases the bound. This proves
the first line of~\eqref{fv:eq:two-range} without assuming a
residual budget before the first failure.

\paragraph{The other height range.}
FV4 also proves, for the same omissions and the full actual
contact domain,
\[
 W+\frac43(C_0+C_1)\ge T.
\]
For $B,C,D$ the coordinate coefficient-one proof already uses
only that both heights are at least one. For $A$, write
$C=C_0+C_1$. If $C\ge3T/4$, nonnegativity of $W$ suffices.
Below that threshold the contact gap and original budgets
restrict the relevant cyclic distances to one and two; in the
remaining range polarization leaves the nonnegative margin
$d(m-4d-3\varepsilon)/(3m)$. The small orders and exceptional
factors are the separately specified finite convex-quadratic
domains in FV4. Their certificates include the omitted-node
possibilities and every cheap support. Thus the inequality covers
all ranks, with no cutoff on a contact magnitude. Summing it and
using~\eqref{fv:eq:two-budgets} proves the second line of
\eqref{fv:eq:two-range}.

\subsubsection{A narrow facet without a cusp}

The alternative is a compact facet. We use its actual ambient
faces, without assuming its induced angles are Coxeter. A compact
two-face has positive-definite corner diagrams. For a triangle,
a minimal indefinite star contains all three black nodes and at
most seven whites. A spanning tree joining the marks supplies
two distinct corner paths of length at most eight. For a
quadrilateral, the whites with either opposite pair are indefinite:
a positive or affine block would give an actual finite or ideal
intersection of the opposite sides. Minimal opposite cores each
have at most eight whites and cannot be orthogonal. A shortest
path in their overlapping or adjacent union therefore joins
consecutive black sides through at most 17 edges. The complete
core argument is also recorded in FV4.

Consequently unit weight at ambient spherical distance at most
17 pays two on a compact triangle and one on a compact
quadrilateral. Its cost at an ambient finite vertex is at most
$17n$: components of rank at most 35 have at most
$\binom r2\le17r$ pairs; larger spherical components are paths
or $D$ forks, with at most $r$ pairs at each fixed distance.

A compact facet of dimension $d=n-1$ is simple. Its two-face
corners form a subset of these ambient vertex pairs, so their
total charge is at most $17(d+1)f_0$. Its symmetric nonnegative
$h$-vector has $h_0=h_d=1$. Pairing the coefficients
$\binom k2$ and $\binom{d-k}2$ gives
\[
 \frac{f_2}{f_0}>
 \begin{cases}d(d-2)/8&d\text{ even},\\
 (d-1)^2/8&d\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The endpoint coefficients make the inequality strict. Its average
required payment $5f_2/f_0-\binom d2$ therefore exceeds
$d(d-6)/8$ or $(d-1)(d-5)/8$. Subtracting the available
$17(d+1)$ gives, at $d=143+2t$ and $144+2t$, respectively,
\[
 t^2/2+36t+3/2,\qquad t^2/2+73t/2+19,
\]
both positive for $t\ge0$. Thus a compact facet forces $n\le143$.
The cusp and compact alternatives exhaust the finite-volume facet
supplied by the narrow-facet theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Independent higher-dimensional exclusion]\label{prop:fvtail}
There is no finite-volume hyperbolic Coxeter polytope in any dimension
$n\ge59$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The dimension-specific families exclude $59\le n\le163$. For
$n\ge164$, the framing-wall and two-budget argument gives
$-(x,y)\ge14$, contradicting narrowness, if the narrow facet has a cusp.
If it has none, the compact-facet comparison already excludes $n\ge144$.
These alternatives exhaust the facet supplied by the narrow-facet theorem.
\end{proof}
Together with the contradiction in dimension $58$, this proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(i).


\section{The compact bound}\label{sec:compact}
The compact argument turns the geometry at a farthest vertex into a
Euclidean budget. Each whole irreducible component consumes part of one
vector of squared norm less than one. The main difficulty is obtaining
simultaneous lower bounds: pressures from several actual walls may share
coordinates, and their best separate estimates cannot simply be added.
We first prove the projection and reference-vector principles that handle
this dependence, then explain the component reduction and the final
whole-$D_{18}$ mechanism.

Catalogue C1 supplies the complete component inventories and proper-row
proofs, and C2 supplies the terminal whole-component and higher-rank cases.
References with a prefix $B$ below denote exact numbered proofs in Part V
of the pinned complete edition, called Addendum C. We retain these
references at the points where their full cases enter the argument.

\begin{theorem}[Compact dimension bound]\label{compact:thm:main}
Every compact hyperbolic Coxeter polytope has dimension at most $17$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Farthest vertices and projections}
\label{compact:sec:geometry}
Use the hyperboloid model with Lorentz signature \((-+\cdots+)\).
Every wall has an outward unit spacelike normal \(n\), and the polytope
is given by \(\ip{r}{n}\leq0\). Distinct actual wall normals have
nonpositive products. Fix a strict interior point \(z\), choose an
actual vertex \(p\) farthest from \(z\), and write
 \begin{equation}
\label{eq:original}z=E(p-x),\qquad E=-\ip{z}{p}>0,\qquad
 x\in p^\perp,\qquad \norm{x}^{2}=1-E^{-2}<1.
\end{equation}
The space \(p^\perp\) is Euclidean. The incident normals at \(p\) form
a positive definite spherical diagram; compact acute simplicity
implies that there are exactly \(n\) of them in dimension \(n\).
The whole irreducible components $T$ have orthogonal projections with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \sum_T e_T=\norm{x}^2<1,\qquad e_T=\norm{x_T}^2.
\end{equation}
We retain these original normals and this original choice of
 \(p,z,x,E\) throughout an energy argument.

The function \(r\mapsto-\ip{z}{r}\) takes its maximum on a compact
hyperbolic polytope at a vertex. Its restriction to a unit speed
geodesic has positive second derivative equal to the function itself,
so a maximum on a segment occurs at an endpoint; successively
passing to proper faces proves the assertion. Consequently
 \begin{equation}
\label{eq:allpoint}-\ip{z}{r}\leq E\quad\text{for every actual point }r\in P.
\end{equation}
At an adjacent vertex \(q\), write \(z=E_q(q-y)\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:allpoint} gives a comparison factor \(E/E_q\),
not a claim that \(q\) is farthest.

\subsubsection{Positive inverse matrices and feasible projections}
Inverse positivity is Lemma~\ref{lem:positive}. We record its explicit
projection formula in the coordinates used below.

\begin{lemma}[An actual positive projection]
\label{compact:lem:projection}
Let \(n_j\), \(j\in J\), be actual wall normals with positive
definite Gram matrix \(B_J\), and let \(z\) be strict interior.
Put
 \[
 q_0=z-\sum_{i,j\in J}(B_J^{-1})_{ij}\ip{z}{n_j}n_i.
\]
Then \(q_0\) is future timelike, orthogonal to all selected walls,
and has negative product with every unselected actual wall.
Its normalization is a point of the original polytope.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The added coefficients are nonnegative by Lemma~\ref{lem:positive}.
For an unselected wall every added product is nonpositive and the
original product is negative; the selected products vanish.
Moreover
 \[
 q_0^2=z^2-
   (\ip{z}{n_i})_{i\in J}^{T}B_J^{-1}
   (\ip{z}{n_i})_{i\in J}<0.
\]
Since \(\ip{z}{q_0}=q_0^2<0\), it has future orientation.
All original wall inequalities survive normalization.
If fewer than \(n\) walls are selected this is a point on a face,
not an asserted full-dimensional vertex.
\end{proof}
The same sign check applies to positive combinations of actual
normals below: selected products are calculated explicitly; every
unknown original wall has nonpositive product with every summand.
A nonzero feasible future null vector would give a point of the
ideal boundary of \(P\), contradicting compactness. A positive
affine Coxeter kernel on actual walls is an obstruction after its
future sign and all remaining wall products have been checked.

\begin{lemma}[Future-point pressure]
\label{lem:pointpressure}Suppose \(U\in p^\perp\), \(\xi=A p-U\) is future timelike
with \(\xi^2=-k<0\),
and its normalization is feasible against every actual wall.
Then the same original vector satisfies
 \[
 \ip{x}{U}\geq A-\sqrt{k}.
\]
At a different actual vertex \(q\), use the decomposition
 \(\xi=Aq-U\) with \(U\in q^\perp\). With all-point comparison
factor \(\kappa\), the corresponding inequality is
 \(\ip{y}{U}\geq A-\kappa\sqrt{k}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The spatial hypothesis gives \(A=-\ip{p}{\xi}>0\).
Apply \eqref{eq:allpoint} to \(\xi/\sqrt{k}\):
\(-\ip{z}{\xi/\sqrt{k}}=E(A-\ip{x}{U})/\sqrt{k}\leq E\).
The modified comparison is the identical calculation.
\end{proof}
For any Euclidean subspace $S\subset p^\perp$ containing $U$, let $x_S$
be the orthogonal projection of $x$ onto $S$. If $U\ne0$, then
$\norm U^2=A^2-k$; Cauchy therefore gives
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:pointenergy}\norm{x_S}^2
 \geq\frac{A-\sqrt{k}}{A+\sqrt{k}}.
\end{equation}
The case $U=0$ gives the same nonnegative bound directly.
Normalize a future vector, not a spacelike correction.
For a cap \(c=A p-v\), clear a positive definite old block \(J\):
\[
 w=\sum_{i,j\in J}(B_J^{-1})_{ij}\beta_j n_i,\quad
 \beta_j=-\ip{c}{n_j}\geq0,\quad
 \eta=c+w,\quad \delta=\eta^2=1-\beta^TB_J^{-1}\beta.
\]
If \(\delta<0\), \(\eta\) is the future vector to normalize.
If \(\delta>0\), the actual finite projection instead uses
 \[
 p+\frac{A}{\delta}\eta,\qquad
 \left(p+\frac{A}{\delta}\eta\right)^2=-1-\frac{A^2}{\delta}.
\]
When its selected products are nonpositive, its positive
coefficients check all unselected walls. The pressures are
 \begin{equation}
\label{eq:corepressure}\ip{x}{v-w}\geq A-\sqrt{-\delta},\qquad
 \ip{x}{v-w}\geq
 \frac{A}{1+\sqrt{\delta/(A^2+\delta)}}.
\end{equation}
Old normals lie in \(p^\perp\), so \(v-w\) denotes the spatial
part of the corresponding displayed Lorentz vector.

\subsubsection{Opposite walls and common components}
Let \(B\) be the original incident Gram matrix, \(C=B^{-1}\), and
 \[
 \omega_i=\sum_j C_{ij}n_j,\qquad
 d_i=C_{ii},\qquad u_i=\omega_i/\sqrt{d_i}.
\]
An actual opposite wall has normal
 \[
 c=A p-v,\quad A>0,\quad
 v=\sum_i\beta_i\omega_i,\quad \beta_i=-\ip{c}{n_i}\geq0,\quad
 A^2=\beta^TC\beta-1.
\]
Strict interiority gives
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:actualinterior}\ip{x}{v}<A.
\end{equation}
Along the original edge in direction \(-u_i\), of length \(L_i\),
the original farthest comparison gives
 \begin{equation}
\label{eq:edge}\ip{x}{u_i}\geq\tanh(L_i/2).
\end{equation}
Whole-component projection energies sum to \(\norm{x}^2\).

\begin{proposition}[Reciprocal shadows; internal geometric interface]
\label{prop:shadows}For an actual cap put \(T_i=(C\beta)_i/(A\sqrt{d_i})\).
If \(T_i>1\), it is the opposite wall of edge \(i\), and
 \(\tanh L_i=1/T_i\). If it is not that opposite wall, then
 \(\tanh L_i\geq T_i\). The value \(T_i=1\) is incompatible
with a finite endpoint. For distinct actual opposite walls,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shadow}\tanh L_i\,\tanh L_j\geq
 K_{ij},\qquad K_{ij}=C_{ij}/\sqrt{d_i d_j}.
\end{equation}
Opposite walls of orthogonal original directions cannot coincide.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Schur identity says that
 \(C-C_{\cdot i}C_{i\cdot}/d_i\) vanishes on row and column \(i\);
elsewhere it is the inverse of the retained old Gram matrix.
It is entrywise nonnegative. Let \(d=Dp-\sum_j\gamma_j\omega_j\)
be the actual opposite of edge \(i\), distinct from \(c\).
Acuteness and the Schur identity give
 \[
 AD\geq\beta^TC\gamma
 \geq(C\beta)_i(C\gamma)_i/d_i.
\]
The actual endpoint equation is
 \(\tanh L_i=D\sqrt{d_i}/(C\gamma)_i\), so
 \(\tanh L_i\geq T_i\). If \(T_i>1\) this alternative is
impossible, forcing \(c=d\); its endpoint equation gives
 \(\tanh L_i=1/T_i\).
If \(T_i=1\), the distinct alternative is impossible and the
same-wall equation would give an ideal endpoint. This is
excluded by compactness.
For the pair inequality, first use the contacts of the actual
opposite of \(i\) to get \(T_j\geq K_{ij}/\tanh L_i\);
the distinct alternative at edge \(j\) yields the assertion.

Finally suppose the same wall closes orthogonal directions
 \(i,j\). Clearing every other old normal adds positive
coefficients and gives
 \[
 c_F=A p-\frac{A}{r_i}u_i-\frac{A}{r_j}u_j,\qquad
 r_i=\tanh L_i,\quad r_j=\tanh L_j.
\]
Its product with the strict interior point remains negative,
so \(\ip{x}{u_i}/r_i+\ip{x}{u_j}/r_j<1\).
But \eqref{eq:edge} makes this sum at least
 \(1+(\tau_i^2+\tau_j^2)/2>1\),
where \(\tau_i=\tanh(L_i/2)\). This contradiction proves
the orthogonal assertion.
\end{proof}
The displayed pair inequality concerns full tangents, not
half tangents.
For an opposite wall, its retained foreign component can have
at most one contact: two would make a cycle in its actual
finite replacement. A positive foreign cost is
 \(q=\beta_j^2C_{jj}\geq1/4\). In a nontrivial component
 \(C_{jj}\geq4/3\), so the only cost below \(1/3\) is a
simple \(3\)-contact on an \(A_1\), of cost \(1/4\).
Moreover
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:foreigncost}q<A^2.
\end{equation}
Otherwise the actual reciprocal test would close that foreign
direction too, violating the orthogonal assertion, with the
equality case giving a null endpoint.
Set \(F(t)=2t/(1+t^2)\). For unit directions of correlation \(k\)
in a component of energy at most \(e\), positive heights satisfy
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:pairupper}\tau_i+\tau_j\leq\sqrt{2e(1+k)},\qquad
 F(\tau_i)F(\tau_j)\leq\frac{4s}{(1+s)^2},\quad
 s=\frac{e(1+k)}2.
\end{equation}
The second inequality follows from concavity of \(\log F\) on
 \((0,1)\). If its upper bound is smaller than \(K_{ij}\),
Proposition~\ref{prop:shadows} forces a common actual opposite.
Its finite-white extension or positive affine kernel then gives
additional actual pressures.

\subsection{Quadratic estimates from reference vectors}
\label{compact:sec:references}
The elementary estimate behind every certificate is
 \begin{equation}
\label{eq:square}\norm{x}^2\geq2\ip{x}{W}-\norm{W}^2.
\end{equation}
Every term in \(\ip{x}{W}\) must be justified.
Write \(w=\sum_i b_iP_i\), \(b_i\geq0\), with actual pressures
 \(\ip{x}{P_i}>a_i\), and demand \(A_w=\sum_i b_i a_i\).
For a new actual pressure \(\ip{x}{Z}\geq h\), choose
 \[
 Q=Z-\sum_i t_iP_i,\qquad W=w+\mu Q.
\]
Before using pressures require \(\mu\geq0\) and check every
 \(b_i-\mu t_i\geq0\).
Then
 \begin{equation}
\label{eq:payment}\ip{x}{W}\geq A_w+\mu(h-\sum_i t_i a_i),\quad
 \norm{W}^2=\norm{w}^2+2\mu\ip{w}{Q}+\mu^2\norm{Q}^2.
\end{equation}
The available price \(b_i\) is individual to anchor \(i\).
The first inequality is strict if some strictly constrained old
anchor retains a positive coefficient, or if \(\mu>0\) and the
new pressure is strict. For multiple corrections every new weight
must be nonnegative; their combined debit at each anchor and all
mutual cross terms must be retained.

For $P_i=e_1+\cdots+e_i$, increasing concave interpolation through
$(0,0)$ and actual floors $\ip x{P_i}>f_i$ gives a reference with
successive slopes as its coordinates. Its coefficients in the $P_i$ are nonnegative slope
decreases, and its demand equals its square. Deleting a knot between intervals of lengths $a,b$ and slopes
$s_1,s_2$ loses $ab(s_1-s_2)^2/(a+b)$. For unit adjacent
spacings this is half the squared slope difference. For the
$\sqrt i-\mathrm{constant}$ floors at $i\ge5$, such a unit-neighbor
loss is $<1/3200$; nonadjacent omissions have disjoint intervals
and their losses add. End omissions use their exact chord costs.
An omitted floor has coefficient zero.

References are Euclidean vectors: actual feasible points prove the pressures, and nonnegative coefficients combine them in \eqref{eq:square}.

\subsubsection{Common-wall forcing and the short defects}
For the following small-component calculations, let $T$ be the actual
whole component, $C_T=B_T^{-1}$ its inverse Gram matrix, and $\beta_T$
the cap's contact vector on $T$. Write
\[
 q=\beta_T^TC_T\beta_T,\qquad Q=\sum_{U\ne T}\beta_U^TC_U\beta_U,
 \qquad h=A^2=q+Q-1>0.
\]
Thus $q$ denotes the own-component cost and $Q$ the full foreign cost.

\begin{lemma}[Common-wall forcing and isolation]\label{compact:app:lem:ordinaryforcing}
Suppose the energy of a component is at most $e$, with $0\le e<1$. A pair for which
the right side of \eqref{eq:pairupper} is below $K_{ij}$ has one
actual opposite. If one wall closes every direction of the component,
then, with $v_i=(C\beta)_i/\sqrt{d_i}$ and
 $R_e^2=4e/(1+e)^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{compact:app:eq:commoncost}
 q-1\le h\le R_e^2\min_i v_i^2.
\end{equation}
If $h<1/4$, it has no foreign contact. In a path, a wall closing
every direction can have multiple contacts only at the two endpoints;
in a $D_4$ star it has at most one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is \eqref{eq:pairupper}. For a closed direction,
$\tanh L_i=A/v_i$ and $\tau_i^2\le e$, which gives
\eqref{compact:app:eq:commoncost}. Every nonzero foreign cost is at least $1/4$
and is strictly below $h$ by \eqref{eq:foreigncost}; hence the isolation
assertion. If the old path between two contacts omits a direction
closed by the wall, deleting that direction leaves a cycle in an
actual finite replacement. This is impossible. The same argument
applies to every two contacts in a $D_4$ star. Finally, a single-contact
own cost $q=1$ gives an affine principal set, impossible even in the
presence of further spectators.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What can be checked.}

certificate, regenerating a finite computation and checking a conditional
formal deduction. The finite-volume current-rule conjunction has recorded
execution for $259$ scoped units, including all $64$ selected application
shards. This does not constitute a fresh combined replay of its older
$146$-job inventory or regeneration of every inherited classification.
The public companion identifies the small-input checks available from a
Git checkout and the operations whose larger inputs are not distributed.
No asset from the separately indexed bulk collection is included in the
arXiv source archive.
