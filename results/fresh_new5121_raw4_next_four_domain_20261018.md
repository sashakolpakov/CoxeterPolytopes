## Why the improvement ceiling is rigorous

Let t_v be the mass of the current exact attaining local cover, mu_v the
current vertex multiplier, and e_v the sum of the added actual corner
demands in that forest. Old cliques remain valid because every old-old
outcome is fixed. For each added orbit, choose one of its literal stars
as a singleton and give it mass equal to that orbit's added demand. Such
a singleton is a clique even if every new pair becomes incompatible.
Together these vectors cover the new demands with mass at most t_v+e_v.
No physical realization of this cover mixture is needed.

Consequently the required monotone repair is at most

    mu'_v = max(mu_v, t_v + e_v).

For a previously unrepresented forest, t_v=0; any already-positive mu_v
still supplies slack. The total added demand is exactly six times the sum
of the four face masses, rather than the number of orientations:

    sum e_v = 4836537/500000000.

Ignoring all prior slack would give the weaker ratio lower bound
12.006051694876941. Using each saved t_v and mu_v improves the total possible
multiplier increase to

    sum (mu'_v-mu_v) = 2427124573/360000000000.

This gives the displayed12.040332429991919 bound. The same constructed
upper repair works for every weaker set of new pair exclusions, since
removing fewer edges cannot invalidate its old-clique/singleton cover.
All existing matrix columns, including the14 two-owner columns, survive
any such monotone increase in mu with alpha fixed.

The argument does not limit new cross-vertex constraints, constraints that
exclude a singleton occurrence itself, additional classes, or a changed
choice of face masses. It does show that performing these18,147 pair tests
alone cannot eliminate the current target12 witness. No local or global
optimizer was invoked.
