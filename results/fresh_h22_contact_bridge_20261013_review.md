`x=alpha v-sum gamma_i d_i`, with `alpha>0`, `gamma_i>0`,

and `(e,d_i)>=0`. If `a<=0`, then `(r,x)>=0`, contradicting the strict interior condition. Consequently `a>0`, and every admissible candidate occurs uniquely in

## Normalization and interval completeness

For a wall w with squared norm n, put `a_w=(v-e,w)`, `b_w=(v+e,w)` and `T=t²`. The exact formulas are

`(r,w)=(a_w T-b_w)/sqrt(2T)`,

`cos(theta)=(-a_w sqrt(T)+b_w/sqrt(T))/(2sqrt(n))`,

and finite or parallel contact necessarily satisfies

`(a_w T-b_w)² <= 4nT`.

The initial continuous scout omitted a factor of sqrt(2), using `2nT` and denominator `sqrt(2n)`. The final verifier uses `4nT` and denominator `2sqrt(n)` throughout. The original bounded norm-two quadratic scout was unaffected.

The numerical file now only selects proof witnesses. The exact verifier reconstructs the coefficients and checks their signs. A lower cutoff is rounded down, an upper cutoff is rounded up, and unused constraints are simply dropped. Thus the retained interval is a superset of every possible candidate; it need not be the sharp feasible interval.

The strict conditions `T>1` and positive height are relaxed to closed necessary bounds, so no boundary candidate disappears through an open-endpoint convention.
