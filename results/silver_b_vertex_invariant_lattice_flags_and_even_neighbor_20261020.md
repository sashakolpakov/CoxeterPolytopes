# Silver B-vertex lattices: complete flags and the even neighbor

20 October 2026. Paper derivation only; no imports, enumeration, checker,
Lean or TeX execution. Prepared for full root and outside review. The
lattice classification and its proposed geometric application below are
**not yet promoted** to a new dimension exclusion.

Put p=√2, α=1+p, O=Z[p], and

    L=O e0 ⊕ O e1 ⊕ ... ⊕ O en,   Q(t,x)=−α t²+Σxi².

Let W0 be generated by all signed spatial permutations and the reflections
in ci=e0−α ei. These have norm pα and on coordinates (t,xi) act by

    Si = [[α,p], [−pα,−α]],
    (Si−1)(t,x)=p(t+xi)(e0−α ei).                         (1)

The question is about an arbitrary W0-invariant O-lattice, not just L or
its rational quadratic space. The elementary arithmetic facts used are:
O is a PID, its units are ±α^Z, and its only dyadic prime is (p), with
2=p². The norm-Euclidean nearest-coefficient argument proves the PID
claim. For the unit claim, rescale a physically positive unit into
[1,α); the coefficient (u−u')/(2p), with u'=±1/u, is integral only for
u=1. In particular the totally positive units are the squares α^(2Z).

## 1. Classification to be reviewed

For n≥3, every W0-invariant lattice Λ can be multiplied by a scalar
without changing its orthogonal group so that

    O(Λ) ⊂ O(L),

or, only when n is even,

    O(Λ) ⊂ O(S),   S=L+O j/p,   j=(0;1,...,1).            (2)

This is containment of the full integral isometry groups in the displayed
coordinates. It is not an assertion that all the lattices are homothetic
or isometric. All cases and the canonical recovery operations are given
below.

### 1.1 Odd primes and the two-level dyadic bound

At an odd prime, spatial sign changes split Λ into its time and spatial
coordinate ideals. Permutations identify all spatial ideals. The two
off-diagonal entries of Si are units, so its time ideal is the same.
Thus Λ is a scalar multiple of L at every odd prime. The PID property
allows these scalars to be removed simultaneously. Localize now at (p),
and normalize Λ⊂L with at least one unit coordinate in Λ.

The O-algebra generated by W0 contains p²Eij for all spatial i,j:
use 1−(sign change)=p²Eii and multiply by permutations. It also contains
p²E00. Multiplying these matrices by Si−1 in (1), and subtracting the
known diagonal terms, gives p³E0i and p³Ei0, since α is a unit. Hence
p³End(L) is in this algebra and primitivity gives p³L⊂Λ.

In fact

    p²L ⊂ Λ ⊂ L.                                        (3)

If v∈Λ has a unit spatial coordinate, the p²Eij first give every p²ej.
If its time coordinate is a unit, p²v gives p²e0 as well. Otherwise
t+xi is a unit, so (1) gives p(e0−αei), and multiplying by p gives
p²e0. If instead only the time coordinate of v is a unit, (1) gives
p(e0−αei) for every i. For j≠i another application gives
p²(e0−αej). Also p²v differs from a unit multiple of p²e0 by p³L,
already in Λ. This again supplies all p²ej. This proof only needs n≥2.

### 1.2 Codes, all possible lifts, and notation

Write V=F2 e0 ⊕ F2^n and let U be the top image Λ mod p, and T the
middle image of p^(-1)(Λ∩pL) mod p. Then 0≠U⊂T⊂V. Since
O/(p²)=F2[p]/(p²), every lattice in (3) has a unique description

    Λ = {u+p f(u)+p t (mod p²L): u∈U, t∈T},             (4)

where f:U→V/T is F2-linear. Spatial-permutation invariance says that
U,T and f are S_n-equivariant. The cap condition in (1) says

    Ni U⊂T,   Ni(t,x)=(t+xi)(e0+ei).                    (5)

Use these codes:

    D=<d>, d=(1;1^n);    J=<j>, j=(0;1^n);
    E={(0,x):Σxi=0};     H={(t,x):t=Σxi};
    M={(0,x)};           Z=<e0,j>.

For a code A put L_A={y∈L:y mod p∈A}. Every permutation-invariant
code is among

    0, <e0>, J, D, Z, E, E+<e0>, M, H, V.              (6)

Indeed a vector with nonconstant spatial coordinates gives, by a
transposition, a vector (0,ei+ej); conjugating gives E. The quotient
V/E has dimension two and trivial action, giving the last five codes.
Otherwise the code is a subspace of Z, giving the first five. Coinciding
codes at small n do not affect the argument; n≥3 is sufficient here.

If U is not D, some Ni is nonzero on U. Permutations then force all
e0+ej into T, hence T⊃H. There are exactly these cases:

* T=V: Λ=L_U, with U any nonzero code in (6); f=0.
* T=H, U≠D: U=H, E, or (n even) J.
  For U=H the two lifts are f=0 and f(u)=t e0 mod H.
  For U=E only f=0 occurs. For U=J either f(j)=0 or e0 mod H occurs.
* U=D: T is D, Z, V, or H when n is odd, or E+<e0> when n is even.
  The respective lift representatives f(d) are {0,e0}, {0}, {0},
  {0,e0}, and {0,e1}.

For completeness, Hom_Sn(E,F2)=0: all pair vectors ei+ej have the
same image, and (ei+ej)+(ej+ek)=ei+ek makes it zero. Hom_Sn(H,F2)
is generated by t. For U=D, a class fixed modulo D or Z has a constant
spatial representative: a transposition difference has support two and
cannot be the time-nonzero diagonal or the all-ones vector when n≥3.
This proves the displayed lift list, including the two trivial quotients.

### 1.3 Canonical recovery for every first-level code

For any module A in the quadratic space define

    N(A)={x∈A:Q(x)∈O}.

We use this only where the set is explicitly a lattice. Orthogonal
isometries preserve N(A), scalar multiples, and the bilinear dual A#.
For an integral y the following exact dyadic congruence is useful:

    Q(y) ≡ Σ lift(xbar_i) − (1+p) lift(tbar) (mod p²),

where tbar, xbar_i are the residues modulo p in F2, and lift denotes
their canonical representatives 0 or 1. This is not reduction of the
coordinates modulo 2=p².

Thus Q(y)∈p²O exactly when y mod p∈E. In particular

    N(p^(-1)L_A)=p^(-1)L_(A∩E).                        (7)

Here and throughout this section the computations are local at (p).
The following table gives a canonical operation recovering L, or S for
even n, from every first-level L_A. A displayed recovery of L or S is
literal, not just up to a basis change.

| A | Module on which N is taken | Recovered module |
|---|---|---|
| 0 or V | no operation: L_A=pL or L | O(L_A)=O(L) |
| <e0> or D | p^(-1)L_A | L |
| J or Z | p^(-1)L_A | L if n odd; S if n even |
| M | L_M#=L+O e0/p | L |
| E | L_E#=L+O e0/p+O j/p | L if n odd; S if n even |
| E+<e0> | L_(E+<e0>)#=L+O j/p | L if n odd; S if n even |
| H | L_H#=L+O w/p, w=(−α^(-1);1^n) | L |

The first four recovery rows follow directly from (7). The next three
are the same congruence on the indicated coordinate codes. In the last
row w≡d mod p, so L+Ow/p=p^(-1)L_D and (7) again gives L.
The dual formulas follow either from the defining single congruence or
from the coordinate pairing diag(−α,1,...,1). This verifies every code
in (6), without an unlisted lattice-equivalence assertion.

### 1.4 The remaining two-level lattices

First suppose U=D. In (4), p^(-1)Λ is generated by d/p+f(d) and L_T.
For a unit coefficient of this fractional generator, its norm has
negative valuation: Q(d)=n−α has valuation zero for n even and one
for n odd, while its cross term with every integral vector is integral
because 2/p=p. Consequently

    N(p^(-1)Λ)=L_T.                                    (8)

The table now handles every possible T and every lift, including f≠0.

It remains to consider T=H and U=H,E,J. Put w=(−α^(-1);1^n).
Every vector congruent to d mod p has norm valuation zero or one
by (7). This observation rules out the apparently deeper fractions.

For U=H the two lattices are the kernels

    (1−a p)t+Σxi ≡0 (mod p²),   a=0 or 1.

Their duals are L+O w_a/p², where
w_a=(−α^(-1)(1−a p);1^n)≡d mod p. A coefficient of valuation zero
or one gives a negative norm valuation; the cross term with L is
integral since 2/p²=1. Therefore N(Λ#)=L in both cases.

For U=E the lattice is

    t≡0 (mod p),   t+Σxi≡0 (mod p²),

and its dual is L+O e0/p+O w/p². A unit coefficient on w/p² has norm
valuation −4 or −3; the remaining square term has valuation at least
−2 and the cross term at least −1, so cancellation is impossible.
After its coefficient becomes divisible by p, the surviving module is
L+Oe0/p+Ow/p=p^(-1)L_Z. Hence N(Λ#) is L for n odd and S for n even.

Finally let n be even and U=J. For a=0,1 a full lift is

    Λ=O(p a;1^n)+p L_H.

Its dual consists of

    x=b w/p²+y/p,   y∈L,   Σyi≡a b (mod p).             (9)

This follows by pairing with pL_H and the displayed generator;
the term b n/p² is integral because n is even. A unit b has a
valuation −4 norm term, while the other terms have valuation at least
−2 and −1, so b must be divisible by p for x to have integral norm.
Write b=p c. Then the numerator y+c w has even spatial sum. Congruence
(7) says its time coordinate must also be divisible by p. Consequently

    N(Λ#)=K0=O e0 ⊕ p^(-1){x∈O^n:Σxi∈pO}.

Its dual is K0#=Oe0+pO^n+Oj=L_Z. The first-level table recovers S
canonically from this dual. This deals with both lifts a and completes
all cases in (4).

Combining the local recoveries with the odd-prime normalization proves
the containment (2) globally: S differs from L only at (p). No
commensurability-to-reflectivity implication has been used.

## 2. The even neighbor: a full coordinate-facet obstruction

### 2.1 Complete primitive-root pool

We have S#⊂L⊂S. For a primitive reflector v∈S of norm q,
reflection integrality is 2v/q∈S#. Primitivity in the free O-module S
therefore gives q|2. The two real signs of q are positive. Up to
unit-square rescaling, the exact pool is

    q=1, X=pα=2+p, 2.                                  (10)

An S-vector is either integral or has time coordinate in O and every
spatial coordinate in O+1/p. In the latter case norm 2 is impossible:
2v/q=v∈S#⊂L. This is a restriction on reflectors, not on all vectors.

### 2.2 Every induced facet mirror is a standard mirror

The coordinate sign change in ei preserves S. For an independent wall
v that actually crosses ei's hyperplane, let k=vi. The physical
two-wall Gram is positive definite, and the other real form is positive
definite, so

    k²<q,   (k')²<q'.                                  (11)

If k∈O, the complete possibilities are:

| q | k |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 0, ±1 |
| X | 0 |

If k∈O+1/p, they are:

| q | k |
|---|---|
| 1 | ±1/p |
| X | ±1/p, ±(1+1/p) |

To verify completeness, write k=a+b p in the first table and
k=a+(b+1/2)p in the second. Adding (11) bounds
2a²+4b² or 2a²+4(b+1/2)² by Tr(q), which is 2 or 4. The individual
conjugate inequality removes precisely the opposite-sign mixed cases.

Project v by erasing coordinate i. In the integral cases k=0, its
projection is integral of norm q and 2/q∈O. When q=2,k=±1 its
projection has norm 1. These are standard L_(n−1) reflector rays.
The fractional cases also give standard rays after the following
literal integral scalings:

| q,k | projected norm | multiplier | resulting norm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, ±1/p | 1/2 | p | 1 |
| X, ±1/p | 3/2+p=α²/2 | p/α | 1 |
| X, ±(1+1/p) | 1/2 | p | 1 |

The multipliers clear every other half-coordinate, and the time
coordinate remains integral. Thus **every induced facet mirror is a
member of the standard L_(n−1) arrangement**. Surjectivity is unnecessary
and is not asserted.

### 2.3 The coordinate wall really is a full-chamber facet

At the time-axis point, a fractional zero-height root has spatial
coordinates ai+(bi+1/2)p. Its norm trace is at least n, because each
coordinate contributes 2ai²+4(bi+1/2)²≥1. This exceeds Tr(q)≤4.
For integral zero-height roots, norm X is impossible since the
p-coefficient of a sum of squares is even. Norms 1 and 2 give exactly
the standard B_n mirrors: coordinate signs and coordinate permutations.
Hence the complete zero-height arrangement at this finite hyperbolic
point has B_n chambers, and ei is an actual facet of a full chamber
whose germ is one such B_n chamber.

Full-arrangement chambers are transitive under their reflection group,
so the hypothesized compactness applies to this chamber as well. Its
ei facet would then be compact as an
ordinary hyperbolic polytope; no intrinsic-Coxeter-facet assumption is
needed. By Section 2.2 it is bounded by a subset of standard L_(n−1)
mirrors. Choose a generic interior point avoiding those full-arrangement
mirrors. Its entire standard chamber has the same sign for every
selected bounding mirror, and is contained in the facet. The standard
chamber has infinite volume for n−1≥9 by the explicit inherited input.
It cannot be contained in a compact facet. This proves the claimed
neighbor obstruction conditional on that inherited theorem.

## 3. Proposed application and exact limits

The purpose of the classification is to repair the lattice gap for a
whole B_n vertex with a long-end isolated native-8 cap. In the displayed
normalization the old B_n group consists of signed permutations and the
cap is precisely c=e0−αe1, so any actual arithmetic group preserving an
O-lattice and containing this star preserves a lattice covered by
Section 1. A compact reflection subgroup of O(L) or O(S) would make
the respective full reflection chamber compact, since that chamber is
contained in a compact subgroup chamber. Section 2 and the inherited
standard theorem are the intended final obstruction.

must be stated explicitly in any final geometric theorem. This note
does not classify all Q(√2) forms or all arithmetic Coxeter vertices,
does not remove isolated 5/12 caps in other fields, and does not claim a
