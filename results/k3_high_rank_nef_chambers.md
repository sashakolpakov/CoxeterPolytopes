# Full K3 nef chambers in hyperbolic dimensions 14 through 18

## Result

There are published, noncompact finite-volume Coxeter polytopes in every
hyperbolic dimension $14,\ldots,18$, obtained as full nef chambers of K3
surfaces. In negative-definite ADE convention, the five Néron--Severi
lattices are:

| $\rho$ | $\dim\mathbb H=\rho-1$ | $NS(X)$ | $|\operatorname{disc}NS|$ | all walls | $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$, general $X$ |
|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| 15 | 14 | $U\oplus E_8(-1)\oplus D_4(-1)\oplus A_1(-1)$ | 8 | 21 | $(\mathbf Z/2)^2$ |
| 16 | 15 | $U\oplus E_8(-1)\oplus D_6(-1)$ | 4 | 19 | $(\mathbf Z/2)^2$ |
| 17 | 16 | $U\oplus E_8(-1)\oplus E_7(-1)$ | 2 | 19 | $(\mathbf Z/2)^2$ |
| 18 | 17 | $U\oplus E_8(-1)^2$ | 1 | 19 | $(\mathbf Z/2)^2$ |
| 19 | 18 | $U\oplus E_8(-1)^2\oplus A_1(-1)$ | 2 | 24 | $\mathfrak S_3\times\mathbf Z/2$ |

Here “all walls” means all irreducible smooth rational curves, not a chosen
root subsystem. Roulleau's atlas lists the complete curve configurations in
Section 15, following Kondō and Nikulin. The exact intersection data are
archived in
[k3_high_rank_nef_chambers.json](../data/known_examples/k3_high_rank_nef_chambers.json).

These examples are known. The claim here is not the discovery of five new
Coxeter polytopes. The useful new viewpoint is that they are exact
high-dimensional calibration objects for an algebro-geometric inverse-design
program: their hyperbolic measure, walls, finite volume, and cusps all have
intrinsic divisor-theoretic meanings.

## The finite-volume theorem

Let $X$ be a complex projective K3 surface, let $N=NS(X)$ have rank
$\rho\geq3$, and choose the component $\mathcal C^+$ of its positive cone
containing an ample class. Put

$$
\mathbb H_N=\{D\in\mathcal C^+:D^2=1\},\qquad
P_X=\operatorname{Nef}(X)\cap\mathbb H_N.
$$

Let $W(N)$ be generated by the integral reflections

$$
s_C(x)=x+(x\mathbin{\cdot} C)C,\qquad C^2=-2.
$$

The K3 Weyl-chamber theorem makes $P_X$ a fundamental chamber for $W(N)$.
The group $O^+(N)$ is an arithmetic lattice in $O(1,\rho-1)$, and global
Torelli identifies the nef-chamber stabilizer with the image of
$\operatorname{Aut}(X)$, up to finite kernel and finite index. Consequently

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Vol}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(P_X)<\infty
\iff [O^+(N):W(N)]<\infty
\iff |\operatorname{Aut}(X)|<\infty.
}
$$

Artebani--Hausen--Laface, Theorems 2.7 and 2.11, give the intrinsic algebraic
completion:

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Vol}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(P_X)<\infty
\iff \operatorname{Eff}(X)\text{ is rational polyhedral}
\iff \operatorname{Cox}(X)\text{ is finitely generated}.
}
$$

Thus the full divisor chamber has finite hyperbolic volume exactly for a
Mori-dream K3 surface.

The metric itself also comes from divisor algebra. On the big and nef cone,

$$
\operatorname{vol}_X(D)=D^2,\qquad
\Phi(D)=-\tfrac12\log\operatorname{vol}_X(D).
$$

At $D^2=1$, for tangent vectors $D\cdot u=D\cdot v=0$,

$$
\operatorname{Hess}_D\Phi(u,v)=-u\cdot v,
$$

which is the hyperbolic metric. Finite chamber volume can therefore be read
as finite Hessian volume of the unit-volume nef polarizations.

## Why the five configurations are Coxeter polytopes

On a K3 surface every irreducible negative curve is a smooth rational
$(-2)$-curve. Its orthogonal hyperplane is a nef wall. If $C\ne D$ are two
such curves, then $C\cdot D\geq0$, and their normalized wall normals give:

| $C\cdot D$ | wall relation |
|---:|---|
| 0 | angle $\pi/2$, Coxeter exponent 2 |
| 1 | angle $\pi/3$, Coxeter exponent 3 |
| 2 | parallel walls meeting at infinity |
| $>2$ | ultraparallel walls; distance $\operatorname{arcosh}(C\cdot D/2)$ |

Roulleau's complete curve lists therefore give every facet and the complete
wall Gram matrix. Kondō proves the displayed automorphism groups for a
general period. Their finiteness and the theorem above prove finite
hyperbolic volume without a separate polyhedral-volume computation.

All five lattices contain $U$. An integral isotropic ray can be moved by the
Weyl group to the nef boundary, where it is the class of a genus-one pencil.
It produces an ideal cusp. Hence all five polytopes are noncompact, despite
having finite volume.

The curve-intersection graph should not be called the Coxeter diagram without
translation. In particular, an intersection-2 edge is a parallel-wall
relation and an intersection-6 edge is ultraparallel at distance
$\operatorname{arcosh}3$. These integral full nef chambers have no direct
Coxeter exponent-5 edge.

## Published models

Roulleau supplies the following geometric realizations.

- Rank 15: a double cover of $\mathbf P^2$ branched over an irreducible
  sextic of geometric genus 4 with an $A_{11}$ singularity. Distinguished
  curves map to the tangent line at the singularity, three bitangent lines
  through it, and a conic six-tangent to the sextic.

- Rank 16: a double plane branched over a sextic with an $A_{13}$
  singularity.

- Rank 17: a singular quartic model in $\mathbf P^3$; the coarse moduli
  space is an open subset of $\mathbf{WP}^3(2,3,5,6)$.

- Rank 18: a double plane branched over a sextic with an $A_{17}$
  singularity. Its 19-wall graph is the classical Vinberg graph.

- Rank 19: Section 15.5 gives the complete 24-curve graph, but does not give
  a comparable explicit projective equation there.

The groups in the table are the groups of a general member. Special periods
may have finite extensions. This does not affect reflectivity or finite
volume of the lattice chamber.

## An explicit very-general rank-18 Inose carrier

The rank-18 row now has a second explicit algebraic realization.  For

\[
A\bigl(A^3-(B+1)^2\bigr)\bigl(A^3-(B-1)^2\bigr)\ne0,
\]

take the minimal resolution of the classical Inose pencil

\[
y^2=x^3-3At^4x+t^5(t^2-2Bt+1).
\]

Its fibers are `II*+II*+4I1`.  Thus the trivial lattice is the unimodular
rank-18 lattice `U+E8(-1)^2`.  The residual quartic

\[
R(t)=(t^2-2Bt+1)^2-4A^3t^2
\]

recovers both `B` and `A^3` from its coefficients, up to finite
identifications.  The family consequently has the full two dimensions of
the rank-18 lattice-polarized period domain, and the very-general Picard
lattice is exactly `U+E8(-1)^2`.  Unimodularity simultaneously rules out
torsion, glue, and same-rank saturation alternatives.

Weyl transport identifies its nef cone with the complete 19-wall row in the
table.  The exact Vinberg audit gives 729 elliptic edges, 80 finite vertices,
two ideal vertices, three full affine-neighborhood audits, and no failures,
so this is the finite-volume noncompact `H17/19` chamber.  The equation is
classical and the chamber is known; the project-level positive result is the
fully checked equation-to-chamber realization.  See the
[dedicated theorem](ue8x2_inose_h17_family.md) and
[certificate](../data/enumerations/ue8x2_inose_h17_family.json).

## A derived equation-level rank-19 model

The following equation is a derived Inose-form realization, not an equation
quoted from Roulleau. For a parameter $s$ satisfying the genericity
conditions $s^3\ne0,1$, take the minimal resolution of

$$
y^2=x^3-3s^2t^4x+
t^5\bigl(t^2-2(1-s^3)t+1\bigr).
$$

For a Weierstrass equation $y^2=x^3+a_4x+a_6$, direct factorization gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_4&=144s^2t^4,\\
\Delta
&=-16(4a_4^3+27a_6^2)\\
&=-432t^{10}(t-1)^2
\bigl(t^2+(4s^3-2)t+1\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

At $t=0$, the valuations of $(a_4,a_6,\Delta)$ are $(4,5,10)$, hence the
fiber is $II^*$. With $u=1/t$ and the K3 changes
$a_{4,\infty}=u^8a_4(1/u)$ and
$a_{6,\infty}=u^{12}a_6(1/u)$, the same valuations occur at infinity, so
there is a second $II^*$ fiber. At $t=1$, $c_4\ne0$ and
$\operatorname{ord}_{t=1}\Delta=2$, giving $I_2$. The quadratic factor has
discriminant

$$
16s^3(s^3-1),
$$

so generically its two roots are distinct and give two $I_1$ fibers. The
Euler numbers add to

$$
10+10+2+1+1=24.
$$

The trivial lattice is

$$
U\oplus E_8(-1)^2\oplus A_1(-1),
$$

of rank 19 and discriminant 2. The marked fiber configuration varies with
$s^3$, so the family is non-isotrivial. Away from the countable
Noether--Lefschetz jumping set, $\rho=19$. Shioda--Tate then gives
Mordell--Weil rank zero. Since the discriminant form on the $A_1$ factor has
no nonzero isotropic subgroup, there is no proper even overlattice of the
same rank. Thus the generic Néron--Severi lattice is exactly the rank-19
lattice in the table.

The factorization and local valuation claims are included in the exact
verifier. The generic Picard-rank conclusion uses the usual
Noether--Lefschetz argument for this non-isotrivial one-parameter
lattice-polarized family.

This derived model is now promoted to a standalone positive theorem with
the complete 24-wall and Vinberg handoffs; see the
[rank-19 Inose-collision package](ue8x2a1_inose_h18_family.md) and its
[replayable certificate](../data/enumerations/ue8x2a1_inose_h18_family.json).

## Exact reconstruction and diagram audit

The machine-readable matrices were transcribed from Roulleau's TikZ source
and checked by exact rational congruence, not floating-point eigenvalues.

| $\rho$ | wall matrix size | exact rank | inertia $(+, -, 0)$ | determinant of recorded basis |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 15 | 21 | 15 | $(1,14,6)$ | $8$ |
| 16 | 19 | 16 | $(1,15,3)$ | $-4$ |
| 17 | 19 | 17 | $(1,16,2)$ | $2$ |
| 18 | 19 | 18 | $(1,17,1)$ | $-1$ |
| 19 | 24 | 19 | $(1,18,5)$ | $2$ |

For ranks 15 through 18 the recorded bases are $A_1,\ldots,A_\rho$. In rank
19 the basis is

$$
\{A_1,\ldots,A_5,A_7,\ldots,A_{19},A_{21}\}.
$$

The basis determinants agree in absolute value with the discriminants of the
published lattices. This is a consistency check on the transcription; the
identification of the lattice and completeness of the curve list remain
published source facts.

### Rank-15 redraw error

Roulleau's rank-15 TikZ redraw makes the six segments

$$
A_{20}A_{17},A_{20}A_{18},A_{20}A_{19},
A_{21}A_{14},A_{21}A_{15},A_{21}A_{16}
$$

thin. They must have intersection 2. There are two independent checks:

1. Kondō's original Figure 5 displays the corresponding double lines.
2. Roulleau states $2D_2=A_{20}+A_{21}$ and
   $D_2\cdot A_j=1$ for $j=14,\ldots,19$. Since the opposite top curve is
   disjoint in each case, these relations force all six intersections to
   equal 2.

Reading the redraw literally gives a $21\times21$ matrix of rank 19 and
inertia $(3,16,2)$, which cannot lie in a rank-15 hyperbolic Néron--Severi
lattice. Correcting the six weights gives rank 15 and inertia $(1,14,6)$.

### Rank-19 overlaid chords

In the rank-19 TikZ diagram, thick chord segments are drawn on top of longer
thin chords. The consistent reading is:

- the outer triangle has intersection 1 along each consecutive pair;
- the three inner chains
  $1-19-22-10$, $7-20-23-16$, and $13-21-24-4$ have weights
  $(1,2,2)$;
- the inner triangle on $22,23,24$ has intersection 6 on all three sides.

That transcription has rank 19 and inertia $(1,18,5)$.

## Sharp K3 boundary and its scope

Artebani--Hausen--Laface, Theorem 2.12, records one eligible
finite-automorphism Picard lattice in each rank $15,16,17,18,19$, and none in
rank 20. Combined with the finite-volume theorem, this gives the sharp
statement

$$
\boxed{
\text{A full finite-volume K3 nef/root chamber has dimension at most 18.}
}
$$

The bound is attained by
$U\oplus E_8(-1)^2\oplus A_1(-1)$.

This is a boundary for **full rational K3 nef chambers**, not for all
hyperbolic Coxeter polytopes and not for an irrational eigenspace section of
a larger divisor lattice. For a decorated pair $(X,\tau)$, an eigen-nef slice
needs separate proofs that its restricted reflection arrangement is complete
and that the relevant centralizer is an arithmetic lattice. Finite volume of
the ambient chamber does not automatically imply finite volume of the slice.

Similarly, $P_X/\operatorname{Aut}(X)$ has arithmetic finite volume much more
generally; that quotient must not be confused with finite volume of the full
reflection chamber $P_X$.
