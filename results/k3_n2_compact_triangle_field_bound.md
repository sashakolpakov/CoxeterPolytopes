# Finite-area K3 Coxeter triangles have a finite field set

**Status (2026-08-11): proved for every full-trace complex-K3 field
degree, including ideal vertices.** Let a rank-three module over a totally
real field return an exact finite-area Coxeter triangle in \(\mathbf H^2\).
The full trace has rank
\(3d\), hence

\[
                              d=[k:\mathbf Q]\le6.
\tag{0.1}
\]

Every pair of triangle facets meets in \(\mathbf H^2\) or is asymptotic at
an ideal vertex. Pair cyclic Cartan products therefore have magnitude at
most \(4\) at every real embedding, while the sole simple length-three
cyclic product has magnitude at most \(8\). If \(k\) is the
ground field generated by this integral cyclic packet, a bounded primitive
combination gives

\[
                 |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|\le256^{30}.
\tag{0.2}
\]

Hermite--Minkowski gives a finite field set.  No angle list,
reflective-lattice classification, imposed height, or Gram-determinant
closure is used.  Exact same-chamber return, complete reflection saturation,
and relative reflection maximality are retained, but unused in the height
estimate.

## 1. Exact carrier package

Let \(k\) be totally real of degree \(d\), let \(\mathcal O\subset k\) be a
returned order, and let \(M\) be a finite projective rank-three
\(\mathcal O\)-module with an admissible symmetric \(k\)-form \(h\).  At one
distinguished embedding \(\sigma_0\), the form has signature \((2,1)\); at
all other embeddings it is positive definite.  Assume:

1. a minimal complex projective K3 surface \(X\) has Picard lattice equal
   to the primitive full trace of \((M,h)\), and the returned order acts
   self-adjointly by divisor-product correspondences;
2. the prescribed projectivized eigen-nef chamber is exactly a finite-area
   Coxeter triangle in \(\mathbf H^2\), with every vertex finite or ideal;
3. its three facet reflections preserve \(M\), the restricted K3 mirror
   packet is complete and saturated, and the group is reflection-maximal
   among integral \(k\)-linear reflections of the declared order host; and
4. \(k\) is the ground field generated by the cyclic Cartan products of
   the three returned facet reflections.

The primitive full trace has rank \(3d=\rho(X)\le20\), proving (0.1).  The
rational case is already finite, so one may assume \(2\le d\le6\).

## 2. Every simple cyclic coordinate is automatically bounded

Choose facet normals \(e_0,e_1,e_2\).  Formally normalize them to unit
length at each real place. Since every pair of sides of a finite-area
triangle meets or is asymptotic, its normalized correlation magnitude is at
most one at \(\sigma_0\).
At every definite embedding, strict Cauchy--Schwarz gives the same bound.

For a pair \(i,j\), the cyclic Cartan product

\[
 p_{ij}=a_{ij}a_{ji}
       ={4h(e_i,e_j)^2\over h(e_i,e_i)h(e_j,e_j)}
\tag{2.1}
\]

belongs to \(\mathcal O\): it differs by an integer from the module trace
of the product of two integral facet reflections.  At every embedding
\(\sigma\),

\[
                             0\le\sigma(p_{ij})\le4.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The only additional simple cyclic coordinate is the directed triangle
product

\[
                             c_3=a_{01}a_{12}a_{20}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Reflection-word trace induction on the finite projective order-module puts
\(c_3\) in \(\mathcal O\).  At every embedding,

\[
                              |\sigma(c_3)|\le8.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Right-angle zeros cause no exception: they simply delete the corresponding
pair and triple cyclic coordinates.  Thus the complete simple cyclic packet
has the uniform conjugate bound

\[
                                      H=8.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Every closed directed walk decomposes into simple directed cycles by cutting
at repeated vertices. On three vertices the simple cycles have length one,
two, or three and contribute respectively the constant \(a_{ii}=2\), a pair
coordinate \(p_{ij}\), or \(c_3\) and its equal reversed value. Hence this
bounded simple packet generates every cyclic Cartan product and therefore
the minimal Vinberg field.

## 3. Primitive element and discriminant

By hypothesis, the packet in Section 2 generates \(k\).  Choose a minimal
field-generating subpacket.  Every strict adjunction has degree at least two,
so \(d\le6\) requires at most two generators, say
\(\theta_1,\theta_2\).

Among

\[
                         \theta_1+c\theta_2,
                         \qquad 0\le c\le15,
\tag{3.1}
\]

some element is primitive.  Indeed, each unordered pair of the at most six
real embeddings forbids at most one coefficient \(c\), and there are at
most \(\binom62=15\) pairs.  The selected primitive algebraic integer
\(\theta\) satisfies

\[
                              |\sigma(\theta)|\le16H=128.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Its Vandermonde discriminant therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|
 &\le|\operatorname {Disc}(\mathbf Z[\theta])|\\
 &\le(2\cdot128)^{d(d-1)}\\
 &\le256^{30},
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

which proves (0.2) and the finite field set.

### Theorem 3.1 (finite-area-triangle field theorem)

Every exact full-trace complex-K3 carrier satisfying Section 1 and having
a finite-area Coxeter triangle as its prescribed eigen-nef chamber belongs to
a finite set of totally real cyclic ground fields.  The explicit bound is
(0.2).

## 4. Scope

This theorem does not classify triangle angles or assert that every
\(n=2\) chamber is triangular. It covers finite and ideal vertices, but not
truncated ultraideal polyhedra, gratuitous operator extensions larger than
the cyclic ground field, or the construction of an exact K3 carrier. It proves a finite
field set rather than merely bounded degree, but it does not establish the
incidence-independent automatic-height implication for arbitrary compact
or finite-volume polygons.
