# Reflection maximality removes the hidden-wall debt on the strong-Coble lane

**Status (2026-08-10): proved, carrier-relative exact-return theorem.**
The input still has to construct a strong Coble surface and realize every
proposed facet ray by an effective irreducible curve.  Once that geometric
existence step is done, however, no independent enumeration of all other
negative curves is required.  Relative reflection maximality forces the
proposed Coxeter chamber to be the complete nef cone.

This is the exact place where maximality has useful algebro-geometric
content.  It does **not** create the Coble surface or the equation
`B=-2K`; the maximal full-nef `(2,3,infinity)` blowup of `F_4` remains the
counterexample to that false implication.  Maximality is used only after the
strong-Coble branch has been constructed.

## 1. Setup

Let `S` be a smooth projective rational surface over an algebraically closed
field.  Suppose that

\[
 B=B_1+\cdots+B_b=-2K_S                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

is a nonempty disjoint union of smooth rational curves with
`B_i^2=-4`.  Put `L=NS(S)` and use the standard adapted integral host

\[
 M=\left\langle L,{B_1\over2},\ldots,{B_b\over2}\right\rangle,
 \qquad (\ ,\ )_M=2(\ ,\ )_L.                              \tag{1.2}
\]

Let `P` be the ideal closure of a finite-sided finite-volume Coxeter chamber
in the projectivized positive cone of `L`.  Its oriented facet rays are represented by distinct
effective irreducible negative curves `D_1,...,D_f` on `S`, and the defining
halfspaces are

\[
 P=\{[x]:(x,D_j)\geq0\text{ for every }j\}.                 \tag{1.3}
\]

Assume their reflections preserve `M`, and write `W(P)` for the group they
generate.  The only group hypothesis is

> `W(P)` has no proper discrete reflection overgroup inside `O^+(M)`.

Global reflection maximality or maximality among arithmetic reflection
groups is stronger and also suffices.  No maximality of the integral lattice,
automorphism group, or preselected wall list is being substituted for this
hypothesis.

## 2. Every negative curve is an adapted integral root

### Lemma 2.1

If `C` is an irreducible curve with `C^2<0`, then exactly one of the following
holds:

1. `C=B_i` for some `i`, so `C^2=-4` and `C/2` is a square-minus-two root of
   `M`;
2. `C` is a smooth rational `(-2)`-curve and `B.C=0`;
3. `C` is a smooth rational `(-1)`-curve and `B.C=2`.

In every case reflection in the ray of `C` preserves `M`.

### Proof

Suppose first that `C` is not a component of `B`.  Distinct effective curves
have nonnegative intersection, so `B.C>=0`.  Adjunction and (1.1) give

\[
 2p_a(C)-2=C^2+K_S.C=C^2-{B.C\over2},
\]

or equivalently

\[
 C^2=2p_a(C)-2+{B.C\over2}.                                \tag{2.1}
\]

If `p_a(C)>=1`, the right side is nonnegative.  Thus negativity forces
`p_a(C)=0`, and then

\[
 C^2=-2+{B.C\over2}<0.
\]

Integrality makes `B.C` even, leaving precisely `(C^2,B.C)=(-2,0)` or
`(-1,2)`.  An integral curve of arithmetic genus zero is smooth and rational.

For a `(-2)`-curve, every `B_i.C` is zero.  Its usual reflection is integral
on `L` and fixes each half-boundary.  For a `(-1)`-curve, the doubled form
makes `C` a square-minus-two root; for `x in L` and every `i`,

\[
 s_C(x)=x+2(x.C)C,
 \qquad
 s_C(B_i/2)=B_i/2+(B_i.C)C,                                \tag{2.2}
\]

which lie in `M`.  Finally `B_i/2` has square `-2` in the doubled form, and

\[
 s_{B_i/2}(x)=x+(B_i.x)B_i/2                               \tag{2.3}
\]

preserves `M`; disjointness fixes all other half-boundaries.  This proves the
classification and integrality.  \(\square\)

The point of the lemma is that it classifies **all** irreducible negative
curves from the log equation and adjunction.  It does not assume that the
displayed facets already generate the Mori cone.

## 3. Saturation turns existence into exact nef return

### Theorem 3.1 (maximal strong-Coble return)

Under the hypotheses of Section 1,

\[
                         P=\mathbf P(\operatorname {Nef}S). \tag{3.1}
\]

Consequently the normalized Gram matrix, Coxeter labels, complete wall set,
face lattice, and finite-volume type returned by the carrier are literally
those of the original `P`.

### Proof

Let `R(M)` be the group generated by every integral hyperbolic reflection of
`M`.  For any such reflection `s`, the group `<W(P),s>` remains discrete
inside the discrete arithmetic group `O^+(M)` and is generated by
reflections.  Relative reflection maximality therefore gives

\[
                              W(P)=R(M).                    \tag{3.2}
\]

This is the saturation lemma; no reflectivity classification is used.

Choose an ample divisor `A` on `S`.  Every displayed facet curve is
effective, hence `A.D_j>0`.  By (1.3), `[A]` lies in the open chamber `P`.

Now let `C` be any irreducible curve on `S`.

* If `C^2<0`, Lemma 2.1 says that its reflection lies in `R(M)=W(P)`.  Its
  mirror therefore cannot meet the interior of the fundamental chamber `P`.
  Since `A.C>0` at one interior point, `(x,C)>0` throughout the interior and
  `(x,C)>=0` on the closure of `P`.
* If `C^2>=0`, the ample inequality `A.C>0` places `C` in the closure of the
  same positive-cone component as `A`.  Every `x` in the closure of `P` lies
  in that component, so the Lorentzian Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
  `(x,C)>=0`.

Thus every class in the closed cone over `P` has nonnegative intersection
with every irreducible curve, so `P` is contained in the projectivized nef
cone.  Conversely, every nef class has nonnegative intersection with the
displayed effective facet curves, and (1.3) gives the reverse containment
for positive-square classes.  A nef isotropic ray follows either by taking
the closure or by applying the same statement to `x+epsilon A` and letting
`epsilon` tend to zero.
This proves (3.1).  \(\square\)

The same proof works with arithmetic-reflection maximality: `W(P)` is already
a finite-covolume subgroup of `O^+(M)`, and every discrete overgroup has
finite index and remains arithmetic.  Maximality as an arbitrary arithmetic
group is not required.

## 4. Compiler consequence

For a reflection-maximal raw Coxeter input that has passed the numerical
Stage-0 standard-Coble gate, PB-Atlas Stage B now has the following exact
division of labour.

It **must** still prove, from the universal cluster equations, that:

1. the cluster/incidence constructible set is nonempty;
2. `B_1,...,B_b` are disjoint smooth rational `(-4)` curves with
   `sum B_i=-2K`;
3. every proposed facet ray is represented by the required effective
   irreducible curve; and
4. the marking and oriented intersection matrix return the input Gram data.

After those four geometric gates, Theorem 3.1 supplies automatically:

* exclusion of every hidden negative-curve wall;
* completeness of the nef/Mori cone;
* literal same-`P` return; and
* the correct finite-volume/face data already certified on the Coxeter side.

Thus the old instruction “enumerate all hidden negative curves” is stronger
than necessary on this maximal standard-Coble lane.  The remaining hard
problem is genuine incidence nonemptiness and irreducibility, not an
unbounded second root search.

Combining exact return with the canonical K3 double cover gives the
AG-derived dimension bounds

\[
 n\leq19\quad\text{over }\mathbf C,
 \qquad n\leq21\quad\text{in odd positive characteristic}. \tag{4.1}
\]

Combining it instead with the finite-host saturation theorem gives finitely
many abstract reflection-maximal chambers in the declared carrier class,
without ABSW, Prasad, an arithmetic-volume formula, or a separate facet-degree
bound.

## 5. Exact limits

This theorem does not claim any of the following.

* Bare reflection maximality implies `B=-2K` or even produces a surface.
* Every arithmetic Coxeter chamber has a rational or Coble carrier.
* A numerical Stage-0 branch is automatically effective.
* The analogous direct `(-1)/(-2)` lane has hidden-wall elimination without
  an additional global argument.  Equation (2.1), which is decisive here,
  uses `B=-2K`.
* Surface moduli are finite.  The conclusion is exactness of the abstract nef
  chamber, not rigidity of the carrier.

The maximal `Bl_p(F_4)` triangle lies outside the hypotheses because its
half-boundary does not satisfy `K+B/2=0`; it remains the sharp guard against
using maximality before the AG branch gate.
