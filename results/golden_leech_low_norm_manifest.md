# Golden Leech H24 coefficient-direction manifest through norm 128

This is a finite exact screen for the diagonal family

\[
L_a=\langle-a\rangle\oplus(\Lambda_{24}\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\mathbf Z[\phi]),
\qquad \phi^2=\phi+1,
\]

where `a` has signs `(+,-)`.  It enumerates every squarefree prime-ideal
direction of absolute norm at most 128.  The result is **47 directions** and
**28 prime-ideal atoms**.  Every direction's complete first necessary Leech
packet has a strict hyperideal fixed-direction witness.  Thus none of these
47 bounded prefixes has finite volume at its first necessary packet.

That last sentence is deliberately a prefix result.  Later mirrors can cut
the displayed open patch.  This is not a census of all integral hosts and not
a proof of global nonreflectivity.

## Coefficient directions and exact lineage

The ring `Z[phi]` is norm-Euclidean, so it has class number one.  Its units
are `+/-phi^n`; `phi` has norm `-1` and signs `(+,-)`.  Consequently the
narrow and ordinary class groups agree, and the totally positive units are
exactly `phi^(2n)`, all unit squares.  Each squarefree ideal product therefore
has a canonical sign-`(+,-)` generator after choosing the recorded
`phi^(2Z)` fundamental domain.

The prime decomposition was not inferred from rational gcds.  The exact atom
list contains:

- inert `(2)`, `(3)`, `(7)`, of ideal norms `4`, `9`, `49`;
- the ramified prime over `5`, generated by `2+phi`, of norm `5`;
- both conjugate primes over each of
  `11,19,29,31,41,59,61,71,79,89,101,109`.

Taking all squarefree products whose ideal norm is at most 128 gives 47
directions.  Both split choices are kept separately.  For example, the two
norm-11 directions have canonical coefficients `-1+3phi` and `-2+3phi`, and
their product is the rational-content direction `-11+11phi` of ideal norm
121.  The JSON stores the atom labels and generators for every row.

This identifies coefficient *directions* in `K^*/K^{*2}` under square
factors.  It is a rational quadratic-space classification of the displayed
coefficient direction, not an integral-host classification: multiplying `a`
by a nonunit square need not preserve the integral lattice.

## No height-zero mirrors

A primitive height-zero mirror would have `q=y^2` generating `(2)`: evenness
gives `(2)|q`, while `q|2(y,Lambda)` and primitive unimodularity give the
opposite valuation bound.  After unit rescaling one would have `y^2=2`, hence
`Tr(y^2)=4`.

Write `y=u+phi*v`, with `u,v` in the Leech lattice.  Exactly

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(y^2)=u^2+(u+v)^2+2v^2.
\]

The Leech minimum is four.  If `v=0`, the first two terms coincide and sum to
at least eight; if `v` is nonzero, `2v^2` is already at least eight.  This
contradiction proves that the positive-height search starts with no hidden
height-zero reflection group.

## Complete local mirror-norm classes

For a primitive vector `r=(x,y)`, reflection integrality is

\[
(q(r))\mid 2\,(a x,(y,\Lambda)).
\]

At an odd prime ideal `P` dividing squarefree `(a)`, the norm valuation is
independently zero or one.  Valuation one forces `y` into `P Lambda`; since
`x` is then a unit, `q=-a x^2 mod P^2` makes the valuation exactly one.
No prime away from `2a` can occur.

The dyadic calculation is different from the ramified silver field.  Here
`(2)` is inert:

- if `(2)` does not divide `(a)`, the dyadic norm exponent is `0` or `1`;
- if `(2)` divides squarefree `(a)`, evenness and primitivity force exponent
  exactly `1`;
- exponent `1` is absorbed by the factor `2` in the reflection formula, so
  there is no dyadic spatial-divisibility gate.

Thus, for every subset `S` of odd prime ideals dividing `(a)`, with generator
`d_S`, the mirror ideal is `d_S` or `2d_S` when `(2)` does not divide `(a)`,
and only `2d_S` when it does.  The backend canonicalizes the totally positive
norm generator modulo `phi^(2Z)` while retaining `d_S` for the exact spatial
gate `y=d_S z`.

Checks visible in the artifact include

```text
a = phi-1:       q = 1, 2
a = 2(phi-1):    q = 2
a = 2phi-1:      q = 1, 2, 2+phi, 4+2phi
```

The last two classes in the ramified norm-five row have spatial gate
`y in (2+phi)Lambda`.  The coefficient `-11+11phi`, containing both primes
over 11, has `2*2^2=8` local classes because the conjugate choices vary
independently.

## Complete first necessary packets

For a canonical mirror norm `q` and positive control `x`, the exact search
requires

\[
2ax/q\in\mathbf Z[\phi],\qquad
T=q+ax^2\succeq0,\qquad H=ax^2/q.
\]

If the odd spatial gate is `d`, it enumerates `z^2=T/d^2`.  Writing
`z=u+phi*v` and `T/d^2=A+Bphi` gives the exact integral Gram equations

\[
u^2+v^2=A,\qquad 2u\mathbin\cdot v+v^2=B.
\]

Evenness, Cauchy--Schwarz, and the Leech minimum make this a finite list.  The
backend then checks every integral binary combination which could have norm
below four, not only a short hand-picked list.  A surviving triple is still
only necessary: it may fail Leech realization or Vinberg acceptance.

## Uniform strict hyperideal obstruction

For each row and each fixed Leech direction of norm
`D in {4,6,8,10}`, an exhaustive integral `3x3` Gram positivity/minimum-four
calculation bounds the pairing with every surviving binary Gram type.  Let
`M_i` be that bound for component `i`, and let

\[
B_i=ax_i/d_i,
\qquad c_{\max}=\min_i B_i/M_i.
\]

The affine vector `(1,c d)` satisfies every wall inequality whenever
`c<c_max`; it is hyperideal when `D c^2-a>0`.  For every one of the 47 rows,
one of the four direction norms has

\[
D c_{\max}^2-a>0.
\]

The artifact records more than the limiting inequality: it chooses an exact
integer `n`, takes `c=((n-1)/n)c_max`, and stores a positive exact minimum wall
margin and positive exact hyperideal excess.  Therefore even the necessary
super-packet cannot close any of the 47 first prefixes.  Any realized and
accepted subset has no stronger first-packet closure.

The tightest row is the `p41-` direction

\[
a=-5+6\phi,\qquad |N(a)|=41,
\qquad H_1=122+198\phi.
\]

It has twelve surviving Gram types.  The best fixed direction has `D=4`,
pairing bound `M=17+26phi`, wall bound `B=16+30phi`, and

\[
c_{\max}=-\frac{92}{55}+\frac{94}{55}\phi,
\]

\[
4c_{\max}^2-a=
\frac{3373}{121}-\frac{10398}{605}\phi>0.
\]

Numerically the limiting excess is only about `0.06724`.  The archived strict
choice uses `n=142`; its exact margin and excess are

\[
\frac8{71}+\frac{15}{71}\phi>0,
\]

\[
\frac{16807457}{609961}
-\frac{51937482}{3049805}\phi>0,
\]

the latter about `2.21e-4`.  This makes `a=-5+6phi` the natural second-packet
priority within this bounded golden Leech family.

## Exact scope

The exhaustive claims are limited to squarefree coefficient-ideal directions
of ideal norm at most 128, the diagonal Leech-spatial family above, the stated
complete primitive local mirror classes, and the first necessary height
prefix.  The screen does not decide Leech realization of every Gram type and
does not enumerate later accepted Vinberg walls.

It therefore does **not** prove nonreflectivity, nonexistence, compactness, or
novelty for any full lattice.  It also says nothing exhaustive about nonunit
square multiples, other Niemeier spatial lattices, coefficients beyond the
norm cutoff, or the silver and cubic fields.  Its output is a reproducible
negative first-prefix census and a ranked continuation target.
