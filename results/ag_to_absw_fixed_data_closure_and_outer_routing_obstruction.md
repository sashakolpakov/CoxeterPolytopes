# AG-to-ABSW: fixed-data closure and the outer-routing obstruction

**Status (2026-08-11): exact conditional theorem plus exact obstruction.**
For a reflection-maximal class, the downstream chamber-counting problem is
now closed once algebraic geometry supplies finitely many native pairs
\((k,n)\). It is not necessary
to bound ambient N\'eron--Severi lattices, projector denominators, orthogonal
complements, or glue. The required downstream input is the native arithmetic
reflection lattice up to similarity.

The upstream essential-surjectivity problem is not closed. In fact, the
current complex rational/full-trace/projected-root K3 routing disjunction is
false on an absolutely reflection-maximal rank-22 chamber. Thus the result
below is a genuine conditional recovery of ABSW-type finiteness, not an
AG-only proof of the global ABSW theorem.

## 1. The finite-pair theorem

For a finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chamber
\(P\subset\mathbf H^n\), write \(k(P)\) for its minimal Vinberg ground field,
\(m(P)=n+1\), and \(W(P)\) for its side-reflection group.

### Theorem 1.1 (finite native pairs imply finite maximal chambers)

Let \(\mathfrak C\) be a class of finite-sided finite-volume arithmetic
Coxeter chambers of dimension at least two. Assume that every \(W(P)\) is
maximal among discrete reflection groups, or merely maximal among arithmetic
reflection groups. If

\[
       \{(k(P),m(P)):P\in\mathfrak C\}
\]

is finite, then \(\mathfrak C\) contains only finitely many unmarked
normalized chamber classes.

#### Proof

Fix \(P\), its admissible \(k\)-space \(V\), and an integral
\(\mathcal O_k\)-lattice \(L\subset V\) preserved by \(W(P)\). Let \(R(L)\)
be the group generated by all integral native hyperplane reflections of
\(L\). Then

\[
                    W(P)\subseteq R(L)\subseteq O^+(L).
\]

Both endpoint groups have finite covolume. Hence \(W(P)\) has finite index
in \(O^+(L)\), so \(R(L)\) also has finite index. Since
\([O(L):O^+(L)]\le2\), \(L\) is reflective in the precise sense of
Nikulin's fixed-data theorem.

For fixed totally real \(k\) and fixed \(m\ge3\), Nikulin's Theorem 5.2.1
gives only finitely many similarity classes of such reflective hyperbolic
\(\mathcal O_k\)-lattices. Similarity is exactly the correct equivalence:
common rescaling cancels from every reflection and from every normalized
Coxeter Gram entry.

For each remaining host, maximality gives

\[
                             W(P)=R(L).
\]

The complete locally finite mirror arrangement has one \(R(L)\)-orbit of
chambers, by a finite generic-gallery argument. Thus every oriented native
similarity host contributes at most one unmarked normalized chamber. Taking
the finite union over the assumed finite pair set proves the theorem.
\(\square\)

## 2. What an exhaustive AG compiler would imply

Suppose a class of raw maximal arithmetic chambers admitted an exhaustive
exact compiler into the currently proved carrier species. Their branchwise
outputs are:

| carrier species | dimension/rank output | field output |
|---|---|---|
| direct rational full-NS/full-nef | \(n\le11\) | \(k=\mathbf Q\) |
| rational weak-log full-nef | \(n\le25\) | \(k=\mathbf Q\) |
| exact complex-K3 native or projected eigenslice, retaining the full native trace, admissibility, and integral order action when \(k\ne\mathbf Q\) | \({[k:\mathbf Q]}(n+1)\le20\), hence \(n\le19\) | noncompact \(k=\mathbf Q\); compact fields lie in a finite set, with \(\lvert\operatorname {Disc}k\rvert\le(32\cdot14^{10})^{30}\) |

Therefore an exhaustive stack of these kinds would give

\[
             n\le25,\qquad
             \{k(P)\}\text{ finite},
\]

and Theorem 1.1 would then give finitely many normalized maximal chambers.
This is the exact AG-to-fixed-data-to-ABSW composition. Infinite projected
ambient hosts are harmless: the golden scaling/glue family changes
\(\operatorname {NS}\) and its discriminant but fixes the native similarity
class and the normalized chamber.

The fixed-data step is not an AG theorem. It imports Nikulin's reflective-
lattice finiteness theorem. Removing that input leaves a precise missing
lemma: one must construct a bounded **connected** full-rank native root frame,
or an equivalent finite native order/volume datum. A minimal-aperture
projected physical star can be bounded and spanning while its nonzero Gram
graph is disconnected; a right-angled facet isolates one orthogonal block
and leaves its relative scale uncontrolled.

## 3. The current complex outer compiler is not exhaustive

Let \(P_{\mathrm {Bor}}\subset\mathbf H^{21}\) be Borcherds' 210-wall
chamber for

\[
                         L=U\oplus D_{20}(-1).
\]

The exact
[rank-22 outer-routing counterexample](borcherds_h21_outer_routing_counterexample.md)
and its
[independent audit](borcherds_h21_outer_routing_counterexample_independent_hostile_audit.md)
prove the following.

1. \(P_{\mathrm {Bor}}\) is arithmetic, finite-volume, noncompact, and
   absolutely reflection-maximal. Absolute maximality is proved without
   Esselmann's classification: the 42 short roots generate \(L\), their odd
   graph is connected, and the wall group acts as
   \(GL(2,\mathbf F_2)\) on \(A_L=(\mathbf Z/2)^2\). Hence a stable lattice
   between \(L\) and \(L^\vee\) is only \(L\) or \(L^\vee\), which have the
   same full reflection group.
2. A complex K3 full-trace or projected-root return is impossible because
   an injective rank-22 Lorentzian image would require
   \(22\le\rho(X)\le20\).
3. The direct rational branch is impossible. The weak-log compiler is also
   empty after exhausting the two rational physical square classes.
   The two surviving dual adjunction colourings each contract 21 disjoint
   \((-1)\)-curves and force the \(PG(2,4)\) point-line incidence on the
   remaining 21 boundary images and the 21 complex contraction points. Its
   Fano subplane has diagonal-point determinant \(-2\), so it cannot occur
   over \(\mathbf C\).

Consequently the present complex rational/projected-K3 disjunction cannot be
used as a universal AG proof of ABSW or even of its global dimension bound.
This is an obstruction to the compiler, not to the chamber.

Indeed, Dolgachev--Kondō's supersingular K3 surface of Artin invariant one
in characteristic two has this same Picard lattice. Its 42 short roots are
actual smooth rational curves, while the 168 long reflections are Cremona
actions rather than 168 additional nef-wall curves. Thus the counterexample
also identifies the plausible missing species: a mixed divisor/action
carrier in positive characteristic. No class-wide compiler or finite-pair
theorem for that species has been proved here.

If such a mixed K3/action compiler were made class-wide with an injective
native block, the K3 Picard ceiling in arbitrary characteristic,
\(\rho\le22\), would give the attractive bound \(n\le21\), with
\(P_{\mathrm {Bor}}\) at the endpoint. A full-trace nonrational version would
instead retain the sharper necessary inequality
\({[k:\mathbf Q]}(n+1)\le22\). This is a precise proposed route, not a theorem
of the present package.

## 4. Rank eligibility alone is not projected-K3 universality

The rank-20 endpoint gives an independent obstruction beyond the trivial
rank-overflow argument. Vinberg's \(X_3\) chamber in
\(\mathbf H^{19}\) has a complete 36-wall packet on

\[
       U\oplus2E_8(-1)\oplus A_2(-1),
\]

with 24 primitive norm-\(-2\) lines and 12 primitive norm-\(-6\),
divisibility-three lines. Any exact projected complex-K3 eigenslice has
rank 20, so \(\rho=20\) and the projector complement vanishes. A common
rational similarity and ambient square-\((-2)\) lifts would force

\[
                      ca^2=1,\qquad 3cb^2=1,
                      \qquad (a/b)^2=3,
\]

which is impossible over \(\mathbf Q\). Hence even a chamber inside the K3
rank range need not have an exact-module projected root return. It does have
a weaker mixed-divisor K3 realization—24 curve walls and 12 additional
divisor cuts—showing again that arbitrary NS inequalities are much weaker
than exact nef/root-wall geometry. This chamber is complete for its pinned
host but is not asserted absolutely reflection-maximal, so it attacks
all-finite-volume universality rather than the maximal-only quantifier.

and its

## 5. What must actually be encoded geometrically

The answer is not simply “the Coxeter Gram matrix sits in
\(\operatorname {NS}\).” There are three distinct levels.

1. **Literal full-nef K3 return.** If every wall is the same in-trace
   primitive physical \((-2)\)-curve and the complete nef chamber is returned,
   finite-volume geometry gives a bounded spanning root packet. Then the
   Picard hosts are finite and K3 Weyl transitivity gives one chamber orbit.
2. **Projected K3 return.** The carrier must additionally record actual
   ambient \((-2)\)-root lifts, an ample eigenframe, the self-adjoint projector,
   complement and glue, and an exhaustive no-zero-projection/no-hidden-cut
   test. These data can return individual chambers, but their ambient hosts
   need not be finite.
3. **Abstract maximal-chamber counting.** Ambient host finiteness is
   unnecessary. What matters is the native arithmetic reflection lattice
   up to similarity. Once AG bounds the pair \((k,n)\), Nikulin's fixed-data
   theorem and reflection maximality finish the count.

For canonical/log carriers one must also retain simultaneous prime
effectivity and the canonical/MMP compatibility. For mixed action carriers
one must explain which divisor walls and which cohomological or birational
reflections reproduce the raw side group. A bare lattice embedding, or an
arbitrary list of divisor inequalities, supplies none of these return gates.

## 6. Final verdict

The strongest correct statement is therefore:

> **Conditional ABSW recovery.** Any exhaustive exact algebraic-geometric
> compiler for a class of maximal finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chambers
> which produces only finitely many native pairs \((k,n)\) yields finitely
> many normalized chambers, by Theorem 1.1. Exact complex-K3 returns already
> supply this finite-pair output in their declared domain.

But:

> **No global AG-only theorem from the current stack.** The absolutely
> maximal Borcherds \(\mathbf H^{21}\) chamber lies outside all current complex
> rational and projected-root K3 branches. A broader mixed
> positive-characteristic divisor/action category is geometrically suggested
> but not universally constructed. Moreover, the finite-pair-to-chamber
> step presently uses Nikulin's fixed-data reflective-lattice theorem.

Thus one can recover ABSW finiteness **conditionally and cleanly** from AG
rank/field bounds plus fixed-data lattice finiteness. One cannot honestly
claim a new proof of the global ABSW theorem purely from the NS-lattice
geometry developed so far.
