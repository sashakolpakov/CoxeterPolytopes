# The adapted `P10` obstruction: maximality and both canonical escapes still do not create the physical boundary

**Status (2026-08-11): exact surface-free saturation theorem and complex
decorated-carrier no-go.**  This note isolates the sharp remaining failure of
property 4.  No Nikulin--Vinberg reflective-lattice classification, chamber
census, or surface is used to prove the raw Coxeter statement.

There is a finite-volume chamber on the standard marked lattice

\[
                         L=I_{1,10}
\]

with ten ordinary `(-2)` facets, one ordinary `(-1)` facet, and one
twice-primitive physical `(-4)` facet.  It has both canonical escapes,
passes the favourable weak-incidence gate, and its side group contains
**every** integral reflection of `L`.  Nevertheless it has no exact complex
full-nef rational carrier with that physical colouring.

This is a decorated-carrier obstruction, not an obstruction to the
uncoloured polytope.  The uncoloured chamber is classical `P10` and does
have a rational flex-cluster full-nef carrier.  On that carrier, however,
the primitive anticanonical wall is an irreducible genus-one `(-1)` curve;
it is not half of a reduced smooth rational `(-4)` boundary.  The lattice
and mirror arrangement cannot distinguish those two geometric meanings.

## 1. The raw physical packet

Use the basis `H,E_1,...,E_10`, with

\[
 H^2=1,\qquad E_i^2=-1,
\]

and put

\[
 K=-3H+\sum_{i=1}^{10}E_i,
 \qquad F=-K=3H-\sum_{i=1}^{10}E_i.                 \tag{1.1}
\]

Thus `K` is characteristic and

\[
                         K^2=F^2=-1=10-\operatorname{rk}L.   \tag{1.2}
\]

Take the primitive mirror normals

\[
\begin{aligned}
 e&=E_{10},\\
 d_i&=E_i-E_{i+1}\quad(1\le i\le9),\\
 c&=H-E_1-E_2-E_3,\\
 f&=F.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{1.3}
\]

The **physical** normal on the final mirror is not `f`; it is

\[
                              B=2f.                         \tag{1.4}
\]

The numerical adjunction colours are exactly

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
        &e&d_i,c&B\\ \hline
 C^2    &-1&-2&-4\\
 K.C    &-1&0&2.
\end{array}                                                  \tag{1.5}
\]

In particular `B` is twice primitive.  The adapted module is not a larger
hidden lattice:

\[
             M=\langle L,B/2\rangle=L,
\]

with the form doubled in the standard adapted convention.  All twelve side
reflections are integral on `M`.

Let `W` be the homogeneous wall cone

\[
 W=\{x:x.e\ge0,\ x.d_i\ge0,\ x.c\ge0,\ x.f\ge0\}.    \tag{1.6}
\]

The two canonical orientations both escape:

\[
                    K.e=-1<0,\qquad F.f=-1<0.               \tag{1.7}
\]

Thus neither `K` nor `-K` lies in the proposed chamber cone.  This removes
the canonical-orientation defect of the earlier rank-three triangle.

The natural wrong-log correction supplies no positivity:

\[
                D=-\left(K+\frac12B\right)=F-f=0.           \tag{1.8}
\]

This is not caused by a hostile local incidence.  There is only one boundary,

\[
              B.d_i=B.c=0,\qquad B.e=2,                    \tag{1.9}
\]

so the packet passes the favourable conditions `W1--W3` exactly.  It is the
strong-Coble equality branch at the level of the lattice.

## 2. Exact finite volume without a chamber census

The nonorthogonal Coxeter pairs are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{pair}&\text{order}\\ \hline
d_i,d_{i+1}\ (1\le i\le8)&3\\
d_3,c&3\\
d_9,e&4\\
e,f&\infty.
\end{array}                                                  \tag{2.1}
\]

All other pairs are orthogonal.  This already proves that the displayed
halfspaces form a Coxeter packet.

For volume, the certifier performs the complete exact dual calculation in
dimension eleven.  The first eleven normals in (1.3), omitting `f`, have
determinant `-1`; hence the cone is pointed and full-dimensional.  Every
extreme ray is therefore the null line of at least ten independent facet
equations.  Enumerating the `binomial(12,10)=66` possible active sets over
`Q` gives exactly

\[
                17\text{ positive rays},\qquad
                 2\text{ null rays},\qquad
                 0\text{ negative rays}.                   \tag{2.2}
\]

All nineteen have positive `H` coordinate and all mutual Lorentz products
are nonnegative.  Their sum is the strict interior vector

\[
 A=(137,-47,-45,-43,-41,-39,-37,-35,-33,-31,-30),          \tag{2.3}
\]

with

\[
 A^2=3940,qquad
 (A.e,A.d_1,\ldots,A.d_9,A.c,A.f)
 =(30,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,1,2,30).                             \tag{2.4}
\]

Every facet's active rays have rank ten, so no inequality is redundant.
Since a finite-sided hyperbolic polytope whose projective vertices are all
finite or ideal is a finite union of finite-volume finite/ideal simplices,
the chamber has finite volume.  The two null rays are isolated because their

An important corollary of the same dual calculation is

\[
                        x\in W\Longrightarrow x^2\ge0.      \tag{2.5}

Indeed `W` is generated by the nineteen future nonspacelike rays, and the
Lorentz product of any two such rays is nonnegative.

## 3. Classification-free saturation of every lattice reflection

### Theorem 3.1

The reflections in (1.3) generate every integral hyperbolic reflection of
`I_(1,10)`.  Consequently their chamber is relatively reflection-maximal in
`O^+(M)=O^+(L)`.

### Proof

Let `u` be the primitive normal of an integral hyperbolic reflection.  Since
`L` is unimodular, `u` has divisibility one.  Integrality of

\[
              s_u(x)=x-\frac{2(x.u)}{u^2}u
\]

then forces `u^2` to divide `2`.  Therefore

\[
                             u^2=-q,\qquad q\in\{1,2\}.      \tag{3.1}
\]

The reflections in `e,d_1,...,d_9` generate all signed permutations of the
ten exceptional coordinates.  Up to those reflections and an overall sign,
write

\[
 u=(a,-m_1,\ldots,-m_{10}),\qquad
 a\ge0,\quad m_1\ge\cdots\ge m_{10}\ge0.                   \tag{3.2}
\]

The norm equation is

\[
                         \sum m_i^2=a^2+q.                  \tag{3.3}
\]

If `a=0`, (3.3) leaves one unit coordinate for `q=1`, or two unit
coordinates for `q=2`.  Signed permutations reduce these to `e` and `d_9`.
Assume henceforth `a>0`.  Equation (3.3) gives `m_i\le a`.

Put

\[
                       s=m_1+m_2+m_3.                       \tag{3.4}
\]

If `s>a` and `s<3a`, reflection in `c` changes the time coefficient to

\[
                             a'=2a-s,
\]

so `|a'|<a`.  If `s=3a`, then the first three multiplicities equal `a`, and
(3.3) forces `a=1,q=2` with all remaining multiplicities zero.  This is the
simple root `c` itself.  Thus Cremona reflection either terminates or strictly
decreases `|a|`.

It remains to treat the reduced case

\[
                             s\le a.                         \tag{3.5}
\]

Put `T=sum m_i`.  The inequalities in (3.2) and (3.5) say that `u` pairs
nonnegatively with `e,d_i,c`.  If `3a-T\ge0`, it also pairs nonnegatively
with `f`, placing the negative vector `u` in `W`, contrary to (2.5).  Hence

\[
                          d:=T-3a\ge1.                       \tag{3.6}
\]

Reflection in `f` changes the time coefficient to

\[
                          a'=a-6d.                           \tag{3.7}
\]

We claim that either `u=f` or `d<a/3`.  Suppose instead `d\ge a/3`.
Cauchy--Schwarz on the first three and last seven multiplicities gives

\[
 a^2+q
 =\sum m_i^2
 \ge \frac{s^2}{3}+\frac{(T-s)^2}{7}.                       \tag{3.8}
\]

The right side is decreasing for `0\le s\le a`, because its unconstrained
minimum is at `s=3T/10\ge a`.  Therefore

\[
 a^2+q
 \ge \frac{a^2}{3}+\frac{(2a+d)^2}{7}
 \ge \frac{10a^2}{9}.                                      \tag{3.9}
\]

Thus `a^2\le9q\le18`, so `a\le4`.  The four cases are elementary.

- If `a=1`, then all `m_i` are zero or one, so `T=sum m_i^2\le3`,
  contradicting `T>3`.
- If `a=2`, then `T\le sum m_i^2\le6`, contradicting `T>6`.
- If `a=3` and some `m_i\ge2`, then
  `sum m_i(m_i-1)\ge2`, hence `T\le9`, contradicting `T>9`.
  Thus all `m_i` are zero or one.  The only solution is
  `q=1,m_i=1` for all ten `i`, namely `u=f`.
- If `a=4`, integrality gives `d\ge2`, hence `T\ge14`.  But
  `sum m_i(m_i-1)=16+q-T\le4`; no `m_i` is at least three and at most two
  entries can equal two.  Therefore `T\le12`, a contradiction.

This proves the claim.  Away from the simple root `f`, (3.7) now gives

\[
                            |a-6d|<a.                        \tag{3.10}
\]

After changing overall sign and sorting again, the positive integer `|a|`
strictly descends.  The process terminates at one of `e,d_9,c,f`.  Reversing
the sequence expresses the reflection in every `u` as a conjugate of a side
reflection.  Hence the side group contains every integral reflection.
No proper reflection overgroup inside `O^+(L)` remains.  \(\square\)

The finite script enumerates all reduced roots through `a=12` as a hostile
regression test, but the proof above is the unbounded theorem.  Maximality is
not inferred from a bounded root search or from the name `P10`.

## 4. The complex physical full-nef carrier is impossible

Assume for contradiction that a smooth projective rational surface over
`C` realizes the marked packet literally:

1. `NS(S)=L` and `K_S=K` under the marking;
2. the twelve physical normals in (1.5) are represented by the required
   distinct smooth rational negative curves; and
3. the displayed chamber is the entire projectivized nef cone.

The boundary would be a reduced irreducible smooth rational curve

\[
                            B=-2K_S.                         \tag{4.1}
\]

Moreover `|-K_S|` is empty.  Indeed, if `A` were effective with
`A\sim-K_S`, then

\[
                             A.B=-2<0,
\]

so `A` and the irreducible `B` would share `B` as a component.  But an ample
degree immediately rules out `B\le A`, since `[B]=2[A]`.  Thus `S` would be
a complex one-boundary strong Coble surface.

The ten `(-2)` facets are exactly the effective irreducible Coble--Mukai
roots.  A hidden irreducible `(-2)` curve could not be invisible in a literal
full nef cone: for an ample `H`,

\[
                  H+\frac{H.C}{2}C
\]

is a positive-square nef class in `C^perp`, strictly positive on every other
irreducible curve.  Hence `C^perp` is a genuine nef facet.

The raw root chamber has finite volume, so the standard
Dolgachev--Kondō finite-index criterion forces finite automorphism group.
Kondō's exhaustive complex classification of finite-automorphism
one-boundary Coble surfaces then leaves Types I and II, and each has twelve
effective irreducible Coble--Mukai roots.  The proposed chamber has ten.
This contradiction is the already frozen AG obstruction in
[`coble_nodot_maxdegree3_closure.md`](coble_nodot_maxdegree3_closure.md),
whose exact archive is SHA-pinned by the new certifier.

Therefore:

> **Maximal decorated nonrealizability.**  Finite volume, odd unimodularity,
> a standard characteristic canonical marking, both canonical escapes,
> exact physical adjunction colours, favourable weak boundary incidence,
> and relative reflection maximality do not force a complex rational
> full-nef carrier with simultaneous physical curve effectivity.

The raw saturation theorem is project-native and classification-free.  The
terminal nonrealizability input is an AG classification of complex Coble
surfaces, not a Nikulin--Vinberg classification of reflective lattices.

## 5. Exact lineage and why the uncoloured carrier does not help

The Coxeter support (2.1) identifies the uncoloured packet with the familiar

```text
classical P10 = R12-E8A1x2-C2-H10-12.
```

This identification is only lineage; Theorem 3.1 independently proves the
maximality needed here.

It is not the frozen two-boundary `H11/26` chamber.  The invariants already
separate them:

| packet | lattice | hyperbolic dimension/facets | boundaries |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| this packet | `I_(1,10)` | `H10/12` | 1 |
| Type-I `H11/26` | `I_(1,11)` | `H11/26` | 2 |

The Type-I `H11/26` physical split is
`2*(-4)+10*(-2)+14*(-1)`, so neither its full Gram nor its support can be
isomorphic to (1.3).  It is also not either one-boundary complex full-nef
chamber `H10/22` or `H10/30`, both of which have twelve Coble--Mukai roots.

The uncoloured `P10` nevertheless has a rational full-nef realization: blow
up ten successive flex-cluster points on a smooth plane cubic.  The same
primitive class `F=-K` is then represented by the strict cubic.  Adjunction
gives

\[
                 p_a(F)=1+\frac{F^2+K.F}{2}=1,              \tag{5.1}
\]

so this is an irreducible genus-one `(-1)` curve.  The numerical physical
class `B=2F` has the correct formal data

\[
                         B^2=-4,\qquad K.B=2,
\]

but on the flex carrier it is the nonreduced double of the genus-one wall,
not a reduced smooth rational curve.  Thus the positive uncoloured carrier
does not repair the decorated property-4 failure.

This is the cleanest available demonstration that numerical adjunction is
not an effectivity or reducedness theorem.

## 6. What property 4 must return

The obstruction survives even though the wrong-log class is nef (`D=0`)
and all weak incidences hold.  Therefore the missing invariant cannot be
only a lattice vector in the homogeneous wall cone, and it cannot be only
`D`-nefness.

The exact missing gate is:

> **P-native simultaneous reduced-irreducible effectivity.**  From the raw
> marked walls, construct a geometric point of a cluster/Hilbert/period
> incidence space on which every ordinary normal and every physical
> divisibility-two class is represented simultaneously by the prescribed
> smooth rational curve.  In particular, certify that `B=2b` is a reduced
> irreducible rational divisor, rather than a merely numerical class or the
> nonreduced double of a genus-one primitive mirror `b`.

A semiampleness/localization certificate may be part of such a gate, but it
must produce the section and verify reducedness, irreducibility, smoothness,
and simultaneous incidences.  Reflection saturation can then remove hidden
walls; it cannot create those sections.

## 7. Scope

This note does **not** claim:

- that abstract `P10` does not exist;
- that `P10` has no rational carrier with a different geometric colouring;
- nonrealizability in positive characteristic;
- a universal dimension bound for all adapted hosts;
- that hostile `H1/H2/H3` incidences have been classified; or
- that Kondō's classification is replaced by the raw descent.

It proves the stronger local warning that even the favourable
`H1/H2/H3`-free case can fail at the physical effectivity gate.  Any
universal adapted theorem must distinguish the genus-one anticanonical-wall
branch from the reduced anti-bicanonical boundary branch.
