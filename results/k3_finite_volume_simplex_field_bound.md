# Finite-volume K3 Coxeter simplices have a finite field set

**Status (2026-08-11): proved for every dimension and field degree allowed
by a full-trace complex-K3 carrier.** Let \(P\subset\mathbf H^n\),
\(n\ge2\), be a finite-volume Coxeter simplex with \(m=n+1\) facets. Suppose
an exact eigen-nef carrier returns a rank-\(m\) module over its minimal
Vinberg field \(k\), and a complex projective K3 surface has Picard lattice
equal to the primitive full trace. Then

\[
  m[k:\mathbf Q]\le20.                                   \tag{0.1}
\]

Every two facets of an ordinary finite-volume simplex meet at a finite or
ideal ridge. Hence every pair cyclic Cartan product has every conjugate at
most \(4\). Symmetry then bounds a simple directed cycle of length \(\ell\)
by \(2^\ell\). Reflection integrality puts all these cycles in the returned
order, while elementary cycle decomposition shows that they generate the
minimal Vinberg field. Combining this with (0.1) gives the uniform bound

\[
  |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|\le 2^{240}=256^{30}.          \tag{0.2}
\]

Thus exact full-trace K3 carriers of finite-volume Coxeter simplices use only
finitely many fields. No Coxeter-simplex classification, angle list,
reflective-lattice census, imposed correspondence height, or reflection
maximality enters the arithmetic bound.

## 1. Exact carrier hypotheses

Let \(\mathcal O\subset k\) be an order in a totally real field and let
\(M\) be a finite projective rank-\(m\) \(\mathcal O\)-module with an
admissible symmetric \(k\)-form \(h\). At one embedding \(\sigma _0\), the
form has signature \((n,1)\); at every other embedding it is positive
definite. Assume:

1. a minimal complex projective K3 surface \(X\) has
   \(\operatorname {NS}(X)\) equal to the primitive full trace of \(M\),
   and the returned order acts self-adjointly by algebraic
   correspondences;
2. the prescribed projectivized eigen-nef chamber is exactly the same
   finite-volume Coxeter simplex \(P\), including its finite and ideal
   faces;
3. every facet reflection preserves \(M\); and
4. \(k\) is the minimal Vinberg field generated by the cyclic Cartan
   products of those facet reflections.

Completeness, saturation and declared-host reflection maximality may be
retained in the carrier package, but are not used below. Equality with the
full Picard trace gives

\[
  md=\rho(X)\le20,
  \qquad d=[k:\mathbf Q],                                 \tag{1.1}
\]

which proves (0.1). If \(d=1\), the field is already \(\mathbf Q\), so the
proof may assume \(d\ge2\). In particular \(3\le m\le10\) and \(d\le6\).

## 2. Every simple cycle is integral and bounded

Choose facet normals \(e_0,\ldots,e_{m-1}\), and use

\[
  a_{ij}=\frac{2h(e_i,e_j)}{h(e_i,e_i)}.                  \tag{2.1}
\]

Put \(P_i=1-s_i\). Since \(s_i\) preserves \(M\), the rank-one operator
\(P_i\) is \(\mathcal O\)-linear. For every directed cycle
\(C=(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 c(C)
 &=a_{i_1i_2}a_{i_2i_3}\cdots a_{i_\ell i_1}\\
 &=\operatorname {Tr}_{\mathcal O}
   (P_{i_1}P_{i_2}\cdots P_{i_\ell})
 \in\mathcal O.                                           \tag{2.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

For a pair of facets, write

\[
  p_{ij}=a_{ij}a_{ji}
        =\frac{4h(e_i,e_j)^2}{h(e_i,e_i)h(e_j,e_j)}.       \tag{2.3}
\]

Every proper rank-two subdiagram of a finite-volume Coxeter simplex is
elliptic or parabolic. Therefore

\[
  0\le\sigma _0(p_{ij})\le4.                              \tag{2.4}
\]

At a definite embedding, Cauchy--Schwarz gives the strict version of (2.4).
Symmetry of \(h\) makes a directed simple cycle equal to its reversal, and
hence

\[
  c(C)^2=\prod_{r=1}^{\ell}p_{i_ri_{r+1}},
  \qquad i_{\ell+1}=i_1.                                  \tag{2.5}
\]

At every embedding \(\sigma\), equations (2.4)--(2.5) give

\[
  |\sigma(c(C))|\le2^\ell\le2^m.                          \tag{2.6}
\]

Every closed directed walk decomposes into simple directed cycles by cutting
at repeated vertices. Thus all cyclic Cartan products lie in the algebra
generated by the bounded simple cycles, and those cycles generate the
minimal Vinberg field by hypothesis 4.

## 3. A uniformly bounded primitive element

Choose a minimal field-generating sequence of simple cycles. Every strict
intermediate extension has degree at least two, so its length is at most
\(\lfloor\log _2d\rfloor\). Because \(d\le6\), at most two cycles are
needed; call their coordinates \(\theta _1,\theta _2\).

If two are needed, one of

\[
  \theta _1+q\theta _2,
  \qquad 0\le q\le\binom d2,                              \tag{3.1}
\]

is primitive. For each unordered pair of embeddings, at most one value of
\(q\) makes their images equal, and joint field generation prevents a pair
from agreeing for every \(q\). Since \(d\le6\), one may use the uniform grid
\(0\le q\le15\). Equation (2.6) gives a primitive algebraic integer
\(\theta\) satisfying

\[
  |\sigma(\theta)|\le16\cdot2^m=2^{m+4}.                  \tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently, differences of conjugates are at most \(2^{m+5}\), and

\[
  |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|
  \le2^{(m+5)d(d-1)}.                                     \tag{3.3}
\]

The integer possibilities with \(m\ge3\), \(d\ge2\), and \(md\le20\)
give

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
m&3&4&5&6&7\le m\le10\\ \hline
d\text{ at most}&6&5&4&3&2\\
(m+5)d(d-1)\text{ at most}&240&180&120&66&30.
\end{array}                                                \tag{3.4}
\]

Thus (3.3) implies (0.2). Hermite--Minkowski proves the finite field set.

### Theorem 3.1

Every exact full-trace complex-K3 carrier satisfying Section 1 and returning
a finite-volume Coxeter simplex has ground field in the finite set bounded
by (0.2).

## 4. Scope

The theorem does not construct the K3 carrier, prove essential surjectivity,
or classify Coxeter simplices. It applies to compact simplices and to
finite-volume simplices with ideal faces. It does not apply unchanged to
truncated hyperideal polytopes or general higher-facet polytopes, because
those may contain ultraparallel facet pairs with unbounded distinguished
cyclic products.
