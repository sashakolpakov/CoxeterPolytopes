# The wrong-log rational escape: an orbifold rank bound and its exact failure locus

**Status (2026-08-10): proved over \(\mathbf C\), carrier-relative.**
This note closes the \(\mathbb F_4\)-type part of the square-minus-four
escape which was left outside the direct and standard-Coble lanes.  It gives
the explicit project-native bound

\[
                         \rho(S)\leq26,\qquad
                         \dim\mathbf P(\operatorname {Nef}S)\leq25.       \tag{0.1}
\]

The only external inequality is semipositivity of the orbifold second Chern
class for a complex klt variety with nef anticanonical divisor.  No
Nikulin--Vinberg reflective-lattice classification, ABSW, Prasad formula, or
arithmetic-volume bound is used.  Every reduction from the physical wall
alphabet to the singular surface and every numerical inequality after that
input is proved below.

This is not a universal raw-polytope theorem.  The input is already a literal
full-nef rational carrier with a complete geometric Mori alphabet.  Nor does
reflection maximality force the incidence hypotheses below.  The final
section identifies the precise non-klt incidence locus which remains open.

## 1. Exact carrier hypotheses

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective rational surface over \(\mathbf C\), with
\(\rho=\rho(S)\geq3\).  Assume that its closed Mori cone is rational
polyhedral and is generated by finitely many smooth rational negative curves.
Consequently these curves span \(N_1(S)_{\mathbf Q}\).

Assume the physical wall alphabet consists only of

\[
 E^2=-1,\qquad R^2=-2,\qquad B_i^2=-4,                       \tag{1.1}
\]

and put \(B=\sum_{i=1}^{b}B_i\), with \(b>0\).  The \(B_i\) are **all**
physical square-minus-four generators, not a convenient subset.  This is the
alphabet forced by the rational reflection-integrality gate once the
standard half-boundary module is fixed; effectivity, smoothness, rationality,
and completeness remain geometric hypotheses here.

For the Coxeter application use the standard integral host

\[
 M=\left\langle\operatorname {NS}(S),B_1/2,\ldots,B_b/2\right\rangle,
 \qquad q_M=2q_{\operatorname {NS}(S)},                       \tag{1.2}
\]

allowing \(B_i/2\) already to lie in \(\operatorname {NS}(S)\) in the
twice-primitive case.  Integrality makes \(B_i.B_j\) even.  If a
square-minus-two wall \(R\) has \(B_i.R>0\), the Coxeter-angle condition
rules out \(B_i.R=1\): the normalized product would be
\(1/\sqrt8\), strictly between the values for \(m=2\) and \(m=3\).
Thus a positive \(B_i.R\) is at least \(2\).

## 2. The exact anticanonical incidence test

Define the natural wrong-log divisor

\[
                  D=-\left(K_S+\frac12B\right).               \tag{2.1}
\]

Adjunction gives \(K_S.B_i=2\), \(K_S.R=0\), and \(K_S.E=-1\).
Direct substitution gives the complete pairing table

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D.B_i&=-\frac12\sum_{j\ne i}B_i.B_j,\\
 D.R&=-\frac12B.R,\\
 D.E&=1-\frac12B.E.                                          \tag{2.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

### Proposition 2.1 (weak index-two incidence is exactly antinef-log)

Under the complete-Mori hypothesis, \(D\) is nef if and only if

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(W1)}&B_i.B_j=0\quad(i\ne j),\\
\text{(W2)}&B.R=0\quad\text{for every square-minus-two generator }R,\\
\text{(W3)}&B.E\leq2\quad\text{for every square-minus-one generator }E.
\end{array}                                                   \tag{2.3}
\]

If these conditions hold, then \(D\equiv0\) if and only if
\(B.E=2\) for every square-minus-one generator.  In that case

\[
                              B=-2K_S                          \tag{2.4}
\]

in \(\operatorname {Pic}(S)\), so this is exactly the standard Coble
branch.  Otherwise some \(E\) has \(B.E=0\) or \(1\), \(D\ne0\), and the
carrier is a genuine wrong-log weak-index-two branch.

#### Proof

Equation (2.2), nonnegativity of intersections between distinct effective
curves, and complete Mori generation prove the nef equivalence.  Under
(W1)--(W3), \(D\) is already zero on every \(B_i\) and \(R\), and it is zero
on an \(E\) exactly when \(B.E=2\).  The generators span numerical curve
space, so vanishing on all of them is numerical triviality.  On a rational
surface \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(S)=0\) and \(\operatorname {Pic}(S)\) is
torsion-free.  Thus \(2D=-2K_S-B\equiv0\) implies (2.4).  The converse is
immediate. \(\square\)

The maximal \((2,3,\infty)\) chamber on
\(\operatorname {Bl}_p\mathbb F_4\) belongs to the strict branch, not to the
remaining failure locus: it satisfies all three weak inequalities but has
two square-minus-one degrees \(0\) and \(1\).

## 3. Contracting the boundary

Assume (W1)--(W3).  The \(B_i\) are disjoint and their intersection matrix is
\(-4I_b\).  There is a projective contraction

\[
                         f:S\longrightarrow Y                \tag{3.1}
\]

of precisely these curves.  One direct projectivity certificate is obtained
from an ample Cartier divisor \(A\): the rational divisor

\[
 H=A+\sum_i\frac{A.B_i}{4}B_i                                \tag{3.2}
\]

is nef and big, has \(H.B_i=0\), and has positive intersection with every
other irreducible curve.  More explicitly,

\[
 H^2=A^2+\sum_i\frac{(A.B_i)^2}{4}>0,
 \qquad \operatorname {Null}(H)=\coprod_iB_i.
\]

Choose \(m>0\) so that \(L=mH\) is Cartier.  Its
restriction to each \(B_i\cong\mathbf P^1\) has degree zero and is therefore
trivial.  Put \(\Delta=B/2\).  The snc pair \((S,\Delta)\) is klt, \(L\) is
nef, and for every \(a>0\)

\[
            aL-(K_S+\Delta)=aL+D
\]

is nef and big.  The klt surface base-point-free theorem makes \(L\)
semiample.  The Stein factorization of the resulting morphism
contracts exactly its null locus \(\coprod_iB_i\), proving both existence and
projectivity of (3.1).  This is the precise contraction theorem used here.
In particular, \(Y\) has an ample, hence nef and big, class.

Here is also the local singularity check, rather than an assumption hidden
in the notation.  At \(f(B_i)\) the complete minimal-resolution graph is the
single vertex \([-4]\).  For every positive exceptional cycle \(mB_i\),

\[
 p_a(mB_i)=1+\frac{(mB_i)^2+K_S.(mB_i)}2
           =1-2m^2+m\leq0.                                  \tag{3.3}
\]

Artin's rationality criterion makes the contracted germ rational.  The
one-string Hirzebruch--Jung contraction theorem then identifies the string
\([4]=4/1\) with the cyclic quotient singularity

\[
                              \frac14(1,1).                   \tag{3.4}
\]

In particular \(Y\) is normal, projective, klt, and
\(\mathbf Q\)-factorial.  Since \(f\) contracts precisely the \(B_i\), the
relative numerical sequence is

\[
 0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_i\mathbf Q[B_i]
  \longrightarrow N_1(S)_{\mathbf Q}
  \longrightarrow N_1(Y)_{\mathbf Q}\longrightarrow0.
\]

The matrix \(-4I_b\) makes the exceptional classes independent.  Thus the
relative Picard rank is exactly \(b\), so

\[
                       \rho(Y)=\rho(S)-b.                     \tag{3.5}
\]

The quotient singularities make \(K_Y\) \(\mathbf Q\)-Cartier.  Write
\(K_S=f^*K_Y+\sum_i a_iB_i\).  Since \(f^*K_Y.B_i=0\), adjunction and
disjointness give \(2=K_S.B_i=-4a_i\).  Hence

\[
 K_S=f^*K_Y-\frac12B,\qquad
 f^*(-K_Y)=-\left(K_S+\frac12B\right)=D.                      \tag{3.6}
\]

Thus \(-K_Y\) is nef.  This is the geometric meaning of the weak incidence
table: the wrong-log class is the pullback of the anticanonical class of a
klt quotient surface.  The Coble equality is the special case
\(K_Y\equiv0\).

## 4. The orbifold bound

### External AG input, pinned exactly

Iwai--Jinnouchi--Zhang, *Semipositivity of the orbifold second Chern class in
Fujiki's class*, [arXiv:2607.18699v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18699v1),
Theorem 1.3 (source label `thm-nef-anti-canonical`), proves:

> If a compact normal complex analytic klt variety admits a nef and big
> class and has nef anticanonical divisor, then its orbifold second Chern
> class is semipositive.

In dimension two there are no auxiliary polarization factors, so for \(Y\)
this says

\[
                              \widehat c_2(Y)\geq0.            \tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (explicit wrong-log Picard bound)

Under the hypotheses of Sections 1 and 2,

\[
                              \rho(S)\leq26.                  \tag{4.2}
\]

Hence a literal full-nef Coxeter chamber on \(S\) has hyperbolic dimension at
most \(25\).

#### Proof

A rational surface satisfies

\[
 e(S)=c_2(S)=2+\rho,\qquad K_S^2=10-\rho.                    \tag{4.3}
\]

For a compact complex surface with isolated quotient singularities, the
orbifold definition (equivalently orbifold Gauss--Bonnet) is

\[
 \widehat c_2(Y)=e(Y_{\rm reg})+\sum_{p\in\operatorname {Sing}Y}
                  \frac1{|G_p|}
                =e(Y)-\sum_p\left(1-\frac1{|G_p|}\right).     \tag{4.4}
\]

Contracting one \(\mathbf P^1\) to a point lowers the ordinary Euler number
by one.  Here every local group is \(G_p=\boldsymbol\mu_4\), so its orbifold
contribution is \(1/4\) instead of \(1\).  Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat c_2(Y)
 &=e(Y)-b\left(1-\frac14\right)\\
 &=(2+\rho-b)-\frac34b\\
 &=2+\rho-\frac74b.                                          \tag{4.5}
\end{aligned}
\]

The IJZ inequality (4.1) gives

\[
                              7b\leq4\rho+8.                  \tag{4.6}
\]

Independently, nefness of \(D=f^*(-K_Y)\) gives \(D^2\geq0\).  Disjointness,
adjunction, and (4.3) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D^2
 &=\left(K_S+\frac12B\right)^2\\
 &=K_S^2+K_S.B+\frac14B^2\\
 &=(10-\rho)+2b-b\\
 &=10-\rho+b\geq0.                                           \tag{4.7}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(b\geq\rho-10\).  Combining this with (4.6) yields

\[
 7(\rho-10)\leq4\rho+8,\qquad 3\rho\leq78,
\]

which is (4.2). \(\square\)

Only the sign \(\widehat c_2(Y)\geq0\) is imported from the 2026 IJZ
theorem.  The Coxeter-to-log incidence table, projective quotient
construction, discrepancy, Picard drop, orbifold local contributions,
Noether identities, and elimination of \(b\) are the elementary
surface-geometric part of the present argument.  This is not a hidden
reflective-lattice rank bound, and it does not use reflection maximality.

### Corollary 4.2 (carrier-defined ABSW-type finiteness)

Add relative reflection maximality inside \(O^+(M)\).  Ranks are now bounded
by \(26\).  For each rank there are finitely many rational-surface
Néron--Severi lattices, and the standard exponent-two host lies in the finite
interval

\[
 \operatorname {NS}(S)(2)\subseteq M
       \subseteq\frac12\operatorname {NS}(S).                 \tag{4.8}
\]

Maximality saturates the facet group to all reflections of \(M\), and the
full mirror arrangement has one chamber orbit.  Hence there are only
finitely many abstract reflection-maximal chambers **already admitting**
literal carriers satisfying (W1)--(W3).

This is not essential surjectivity from raw Coxeter data.  Once a literal
rational carrier with the standard wall module is constructed, the only
additional geometric compiler input used by Theorem 4.1 is the complete
Mori alphabet (hence its spanning property): that is what upgrades the
finite facet pairing table to nefness of \(D\).  Constructing such a carrier
from arbitrary raw Coxeter data is still a separate surjectivity gate; the
maximality assertion of this corollary must also be verified independently.

## 5. Why the remaining incidence locus is genuinely different

The weak inequalities are also the exact limit of every ordinary klt
log-Fano attempt supported on the physical negative walls.

### Proposition 5.1 (supported-boundary no-go)

Let

\[
                         \Theta=\sum_C a_C C,\qquad 0\leq a_C<1,           \tag{5.1}
\]

be any effective real boundary supported on the complete negative-wall set.
If any one of the following occurs,

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(H1)}&B_i.B_j>0\text{ for two boundary components},\\
\text{(H2)}&B_i.R>0\text{ for a square-minus-two wall},\\
\text{(H3)}&B.E=\sum_iB_i.E\geq3
             \text{ for a square-minus-one wall},
\end{array}                                                   \tag{5.2}
\]

then \(-\left(K_S+\Theta\right)\) cannot be nef.

#### Proof

Write \(a=a_{B_i}\).  Testing on \(B_i\) gives

\[
 2-4a+\sum_{C\ne B_i}a_C(B_i.C)\leq0,
\]

so every square-minus-four coefficient \(a_{B_i}\) must satisfy
\(a_{B_i}\geq1/2\).

For (H1), put \(I=B_i.B_j\).  The standard host makes \(I\) even, so
\(I\geq2\).  If the two coefficients are \(a,a'\), their two inequalities
give

\[
 4a\geq2+Ia',\qquad 4a'\geq2+Ia.
\]

For \(I=2\), addition forces \(a+a'\geq2\); for \(I\geq4\), addition is
already impossible.  Both contradict \(a,a'<1\).

For (H2), put \(I=B_i.R\geq2\) and \(c=a_R\).  The two tests give

\[
 4a\geq2+Ic,\qquad 2c\geq Ia.
\]

At \(I=2\) they force \(a\geq1\), and for \(I>2\) they are stronger.

For (H3), put \(t_i=B_i.E\), \(T=\sum_i t_i\geq3\),
\(Q=\sum_i t_i^2\), and \(c=a_E\).  Testing every \(B_i\) and then \(E\),
while dropping only nonnegative contributions, gives

\[
 a_i\geq\frac12+\frac{t_i c}{4},\qquad
 c\geq\sum_i t_i a_i-1.
\]

Consequently

\[
                 \left(1-\frac Q4\right)c\geq\frac T2-1.    \tag{5.3}
\]

If \(Q\geq4\), its left side is nonpositive whereas its right side is
positive.  If \(Q<4\), integrality and \(T\geq3\) force
\((T,Q)=(3,3)\), and (5.3) forces \(c\geq2\).  Both conclusions contradict
\(0\leq c<1\).  All omitted wall intersections are nonnegative and only
strengthen these inequalities. \(\square\)

Thus neither changing the coefficient \(1/2\) nor adding the other physical
walls repairs the hostile locus while keeping discrepancies bounded away
from zero.  Geometrically, contracting an (H1) pair already asks for
coefficient one in the discrepancy solution; the natural singular model is
log canonical, not klt.  The IJZ orbifold theorem and ordinary
\(\varepsilon\)-log-Fano boundedness therefore stop at exactly the same
boundary.

### Four geometric hostile controls

The three failures are realizable on smooth rational surfaces; they are not
formal empty cases.  Let \(H,F\) be the ruling classes on
\(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\), with \(H^2=F^2=0\) and \(H.F=1\).
Blow up only private smooth points of the indicated curves, never their
mutual intersection points.

1. Two smooth \((1,1)\)-curves intersect twice.  Six private blowups on each
   make both strict transforms square \(-4\), retaining intersection \(2\).
   This realizes (H1).
2. Two smooth \((1,1)\)-curves again intersect twice.  Six private blowups
   on the first and four on the second give squares \(-4\) and \(-2\),
   retaining intersection \(2\).  This realizes (H2).
3. Smooth curves of bidegrees \((1,1)\) and \((1,2)\) intersect three times.
   Six private blowups on the first and five on the second give squares
   \(-4\) and \(-1\), retaining intersection \(3\).  This realizes (H3).

The distributed edge case in (H3) is also geometric.  Take three distinct
fibers of one ruling and one fiber of the other ruling.  The first three are
pairwise disjoint and each meets the fourth once.  Four private blowups on
each of the first three make them \((-4)\)-curves; one private blowup on the
fourth makes it a \((-1)\)-curve.  Thus every component degree is only one
but the aggregate degree is \(B.E=1+1+1=3\), exactly the case missed by a
componentwise H3 test.

Adjunction gives canonical degrees \(2,0,-1\) as required.  These are local
geometric obstruction controls, not claims that the resulting whole nef
cones are reflection-maximal Coxeter chambers.

## 6. The \(\mathbb F_4\) coefficientwise calibration

For the blowup of \(\mathbb F_4\) at the positive-section torus fixed point,
use the basis \((B,F,E)\), put \(G=F-E\), and recall

\[
 B^2=-4,\quad E^2=G^2=-1,\quad
 B.E=0,\quad B.G=1,\quad K=(-2,-6,1).                         \tag{6.1}
\]

For \(\Delta=B/2\),

\[
 (K+\Delta).B=0,\qquad
 (K+\Delta).E=-1,\qquad
 (K+\Delta).G=-\frac12.                                      \tag{6.2}
\]

Thus \(D=-(K+\Delta)\) is nef and nonzero, coefficient by coefficient.  Here

\[
 \rho=3,\qquad b=1,\qquad D^2=8,\qquad
 \widehat c_2(Y)=2+3-\frac74=\frac{13}{4}.                    \tag{6.3}
\]

The maximal triangle is therefore a positive calibration of Theorem 4.1,
not a counterexample to it.  It remains a counterexample only to the false
claim that maximality forces \(K+B/2=0\).

## 7. Exact remaining lemma

There is no unbounded reflection-maximal carrier counterfamily here: this
note neither constructs one nor permits one in the weak incidence lane.  The
unresolved rational square-minus-four class has now narrowed to carriers
whose complete physical Mori alphabet contains at least one of (H1)--(H3),
with (H3) understood in its aggregate form.

The missing theorem must do one of two genuinely new things:

1. derive from global reflection maximality and complete-nef geometry that
   (H1)--(H3) cannot occur above an absolute rank; or
2. replace the klt anticanonical contraction by another bounded carrier for
   this explicitly non-klt incidence locus.

Local reflection saturation is insufficient: (H1) and (H2) are ordinary
rank-two Coxeter configurations, and the extra roots they generate already
belong to the same reflection subgroup.  An argument which simply reapplies
the standard log boundary with different coefficients cannot work by
Proposition 5.1.  This is the precise obstruction left for the project-native
AG path.
