# Vinberg integral points on exact quadric-arrangement moduli

**Status (2026-08-12): exact arithmetic-moduli translation, an unconditional
all-field finiteness theorem on the maximal Lambert stratum, and a sharp
global nonclosure theorem for Northcott, Shafarevich, special-point,
Lawrence--Venkatesh, and bounded-denominator arguments. This is not a proof
of global arithmetic-reflection finiteness.**

## 1. Outcome

For a marked quadric-arrangement carrier, Vinberg arithmeticity has a clean
arithmetic-geometric meaning. After quotienting the independent rescalings
of the facet roots, the regular invariant coordinates are the cyclic Cartan
products. Arithmeticity says that these coordinates are algebraic integers,
that the selected real point has Lorentzian signature, and that every other
real conjugate lies in the positive-definite region.

Thus the desired locus really is an integral-and-archimedean locus in an
affine invariant quotient of arrangement moduli. It is not automatically a
set of CM or Shimura-special points, nor a Shafarevich set with fixed field
and fixed bad-prime set.

There is one new positive theorem. On the exact stable-pair curve of maximal
Lambert quadrilaterals, the **entire** Vinberg-arithmetic locus is finite even
when the ground field varies. If

\[
                    (A-4)(B-4)=4                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

is the rank equation for the two dotted pair cycles, put \(u=A-4\). After
interchanging the two dotted pairs, arithmeticity gives

\[
 u_0\in(0,2],\qquad u^\sigma\in(-4,-1)
 \quad(\sigma\ne\sigma_0).                                             \tag{1.2}
\]

The explicit integer polynomial

\[
\begin{aligned}
 p(T)&=(T+1)(T+2)(T+3)(T+4),\\
 F(T)&=p(T)(p(T)+1)
\end{aligned}                                                          \tag{1.3}
\]

satisfies

\[
 \|F\|_{[-4,-1]}={225\over256}<1,
 \qquad
 \|F\|_{[0,2]}=129960.                                                  \tag{1.4}
\]

Taking the integer norm of \(F(u)\) proves

\[
                         [k:\mathbf Q]\le92.                            \tag{1.5}
\]

All conjugates of \(u\) have absolute value at most four, so coefficient
bounds for its monic minimal polynomial give only finitely many \(u\), hence
only finitely many labelled Lambert Grams and fields. This corrects the
earlier suggestion that this curve needs a separately supplied degree bound.

The mechanism does **not** globalize formally. Two exact integral packets
exhibit the two missing Northcott inputs separately:

* the root-of-unity cycles
  \(2+\zeta_m+\zeta_m^{-1}=4\cos^2(\pi/m)\) have bounded height and all
  conjugates in \((0,4)\), but unbounded degree; and
* powers of one Salem number give integral dotted cycles with fixed field
  degree and all nongeometric conjugates in \((0,4)\), but unbounded
  distinguished height.

The interval \([0,4]\) has logarithmic capacity one. It is exactly the
critical interval on which these cyclotomic and Salem-trace packets live.
The Lambert determinant relation improves it to the strict contraction
\([-4,-1]\); a general finite-volume arrangement has no known uniformly
chosen coordinate with that property.

Finally, an explicit Farey-polygon sequence gives actual arithmetic
finite-area Coxeter chambers in \(\mathbf H^2\), over \(\mathbf Q\), with
unbounded facet count, stable-pair volume, Gram height, and bad primes. They
are nonmaximal, of index tending to infinity in one arithmetic reflection
group. Thus arithmeticity and finite volume do not supply the missing
bounds. Absolute reflection maximality would have to be converted into a
new Diophantine height/complexity theorem. No standard AG theorem makes
that conversion.

## 2. Literal arithmetic locus in the invariant quotient

Let \(r_1,\ldots,r_f\) be facet roots in an admissible quadratic space
\((V,q)\), and put

\[
                 a_{ij}={2q(r_i,r_j)\over q(r_i,r_i)}.                  \tag{2.1}
\]

Replacing \(r_i\) by \(\lambda_i r_i\) sends \(a_{ij}\) to
\(\lambda_j\lambda_i^{-1}a_{ij}\). Hence a directed closed walk

\[
              b_C=a_{i_1i_2}a_{i_2i_3}\cdots a_{i_si_1}                \tag{2.2}
\]

is invariant under all root rescalings. Conversely, the invariant monomials
for this diagonal torus action are generated by directed cycles. Gram-rank
relations cut out the arrangement locus in this cycle-coordinate quotient.

Vinberg's criterion says, in this notation, that

1. the field generated by the \(b_C\) is totally real;
2. every \(b_C\) is an algebraic integer; and
3. at every nonselected real embedding the conjugate Gram is positive
   semidefinite, and the native ambient form is positive definite.

For a two-cycle,

\[
 t_{ij}=a_{ij}a_{ji}
       ={4q(r_i,r_j)^2\over q(r_i,r_i)q(r_j,r_j)},                     \tag{2.3}
\]

the last condition gives

\[
                    0\le \sigma(t_{ij})\le4                            \tag{2.4}
\]

at every nongeometric embedding. At the geometric embedding the value is
\(4\cos^2(\pi/m)\) for an intersecting Coxeter pair, \(4\) for a parallel
pair, and \(4\cosh^2d>4\) for an ultraparallel pair.

Therefore:

~~~text
Vinberg arithmeticity
  = integral cycle coordinates on the affine arrangement quotient
    + one Lorentzian real embedding
    + positive-definite conjugate embeddings.
~~~

Two cautions matter. Individual Cartan entries need not be integral; the
cyclic invariant coordinates are. Also, integral invariant coordinates are
not the same as good reduction of the quadric and all marked hyperplanes.
Good reduction requires the quadric, incidence, and discriminant minors to
stay invertible. Vinberg integrality imposes no uniform restriction on the
primes dividing those minors.

## 3. A contracting-coordinate lemma

> **Lemma 3.1 (one exceptional conjugate).** Let \(\alpha\) be a totally
> real algebraic integer of degree \(d\), with one selected conjugate in a
> compact interval \(J\) and every other conjugate in a compact interval
> \(I\). Suppose \(F\in\mathbf Z[T]\), \(F(\alpha)\ne0\), and
>
> \[
>       \rho=\sup_I|F|<1,\qquad M=\max(1,\sup_J|F|).                    \tag{3.1}
> \]
>
> Then
>
> \[
>                 d\le1+{\log M\over\log(1/\rho)}.                     \tag{3.2}
> \]
>
> If \(I\cup J\) is bounded, only finitely many such \(\alpha\) occur.

Indeed, \(F(\alpha)\) is a nonzero algebraic integer, so

\[
 1\le\left|N_{\mathbf Q(\alpha)/\mathbf Q}F(\alpha)\right|
   \le M\rho^{d-1}.                                                     \tag{3.3}
\]

This proves (3.2). If all conjugates have absolute value at most \(H\), the
coefficients of the monic minimal polynomial are bounded by
\(\binom djH^j\). Bounded \(d\) and bounded integral coefficients leave a
finite list.

There is a useful field-primitivity addendum. Let \(k\) be totally real,
\(\alpha\in k\), and suppose precisely one embedding of \(k\) sends
\(\alpha\) to \(J\), while every other embedding sends it to a disjoint
interval \(I\). Then \(k=\mathbf Q(\alpha)\). If the extension were proper,
the selected embedding of \(\mathbf Q(\alpha)\) would have at least two real
extensions to \(k\), producing two \(J\)-values.

For a moduli application one needs one more geometric hypothesis. If a
fixed finite-type exact-Gram stratum is quasi-finite over a fixed tuple of
integral cycle coordinates, and Lemma 3.1 bounds those coordinates, then
the arithmetic Gram image on that stratum is finite. Neither finite type
nor properness alone supplies \(\rho<1\).

## 4. All-field Lambert finiteness

Use the exact Lambert family and notation of
[the stable-pair theorem](maximal_lambert_exact_stable_pair_and_vhs_obstruction.md):

\[
 A=4x^2,\qquad B=4y^2,\qquad (A-4)(B-4)=4.                            \tag{4.1}
\]

Let a member be Vinberg arithmetic over its minimal ground field \(k\).
The two length-two cycles \(A,B\) are algebraic integers. At the selected
place \(A,B>4\). Set

\[
                         u=A-4,\qquad v=B-4.                           \tag{4.2}
\]

Then \(u,v\) are algebraic integers and \(uv=4\). Interchanging the two
opposite facet pairs if needed makes

\[
                              0<u_0\le2.                               \tag{4.3}
\]

At a nonselected embedding, the conjugate three-dimensional ambient form is
positive definite. In particular, each relevant two-root principal block is
positive definite, which gives \(0<\sigma(A),\sigma(B)<4\). Therefore
\(-4<\sigma(u),\sigma(v)<0\). The equation
\(\sigma(u)\sigma(v)=4\) sharpens this to

\[
                           -4<\sigma(u)<-1.                            \tag{4.4}
\]

The sign intervals are disjoint, so Lemma 3.1's addendum gives
\(k=\mathbf Q(u)\). Equivalently, this follows directly from minimality of
the Vinberg cycle field: the nonzero cycle graph is a tree, its field is
generated by \(A,B\), and \(B-4=4/(A-4)\).

We verify (1.4) directly. Write \(Y=T+5/2\). On \(T\in[-4,-1]\),
\(Y^2\in[0,9/4]\), and

\[
 p(T)=(Y^2-9/4)(Y^2-1/4)
      =Y^4-{5\over2}Y^2+{9\over16}.                                  \tag{4.5}
\]

The quadratic in \(Y^2\) has range \([-1,9/16]\). Hence

\[
       |p(T)(p(T)+1)|\le {225\over256}.                                \tag{4.6}
\]

On \([0,2]\), all four factors in \(p\) are positive and increasing, so

\[
                   0<F(T)\le360\cdot361=129960.                       \tag{4.7}
\]

In particular \(F(u)\ne0\). Taking norms gives

\[
 1\le129960\left({225\over256}\right)^{d-1}.                           \tag{4.8}
\]

The exact integer comparisons

\[
 129960\,225^{91}>256^{91},\qquad
 129960\,225^{92}<256^{92}                                             \tag{4.9}
\]

show that \(d\le92\). Since every conjugate of \(u\) has absolute value at
most four, Lemma 3.1 gives a finite list of \(u\). Equation \(v=4/u\)
determines \(v\), and the labelled Lambert Gram is determined by
\(A=u+4\) and \(B=v+4\).

> **Theorem 4.1 (all-field Lambert finiteness).** The exact Lambert
> stable-pair curve contains only finitely many Vinberg-arithmetic labelled
> Coxeter Grams over all totally real ground fields. Every such field has
> degree at most \(92\). Consequently its absolutely reflection-maximal
> arithmetic locus is finite as well.

Absolute maximality is not used in this proof. It remains a required
control: outside finitely many real parameters the Lambert family is
absolutely reflection-maximal, so maximality alone leaves the whole moving
curve. Arithmeticity cuts it to a finite set through (4.4).

## 5. Why the contraction is not automatic

### 5.1 Roots of unity: bounded height, unbounded degree

For an odd prime \(\ell\), let

\[
 \eta_\ell=2+\zeta_\ell+\zeta_\ell^{-1}
           =4\cos^2(\pi/\ell).                                        \tag{5.1}
\]

This is an algebraic integer of degree \((\ell-1)/2\), and all its
conjugates lie strictly between zero and four. It is exactly the two-cycle
of a Coxeter edge labelled \(\ell\). Its logarithmic height is at most
\(\log4\), while its degree is unbounded.

Thus the finite-angle Vinberg packet already contains infinitely many
integral points of bounded height. These values come from torsion points of
\(\mathbf G_m\), so calling the inputs special does not make them finite.
The roots of unity witness the critical capacity-one behavior of \([0,4]\).

### 5.2 Salem powers: bounded degree, unbounded height

Let \(\lambda>1\) be a Salem number and put

\[
                    t_r=2+\lambda^r+\lambda^{-r}\qquad(r\ge1).         \tag{5.2}
\]

The trace field \(\mathbf Q(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\) is totally real. At its
selected embedding \(t_r>4\) and \(t_r\to\infty\); at every other embedding

\[
                  t_r=2+2\cos(r\theta)\in(0,4).                       \tag{5.3}
\]

The endpoints cannot occur, since that would make a nonreal conjugate of
the Salem number a root of unity. Each \(t_r\) is an algebraic integer and
the field degrees are bounded by the fixed trace-field degree, but
\(h(t_r)\to\infty\).

This is a literal Vinberg packet. The integral Cartan matrix

\[
 C_r=\begin{pmatrix}2&-t_r\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}                          \tag{5.4}
\]

is symmetrized by

\[
 B_r=\begin{pmatrix}2&-t_r\\-t_r&2t_r\end{pmatrix}.                    \tag{5.5}
\]

Its determinant is \(t_r(4-t_r)\): it is Lorentzian at the selected place
and positive definite at every other place. The unique nontrivial simple
cycle is the algebraic integer \(t_r\). These give compact arithmetic
Coxeter intervals in \(\mathbf H^1\).

The example is deliberately below the required \(n\ge2\) scope. Its exact
role is to disprove any height bound using only cycle integrality, fixed
field degree, and definite conjugates. A finite-volume
higher-dimensional Gram must contribute additional determinant/face
couplings, as the Lambert equation does.

For a concrete choice, Lehmer's Salem polynomial

\[
 z^{10}+z^9-z^7-z^6-z^5-z^4-z^3+z+1                                 \tag{5.6}
\]

has trace polynomial

\[
 x^5+x^4-5x^3-5x^2+4x+3,                                               \tag{5.7}
\]

with one root above two and four roots in \((-2,2)\). Formula (5.2) follows
from the integral recurrence
\(S_0=2,\ S_1=x,\ S_{r+1}=xS_r-S_{r-1}\).

### 5.3 Arithmetic finite-area polygons with escaping complexity

There is also a genuine \(n=2\), finite-volume control. Use

\[
 q(X)=-X_0^2+X_1^2+X_2^2                                               \tag{5.8}
\]

and let \(P_N\) be the ideal polygon with cyclic vertices

\[
                         \infty,0,1,\ldots,N.                          \tag{5.9}
\]

It is the union of the \(N\) ideal triangles
\((\infty,j-1,j)\). Its side normals can be chosen as

\[
\begin{aligned}
 v_0&=(0,1,0),\\
 r_j&=(-(j^2-j+1),\,1-2j,\,-j^2+j+1),\quad1\le j\le N,\\
 w_N&=(-N,-1,-N).
\end{aligned}                                                          \tag{5.10}
\]

They have norm one, and

\[
                       q(r_i,r_j)=1-2(i-j)^2.                          \tag{5.11}
\]

All reflections preserve the integral Lorentz lattice. Consecutive sides
are parallel, so every angle is zero and

\[
                     \operatorname {area}(P_N)=N\pi.                  \tag{5.12}
\]

The \(N=1\) group is the arithmetic ideal-triangle reflection group. The
displayed union proves

\[
                     [W(P_1):W(P_N)]=N.                               \tag{5.13}
\]

Thus every \(P_N\) is arithmetic and finite area, but nonmaximal for
\(N>1\). The first and last semicircular sides have cycle

\[
             T_N=4\bigl(2(N-1)^2-1\bigr)^2,                            \tag{5.14}
\]

so Gram height is unbounded over the fixed field \(\mathbf Q\). The facet
count is \(f=N+2\).

These also give exact log stable-pair carriers. With \(Q_N\) the carrier
conic and \(H_i\) the polar lines, put

\[
                 \Delta_N=Q_N+{1\over2}\sum_{i=1}^{N+2}H_i.            \tag{5.15}
\]

At an ideal vertex the conic and two adjacent lines have three distinct
tangent directions and total coefficient two, so the pair is log
canonical. There are no triple line concurrences; for example

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \det(r_i,r_j,r_k)&=4(i-j)(i-k)(j-k),\\
 \det(v_0,r_i,r_j)&=2(i-j)(i+j-1),\\
 \det(w_N,r_i,r_j)&=-2(i-j)(i+j-2N-1),\\
 \det(v_0,w_N,r_i)&=2N.
\end{aligned}                                                          \tag{5.16}
\]

For \(1\le i<j\le N\), every displayed factor is nonzero.

Moreover

\[
 K_{\mathbf P^2}+\Delta_N={N\over2}H,\qquad
 (K+\Delta_N)^2={N^2\over4}.                                          \tag{5.17}
\]

The pairs are stable, but their volume and boundary-component count escape,
so they do not lie in one fixed KSBA stack.

The bad-prime escape is explicit and is not an artifact of the chosen
coordinates. The ordered cusp points
\(\infty,0,1,N\) on the carrier conic have cross-ratio

\[
                       [\infty,0;1,N]=N.                              \tag{5.18}
\]

For \(N=\ell\) prime this invariant has positive \(\ell\)-adic valuation,
so the four sections cannot remain distinct in any good marked model.
In the displayed integral coordinates the same degeneration appears as
the two vertical root lines \(v_0,w_\ell\) becoming projectively equal
modulo \(\ell\). Hence no finite bad-prime set works for the sequence.

Therefore:

~~~text
Vinberg integrality + conjugate definiteness + finite hyperbolic volume
  -/-> bounded Gram height, facets, stable volume, or bad-prime set.
~~~

Absolute reflection maximality is exactly the hypothesis missing from this
sequence. The point is not that maximality is irrelevant, but that a new
theorem must convert it into the missing bounds.

## 6. Audit of the proposed arithmetic-geometry theorems

### 6.1 Northcott and moduli heights

Northcott finiteness needs both a height bound and a degree bound. Sections
5.1 and 5.2 separately violate those inputs while satisfying the native
integrality and conjugate-interval conditions.

An ample CM, Hodge, or KSBA moduli line defines a height; positivity does
not give an upper bound for it. The rational points \(x=N\) in the exact
Lambert stable-pair family are absolutely reflection-maximal for all but
finitely many \(N\), yet have unbounded Gram and moduli height. They fail
arithmeticity, but prove that maximality and moduli positivity do not supply
the height bound. The arithmetic cut must do additional Diophantine work.

### 6.2 Shafarevich and good reduction

Shafarevich theorems fix a number field, a finite set \(S\) of places, and
the relevant dimension/deformation/polarization data before concluding
finiteness. Vinberg integrality fixes none globally. The root-of-unity
packet varies field and ramified prime; the Farey polygons keep
\(\mathbf Q\) but vary Hilbert data and acquire a new bad prime.

Properness of stable-pair moduli is not good reduction. It supplies stable
extension after specialization, permits boundary degeneration, and does not
say that the original marked arrangement has good reduction outside one
fixed \(S\).

### 6.3 Lawrence--Venkatesh

Lawrence--Venkatesh work with a fixed variety/family over a fixed number
field and fixed finite bad set. Their general hypersurface application gives
containment in a proper Zariski-closed subset, not an unconditional finite
count in arbitrary dimension and degree. Lawrence--Sawin obtain finiteness
only after fixing an ambient abelian variety, a polarization class, the
field, and \(S\).

The universal Coxeter image is a disjoint union over varying \(n,f,k\),
Hilbert type, and bad set. The exact carrier also supplies no uniform
high-monodromy family satisfying their period-map hypotheses. Applying
these theorems would first require the global boundedness package to be
proved.

### 6.4 Unit equations and Vojta-type results

The Lambert equation is the torus equation \(uv=4\). Over a fixed ring of
\(S\)-integers it has infinitely many solutions obtained by multiplying
\(u\) by a unit and \(v\) by its inverse. Standard \(S\)-unit finiteness
concerns additive nondegenerate equations such as \(u+v=1\), or integral
points on a fixed sufficiently hyperbolic complement. The archimedean
condition (4.4), not the bare torus equation, makes Lambert finite.

General Gram determinantal varieties can contain positive-dimensional tori
and rational subvarieties. Unconditional Vojta/Faltings results fix the
ambient variety, field, divisor, and integrality data. Broad Lang--Vojta
non-density for log-general-type varieties remains conjectural and, even if
granted, would give non-density rather than a global finite count over all
fields and arrangement types.

### 6.5 André--Oort and special points

The arrangement quotient is a rational invariant quotient, not a Shimura
variety supplied with a canonical Hodge datum. Vinberg arithmeticity is
integrality plus real-signature conditions, not complex multiplication.

Even a hypothetical special-point lift would not prove finiteness.
André--Oort describes Zariski closures of special points as unions of
special subvarieties. Positive-dimensional Shimura varieties have infinitely
many special points. The torsion sequence (5.1) is the simplest warning:
special/torsion points can have bounded height and unbounded degree. One
would still need a bounded zero-dimensional special locus.

### 6.6 Bounded denominators

Bounded denominators are weaker than the missing bounds. Both
\(\eta_\ell\) and \(t_r\) are algebraic integers, so their denominator is
already one. One escapes in degree and the other in height. The Farey Grams
are integral over \(\mathbf Z\) and still escape in height, facets, volume,
and bad reduction.

## 7. Mandatory controls

For the exact pair including the carrier quadric,

\[
 K_{\mathbf P^n}+Q+{1\over2}\sum_{i=1}^fH_i
          =\left({f\over2}-n+1\right)H.                               \tag{7.1}
\]

The examples occupy incompatible parts of log moduli:

| carrier | \(n\) | \(f\) | class in (7.1) | role |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Borcherds \(\mathbf H^{21}\) | 21 | 210 | \(85H\) | arithmetic and absolutely maximal; required high-rank control |
| Vinberg \(X3\) | 19 | 36 | \(0\) | arithmetic mixed-root K3 control |
| Lambert | 2 | 4 | \(H\) | fixed stable-pair curve; absolute maximality is cofinite |
| Farey \(P_N\) | 2 | \(N+2\) | \((N/2)H\) | arithmetic finite-area, nonmaximal index-\(N\) escape |

Thus one positivity slogan cannot cover the exact image.
\(\mathbf H^{21}\) forces very large general-type log volume, \(X3\) is
numerically log Calabi--Yau after including the quadric, and Farey polygons
escape through boundary complexity. Lambert shows that a particular rank
equation can nevertheless supply a successful arithmetic contraction.

## 8. The exact missing theorem

A global proof would follow from a theorem that absolute arithmetic
reflection maximality forces all of the following:

1. a finite collection of finite-type exact-Gram strata, or equivalent
   uniform bounds on \(n,f\) and Hilbert/stack complexity;
2. a fixed finite packet of integral cyclic invariants over which the exact
   Gram map is quasi-finite;
3. either a primitive invariant with a strict contracting conjugate region
   as in Lemma 3.1, or independent uniform bounds on field degree and
   moduli height; and
4. for a Shafarevich route, a fixed field and finite bad-prime set, or a
   theorem genuinely not requiring them.

Once these hold, Lemma 3.1 plus Northcott, or a fixed-data Shafarevich
theorem, makes the exact Gram image finite. What is missing is the
implication from absolute reflection maximality to items 1--4.

The controls show why none can be omitted:

* Lambert disproves “maximality implies zero-dimensional stable-pair image”;
* roots of unity disprove “integral plus bounded height implies bounded
  degree”;
* Salem powers disprove “integral plus bounded degree and definite
  conjugates implies bounded height”;
* Farey polygons disprove “arithmetic plus finite volume implies bounded
  presentation, height, volume, or bad set”; and
* \(\mathbf H^{21}\) and \(X3\) prevent replacement by one small fixed
  log-Fano or fixed-volume class.

Accordingly, the universal arrangement carrier plus current
arithmetic-geometry technology does not prove global finiteness. It does
give a rigorous positive local model: the Lambert determinant relation
converts Vinberg integrality into a strict capacity inequality and
finite-izes all fields without importing an arithmetic-reflection
classification.
