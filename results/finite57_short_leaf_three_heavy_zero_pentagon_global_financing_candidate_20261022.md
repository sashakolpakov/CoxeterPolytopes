## 2. Three consecutive heavy corners and intrinsic normalization

Fix the actual full white forest W in(1). At every heavy corner each of
the two affected white components is restored separately. A retained D_s
is extended at its unique long leaf by simple3; a retained A_r is restored
to D_(r+1) by simple3 at its next-to-end node, at either orientation.
No one-node joining or different restoration produces the required whole
2D17+D23 ranks. Call these two restoration classes A and B.

At a finite corner two restorations of the SAME class are impossible:
on D they create a second fork; on A they create degree4 or two forks.
Distinct A/B restorations have original product0, since a bond joining
their already large D factors cannot be finite. Thus any adjacent A/B
restorations form a heavy corner. If all five sides were restorations,
finite corners would force an alternating two-colouring of a5-cycle,
which is impossible. Three heavy corner incidences must therefore form
a single path of three edges in that cycle, using four restoring sides.
Write the cyclic side order

    A1, B1, A2, B2, C.                                (2)

The first three corners are heavy; C is a nonrestoring side. Choose class A
to be short when only one class is short. For later single-path cases we
may reverse(2) and interchange A/B so the affected short path is class A.

Project off ALL actual whites into their3-dimensional Lorentz orthogonal
complement, then normalize each projected side to square1. A finite edge
ensures each projected square is positive. Any two nonadjacent sides have
product strictly below-1. Indeed a positive or affine principal set with
those sides and W would have an actual common finite or ideal point by
the positive-inverse/null argument against EVERY original wall. The closed
nonadjacent sides of this finite pentagon cannot share such a point.

For a retained short path A_r, r=16 or22, the restoring side's raw projected
square is delta_A=4/(r+1). Same-orientation restorations have normalized
shared-white product(r-1)/2. Opposite orientations have shared-white product1.
Consequently, writing x=-A1.A2 in the NORMALIZED projected space, native
original norm-two products give

    x >= (r+5)/4 >=21/4.                              (3)

For opposite orientations the original product cannot be0, since that
would make x1; its first nonzero native magnitude is1. Same orientation
already gives x>=(r-1)/2, which is larger than(3). For a long restoration
the projected square and shared-white product are both1, so its same-class
magnitude is at least2. For a short restoration(3) is stronger. Therefore

    y=-B1.B2 >=2.                                    (4)

These are native gaps on ORIGINAL products after explicit normalization,
not an assertion that the intrinsic side normals are Coxeter normals.

## 3. Complete possible nonrestoring C

Every adjacent original black pair has product0: a nonzero bond makes
their marked nodes adjacent in the same finite component, where the c300
same-component entry is1 in every rank and no held increment deletes it.
This contradicts the assumed zero corner payment. Thus all the consecutive
original products in(2) vanish, including C.A1 and C.B2.

Classify the actual positive edge diagram W+C by finite Coxeter type.
It has rank56, and every white component has rank at least16. An exceptional
finite type is too small. A contact to a retained D can only extend its
long arm by simple3, or join that arm to a path endpoint by simple3.
A contact to one retained A path is endpoint3, endpoint4, or next-to-end3.
Joining two retained paths is finite only at endpoints by simple3, making
a longer A path. Two contacts to one white component make a cycle; a
double internal bond or two large forks is not finite. These observations
exhaust finite A/B/D, with no contact cutoff or omitted exceptional rank.

Now also impose positive definiteness at BOTH adjacent corners C+A1 and
C+B2, keeping all of W. A C restoration in class A/B is impossible as
already noted. A D-to-path joining makes a second large fork when the
relevant short restoration is added; if it touches the long-restoration
class it likewise gives two forks. Joining two D pieces is already not
finite at W+C. A single endpoint4 cannot coexist with the appropriate
short restoration: the resulting large branched component has a4 bond.
An endpoint3 at the wrong end relative to that restoration has branch
arms1,2,r-2 and is not finite at these ranks. It follows that the ONLY
remaining C types are:

1. C has no white contact, or restores the unused third D component;
2. C is simple3 at the far endpoint of ONE short path, opposite the
   adjacent short restoration; or
3. BOTH classes are short and C joins their two far endpoints by simple3.

This list uses the actual finite diagrams and original zero black products.
In particular it does not discard a possible far-end attachment merely
because it is not a restoration. We next exclude the first two types by
the full pentagon Gram, rather than assuming a cheap receiving corner.
