# The biquadratic field Q(√2,√5): complete Lannér catalogue and a compact bound

**Research proof candidate, awaiting separate independent review.** A compact arithmetic hyperbolic Coxeter polytope whose exact ground field is

\[
F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt5)
\]

has dimension at most **20**. The proof uses only distance-at-most-two angle weights. Its local quadrilateral obstruction applies with arbitrarily many white nodes and does not require a bounded attachment search or a whole-star rank equation.

The [standalone checker](../scripts/fresh_sqrt2sqrt5_lanner_catalogue_20261016.py) and [exact certificate](fresh_sqrt2sqrt5_lanner_catalogue_20261016.json) give the complete finite-angle alphabet, all 84 triangle tests, five exact projection costs, 35,904 binary support tests, and the counting identities. No priority or current-best claim is made.

## Published inputs and the ground-field equality

We use the compact acute-face correspondence, simplicity of compact Coxeter chambers, absence of affine principal diagrams, classification of finite Coxeter diagrams, and the symmetric nonnegative h-vector of a simple polytope. The relevant source is [Vinberg, §§1–3 and Proposition 17](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=mmo&option_lang=rus&paperid=444&what=fullt).

[Nikulin, §2 and §3.1, Figure 1 and formula (4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0708.3991) states the cyclic-product arithmeticity criterion, equality of ground fields for hyperbolic principal subsets, and the complete arithmetic Lannér classification of order at least four. Their fields are Q, Q(√2), and Q(√5). Thus no such diagram occurs in the exact-F chamber. Section 3.2 lists arithmetic triangles, including the two found independently below.

Here is the equality argument needed for using that classification. Give normals square two, and let K be the ambient cyclic ground field. The determinant of a nonsingular principal Gram matrix lies in K: each permutation term is a product of cyclic products and rational diagonal entries. For a Lannér principal matrix its determinant is negative physically. At every nonidentity embedding of K, arithmeticity makes the conjugate principal matrix positive semidefinite; its determinant cannot vanish under an embedding, so it is positive definite. If the determinant generated a proper subfield of K, a nonidentity embedding of K fixing that determinant would preserve its negative value, a contradiction. Consequently its determinant, and hence its cyclic field, generates K.

This argument does **not** put every normalized Gram entry in K. The entry field may be larger. Its total reality allows every ground-field embedding to be extended; the principal determinant and squared two-node minors are already in K and have the same values for each such extension. This distinction is also respected by the exact triangle tests.

## 1. The complete finite-angle alphabet

Write

\[
t_m=2\cos(\pi/m),\qquad d_m=t_m^2=2+2\cos(2\pi/m).
\]

The two-node cyclic product gives d_m∈F. For m≥3 its generated real cyclotomic field has degree φ(m)/2, which must divide four. The harmless case m=2 is included separately. Thus φ(m) divides eight. Every prime p dividing m has p−1 dividing eight; hence p∈{2,3,5}, with exponents at most 4,1,1. This yields the complete list

\[
2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12,15,16,20,24,30.
\]

The fields for 12 and 24 contain Q(√3), which is not a subfield of F. The real fields for 15,16,20,30 are cyclic quartic; F is biquadratic, so none can embed in F. The checker constructs their unit groups modulo complex conjugation and verifies an element of order four. The resulting complete alphabet is

\[
\boxed{\{2,3,4,5,6,8,10\}}.
\]

Its squared products are respectively 0,1,2,φ²,3,2+√2,2+φ, where φ=(1+√5)/2. All are integral, and every conjugate is in [0,4). The ring of integers has basis 1,√2,φ,√2φ, since the quadratic discriminants 8 and 5 are coprime. Integrality of the tested finite cyclic products also follows directly from their being products of algebraic-integer values 2cos(π/m); the ring basis is not needed to reject any triangle.

This is an all-label reduction from field degree and Galois group, not a chosen finite label cutoff.

## 2. Complete arithmetic Lannér catalogue

For a finite-label triangle with labels l,m,n, let

\[
P=t_l t_m t_n=\sqrt{d_l d_m d_n}\ge0.
\]

Its cyclic field is generated by d_l,d_m,d_n,P. Its square-two Gram determinant is

\[
\Delta=2(4-d_l-d_m-d_n-P). \tag{1}
\]

The checker enumerates all 84 unordered triples and requires the following:

1. 1/l+1/m+1/n<1, so this is a compact hyperbolic triangle.
2. P belongs to F. An exact successive-quadratic square-root test decides this completely, rather than testing a numerical approximation.
3. The common stabilizer of d_l,d_m,d_n,P in Gal(F/Q) is trivial; hence the cyclic field is exactly F.
4. Δ is positive in each of the other three embeddings of F. All proper squared minors are already positive by Section 1.

Every cyclic product is integral, and the entry field generated by finite cosines is totally real. Thus these conditions are the complete arithmeticity test here, not only necessary filters. In particular, conjugate signs of individual cosine entries outside F are harmless: the determinant depends on P, and every proper two-node determinant depends only on d_m.

The results are:

| Length-three cyclic product outside F | 37 |

The complete order-three catalogue is

\[
\boxed{(2,5,8),\qquad(4,5,5)}. \tag{2}
\]

For (2,5,8), P=0 and Δ=1−2√2−√5. For (4,5,5), P=√2φ² and Δ=−2−3√2−2√5−√10. Both determinant sign vectors, in the order (+,+),(−,+),(+,−),(−,−) on the square roots, are (−,+,+,+).

Order two remains an infinite parameter family: if τ is the squared square-two product, its precise arithmetic conditions are

\[
\tau\in\mathcal O_F,\quad \mathbb Q(\tau)=F,\quad
\tau>4,\quad 0<\sigma(\tau)<4\quad(\sigma\ne\mathrm{id}). \tag{3}
\]

The square-one product is −√τ/2. The inequalities make the entry field totally real and the conjugate two-node Grams positive definite. We do not replace this family by sampled dotted distances. Orders at least four are excluded by the published classification and ground-field equality above. This completes the catalogue in the form needed here.

## 3. A quadrilateral must have a short marked corner

Let W be the white facet support of a compact quadrilateral two-face and let a,b,c,d be its four side nodes in cyclic order. Each W-plus-adjacent-pair Gram is positive definite. Each opposite extension W+{a,c} and W+{b,d} is indefinite: a positive-definite extension would give an actual intersection of the opposite sides by the acute-face correspondence; a singular positive-semidefinite extension would contain an affine principal diagram, excluded by compactness.

Choose minimal indefinite diagrams L⊆W+{a,c} and M⊆W+{b,d}. Each contains both its black nodes, since W plus either single black node is positive definite. Section 2 shows that each has order two or three.

Suppose that at every corner the two marked black nodes have graph distance greater than two, or lie in different components. In particular, all adjacent black contacts are right. Any contact from a black node of L to a white node of M gives a path of length two to some black node of M: the connected triangle M has a nonright contact from that white node to at least one of its black nodes. That pair of black nodes is adjacent in the quadrilateral. Thus such contacts, and the reversed contacts, are forbidden.

If either L or M has order two, these restrictions make the two indefinite diagrams orthogonal, giving two negative directions. If both use the same white node, its nonright contacts to the two opposite pairs give a forbidden length-two corner path. Consequently L and M are triangles on distinct whites u,v. All cross black-to-black and black-to-white contacts vanish. Their only possible connection is u–v, which must be nonright, again to avoid two orthogonal negative blocks.

The checker separately enumerates every binary support for two minimal pieces of orders two or three, including a shared white node and every extra cross contact. It imposes only connectedness, corner forests, and the absence of short marked paths. All 35,904 cases reduce to **16** supports: four connected triangle supports on each side, joined only by u–v. Unlike the prior high-label argument, no distinguished high edge is assumed; the triangle (4,5,5) has none.

We now eliminate the two opposite finite black pairs. Normalize to square one. If one opposite pair has bond m, with other triangle contacts p,q, its white projection cost is

\[
C(m;p,q)=\frac{d_p+d_q+P}{4-d_m}. \tag{4}
\]

There are five distinct positions in (2):

| Triangle | Opposite bond | White contacts | Projection cost |
|---|---:|---|---|
| (2,5,8) | 2 | 5,8 | (7+2√2+√5)/8 |
| (2,5,8) | 5 | 2,8 | 1+√2/2+√5/5+√10/10 |
| (2,5,8) | 8 | 2,5 | 3/2+3√2/4+√5/2+√10/4 |
| (4,5,5) | 4 | 5,5 | 3/2+3√2/4+√5/2+√10/4 |
| (4,5,5) | 5 | 4,5 | 2+√2+3√5/5+2√10/5 |

All but the first have cost strictly greater than two, as checked exactly in F. The first position is impossible independently of its cost. Its white node u is incident to a bond of label eight and to the required nonright bridge u–v. These three nodes lie in a finite corner. Their determinant is at most

\[
1-\cos^2(\pi/8)-1/4<0,
\]

even before any extra bond is included. This contradicts positive definiteness. The elementary fact being used is that a finite bond of label at least six is an entire rank-two component of any spherical diagram containing it.

The four black nodes therefore form a positive-definite direct sum of two rank-two blocks, and each white projection cost is greater than two. Their residual diagonal entries are both less than −1. The residual off-diagonal remains the original u–v product, because each white has no contact to the other opposite pair. Its square is less than one, since the white Gram is positive definite. Thus the residual two-by-two matrix is negative definite, contradicting the Lorentz negative index one.

We have proved that every quadrilateral has a marked corner of graph distance at most two. Additional white nodes cannot repair a principal submatrix with two negative directions. This proof does not require the exact full-star corank, a sealing assertion, a bound on white rank, or a classification of external attachments.

## 4. Distance-two weights exclude all dimensions at least 21

At each planar angle, mark the two nodes defining that two-face in the complete finite vertex diagram. Give the angle weight one if those nodes have graph distance at most two, and zero otherwise. These are component-local nonnegative weights.

A triangular two-face has three black nodes. Any minimal indefinite principal diagram of its star must contain all three, since W plus each black pair is positive definite. The full star is indefinite: after eliminating W, the three bounding normals of its compact hyperbolic triangle span a Lorentz three-space. Hence the minimal diagram is exactly the black triangle and is one of (2). The two nonright bonds of (2,5,8), or the three of (4,5,5), give at least two units across its corners. Section 3 gives at least one on every quadrilateral. A k-gon with k≥5 has nonnegative weight, hence at least 5−k.

In a finite connected Coxeter diagram of rank r, the number of pairs at distance at most two is

\[
(r-1)+\sum_v\binom{\deg(v)}2.
\]

It equals 2r−3 on a nontrivial path, 2r−2 on a D/E tree, and zero on A₁. These exhaust finite connected diagrams; F and H are paths, and I₂ is a single edge. Thus every rank-n vertex forest has total weight at most 2n−2. The checker verifies representative graphs through rank 40, but the asserted all-rank bound follows from the displayed degree formula, not extrapolation.

For a simple n-polytope, summing the face lower bounds gives

\[
\text{total weight}\ \ge\ 5f_2-\binom n2 f_0.
\]

The symmetric nonnegative h-vector gives the strict average lower bounds

\[
\frac{n(n-6)}8\quad(n\text{ even}),\qquad
\frac{(n-1)(n-5)}8\quad(n\text{ odd}).
\]

To see strictness explicitly, pair h_i with h_{n-i} in the identity
\(f_2=\sum_i\binom{n-i}{2}h_i\), while \(f_0=\sum_i h_i\). The minimum paired coefficient occurs centrally, and the extreme coefficients are strictly larger; h₀=hₙ=1. At n=21 the average weight is therefore strictly greater than 40, but every vertex has weight at most 40. For n=21+2u and n=22+2u, lower-minus-upper equals respectively

\[
u^2/2+5u,\qquad u^2/2+11u/2+2.
\]

These coefficient identities are checked exactly and are nonnegative for every integer u≥0. This excludes every n≥21.

## Scope and reproducibility

The theorem is for **compact arithmetic Coxeter chambers with exact ground field F**, not merely a form whose entries can be written over F. In particular, Q(√5) chambers are not covered. It makes no claim that every listed local diagram embeds in a higher-dimensional chamber, nor does it infer finite volume from local compatibility.
