## 2. The rigid plane design

Normalize three proper points and a fourth point by

\[
 A=(1:0:0),\quad D=(0:1:0),\quad G=(0:0:1),
 \quad B=(1:1:1).
\]

The root incidences successively force

\[
 C=(1:1:c),\qquad E=(1/c:1:1),\qquad F=(c:1:c).
\]

The final collinearity determinant is

\[
 \det(E,F,G)=\frac{1-c^2}{c}.
\]

The solution `c=1` collides `C` with `B`; hence, in characteristic zero, the
only nondegenerate solution is `c=-1`.  The seven proper rational points are

```text
A=( 1:0: 0)   D=(0:1: 0)   G=(0:0:1)
B=( 1:1: 1)   C=(1:1:-1)
E=(-1:1: 1)   F=(-1:1:-1).
```

There are three first-near points:

```text

d over D in direction AD (z=0),
g over G in direction DG (x=0).
```

The nine degree-one roots are precisely the projectivized `B3` reflection
arrangement

\[
 x=0, y=0, z=0, x\pm y=0, y\pm z=0, x\pm z=0.    \tag{3}
\]

At `A,D,G` four arrangement lines concur; at `B,C,E,F` three concur.  Of
the 36 pairs of lines, 30 meet at one of the seven blown-up proper points and
the remaining six meet transversely away from the cluster.

## 3. The Hilbert gate forces Choi--Lam

Cubics through the seven proper and three first-near centers give ten exact
linear conditions on ten coefficients.  Their matrix has rank ten, so

\[
                         h^0(-K_S)=0.                 \tag{4}
\]

Sextics double at the complete `7+3` cluster give thirty exact conditions on
28 coefficients.  The matrix has rank 27 and one-dimensional kernel.  Its
primitive generator is equation (1).  Therefore

\[
                         |-2K_S|=\{B\}.               \tag{5}
\]

There is an independent and rather striking lineage here.  Choi and Lam
introduced (1) in their study of extremal positive-semidefinite forms.  Its
nonnegativity follows directly from AM--GM applied to
`x^4y^2,y^4z^2,z^4x^2`; it is a classical non-sum-of-squares example.  Its
seven real projective zeros are exactly the seven proper points above: the
three coordinate points and the four sign points with
`x^2=y^2=z^2`.  Thus the Coxeter incidence compiler has independently landed
on a famous object from real algebraic geometry.

The singular-scheme length agrees with
