# Three finite-volume Coxeter polytopes from Alexeev divisor chambers

## Outcome

The complete mixed-root chambers of Alexeev's reflective lattices
`(20,2,1)` and `(15,9,1)` have exactly three proper subgroup-fixed sections
above `H9`.  All three are genuine finite-volume, noncompact hyperbolic
Coxeter polytopes.

| ledger ID | ambient chamber | diagram subgroup class | section | facets | finite vertices | ideal vertices | hyperideal rays |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `A20-C2a-H12-19` | `(20,2,1)`, 50 walls | involution class of size 10 in `S5` | `H12` | 19 | 527 | 25 | 0 |
| `A20-C2b-H11-20` | `(20,2,1)`, 50 walls | involution class of size 15 in `S5` | `H11` | 20 | 311 | 29 | 0 |
| `A15-C2-H10-34` | `(15,9,1)`, 98 walls | involution class of size 21 in `PGL(2,7)` | `H10` | 34 | 1,774 | 146 | 0 |

This is an exact positive result, not a candidate list.  Complete rational
double description and a separate literal Vinberg edge/parabolic audit both
prove the volume classification.

## The divisor-lattice construction

Alexeev's ancillary matrix file gives the complete reflection chambers.  The
first 20 walls of `(20,2,1)` and the first 15 walls of `(15,9,1)` are integral
lattice bases, so their full wall Gram matrices reconstruct the ambient
Lorentz lattices without a floating-point realization.  Their determinants
are respectively `-4` and `512`.

Exact coloured-graph automorphism and entrywise lattice lifting recover the
full weighted-diagram groups:

- `(20,2,1)`: `S5`, order 120, with 19 subgroup conjugacy classes;
- `(15,9,1)`: `PGL(2,7)`, order 336, with 23 subgroup conjugacy classes.

Every wall permutation used here lifts to an integral unimodular Lorentz
action.  Exhaustive GAP subgroup enumeration leaves only two proper classes
of fixed rank at least 11 in the first stock and one in the second.  Thus the
three rows above exhaust this precisely stated high-dimensional denominator;
they were not selected from an unreported collection of failures.

For a subgroup `H`, the construction takes

`P_H = P intersect Fix_H(L_R)`,

where `P` is the complete ambient chamber and `Fix_H(L_R)` is the subgroup-fixed
Lorentz subspace.  Ambient walls in an `H`-orbit restrict to the same
half-space.  The fixed lattice is always the saturated divisor lattice
`L_H = L intersect Fix_Q(H)`, not merely the lattice generated by restricted
mirrors.

The three saturated fixed lattices are:

| ID | inertia | determinant | absolute Smith diagonal | integral facet reflections? |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| `A20-C2a-H12-19` | `(1,12,0)` | `256` | `1^6,2^6,4` | all 19 |
| `A20-C2b-H11-20` | `(1,11,0)` | `-1024` | `1^2,2^10` | all 20 |
| `A15-C2-H10-34` | `(1,10,0)` | `2048` | `1^2,2^7,4^2` | all 34 |

There is therefore no reflection-lattice enlargement hidden in these three
examples: every final mirror reflection preserves the saturated fixed
lattice itself.

## Literal Vinberg finite-volume proof

For a complete acute-angled Coxeter chamber in `H^n`, the audit used here
enumerates every elliptic rank-`n-1` edge diagram and verifies that it has
exactly two endpoints, each finite or ideal.  It separately enumerates every
connected affine diagram and requires its full orthogonal zero-neighbourhood
to be precisely a certified ideal endpoint of total parabolic rank `n-1`.
This is the literal endpoint/parabolic form of Vinberg's finite-volume test;
the presence of ideal endpoints makes all three chambers noncompact.

| ID | elliptic edge diagrams | finite--finite | finite--ideal | ideal--ideal | connected affine components | bad edges / cusp neighbourhoods |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `A20-C2a-H12-19` | 3,846 | 2,589 | 1,146 | 111 | 59 | `0 / 0` |
| `A20-C2b-H11-20` | 2,229 | 1,312 | 797 | 120 | 64 | `0 / 0` |
| `A15-C2-H10-34` | 12,792 | 6,046 | 5,648 | 1,098 | 426 | `0 / 0` |

An independent GMP `cddlib` calculation enumerates all extreme rays of each
complete homogeneous cone:

- `552 = 527` timelike `+ 25` null `+ 0` hyperideal;
- `340 = 311` timelike `+ 29` null `+ 0` hyperideal;
- `1,920 = 1,774` timelike `+ 146` null `+ 0` hyperideal.

Every cone is full-dimensional and pointed, every retained facet is incident
to an extreme ray, and every archived ray replays exactly against every
inequality.  Hence the geometric ray enumeration and the diagram-theoretic
Vinberg enumeration agree endpoint for endpoint.

## What is algebraic-geometric here, and what is not yet

The ambient `(20,2,1)` lattice is the Picard lattice of the unique K3 surface
in Alexeev's discussion; its 50-wall chamber was already computed by Vinberg
and Kaplinskaja.  Alexeev describes `(15,9,1)` as
`<-2> + (14,8,1)`, the Picard lattice of `Hilb^2(X)` for a K3 surface `X`,
and publishes its complete 98-wall chamber.

Thus the algebraic object supplies exactly the rigid data sought in the
divisor-lattice programme: an integral Lorentz lattice, a complete wall
stock, and a finite chamber symmetry group.  The new operation is an
involution-fixed divisor section of that complete chamber.

The two `(20,2,1)` diagram actions have now crossed the geometric gate.  An
exact common graph glue with the positive complement `<2>+<2>` realizes
`A20_2_1_C01` by a non-symplectic order-four automorphism and
`A20_2_1_C02` by a non-symplectic involution on the same singular K3 surface.
Their fixed Néron--Severi lattices remain exactly the archived rank-13 and
rank-12 lattices.  See the
[two-action K3 lift theorem](a20_c2a_c2b_k3_automorphism_lifts.md).

No corresponding geometric lift is asserted here for the `(15,9,1)`
hyperkähler action.  Integral lattice/chamber symmetry, geometric
automorphism, and the nef cone of a quotient or fixed-locus variety remain
three different statements.  None of the three mixed source chambers is
therefore silently identified with a literal quotient nef cone.

There is now, however, a different geometric realization of its `H10/34`
*chamber*.  The unique reflection-stable even index-four closure of the fixed
wall lattice is explicitly `U+D4(-1)+A1(-1)^5`, the full Picard lattice of
Roulleau's rank-11 K3 stock.  All 34 oriented mirrors and reflections
transport and generate Picard.  A five-reflection Weyl framing followed by
addition of Roulleau's square-18 ample class gives a square-2178 ample divisor
strict on all 34 walls.  The same target is also the doubled dual of the
maximal stable closure of `SS2-V4-H10-34`, so the two source constructions
meet on one complex-K3 carrier.  This realizes the known polytope as a
full-Picard divisor-reflection subsystem; it does not make the original
hyperkähler involution geometric, identify the 34 walls with the complete
90-wall K3 nef cone, or turn the lattice reflections into automorphisms.  See
the [common K3-carrier theorem](h10_34_ud4a1x5_k3_carrier.md).

## Lineage and novelty boundary

The dimensions `H10`, `H11`, and `H12` were all populated long before this
calculation.  Exact local Hasse-invariant comparison places all three fixed
quadratic spaces in the classical odd-unimodular rational similarity class.
Consequently none is claimed to define a new arithmetic commensurability
class.

The `H11` fixed lattice is even 2-elementary with
`(r,a,delta)=(12,10,1)`.  By the uniqueness classification it is exactly

`U + E8(-2) + <-2>^2`.

Alexeev's known full reflection chamber on this lattice has 14 walls.  Since
the 20 displayed reflections are integral and their chamber has finite
volume, the 20-wall group has finite index in that known full reflection
group; the numerical index is open.  This is therefore a new extraction and
possibly a previously unrecorded finite-index Coxeter subgroup chamber, not
a new lattice or maximal reflection group.

The maximal integral reflection-chamber lineage of the 19-wall `H12` and
34-wall `H10` rows remains open.  A bounded exact audit nevertheless proves
that none of the three literal chamber sizes occurs in the pinned CoxIter
snapshot: there are zero `(H12,19)`, `(H11,20)`, or `(H10,34)` headers among
1,214 ordinary records and zero among 2,959 index-two doubling records.
This is useful negative lineage evidence, but it is not a worldwide
publication-priority theorem.
