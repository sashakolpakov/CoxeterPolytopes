## 1. The adapted-cover theorem survives

Let (S) be a smooth projective rational surface over an algebraically
closed field, let the nonempty boundary be a disjoint union of smooth
rational curves (B_i) with (B_i^2=-2m_i), (m_i\geq2), and put

\[
 \Delta=\sum_i(1-1/m_i)B_i,\qquad
 \ell=\operatorname {lcm}(m_i).
\]

Assume (K_S+\Delta\equiv0) and
\(\operatorname {char}k\nmid\ell\).  On a rational surface numerical and
\(\mathbf Q\)-linear triviality agree.  Multiplication by \(\ell\) gives

\[
 D=\sum_i(\ell/m_i)B_i\sim\ell(K_S+\sum_iB_i).
\]

The normalization of the resulting degree-(\ell) Kummer cover is connected:
the valuation vector \((\ell/m_i)_i\) has exact order \(\ell\) modulo
\(\ell\), since for every prime divisor of \(\ell\) some (m_i) contains
its maximal prime power.  Tameness and disjointness make the normalization
smooth.  Strictly locally over (B_i) it is a disjoint union of
\(\ell/m_i\) charts (z^{m_i}=x).

One gap found during the audit was repaired before this PASS: the
ramification formula plus (K_S+\Delta\sim_{\mathbf Q}0) alone proves only
that (K_X) is torsion.  The required integral trivialization comes from
the chosen cover.  If (M=\mathcal O_S(K_S+\sum B_i)) and (Z) is the
reduced ramification divisor, the tautological root has divisor (Z), so

\[
 \mathcal O_X(Z)=\pi^*M,
 \qquad R_\pi=\pi^*(\sum B_i)-Z,
 \qquad \mathcal O_X(K_X)=\pi^*M\otimes\mathcal O_X(-Z)=\mathcal O_X.
\]

The independent Euler check is

\[
 c_2(X)=\ell\left(c_2(S)-2b+2\sum_i\frac1{m_i}\right)
 =\ell\left(12+2\sum_i\frac{(m_i-1)(m_i-2)}{m_i}\right).
\]

Every summand in the last expression is nonnegative and vanishes exactly at
(m_i=2).  Since (X) is connected and (K_X\simeq\mathcal O_X), Serre
duality gives \(\chi(\mathcal O_X)=2-q(X)\leq2\), hence Noether gives
\(c_2(X)\leq24\).  But (c_2(X)\geq12\ell\) and \(\ell\geq2\).  Therefore

\[
 \ell=2,\qquad m_i=2\ \text{for every }i,\qquad q(X)=0.
\]

Thus the cover is K3.  No abelian or Enriques alternative remains under
these exact hypotheses: the former contradicts the Euler lower bound, and
the latter contradicts the integral canonical trivialization (as well as
the resulting Noether equality).  The claimed Picard ceilings (20) in
characteristic zero and (22) in tame positive characteristic follow by
pullback into the K3 N\'eron--Severi group.

## 2. The maximal-triangle counterexample survives

For the blow-up of \(\mathbb F_4\) at the positive-section torus fixed point,
the basis \((B,F,E)\) has Gram matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-4&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad K=(-2,-6,1),\qquad G=F-E.
\]

The remaining toric generators satisfy
(F=E+G) and (B+4F-E=B+3E+4G), so the full Mori cone is generated by
(B,E,G).  Their physical Gram matrix is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-4&0&1\\0&-1&1\\1&1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

After adjoining (h=B/2) and doubling the form, (h,E,G) are integral
norm-(-2) roots with Coxeter labels \((2,3,\infty)\).  The triangle has
area \(\pi/6\).  Any discrete reflection supergroup has finite index and is
again a nonuniform lattice.  A noncompact Coxeter polygon with at least four
sides has area at least \(\pi/2\); the least positive area for a noncompact
triangle is \(\pi/6\), uniquely at \((2,3,\infty)\).  Hence the group is
reflection-maximal.  Nevertheless (B.E=0), (B.G=1), and
(B\neq-2K_S).  Reflection maximality therefore does not imply the Coble
incidence equation.

## 3. Exact scope

The PASS does not cover wild characteristic \(\operatorname {char}k\mid\ell\),
intersecting or singular boundary components, incomplete Mori lists, or an
arbitrarily chosen commensurable lattice.  Those are excluded hypotheses,
not hidden conclusions.  In particular, the result is an obstruction and
rank theorem once a complete weighted Mori alphabet is geometrically
constructed; it is not yet the global raw-Coxeter-to-surface existence
theorem.
