# Metric Coxeter facets: a sharp parent-wall restriction

20 October 2026. Own paper derivation, without a scientific job, checker,
build, Lean module or literature-priority claim. No new uniform compact
or arithmetic dimension ceiling is asserted. The local lemma was proposed
by the coordinating agent for intrinsic labels3 and5; the complete finite
rank-three calculation gives the stronger statement below.

## 1. The field-free local statement

Let P be a compact hyperbolic Coxeter polytope and let F be one of its
actual facets. Suppose F, with its induced metric, is itself a Coxeter
polytope. This last hypothesis is essential: facets of Coxeter polytopes
need not be Coxeter polytopes. If two facets of F meet at angle pi/k,
where k is an integer other than2 or4, then BOTH corresponding parent
facets of P are perpendicular to F.

In particular this holds for every odd k>=3 and for k=6,8,10,... . An
oblique parent can correspond only to a node of the Coxeter diagram of F
whose incident finite labels belong to {2,4}. If it participates in an
intrinsic4-bond, its own angle with F must also be pi/4.

Here and below a dotted edge, which represents disjoint facets, is not
an incident finite label and does not force perpendicularity.

### Proof and full finite table

Let n,m1,m2 be outward unit normals of F and the two parent facets. The
actual codimension-three face has a finite relative-interior point, so
these three normals have positive definite Gram matrix. Write

    ci=-n.mi>=0, b=-m1.m2>=0.

The intrinsic outward unit normals in n-perp are

    mi_bar=(mi+ci n)/sqrt(1-ci^2),

and their intrinsic dihedral cosine is

    (b+c1 c2)/sqrt((1-c1^2)(1-c2^2)).                 (1)

Finite rank-three Coxeter classification consists of reducible diagrams
and A3,B3,H3. The reducible cases give either c1=c2=0 and the unchanged
angle, or an intrinsic right angle. For the irreducible cases the squared
cosine from (1), according to the marked node n, is exactly:

| Finite diagram | Position of n | Intrinsic cosine squared |
|---|---|---|
| A3, path3--3 | endpoint | 1/3 |
| A3 | middle | 1/9 |
| B3, path4--3 | endpoint adjacent to4 | 1/2 |
| B3 | endpoint adjacent to3 | 2/3 |
| B3 | middle | 1/3 |
| H3, path5--3 | endpoint adjacent to5 | (5+sqrt5)/10 |
| H3 | endpoint adjacent to3 | (3+sqrt5)/6 |
| H3 | middle | (5+2sqrt5)/15 |

Only the value1/2 is the squared cosine of pi/k for an integer k>=2.
Here is a complete exact check without a numerical angle comparison.
The field generated by cos^2(pi/k) equals the field generated by
cos(2pi/k), whose degree for k>=3 is phi(k)/2: its cyclotomic Galois
stabilizer consists precisely of the two exponents +1 and-1. Rational
values therefore occur only at k=3,4,6 and are1/4,1/2,3/4; k=2 gives0.
The rational entries in the table leave only1/2. For a quadratic entry
in Q(sqrt5), the only possibilities are k=5,10, with squared cosines
(3+sqrt5)/8 and (5+sqrt5)/8. The other degree-two cases k=8,12 have
fields Q(sqrt2),Q(sqrt3). None of the three H3 entries equals either
Q(sqrt5) possibility. This proves the stated restriction, including its
claim that the exceptional4 case marks the4-end of B3.

## 2. The4 exception is realized compactly and arithmetically

It cannot be removed by assuming arithmeticity. Put phi=(1+sqrt5)/2
and take the chain root Gram matrix

    [[ 4,-2, 0,   0],
     [-2, 2,-1,   0],
     [ 0,-1, 2,-phi],
     [ 0, 0,-phi, 2]].                               (2)

Its Coxeter diagram is [4,3,5]. The leading principal minors are
4,4,4,4-4phi, giving signature(3,1). Every proper principal subdiagram
is spherical: the connected rank-three cases are B3 and H3, and the
remaining cases are reducible finite diagrams. Equivalently their
minors are positive. The resulting hyperbolic simplex is compact; its
inverse Gram has negative entries, so its four vertex rays are timelike
and consistently future-oriented.

At the other embedding phi->1-phi, the same first three leading minors
are4 and the final determinant is positive, so the conjugate form is
positive definite. Every root reflection is integral over Z[phi]: for
the norm-four first root its only off-diagonal reflection coefficient
is-1, and for the norm-two roots all Gram entries are integral. The
cyclic squared entry phi^2 generates Q(sqrt5), verifying the usual
first-type arithmetic conditions.

The first-node facet has intrinsic root Gram

    [[1,-1,0],[-1,2,-phi],[0,-phi,2]],

and is the compact Coxeter triangle with angles pi/4,pi/5,pi/2.
At its intrinsic pi/4 corner one parent is oblique to F and the other
is perpendicular. This is an actual compact arithmetic sharpness
example, rather than a formal local Gram pattern.

## 3. A restriction on the known eight-dimensional facet

The literal relation matrix in
`../data/known_examples/bugaenko_compact_h8.json` has finite nonright
edges, using its zero-based node labels,

    (0,1):5; (1,2):3; (2,3):3; (3,4):3; (4,5):3;
    (5,6):3; (6,7):3; (7,8):5; (3,9):3; (5,10):3.

Every one of the eleven nodes is incident to a3- or5-edge. Consequently
any compact Coxeter polytope having this METRIC polytope as an actual
facet must be perpendicular to it along every adjacent facet. This is
independent of the ambient arithmetic form or lattice. It reduces a
possible extension to an orthogonal collar; it does not rule out such
a collar, bound its caps, or prove that every compact9-polytope contains
this particular metric facet.

## 4. Why universal Coxeter-facet descent cannot be assumed

There is also a global necessary condition. If EVERY metric facet of a
compact Coxeter polytope P is Coxeter, then dim(P)<=8.

Indeed every irreducible finite rank-three diagram in the table has at
least one marked-node projection which is not a Coxeter angle. Thus none
can occur at a vertex of such P. Every irreducible component of a finite
vertex diagram has rank at most2, namely A1 or I2(m).

This component condition is inherited by faces and makes every face
Coxeter. To see this directly, eliminate its white roots in the finite
vertex Gram matrix. Different A1/I2 components remain orthogonal. A
two-black component retains its original I2 angle; a one-black component
only has its norm changed and stays orthogonal to every other component.

It suffices therefore to exclude dimension9. At a vertex of a simple
9-polytope there are at most four I2 components. A corner of a two-face
is nonright exactly when its black pair is one such component. If B is
the total number of nonright two-face corners, then

    B<=4 f0.

A compact hyperbolic triangle has at least two nonright corners, since
two right angles already have sum pi. A compact quadrilateral has at
least one. Polygons with five or more sides give a nonnegative count.
Since the total number of two-face corners is36 f0,

    B>=5 f2-36 f0.                                    (3)

Write h0,...,h9 for the dual simplicial h-vector. The standard
Dehn--Sommerville symmetry and h_i>=0 give the exact identity

    5 f2-36 f0
      =4 f0+100 h0+60 h1+30 h2+10 h3
      >=4 f0+100,

where h0=1. This contradicts (3). The external face-count input is only
the usual nonnegativity of the h-vector of a convex simplicial polytope;
the coefficient identity is displayed in full. The argument for higher
dimensions applies to a9-face, whose vertex components still have rank
at most2.

Thus every compact Coxeter polytope of dimension at least9 necessarily
has a metric facet which is NOT Coxeter. This theorem rules out a
universal descent through Coxeter facets. It does not exclude those
polytopes themselves. A new uniform bound must control such non-Coxeter
facets, or use a different global mechanism; no such step is supplied here.
