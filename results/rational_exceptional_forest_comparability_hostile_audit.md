# Hostile audit of exceptional-forest comparability

## Verdict

**PASS.**  The theorem in
[the comparability note](rational_exceptional_forest_comparability_theorem.md)
is correct with its declared carrier hypotheses.  Incomparability is
exactly what permits two total-exceptional coefficients to be equalized
inside the relative ample cone.  Even boundary parity is exactly what makes
the resulting difference reflection integral on the adapted host.

No classification theorem or exceptional-configuration census is used.
The independent hostile tests reconstruct the proximity weights and the
reflection formula without importing the producer certificate.

## 1. The proximity argument survives satellite points

For total-transform exceptional classes, the strict exceptional component
at \(v\) is

\[
                  \overline E_v=e_v-\sum_{w\to v}e_w,   \tag{1.1}
\]

where the sum includes every later centre directly proximate to \(v\).
Thus a divisor

\[
                         D=-\sum_v\lambda_v e_v
\]

is positive on all contracted prime curves exactly when

\[
                       \lambda_v>\sum_{w\to v}\lambda_w. \tag{1.2}
\]

Writing the difference as a positive slack makes (1.2) triangular in the
reverse blowup order.

The possible hostile failure would be a later exceptional valuation common
to two incomparable branches, because then changing one slack could alter
both prescribed weights.  This cannot occur on a blowup forest.  Once two
exceptional branches separate, their prime components are disjoint, and
later blowups never make disjoint components meet.  A satellite point lies
on two components which already meet; those valuations are comparable.
Consequently the descendant sets of incomparable valuations are disjoint,
including all satellite proximity edges.

Set all other slacks positive and the two selected slacks temporarily to
zero.  If the resulting descendant contributions are \(d_i,d_j\), choose

\[
 T>\max(d_i,d_j),\qquad \tau_i=T-d_i,\quad\tau_j=T-d_j.
\]

This gives strictly positive slacks and
\(\lambda_i=\lambda_j=T\).  The argument is sharp: along a comparable
ancestry chain, (1.2) forces strict decrease, so equalization is
impossible.

Relative Kleiman then applies without another curve classification.  The
contracted cone is generated by the finitely many exceptional prime
components, so \(D\) is \(\pi\)-ample.  For \(H\) ample downstairs,
\(\pi^*H+tD\) is ample for sufficiently small \(t>0\), and its pairing with
\(e_i-e_j\) is zero.

## 2. The reflection and maximality step are exact

For \(r=e_i-e_j\),

\[
 r^2=-2,\qquad s_r(x)=x+(x.r)r,
\]

so \(s_r\) swaps the two total exceptional coordinates and preserves the
full Neron--Severi lattice.  On the doubled adapted host,

\[
 s_r(B_\alpha/2)-B_\alpha/2
                       ={B_\alpha.r\over2}r.            \tag{2.1}
\]

All pairings even therefore imply \(s_r\in O^+(M)\).  The ample mirror
shows \(s_r\notin W(P)\), while
\(\langle W(P),s_r\rangle\subset O^+(M)\) is discrete and is a proper
reflection overgroup.  Finite covolume makes it finite index, so the
arithmetic-reflection formulation fails as well.

The converse parity statement is also sound.  If one pairing is odd and
(2.1) nevertheless belongs to \(M\), then \(r/2\in M\).  Integrality would
force

\[
                 q_M(B_\alpha/2,r/2)=B_\alpha.r/2
\]

to be an integer, a contradiction.  Hence parity is an if-and-only-if test
for this particular reflection on \(M\).

Odd parity does not prove maximality.  It only prevents this one
difference root from supplying an \(M\)-integral discrete overgroup.  If a
different common lattice is preserved, the absolute obstruction may still
survive; otherwise discreteness must be established afresh.

## 3. The actual global gain and its limit

On a reflection-maximal carrier, two incomparable vertices cannot share the
same vector

\[
             (B_1.e_v,\ldots,B_b.e_v)\bmod2.
\]

Every antichain therefore has at most \(2^b\) vertices, and Dilworth gives
a cover by at most \(2^b\) chains.  With no half-boundaries, the color set
has one element and the forest is a single chain.

This is a global width/comparability theorem.  A chain can still have
arbitrary depth, as the affine \(B_m\) packet demonstrates, so no dimension
bound is claimed from this lemma alone.
