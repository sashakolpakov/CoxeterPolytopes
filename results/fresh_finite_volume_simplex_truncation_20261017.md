# Compact simplex ports and exact cusp truncation in finite volume

The disjoint-port theorem extends to **compact simplex faces through finite
vertices** of a finite-volume acute Coxeter polytope, even when its ideal
vertices are nonsimple. It does not extend to triangles containing ideal
vertices. The exact square pyramid below demonstrates the distinction.

Truncating the ideal vertices produces a simple projective polytope and gives
explicit face-count identities. This is not, by itself, a route to a better
dimension bound: the artificial cusp faces already exceed the usual simple
polytope corner-payment budget. A more useful starting point is Khovanskii's
inequality on the original polytope, with the cusp contributions retained.
It leaves a precise missing noncompact-face incidence inequality.

The [standalone Fraction checker](../scripts/fresh_finite_volume_simplex_truncation_20261017.py)
and [exact certificate](fresh_finite_volume_simplex_truncation_20261017.json)
verify the identities against 914 product links, all-rank polynomial
coefficients, and the literal pyramid. The proofs for arbitrary ranks are
given here; bounded checks are not used as extrapolation. No new dimension
bound, external review, or novelty claim is made.

## Geometric scope and primary inputs

Let P be a finite-sided, finite-volume acute Coxeter polytope in hyperbolic
n-space, n >= 3, and use its closed projective polytope in the Klein model.
Write v_f for its number of finite vertices. Finite vertices are simple. At
an ideal vertex v the link is combinatorially

\[
 L_v=\prod_{i=1}^{s_v}\Delta^{r_{vi}},\qquad
 r_{vi}\ge1,\quad \sum_i r_{vi}=n-1.
\]

The projective polytope is simple at edges. These local facts and the later
average-face formula are stated in
[Potyagailo–Vinberg, printed pp.64–65](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43032).
The graph-connectivity input is
[Balinski's original theorem, printed p.432](https://msp.org/pjm/1961/11-2/pjm-v11-n2-p03-s.pdf).
Khovanskii's original
[Theorem 10, printed p.49, and §6](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/askold/1986-Faa-1-english.pdf)
apply the average-face estimate to polytopes simple at edges, explicitly
including finite-volume reflection chambers. All three primary sources were
opened on 2026-10-17.

## Transfer of the compact-simplex port theorem

At a finite vertex a, the incident edges are its ports. Simplicity at a gives
a Boolean face interval: any d ports generate a unique d-face. Relate two
ports only when their two-face is a **compact** triangle.

Suppose the triangles abc and abd share the edge ab. They lie in the
three-face generated by their three ports at a. Their vertices a,b,c,d are
finite. In that three-face a and b each have degree three, with neighbors
exactly the other three displayed vertices. If there were another vertex,
deleting c and d would disconnect {a,b} from it, contrary to Balinski's
three-connectivity. The three-face is therefore a tetrahedron, and it is
compact. This argument does not require any undisplayed vertex to be simple.
Compact-triangle adjacency of ports is consequently transitive.

Let a class have d >= 2 ports, and let F be their generated d-face. The
neighbors a1,...,ad of a are finite because they belong to compact triangles.
Every pair ai,aj is an edge of F. Thus a,a1,...,ad form a complete graph, and
each already has degree d in F. All these vertices are finite and hence
simple in F. There can be no edge to another vertex. Graph connectivity
implies that F has exactly these d+1 vertices, so F is a compact simplex.

Conversely, a compact simplex through a gives a clique, hence lies in one
class. It follows that distinct maximal compact simplex faces of dimension
at least two through a use disjoint ports. The
[frozen compact disjoint-port proof](fresh_simplex_disjoint_ports_20261017.md)
therefore supplies the same one-port capacities at every finite vertex.
An isolated port ending at an ideal vertex is not a compact simplex edge;
it must not be silently included in this conclusion.

The theorem gives no port capacity at a nonsimple ideal vertex, and no
partition of all triangles into compact simplex aggregates. Those are
separate obligations in any finite-volume charge argument.

## Exact counterexample to including noncompact triangles

In the Lorentz form diag(-1,1,1,1), take outward normals

\[
 b=(0,0,0,-1),\quad
 s_1=(1,2,0,1),\ s_2=(1,0,2,1),\
 s_3=(1,-2,0,1),\ s_4=(1,0,-2,1).
\]

Their squares are 1,4,4,4,4. Base/side products are -1, successive side
products are zero, and opposite side products are -4. Thus the chamber has
base/side dihedral angle pi/3, right successive sides, and parallel opposite
sides. H=(2,0,0,1) has square -3 and product -1 with every normal, so it is a
strict common future center.

In the affine chart t=1 the closed polytope is exactly

\[
 0\le z\le1,\qquad |x|,|y|\le(1-z)/2.
\]

It has four finite base vertices (1,+/-1/2,+/-1/2,0) and the ideal apex
nu=(1,0,0,1). Its cusp link is the bounded square Delta1 x Delta1, so this is
a finite-volume Coxeter square pyramid. At the finite vertex
(1,1/2,1/2,0), the two triangular side faces share the edge to nu, whereas
the third two-face through its ports is the square base. Triangle adjacency
is not transitive if these noncompact triangles are allowed.

The checker reconstructs all vertices, active sets, edges and faces from
the inequalities, not from an assumed incidence table. Cutting by z <= 1/2
gives a combinatorial cube. Its new outward normal (1,0,0,2) has product +1
with every old side normal: even this elementary truncation is not acute.
Accordingly no Coxeter-angle or acute local-face theorem is imported to the
truncated polytope.

## Exact truncation counts

Take sufficiently small disjoint projective vertex cuts at all ideal
vertices, and call the result Q. Each new vertex belongs to a truncation
facet and corresponds to a vertex of L_v. Since L_v is simple, this new
vertex is simple. The old finite vertices are unchanged, so Q is a simple
Euclidean/projective polytope.

For one cusp suppress v and put d=n-1, V=f0(L), E=f1(L). Let T and R be the
numbers of triangular and square two-faces of L. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 V&=\prod_i(r_i+1),& E&=\tfrac12 dV,\\
 T&=\tfrac16V\sum_i r_i(r_i-1),&
 R&=\tfrac14V\sum_{i<j}r_ir_j,\\
 f_2(L)&=T+R,&3T+4R&=\binom d2 V.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed a two-face of a product is either a triangular two-face in one
simplex factor or an edge in each of two factors. The other factors supply
vertices. This proves (1) for all ranks, including a two-dimensional link
whose full polytope itself is counted as its one two-face.

Every old positive-dimensional face persists after truncation. The new
positive-dimensional k-faces are precisely the k-faces of the links, while
an old ideal vertex is replaced by V vertices. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
 f_0(Q)&=f_0(P)+\sum_v(V_v-1)=v_f+\sum_vV_v,\\
 f_1(Q)&=f_1(P)+\sum_vE_v,\\
 f_2(Q)&=f_2(P)+\sum_v(T_v+R_v).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Let N_{k,s} count old k-gonal two-faces having s ideal vertices. An ideal
corner contributes one additional side after truncation, so

\[
 f_{2,\ell}(Q)=\sum_{k+s=\ell}N_{k,s}
  +\mathbf1_{\ell=3}\sum_vT_v+\mathbf1_{\ell=4}\sum_vR_v.
\tag{3}
\]

In particular triangles(Q)=N_{3,0}+sum T_v, while
quadrilaterals(Q)=N_{4,0}+N_{3,1}+sum R_v. A noncompact triangle with two
or three ideal corners becomes a pentagon or hexagon, respectively.

Counting old incidences also gives

\[
 2f_1(P)=nv_f+\sum_vV_v,\qquad
 S(P):=\sum_{F^2\subset P}|F^2|
       =\binom n2v_f+\sum_vE_v.
\tag{4}
\]

Here E_v counts old two-faces through v, because they correspond to edges
of its link. The polygon side count equals its vertex count, including
ideal vertices. These formulas do not replace E_v by binom(n,2), which
would incorrectly assume ideal vertices simple.

For D_j(P)=sum_F(j-|F|), (2)–(4) yield

\[
 D_4(Q)=D_4(P)+\sum_v(T_v-E_v),\qquad
 D_5(Q)=D_5(P)+\sum_v(2T_v+R_v-E_v).
\tag{5}
\]

The demand on the surviving old faces, without the new link faces, is

\[
 D_5^{\rm old}(Q)=5f_2(P)-\binom n2v_f-2\sum_vE_v.
\tag{6}
\]

Each old ideal corner is counted twice here: once as its old vertex and
once as the extra side created by truncation.

## Why simple truncation does not close the charge argument

For n >= 4 write

\[
 \eta_n=\begin{cases}4(n-1)/(n-2)&n\text{ even},\\4n/(n-1)&n\text{ odd},\end{cases}
 \quad
 b_n=\begin{cases}n(n-6)/8&n\text{ even},\\(n-1)(n-5)/8&n\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]

The strict simple-polytope inequality S(Q)/f2(Q)<eta_n gives
D5(Q)>b_n f0(Q), since S(Q)=binom(n,2)f0(Q).

Consider the usual nonnegative corner-payment scheme in which every
triangle receives at least 2 and every quadrilateral at least 1; this
suffices to dominate 5-|F| on all two-faces. The artificial faces of one
cusp alone require at least 2T+R, all supported on its V new vertices.
Formula (1) gives the exact all-rank lower bound

\[
 \frac{2T+R}{V}
 =\frac{5\sum_i r_i^2+3d^2-8d}{24}
 \ge\frac{d(d-1)}8,
\tag{7}
\]

because sum r_i^2 >= sum r_i=d. Its excess over b_n is at least

\[
 \frac{(n-1)(n-2)}8-b_n
 =\begin{cases}(3n+2)/8&n\text{ even},\\3(n-1)/8&n\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]

It is strictly positive for every cusp partition. Thus a nonnegative
corner-payment model on Q with total budget at most b_n at **every** new
vertex is impossible already on the new link faces. This is an algebraic
obstruction to this particular uniform-budget method, not an existence
claim about high-dimensional reflection polytopes.

Subtracting those known demands instead leaves

\[
 D_5^{\rm old}(Q)>b_n v_f
       -\sum_v\bigl(2T_v+R_v-b_nV_v\bigr).
\tag{9}
\]

Every displayed cusp deficit is positive. Finite-vertex compact-simplex
port savings alone provide no bound on their sum; finite-volume chambers
can even have no finite vertices. A proof using truncation would therefore
need an additional aggregate bound relating cusp mass to genuine old-face
incidences. It cannot discard the new faces while retaining the original
simple-polytope lower bound.

## A usable original-polytope budget, and the missing theorem

Khovanskii's Theorem 10 avoids the artificial faces altogether. Using its
estimate in the conservative weak form S(P)/f2(P) <= eta_n, (4) gives

\[
 D_5(P)\ge\alpha_n S(P)
 =b_n v_f+\alpha_n\sum_vE_v,
 \qquad \alpha_n=5/\eta_n-1.
\tag{10}
\]

No strictness for this nonsimple-at-vertices application is needed or
claimed here. For a prospective nonnegative original-face payment rule,
let B(a) bound the total payment at a finite vertex a, and C(v) the total
payment over original two-face corners at the ideal vertex v. A necessary
condition for all face lower bounds to hold is

\[
 \sum_{a\ {m finite}}(B(a)-b_n)
 +\sum_{v\ {m ideal}}(C(v)-\alpha_nE_v)\ge0.
\tag{11}
\]

Consequently a strictly negative right-hand upper bound would give a
dimension contradiction. This retains the exact cusp factors and is a
concrete target for a future incidence theorem. It is not yet an inequality
bounding C(v) from above.

| Ambient n | Finite-vertex benchmark b_n | Cusp benchmark per old incident two-face alpha_n |
|---|---:|---:|
| 23 | 99/2 | 9/46 |
| 24 | 54 | 9/46 |
| 25 | 60 | 1/5 |
| 26 | 65 | 1/5 |

The transferred simplex capacities apply only to compact triangles and
their compact maximal simplex aggregates. A complete application of (11)
still needs payments for noncompact triangles and quadrilaterals, their
interaction with the affine product at each cusp, and simultaneous global
incidence restrictions. Local affine classification or the individual
port theorem alone supplies none of these missing inequalities.

There is a published model of the stronger kind of argument needed:
Potyagailo–Vinberg's §§4–5 couple cusp counts across neighboring faces to
obtain a lower bound on the number of facets of a five-dimensional
right-angled polyhedron, then apply the higher-face average inequality.
The right-angle assumptions are essential there; that result is not an
unrestricted estimate. This package changes neither the repository's
[dimension163 proof package](fresh_nikulin_two_budget_frontier_20261015_dimension163.md)
nor any arithmetic bound.
