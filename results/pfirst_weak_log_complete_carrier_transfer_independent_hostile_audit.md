## 2. Semiampleness really supplies global nefness

On a certified affine chart

\[
 A_\alpha=(k[z]/I_\alpha)_{g_\alpha},
\]

write the returned sections of the framed line bundle as local functions
whose numerators are \(n_{\alpha j}\).  The exact identity

\[
 g_\alpha^{N_\alpha}
 =\sum_jq_{\alpha j}n_{\alpha j}
  +\sum_\ell r_{\alpha\ell}I_{\alpha\ell}                  \tag{2.1}
\]

becomes a unit-ideal identity after localizing at \(g_\alpha\).  Denominators
in the local section functions are already units, so those functions
generate the local frame.  The certified cover and transition identities
then make the evaluation map globally surjective.  Hence
\(\mathcal O_S(mD)\) is globally generated and \(D\) is nef.

This implication uses neither the proposed wall inequalities nor a list of
Mori generators.  Conversely, for fixed sections and a fixed localized

denominators produces an identity of the form (2.1).

The nonzero \(\mathbb F_4\) provider was also reconstructed from its archive.
Its five cyclic two-dimensional cones are smooth and form the complete fan
after the stated star subdivision.  The four returned characters contain

\[
 2D=3B+12F-2E,\qquad D^2=8,

rather than merely testing \(D\) on three proposed walls.  The arbitrary

## 3. Reflection maximality removes the hidden-wall census

Once \(D=-(K_S+B/2)\) is actually nef, adjunction gives, for an irreducible
curve \(C\not\subset B\) with \(C^2<0\),

\[
 C^2=2p_a(C)-2+D.C+\frac{B.C}{2}.                           \tag{3.1}
\]

The correction terms are nonnegative.  Therefore \(C\) is a smooth rational
\((-2)\)-curve with \(D.C=B.C=0\), or a smooth rational \((-1)\)-curve with
\(D.C+B.C/2=1\).  Boundary components are the remaining \((-4)\)-curves.
Their reflections all preserve the adapted doubled host

\[
 M=\langle L,B_1/2,\ldots,B_b/2\rangle,\qquad q_M=2q_L.
\]

Indeed, the \((-1)\)-reflection has integral multiplier \(2x.C\) on \(L\)
and \(B_i.C\) on \(B_i/2\); a \((-2)\)-reflection fixes every half-boundary
because \(B_i.C=0\); and \(B_i/2\) is a norm-\(-2\) root.

Let \(R(M)\) be generated by all integral reflections of \(M\).  A missing
reflection would generate with \(W(P)\) a proper discrete reflection
overgroup inside \(O^+(M)\).  Relative maximality therefore forces

\[
                              W(P)=R(M).                    \tag{3.2}
\]

An ample class chooses the effective side of every hidden negative mirror.
Every nonnegative-square irreducible curve lies in the same future causal
cone, so reverse Lorentzian Cauchy--Schwarz gives nonnegative pairing with
the proposed chamber.  Conversely, a nef class lies in the future closure
and satisfies every displayed effective facet inequality.  This proves

\[
                         P=\mathbf P(\operatorname {Nef}S)
\]

including ideal rays.  The argument quantifies over all curves through
adjunction and causal geometry; it never requires a finite complete Mori
list.

## 4. Boundary contraction and the IJZ hypotheses

For disjoint \(B_i^2=-4\), choose an ample divisor \(A\) and set

\[
 H=A+\sum_i\frac{A.B_i}{4}B_i.
\]

Then \(H.B_i=0\), \(H.C>0\) for every other irreducible curve, and
\(H^2>0\).  A Cartier multiple \(L_0\) is nef and big.  The pair
\((S,B/2)\) is klt, and

\[
 aL_0-(K_S+B/2)=aL_0+D
\]

is nef and big.  The surface base-point-free theorem therefore gives a
projective contraction of precisely the \(B_i\).  A one-vertex resolution
string \([4]\) is the quotient singularity \(\frac14(1,1)\), with
discrepancy \(a=-1/2\), because

\[
                         2=K_S.B_i=-4a.
\]

Thus the target \(Y\) is normal, projective, and klt, and

\[
 K_S=f^*K_Y-B/2,\qquad f^*(-K_Y)=D,\qquad
 \rho(Y)=\rho(S)-b.                                        \tag{4.1}
\]

The primary Iwai--Jinnouchi--Zhang source was checked independently:
arXiv:2607.18699v1, Theorem 1.3, source label
thm-nef-anti-canonical, assumes a compact normal complex analytic klt
variety admitting a nef and big class and having nef anticanonical divisor.
Projectivity supplies the nef-and-big class, and (4.1) plus nefness of \(D\)
supplies nefness of \(-K_Y\).  In dimension two its conclusion is exactly
\(\widehat c_2(Y)\geq0\).  The pinned primary TeX hash is
6cb03d24e199e4524ed0e2cec4e7bce6371c2aee09f910bde9deb4a297877517.

Contracting \(b\) projective lines lowers the ordinary Euler number by
\(b\), and replacing each singular point's ordinary contribution by
\(1/4\) gives

\[
 \widehat c_2(Y)
 =2+\rho-b-\frac{3b}{4}
 =2+\rho-\frac{7b}{4}\geq0.                                \tag{4.2}
\]

Also

\[
 D^2=K_S^2+K_S.B+\frac{B^2}{4}
     =10-\rho+b\geq0.                                      \tag{4.3}
\]

The nonnegative combination

\[
 7(10-\rho+b)+(4\rho+8-7b)=78-3\rho
\]

proves \(\rho\leq26\), hence \(n\leq25\).  If \(b=0\), no contraction or
orbifold correction is needed: \(D=-K_S\) is nef and
\(D^2=10-\rho\geq0\), so \(\rho\leq10\) and \(n\leq9\).

## 5. Field and finiteness audit

After exact return, every facet normal is a rational numerical curve class.
All scaling-invariant cyclic Cartan products are therefore rational, so the
minimal Vinberg field is \(\mathbf Q\).

For a rational surface there are only finitely many Néron--Severi lattice
types in each bounded rank: rank two gives \(U\) or \(I_{1,1}\), and rank at
least three gives \(I_{1,\rho-1}\).  For fixed \(L\),

\[
                   L(2)\subseteq M\subseteq\tfrac12L,
\]

so \(M/L\) is a subgroup of the finite group \(L/2L\).  Hence only finitely
many adapted hosts occur.  The mirrors of the discrete group \(R(M)\) are
locally finite.  A generic compact path between two regular chambers crosses
finitely many mirrors; reflecting successively across those walls proves
that the complete arrangement has one chamber orbit.  By (3.2), the supplied
fundamental chamber is a chamber of this complete arrangement.  Each host
therefore contributes at most one unmarked ambient-isometry class.

This proves qualitative finiteness only for chambers already carrying the
stated certificate.  It neither decides reflectivity for every host nor
constructs a carrier for every raw arithmetic polytope.
