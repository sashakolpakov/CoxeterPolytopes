## 4. Injection and global incidence allocation

Map Q in F to its opposite A17 vertex q. A finite vertex of a Coxeter
polytope is simple. Releasing an unordered pair of incident facets
and intersecting the OTHER55 incident facets determines a UNIQUE
two-face through q. The only A17 in q has only one
unordered endpoint pair. Since every face in F uses that pair, two
members of F mapped to q would be the same face. Thus

    Q -> q is injective.                              (3)

This counts actual faces and avoids multiplicity from alternative
core embeddings or choices of proof. Its inverse is not asserted to
exist for every vertex with the displayed forest. The source face has
only one ideal corner, since the other three complete corner Grams
are positive definite, so no second ideal source is charged within Q.

For the precise accounting statement, let w- be w0 with the whole
finite A17 endpoint half deleted; leave every ideal weight unchanged.
Its validity on unrelated faces is a separate obligation. On each Q
in F the two D17 quarters remain, so w- pays1/2. Give a half to
the IDEAL incidence(p,Q), only on these actual faces. That restores
payment1 on Q. Its total new half-charge is |F|/2.

By(3), allocate that debt to the opposite finite vertices instead of
using a factor-envelope bound for the ideal sources. At every recipient
q, there is exactly one removed A17 half and at most one added debt.
Hence its effective finite charge is

    C-(q)+1/2 = C0(q) <=1413/4<364.                   (4)

    sum_finite C- + sum_ideal C0 + |F|/2
