## Complete norm and height-zero scope

For a primitive integral reflection root of norm q, integrality gives
`q | 2 B r`. Multiplying by the adjugate and using primitivity gives
`q | 2 det(B)`. The only prime involved for these hosts is the ramified
prime `(s)`, with `2=s^2`. The ring is norm-Euclidean: rounding both
coefficients of a field element leaves absolute norm at most one half.
Its totally positive units are squares, generated by `lambda^2`.
The table therefore represents every possible norm class, not a selected
small-norm subset. Some listed classes need not have primitive roots.

Unlike the golden-field searches, different listed norms can represent
the same integral mirror by a nonunit rescaling: `2=s^2`. The search
explicitly identifies positive parallel normals at equal height. It
does not mistake the scalar roots of norms one, two, and four for three
different walls.

The positive block's complete zero-height reflection chamber is
`F4+F4+A1`. Dominant enumeration finds two norm-two root orbits per F4
block, the scalar norm-one direction, and only parallel scalar duplicates
at the additional searched norms. The source records the entire finite
census. The nine initial walls are the negatives of the coordinate basis.

For positive height `h=alpha*x^2/q<=30`, both embeddings bound x.
Dominant weights satisfy `w_i^2<=2*(q+alpha*x^2)` at each embedding.
Leading-block Schur complements provide exact recursion bounds; integral
reconstruction, the norm equation, and all reflection coefficients are
checked before chronological selection. Every finite angle in the final
wall list passes the exact test

`4*(r,t)^2/(r^2*t^2) in {0,1,2,3,2+s}`.

These are precisely the possible Coxeter values in this field, with
labels `2,3,4,6,8`. Pairings must also be nonpositive. Acute angles such
as `3pi/8` are not accepted merely because the reflections are integral.

## Two duplicate groups, including the earlier K3 carrier

Put `M=<-lambda>+F4^2+<1>`. Its time and scalar coordinates are x and z.
Write their constant coefficients modulo two as `a_x,a_z`. Since F4 is
even and `(a+bs)^2=a mod2`,

`q(x,y,z) = (a_x+a_z) + a_x*s mod2`.

Consequently the two index-s sublattices

```
L_time   = {x divisible by s} = <-2lambda>+F4^2+<1>,
L_scalar = {z divisible by s} = <-lambda>+F4^2+<2>
```

are intrinsic to the norm form on M. The first is characterized by an
even s-coefficient of q; the second by an even sum of the two coefficients.
Every integral isometry of M therefore preserves both sublattices.

Conversely each smaller lattice has cyclic discriminant module `O/(2)`.
There is only one submodule giving an index-s proper overclosure, namely
M; the whole dual is not integral. Hence every integral isometry of either
smaller lattice extends to M. Under these displayed embeddings their full
integral orthogonal groups, and thus their full reflection groups, coincide.
This is a group identification, not an integral isometry of the lattices.

The earlier silver K3 carrier has field presentation
`saturated C5 + F4 + A1`. The calibration's five roots give an explicit
isometry from its unsaturated C5 Gram to the one-block host. Applying the
stored C5 saturation makes the matrix and its inverse integral, so

`saturated C5 ≅ <-lambda>+F4`.

The old scalar A1 has norm two. Thus the full old carrier is integrally
isometric to `L_scalar` above. The
[identification certificate](fresh_silver_f4_identifications_20261013.json)
includes the literal transport, inverse-integrality checks for C5, and the
index-s embedding of the entire carrier into M.

The old implementation searched the norm-two subgroup. The present search
includes the additional norm classes for the full reflection group; it
does not establish a new arithmetic group. The two 92-wall lists also
coincide exactly after scaling the time coordinate and identifying parallel
normals.

For every alpha, an explicit field matrix transports F4 to the diagonal
four-dimensional form. Its columns are

`(1,-1,0,0), (0,1,-1,0), (0,0,s,0), -(1,1,1,1)/s`.

Thus even the alpha=s host lies in an already scanned diagonal quadratic
space over the field. This matrix has nonintegral entries; it does not
identify the integral lattices or their full groups. No worldwide novelty
is claimed for any host here.
