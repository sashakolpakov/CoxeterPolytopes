# Global exceptional tails: the exact attachment theorem and its sharp limit

**Status (2026-08-10): proved necessary theorem and geometric no-go; no
absolute tail bound is claimed.**  This note audits the last core--tail step
in the rational hostile-incidence reduction.  It uses only the standard
surface host, adjunction, elementary blowups, and real hyperbolic volume.  It
does not use a census or a Nikulin--Vinberg classification.

The positive result is exact: finite volume forces the mixed exceptional
projections, after saturation by the pure-tail reflection group, to span the
entire tail.  The negative result is equally exact: for every \(m\geq2\), an
actual sequence of blowups produces an effective affine \(B_m\) cusp packet
with the correct physical norms and canonical colors, a single mixed
attachment of constant exceptional complexity, and a pure-tail
\(W(B_m)\)-orbit which already spans the whole \(m\)-dimensional tail.

Consequently the attachment and orbit-span consequences presently extracted
from finite volume, even when combined with local adjunction/effectivity,
canonical coloring, and pure-tail reflection saturation, do not turn a
bounded log core into a bounded carrier.  Bare reflection maximality supplies
no missing geometric implication by itself, because it does not make every
conjugate lattice root an effective physical facet and does not preserve the
canonical coloring.  This does not prove an unbounded reflection-maximal
family or rule out a theorem using the full global hypotheses in some other
way.  It identifies the indispensable extra datum which any such proof must
control.

## 1. Conventions and the canonical coloring

Let \(S\to S_0\) be a sequence of \(m\) blowups and use total-transform
coordinates

\[
 \operatorname {NS}(S)_{\mathbf R}
   =\pi^*\operatorname {NS}(S_0)_{\mathbf R}
      \mathbin\perp \bigoplus_{i=1}^m\mathbf R e_i,
 \qquad e_i^2=-1.
\]

The standard host is scaled by

\[
                 q_M=2q_{\operatorname {NS}}.
\]

Adjunction gives the following three physical colors.  If \(R\), \(E\),
and \(B\) are smooth rational curves of square \(-2\), \(-1\), and \(-4\),
respectively, their wall roots and host pairings are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{curve}&\text{wall root }r&q_M(r,r)&q_M(K_S,r)\\ \hline
R&r=R&-4&0\\
E&r=E&-2&-2\\
B&r=B/2&-2&+2.
\end{array}                                                    \tag{1.1}
\]

Thus all physical norm-minus-four roots lie in \(K_S^\perp\), so they span
rank at most \(\rho(S)-1\).  This is a useful realization obstruction, but
it does not eliminate a free exceptional tail.  The long roots

\[
                 e_1-e_2,\ldots,e_{m-1}-e_m
\]

span only the sum-zero \(A_{m-1}\) hyperplane.  The surviving diagonal
direction \(e_1+\cdots+e_m\) is exactly the exceptional component of
\(K_S=\pi^*K_{S_0}+\sum e_i\), and the endpoint \(e_m\) has norm \(-2\) and
canonical color \(-2\).  Hence the factor-of-two convention and every
adjunction color are compatible with arbitrarily large \(m\).

## 2. What finite volume really forces

Write the real carrier as an orthogonal sum

\[
                         V=V_0\mathbin\perp E,
\]

where \(V_0\) has signature \((1,p)\) and \(E\) is negative definite of
dimension \(m>0\).  Let \(P\subset\mathbf H(V)\) be a full-dimensional
Coxeter polyhedron.  If every defining root is pure core or pure tail, the
hyperboloid coordinates

\[
 x=\cosh(t)u+\sinh(t)v,
 \qquad t>0,\quad u\in\mathbf H(V_0),\quad v\in S(E)
\]

have volume density

\[
        \cosh^p(t)\sinh^{m-1}(t)\,dt\,
        d\operatorname {vol}_{\mathbf H(V_0)}(u)
        d\operatorname {vol}_{S(E)}(v).
\]

Pure inequalities are independent of \(t\), and the radial integral from
zero to infinity diverges.  Thus finite volume forces a genuinely mixed
facet.

There is a stronger exact formulation.  Let \(W_T\) be the finite group
generated by the pure-tail facet reflections, and let \(A\subset E\) be the
set of exceptional projections of the mixed facets.  Then finite volume
forces

\[
                         \boxed{\operatorname {span}_{\mathbf R}
                         (W_T A)=E}.                         \tag{2.1}
\]

Indeed, if \(U=\operatorname {span}(W_TA)\ne E\), its orthogonal complement
\(E'=U^\perp\) is \(W_T\)-invariant.  Every mixed or core root lies in
\(V_0\oplus U\).  A pure-tail reflection root lies either in \(U\) or in
\(E'\): invariance of \(U\) and the rank-one reflection formula force this
dichotomy.  All walls are therefore pure for

\[
                         V=(V_0\oplus U)\mathbin\perp E',
\]

and the preceding radial argument gives infinite volume, a contradiction.
This proof is recorded independently in
[`rational_finite_volume_mixed_attachment_lemma.md`](rational_finite_volume_mixed_attachment_lemma.md).

Equation (2.1) is an orbit-span condition, not a statement that the raw
mixed projections span \(E\), and not a lower bound on the number of mixed
facets.  Since the signed-permutation representation of \(W(B_m)\) on
\(E\) is irreducible, the orbit of any one nonzero vector spans \(E\).

## 3. An effective affine \(B_m\) packet for every \(m\)

The weakness in (2.1) is realized by surface geometry, not just by an
abstract lattice.  Let \(S_0=\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\), and let \(c\)
be a fibre, so

\[
                         c^2=0,\qquad K_{S_0}.c=-2.
\]

Blow up a point \(p_1\in c\).  Blow up \(p_2\) at the intersection of the
strict transform of \(c\) with the first exceptional curve.  For
\(j\geq3\), blow up a free point of the newest exceptional curve, away from
all its intersections.  In total-transform coordinates define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \delta_0&=c-e_1-e_2,\\
 \delta_i&=e_i-e_{i+1}\quad(1\leq i<m),\\
 \delta_m&=e_m.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{3.1}
\]

These are the strict transforms of actual smooth rational curves.  Their
physical intersections are

\[
\begin{gathered}
 \delta_0^2=\delta_1^2=\cdots=\delta_{m-1}^2=-2,
 \qquad \delta_m^2=-1,\\
 \delta_0.\delta_1=0,\qquad \delta_0.\delta_2=1,
 \qquad \delta_i.\delta_{i+1}=1\ (1\leq i<m),              \tag{3.2}
\end{gathered}
\]

with every other off-diagonal pairing zero.  When \(m=2\), both
\(\delta_0\) and \(\delta_1\) meet the short endpoint \(\delta_2\).  In the
doubled host these have norms \(-4,\ldots,-4,-2\), and (3.2) is the standard
affine \(B_m\) Coxeter Gram matrix (with the usual \(B_2=C_2\) coincidence
when \(m=2\)).

The exact null cycle is

\[
        \delta_0+\delta_1+2\delta_2+\cdots+2\delta_m=c.     \tag{3.3}
\]

The roots \(\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_m\) form a basis of the negative
exceptional summand, while adjoining \(\delta_0\) adds \(c\).  Hence their
Gram matrix is negative semidefinite of rank \(m\), with radical exactly
\(\mathbf Rc\).  This is an honest affine cusp packet; the statement is
local and does not assert that it extends to a complete finite-volume
chamber.

Adjunction supplies exactly the canonical colors required by (1.1):

\[
 q_M(K_S,\delta_0)=\cdots=q_M(K_S,\delta_{m-1})=0,
 \qquad q_M(K_S,\delta_m)=-2.                              \tag{3.4}
\]

The only mixed simple root is \(\delta_0\), whose tail projection is

\[
                         -e_1-e_2.
\]

Its squared coefficient energy is always \(2\), independently of \(m\).
A sign change in the first coordinate and subtraction produces \(2e_1\);
signed permutations then produce every coordinate direction.  Therefore

\[
 \operatorname {span}_{\mathbf R}
   \bigl(W(B_m)(-e_1-e_2)\bigr)=E                           \tag{3.5}
\]

for every \(m\).  The packet simultaneously satisfies physical
effectivity, adjunction, canonical coloring, affine cusp compatibility, and
the full finite-volume **necessary** projection condition (2.1), while the individual
mixed-facet complexity cannot bound the tail.

## 4. Maximal cusp roots do not repair the implication

The local saturation can be checked without a classification.  In the
model cusp lattice

\[
                  U(2)\mathbin\perp\langle-2\rangle^m,
\]

write a primitive reflecting vector perpendicular to a primitive isotropic
vector \(c\) as

\[
                  r=a c+\sum_i b_i e_i,
 \qquad d=\sum_i b_i^2,qquad r^2=-2d.
\]

Its reflection is integral exactly when

\[
                         d\mid2a,\qquad d\mid2b_i
                         \quad\text{for every }i.           \tag{4.1}
\]

If \(d\geq5\), a nonzero \(b_i\) would give
\(d\leq2|b_i|\leq2\sqrt d\), a contradiction.  The case \(d=3\) is
impossible.  For \(d=4\), exactly one \(b_i\) equals \(\pm2\) and (4.1)
forces \(a\) even, so the vector is nonprimitive and gives the same mirror
as a \(d=1\) root.  The primitive mirrors are therefore precisely

\[
                  a c\mathbin\pm e_i,
 \qquad a c\mathbin\pm e_i\mathbin\pm e_j,                 \tag{4.2}
\]

the short and long affine signed-permutation mirrors.  The simple roots in
(3.1) generate this local affine reflection arrangement.  Thus even local
reflection saturation at the cusp is compatible with arbitrary \(m\).
This calculation is for the displayed split model; a nonsplit overlattice
requires its own divisibility check.

Global reflection maximality still cannot be converted into a physical
wall count by conjugation.  Reflections preserve the lattice form, but
neither the effective cone nor the canonical color.  For example, the
endpoint reflection satisfies

\[
                         s_{e_m}(K_S)=K_S-2e_m\ne K_S.      \tag{4.3}
\]

Accordingly, from \(W(P)=R(M)\) one may conclude that all lattice
reflections lie in the generated group; one may not conclude that every
conjugate root is an effective \((-1)\)- or \((-2)\)-curve, or a new facet
with the adjunction color prescribed by \(K_S\).

## 5. Exact no-go and the additional invariant needed

The preceding construction does **not** prove an unbounded
reflection-maximal family of complete finite-volume nef chambers.  It
instead blocks the proposed local-to-global proof at its exact logical
point:

> Finite volume forces \(W_T\)-orbit spanning of mixed projections, but one
> mixed physical \((-2)\)-curve of constant tail complexity can satisfy
> that condition for an arbitrarily large effective affine \(B_m\) tail.
> Canonical coloring and local reflection saturation add no rank inequality.

Therefore any absolute tail theorem must introduce a genuinely global
quantity which is not present in this packet.  Sufficient conditional
inputs include either of the following.

1. An absolute **total marked-facet degree** bound \(T\) for an integral
   ample marking.  Every physical tail facet then contributes a positive
   integer, so a tail with \(m\) components has \(m\leq T\).  A bound on
   each facet separately is insufficient.
2. A **finite numerical contraction orbit**, or an equivariant
   minimalization theorem, which makes the relevant trace lattice descend
   to the bounded core.  Only then can the established core-rank bounds be
   applied to the full carrier.
3. A theorem that a canonical-preserving effective orbit of mixed facets,
   rather than merely their lattice-reflection orbit, has uniformly bounded
   degree or cardinality.
4. Another global polarization, cusp-complement, or full-facet complexity
   invariant which bounds the total number of physical tail components.

The distinction between total and individual complexity is essential:
(3.1)--(3.5) keep the mixed root's square, canonical color, tail support,
and coefficient energy fixed while \(m\) grows.  The number of physical
tail facets, not the complexity of one attachment, is what remains
uncontrolled.

This is the terminal output of the present bridge audit.  It leaves the
global dimension-bound theorem open, but it removes the false inference
from finite-volume mixing or bare reflection maximality and isolates the
extra invariant that a successful AG proof must supply.
