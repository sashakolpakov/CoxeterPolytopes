# The quartic host is globally nonreflective

The new edge work constructs eight more certified nearest-mirror facets, then proves that **the full reflection chambers of the tested H5 and H9 lattices have infinite volume**. Two integral involutions preserve one full chamber, and their product has infinite order. Thus no further root search in these exact lattices can produce a finite-volume Coxeter polytope. This is a full-host obstruction, not an inference from a finite wall prefix.

The lattices are

\[
M=\langle-(T-1)\rangle+F_4+\langle1\rangle,\qquad
L=\langle-(T-1)\rangle+F_4+F_4+\langle1\rangle,
\]

over $\mathbb Z[T]$, where $T=2\cos(\pi/8)$ and $T^4-4T^2+2=0$. The F4 block has diagonal 2 and successive off-diagonal entries $-1,-\sqrt2,-1$. The [structural certificate](fresh_h9_glued_host_20261013_structure.json) gives the complete primitive norm classes
$1,2+T,T^2,2T^2-T^3,2$ and the admissibility and lattice facts used here. The theorem concerns these integral lattices and their reflection subgroups. It does not exclude a different lattice, a changed quadratic space, or a nonarithmetic construction.

## Global chamber-stabilizer certificate

Both permutations preserve the actual outward-oriented normals exactly, without sign changes or nonunit rescaling. Both maps and inverses are integral and preserve the quadratic form. They fix future timelike points. They extend to L by acting identically on the second F4 block and its four incident simple roots. The [local-automorphism census](fresh_h9_quartic_next_frontier_20261013_symmetries.json) records the matrices and the finite Gram calculations.

An integral lattice isometry normalizes the full root reflection group. A complete local simple system at a finite corner determines one local sector and hence one global chamber, by local finiteness of the mirror arrangement. Permuting its oriented simple normals preserves that sector. The two complete nearest-edge certificates identify these particular sectors with the **same chamber**. The checker explicitly verifies the common oriented simple-root set at the intermediate corner, the same oriented edge normals along each step, and the strict side inequalities for the released and arriving walls. Therefore A and B both preserve that full chamber, rather than merely preserving selected finite wall lists.

Their product has exact H5 trace

\[
\operatorname{tr}(BA)=-14-8T+26T^2+14T^3
\approx148.30849694>6.
\]

The lifted H9 product has trace $-10-8T+26T^2+14T^3>10$. A finite-order real matrix of the corresponding dimension cannot have this trace, so the product has infinite order. The integral isometry group is discrete: the other three embeddings preserve positive definite forms, and bounded matrix coefficients in all embeddings form a finite set. An infinite discrete chamber stabilizer gives infinitely many disjoint translates of a sufficiently small interior ball. Consequently the full chamber has infinite volume.

Any reflection subgroup generated by integral lattice roots lies inside this full reflection group. Its quotient cannot have smaller covolume than the full group. Therefore **neither lattice admits a finite-volume Coxeter polytope whose facet reflections preserve that lattice**.

The complete first-mirror test was then improved to use a coordinate near the corner. Let E be the positive edge span, let $w$ be the released starting wall, and write $b$ for its projection into $E^\perp$, with $B=b^2>0$. If a folded root has fixed edge component $r_E$ and residual norm $R=q-r_E^2>0$, put $c=(r_E,w)$. Its integral pairing $p=(r,w)$ satisfies

\[
(p-c)^2\le RBC,\qquad
C=\frac{(k_0,k_1)^2}{k_0^2k_1^2}
\]

at the real embedding, and $(p-c)^2\le RB$ at each positive definite conjugate. The real inequality is the Lorentz-plane normal formula with $C=\cosh^2$ of the endpoint distance. Distance from the starting corner is monotone along the outgoing segment.

To enumerate this shifted integral coset, choose an integral element a by coefficientwise rounding of c. If the shifted bound is cap, then

\[
(p-a)^2\le2\,\mathrm{cap}+2(a-c)^2
\]

at every embedding. The trace alphabet exhausts this finite superset, and an exact shifted filter removes its extra entries. Fixing p leaves one quadratic equation in the orthogonal plane. This includes nonsquare residual norms and does not require a complete Pell-orbit classification.
