# Full-group K3 enrichments of three Roulleau polytopes

## Positive result

The cyclic K3 realizations found earlier extend to genuine finite groups with
a commuting symplectic involution:

| full BH row | effective K3 group | selected element | archived section |
|---|---|---|---|
| `1.4.3.1` | `C4 x C2` | `g`, order 4, order 2 on `NS` | `R14-UE8A1x4-C2D-H11-19` |
| `1.8.1.1` | `C8 x C2` | `g`, order 8, order 2 on `NS` | `R14-D8D4-T2-H11-16` |
| `1.16.0.1` | `C16 x C2` | `g^2`, order 8, order 2 on `NS` | `R14-D8D4-T2-H11-16` |

Here `s` generates the symplectic kernel `H_s=C2` in the
Brandhorst--Hofmann notation.  The strongest statement is simultaneous:

> One K3 surface with generic
> `NS=U+D8(-1)+D4(-1)` and an effective `C16 x C2` action realizes both the
> finite-volume `H11/16` TYPE_2 polytope and the finite-volume `H10/14`

## Exact group and lattice calculation

The source rows are in Brandhorst--Hofmann's exhaustive classification of
finite K3 automorphism groups
([article](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2023.50),
[database v2.1](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10410278)).  A database row is
an *effective* character: it parametrizes actual pairs `(X,G)`, not merely a
cohomological representation.

For each row the full replay reconstructs the even unimodular K3 lattice,
checks the symplectic generator `s`, the distinguished generator `g`, their
orders and commutativity, and computes

`T = Fix(s) intersect ker Phi_n(g)`,  `NS = T^perp`.

It obtains:

- `1.4.3.1`: `G=C4 x C2`, generic
  `NS=U+E8(-1)+A1(-1)^4`, Picard image `C2 x C2`, and
  `ker(G -> O(NS))=<g^2>=C2`;
- `1.8.1.1`: `G=C8 x C2`, generic
  `NS=U+D8(-1)+D4(-1)`, Picard image `C2 x C2`, and
  kernel `<g^2>=C4`; and
- `1.16.0.1`: `G=C16 x C2`, the same generic `U+D8+D4` Picard lattice,
  Picard image `C4 x C2`, and kernel `<g^4>=C4`.

In all three cases `T` has rank 8, signature `(2,6)`, and determinant 16.
The common fixed lattice of the full group has signature `(1,5)`, so the
group fixes a positive class.  The symplectic coinvariant has rank 8,
determinant 256, and half of its Gram is even, integral, and negative
definite; hence it is rootless.  These are exact effectiveness/Kähler checks,
not signature-only guesses.

## Why these are the same cyclic actions, not genus collisions

The replay restricts the full groups to the four selected cyclic subgroups
and computes, for every divisor of the element order, the integral fixed
lattice rank, determinant, and Smith invariants.  It then checks every row in
the complete purely non-symplectic tables.  The matches are unique:

- `<g>` in `1.4.3.1` is exactly pure row `0.4.3.4`;
- `<g>` in `1.8.1.1` and `<g^2>` in `1.16.0.1` are exactly pure row
  `0.8.1.3`; and
- `<s g>` in `1.16.0.1` is exactly pure row `0.16.0.3`.

The earlier pure-row certificates already prove integral conjugacy to the
archived divisor actions.  The new replay also verifies this directly.  For
each selected action it splits `NS` into fixed and coinvariant lattices,
constructs an explicit integral coinvariant intertwiner, checks a fixed
discriminant-form isometry, and carries all four elements of the primitive
glue graph.  Nikulin's rank-versus-discriminant-length criterion lifts the
fixed discriminant map, so the resulting conjugacy is a conjugacy of the
full integral Picard actions, not just an equality of genus data.

There is a further exact nesting.  Among all 106 full order-eight rows, the
subgroup `<s,g^2>` of `1.16.0.1` uniquely matches `1.8.1.1`.  Thus the positive
lineage is

`0.8.1.3 < 1.8.1.1 = <s,g^2> in 1.16.0.1`,

while the same `1.16.0.1` also contains the pure TYPE_4 action `0.16.0.3`.

## Hyperbolic conclusion and scope

The complete Vinberg audits remain the source of the volume theorem:

- C2D is finite-volume noncompact in `H11`, with 19 facets, 114 finite
  vertices, 13 cusps, and 795 elliptic edges;
- TYPE_2 is finite-volume noncompact in `H11`, with 16 facets, 112 finite
  vertices, 9 cusps, and 750 elliptic edges; and
- TYPE_4 is finite-volume noncompact in `H10`, with 14 facets, 44 finite
  vertices, 3 cusps, and 254 elliptic edges.

All edge and full parabolic-neighbourhood audits pass.  The group database
supplies the actual algebraic surfaces and automorphisms; the Vinberg
certificates supply finite volume.  Neither half substitutes for the other.

The useful positive-search criterion is therefore:

1. intersect an exhaustive effective K3-group character with the desired
   generic divisor lattice;
2. identify a selected cyclic subgroup by its complete integral local type;
3. lift the fixed/coinvariant discriminant maps through the primitive glue;
4. use effectiveness for a common Kähler chamber; and
5. attach the already complete Vinberg volume certificate.

This turns a lattice/genus hit into a genuine group-enriched Coxeter-polytope
realization.
