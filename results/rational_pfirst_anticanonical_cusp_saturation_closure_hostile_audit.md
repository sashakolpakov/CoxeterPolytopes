\]

reflections put it strictly inside the standard signed-permutation/Cremona

\[

old chamber; this is not inferred merely from one test point.

Only one old wall has a unit simple-root expansion.  Thus the eight
Hamming anticanonical decompositions do not remain facet data after

## 3. Maximality descent

The proof that all host reflections are generated is sound.

For a primitive reflecting vector \(u\) in a unimodular lattice, the
divisibility of \(u\) is one.  Integral reflection forces
\(u^2\mid2\), leaving only squares \(-1,-2\).

Signed permutations put such a vector, up to overall sign, in the form

\[
(a,-m_1,\ldots,-m_9),\qquad
m_1\ge\cdots\ge m_9\ge0.
\]

If \(a>0\), negative norm forces
\(s=m_1+m_2+m_3>a\).  The key inequality is

\[
\sum m_i^2\le m_1^2+m_2^2+7m_3^2
             \le(m_1+m_2+m_3)^2.
\]

Each \(m_i\le a\), so \(s\le3a\).  Equality gives precisely the Cremona
simple root.  Otherwise Cremona reflection changes the time coordinate to
\(2a-s\), whose absolute value is strictly smaller than \(a\).  The descent
terminates at a signed coordinate root or coordinate-difference root.

Thus every integral reflection belongs to the displayed Coxeter group.
No finite search, transitivity classification, or unstated arithmetic
maximality theorem is being used.

## 4. Characteristic-cusp rigidity

For any relatively maximal chamber on this host, adjoining an arbitrary
integral reflection stays inside the discrete group \(O^+(L)\).  Therefore
the side group contains every host reflection and equals the group audited
above.  Its chambers are translates of the simplex.

If \(F=-K\) is a cusp, its translate must be one of the simplex's two cusp
rays.  Characteristic parity excludes the second ray, so the translating
element fixes \(F\).  This proves the claimed \(F\)-stabilizer conjugacy.

A physical twice-primitive square-\(-4\) wall has \(K.B=2\), hence
\(F.B=-2\).  It cannot bound a chamber containing \(F\).  The conclusion
of one \((-1)\) wall and nine \((-2)\) walls is therefore correctly scoped
to the primitive anticanonical-cusp lane.

\[

\]

\(4+27\lambda^2\), giving two irreducible nodal cubics.  The infinite

\[
(z^3,\ z-x^3-xz^2).

Three explicit flex-direction blow-ups reduce it, up to local units and a
regular coordinate change, to \((x^6,w)\).  Six further point blow-ups

- \(E_i-E_{i+1}\), \(1\le i\le8\);

\[

A negative horizontal curve must be a section, and the section is unique.
A negative vertical curve must be a displayed fibre component.  This
