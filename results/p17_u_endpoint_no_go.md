# The P17 U-endpoint is not a K3 or ruled-surface carrier

## Exact result

> **Theorem.** Let (X) be a smooth projective surface over a field of
> characteristic zero whose complete curve cone is generated by irreducible
> curves with the 22 facet classes of Vinberg's classical (P_{17}) chamber.
> Suppose a sequence of sixteen facet-curve contractions leaves the rank-two
> cone generated by classes (s,f) with
> 
> \[
> s^2=-2,\qquad f^2=0,\qquad s\mathbin{\cdot}f=1.
> \]
> 
> Then the rank-two surface is neither an elliptic K3 surface with
> (operatorname{NS}=U) nor a geometrically ruled surface over any smooth
> base.

This is a carrier obstruction for one exact endpoint type.  It is **not** an
abstract (P_{17}) nonexistence theorem: Vinberg's finite-volume noncompact
hyperbolic polytope still exists.  It also leaves other kinds of algebraic
surface endpoints open.

## What the facet cone forces

The marking-free contraction replay starts from the 22 standard (P_{17})
facet vectors.  At rank two there are sixteen states: eight have a
square-minus-one section and eight have a square-minus-two section.  In each
of the latter eight states, the sixteen contracted facets recover an
unbranched exceptional chain (E_1,\ldots,E_{16}).  After ordering that chain
from its incidence graph, every endpoint has the same six surviving
profiles:

| curve | class on the rank-two surface | multiplicities at (p_1,\ldots,p_{16}) |
|---|---|---|
| (s) | (s) | (0^{16}) |
| (f) | (f) | (1^2,0^{14}) |
| (C) | (2s+4f) | (1^{10},0^6) |
| (Q) | (2s+5f) | (1^{14},0^2) |
| (A) | (3s+6f) | (2,1^{15}) |
| (S) | (4s+8f) | (2^6,1^{10}) |

Thus the first two centers lie on a fiber (F_0) and on the smooth branch
of (C), so

\[
F_0\mathbin{\cdot}C=2,qquad F_0|_C=2p.
\]

The (C)- and (A)-clusters share precisely the first ten centers.  Noether's
local intersection formula gives

\[
I_p(C,A)=2+9=11,
\]

whereas their classes have (C\mathbin{\cdot}A=12).  The order eleven—not
merely a lower bound—is the decisive obstruction below.

## Elliptic K3 obstruction

Assume the rank-two surface (Y) is an elliptic K3 with section (s), fiber
(f), and (operatorname{NS}(Y)=U).  Put (D=s+2f).  In a minimal
Weierstrass model over (mathbb P^1), with weighted coordinates (z,x,y),

\[
x\in H^0(Y,2D),\qquad y\in H^0(Y,3D).
\]

Since (C\in|2D|) is irreducible and does not contain (s), its affine
equation has the form

\[
x=c(t).
\]

The equality (F_0|_C=2p) says that the two points with fixed (x)-coordinate
coalesce under the Weierstrass involution; hence (y(p)=0).  The fiber is
smooth at (p), because its prescribed multiplicity there is one.  Therefore
the Weierstrass equation has nonzero (x)-derivative at (p), ((t,y)) are
regular local coordinates, and (x_y(p)=0).

A section of (3D) is

\[
a_6(t)z^3+b_2(t)xz+c,y.
\]

If (c=0), its divisor contains (z=0), namely the section (s), so an
irreducible (A\in|3D|) has (c\ne0).  In the coordinates ((t,y)),

\[
\frac{\partial A}{\partial y}(p)=c\ne0.
\]

Thus (A) is smooth at (p), contradicting the forced first multiplicity
(operatorname{mult}_pA=2).

## Ruled-surface obstruction

Now let (Y\to B) be a geometrically ruled surface over an arbitrary smooth
base, with negative section (s^2=-2).  The curve (C), being disjoint from
(s) and meeting every fiber twice, is a finite flat bisection in the affine
line bundle (Y\setminus s\to B).

The trace, or barycenter, of a degree-two divisor gives a section of this
affine-line torsor when (2) is invertible.  Translating to that section
splits the ruled surface:

\[
Y\simeq\mathbb P_B(\mathcal O_B\oplus L^{-1}),qquad \deg L=2.
\]

With (v) the affine fiber coordinate, completing the square writes

\[
C:\quad v^2=\delta,qquad \delta\in H^0(B,L^2).
\]

The divisor (A) is disjoint from (s), so its leading cubic coefficient is
nowhere zero.  Restriction modulo (v^2=\delta) has the unique form

\[
A|_C=P+vQ,qquad
P\in H^0(B,L^3),\quad Q\in H^0(B,L^2).
\]

At (p), the equality (F_0|_C=2p) and smoothness of (C) make (p) a
simple ramification point.  In completed local coordinates one may take
(t=v^2).  Consequently the two summands have opposite parity:

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(P)=2\operatorname{ord}_{b_0}(P),\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_p(vQ)=1+2\operatorname{ord}_{b_0}(Q).
\]

For (P+vQ) to vanish to order at least eleven, one needs

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{b_0}(P)\ge6,qquad
\operatorname{ord}_{b_0}(Q)\ge5.
\]

But (deg L^2=4), so the second inequality forces (Q=0).  Since
(deg L^3=6), a nonzero (P) satisfying the first inequality pulls back
with order exactly twelve.  If (P=0) as well, (A) contains (C).  Hence
an irreducible (A) can have local intersection at most ten or exactly
twelve, never the required eleven.

The barycenter argument is why this proof also covers initially nonsplit
ruled surfaces.
