# Hostile audit: compact direct full-nef surface carriers

**Audit target:**
[`direct_full_nef_surface_compactness_obstruction.md`](direct_full_nef_surface_compactness_obstruction.md),
with the rational rank argument in
[`rational_surface_primitive_wall_dimension_bound.md`](rational_surface_primitive_wall_dimension_bound.md).

**Verdict (2026-08-09): PASS, with a strict strengthening on the rational
branch.**  For a genuine full-nef integral Coxeter chamber of finite volume,
the facet reflection group has finite index in the arithmetic orthogonal
group.  Compactness is therefore equivalent to rational anisotropy, and
Meyer's theorem forces hyperbolic dimension at most three.  Moreover, the
complete nef cone of a rational surface of Picard rank at least two is never
compact at all.

## A. General direct full-nef theorem

Let

```text
L = NS(X)/torsion,   signature(L)=(1,n),   n>=2,
```

and suppose the projectivized **entire** nef cone is a finite-sided Coxeter
polyhedron `P`.  Assume every facet reflection preserves `L`, and assume `P`
has finite hyperbolic volume.  Then, for the group `W` generated by those
reflections,

\[
 [O^+(L):W]<\infty,
 \qquad
 P\text{ compact}\iff L_{\mathbf Q}\text{ anisotropic}.
 \tag{A.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{P\text{ compact}\Longrightarrow n\leq3.}
 \tag{A.2}
\]

### A.1. The finite-index step is valid

Hodge index gives the asserted nondegenerate Lorentz signature on the
torsion-free numerical lattice.  A hyperbolic facet has a negative normal,
so its reflection preserves the selected component of the positive cone.
The integrality hypothesis therefore gives

```text
W <= Gamma := O^+(L).
```

Because `P` is assumed to be a Coxeter chamber, not merely an acute region,
the Coxeter reflection theorem makes `P` a fundamental polyhedron for `W`.
Thus `vol(P)<infinity` says exactly that `W` is a lattice in
`Isom(H^n)`.

For `n>=2`, `Gamma` is an arithmetic lattice and has positive finite
covolume.  A fundamental region for `W` is, modulo null boundaries, the
union of one translate of a `Gamma`-fundamental region for each right coset
in `W\Gamma`.  Hence

\[
 \operatorname{covol}(W)
   =[\Gamma:W]\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma).
\]

Finite covolume on the left forces finite index.  Passing to the effective
projective orthogonal group, or to its orientation-preserving subgroup,
changes only finite kernels or finite indices and does not affect the
argument.

### A.2. The compactness and Meyer steps are valid

The Godement compactness criterion for this rational orthogonal group gives

\[
 \Gamma\backslash\mathbb H^n\text{ compact}
 \iff L_{\mathbf Q}\text{ has no nonzero isotropic vector}.
\]

Compactness is invariant under passage to the finite-index subgroup `W`.
For a locally finite Coxeter tiling, `W\H^n` is compact exactly when its
closed Coxeter chamber `P` is compact.  This proves the equivalence in
(A.1).

Meyer's theorem applies to the indefinite rational quadratic space
`L_Q`.  If `n>=4`, then `dim_Q L_Q=n+1>=5`, so `L_Q` is isotropic.  Equation
(A.1) then makes every finite-volume direct full-nef integral Coxeter
chamber noncompact.  The cutoff `n<=3` is therefore exact for this argument.

### A.3. Assumptions that cannot be dropped silently

- `L` must mean `NS(X)/torsion` (equivalently the numerical lattice), not a
  possibly torsion-containing Neron--Severi group.
- The displayed region must be the whole nef cone and a genuine Coxeter
  fundamental chamber.  A selected divisor subsystem, invariant section,
  or eigen-nef section does not make its reflection group finite index in
  `O^+(L)`.
- Facet reflections must preserve the integral rational lattice `L`.
  Totally real reflection lattices escape the rational Meyer obstruction.
- Finite-sidedness alone is not being substituted for finite volume.  For a
  complete rational-polyhedral nef cone with `n>=2`, finite volume follows
  independently from the convex--Hodge theorem; otherwise it must be
  assumed or certified.
- The arithmetic-lattice proof above is stated for `n>=2`.  Hyperbolic
  dimension one has the familiar split-torus exception and should be handled
  separately; it has no effect on the upper bound.

## B. Rational surfaces are never compact full-nef carriers

Let `X` be a smooth projective rational surface over an algebraically closed
field and assume `rho(X)>=2`.  Contract `(-1)`-curves to a minimal rational
surface.

- If the minimal model is a Hirzebruch surface `F_a`, compose the contraction
  with its ruling `F_a -> P^1`.
- If the minimal model is `P^2`, then `X != P^2`.  In a blowdown sequence the
  surface immediately above `P^2` is `Bl_p(P^2)=F_1`; stop there and compose
  with its ruling.

In both cases there is a morphism `f:X->P^1`.  For a point `p in P^1`, the
class

\[
 F=f^*[p]
\]

is nonzero and nef, and two distinct fibres show `F^2=0`.  Thus its
projective ray is an ideal point of the complete nef cone.  Equivalently,
`F+epsilon A` is ample for every ample `A` and `epsilon>0`, and these positive
rays tend to `[F]` at infinity.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{X\text{ rational},\ \rho(X)\ge2
 \Longrightarrow \mathbf P(\operatorname{Nef}X)\text{ is noncompact}.}
 \tag{B.1}
\]

No polyhedrality, finite-volume, primitivity, or reflection hypothesis is
needed for (B.1).  If the nef cone is polyhedral, strict convexity of the
future light cone also shows that `[F]` is an extremal nef ray.

This strictly supersedes the former compact conclusion `n<=8` in the
primitive rational-wall theorem: there are no positive-dimensional compact
**full-nef rational-surface** carriers.  Its finite-volume conclusion
`n<=9` remains valid and useful.

## C. Replay of the primitive rational-wall rank bound

No flaw was found in the remaining `n<=9` chain.

1. A rational surface has a unimodular intersection lattice: this holds for
   `P^2` and every Hirzebruch surface and is preserved by blow-up.
2. In a unimodular lattice a primitive class has divisibility one.  An
   integral reflection in such a class forces its negative square to divide
   two, hence the wall curve has square `-1` or `-2`.
3. Negativity, implicit in the original proof, follows from being dual to a
   genuine nef facet when `rho>=3`: a positive normal has negative-definite
   perpendicular, while an isotropic normal meets the closed positive cone
   only in one ray, too small for a facet of dimension `rho-1>=2`.
4. Adjunction for a smooth rational wall curve gives
   `-K_X.C=2+C^2 in {1,0}`.  Since the listed curves generate every extremal
   Mori ray, `-K_X` is nef.
5. Hence `K_X^2>=0`, while minimal-model/blow-up arithmetic gives
   `K_X^2+rho(X)=10`.  Thus `rho<=10`, or `n<=9`.
6. At `rho=10`, `K_X` is nonzero (for example, it is characteristic in the
   odd unimodular rational-surface lattice, whereas zero is not), and `-K_X`
   is nef isotropic.  This already proves noncompactness at equality, but
   (B.1) proves noncompactness in every rational rank at least two.

## Scope of the returned theorem

The general result is a theorem about the direct full-nef carrier class.  It
becomes a theorem about a surface-free polytope class `C` exactly when a
`P`-first compiler proves that every `P in C` is the entire nef chamber of a
surface with integral facet reflections.  It does not rule out compact
totally-real eigenslices, invariant sections lacking an integral rational
section lattice, or polytopes for which no such surface promotion exists.

Reference for the arithmetic finite-covolume input: Borel--Harish-Chandra,
*Arithmetic Subgroups of Algebraic Groups*, Ann. of Math. 75 (1962),
485--535, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970210).  The remaining inputs are
the Godement compactness criterion, Meyer's theorem, Hodge index, and the
classical minimal-model classification of rational surfaces.
