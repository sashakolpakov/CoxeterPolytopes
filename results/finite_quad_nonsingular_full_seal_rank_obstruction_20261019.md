## 1. General rank obstruction

Let F be an actual two-dimensional face of an acute Coxeter polytope in
H^n. Let H be a specified core of k distinct facet normals consisting of
four black side walls of F and k-4 of its common supporting white walls.
Suppose its Gram is nonsingular of signature(k-1,1). If every additional
common white wall is orthogonal to all k core normals, this actual face
cannot exist.

Here the number of common supporting white walls is exactly n-2, and
they are linearly independent. For completeness, take a finite point in
the relative interior of F. These normals lie in its positive-definite
orthogonal space. Any relation splits as P=M, where P and M are linear
combinations with positive coefficients and disjoint supports. Acuteness
gives P.M<=0, so P^2=0 and P=0. A strict interior point of the polytope
has negative product with every wall, contradicting such a nonzero
positive combination equalling zero. Thus the supporting normals are
independent. Their span has dimension codim(F)=n-2, so there are n-2.

The additional white span E therefore has dimension

    dim(E)=(n-2)-(k-4)=n+2-k.

The core span is nondegenerate, so it intersects its orthogonal complement
only at zero. Under the full-seal hypothesis E lies in that complement,
and consequently

    dim(span(H)+E)=k+(n+2-k)=n+2.

This is impossible in the ambient Lorentz space of dimension n+1.
The result concerns the *actual full common-white set*: a statement about
only some spectator roots or a selected corner does not suffice.

The nonsingularity assumption is essential. A k-root Lorentzian core of
Gram corank1 has span dimension k-1, and the same orthogonal addition can
have total dimension n+1. Thus this argument does not exclude the genuine
radical D16/D17 cores or contradict the previously constructed D16 prism.
