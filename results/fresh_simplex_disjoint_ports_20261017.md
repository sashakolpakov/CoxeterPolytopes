# Maximal simplex faces use disjoint ports at a simple vertex

The previous simplex-incidence condition can be strengthened: **distinct
maximal simplex faces through a vertex of a simple convex polytope have
disjoint port sets**. Sharing one incident edge is already impossible.
This is a general combinatorial fact; acuteness and arithmeticity are not
needed. It strengthens the previously used intersection-at-most-one rule.

The [exact export](../scripts/fresh_simplex_disjoint_ports_20261017.py) and
[certificate](fresh_simplex_disjoint_ports_20261017.json) add the corresponding
one-port capacities to the existing flexible-simplex domain. They find282
violated port-orbit capacities at136 vertex forests in the saved dual of the
previously successful simplex-packing model. No augmented solve or dimension
exclusion is part of this package.

## Proof

First, a simple convex three-polytope with two adjacent triangular facets is
a tetrahedron. Write the two triangles as abc and abd, sharing edge ab.
The neighbors of a are exactly b,c,d, and those of b are exactly a,c,d,
because every vertex has degree three. If there were any additional vertices,
deleting c and d would disconnect the pair a,b from them. This contradicts
three-connectivity. The four displayed vertices therefore exhaust the
polytope, which is a tetrahedron.

The graph-connectivity input is
[Balinski's theorem, printed p.432](https://msp.org/pjm/1961/11-2/pjm-v11-n2-p03-s.pdf):
the graph of a convex d-polytope remains connected after deleting fewer than
d vertices. The publisher's original paper was checked directly. The argument
above uses only its three-dimensional case.

Now fix a vertex v of a simple n-polytope P. Its incident edges are the ports.
The faces through v form a Boolean interval: any d selected ports generate
a unique d-face. Define two ports to be related when their two-face is a
triangle. If a,b are related and a,c are related, their two triangles lie in
the three-face generated by a,b,c. They share the edge represented by a.
That three-face is simple, so the preceding paragraph makes it a tetrahedron.
Thus b,c are related as well. Triangular adjacency on ports is transitive;
after adding equality it is an equivalence relation, whose components are
complete graphs.

Let B be a component of d ports and F the d-face it generates. Write the
neighbors of v along those ports as v1,...,vd. Every pair vi,vj is joined
by an edge, because the corresponding two-face is the triangle v,vi,vj.
The d+1 vertices v,v1,...,vd therefore form a complete graph in F. Every
one already has degree d within this graph. Since F is simple of dimension d,
there are no edges from these vertices to any further vertex. Connectivity of
the graph of F forces these to be all its vertices. Hence F is a d-simplex.

Conversely, the port set of any simplex face through v is a clique for
triangular adjacency, hence lies in one component. The component simplex
contains that face by the Boolean face interval. Consequently the maximal
simplex faces of dimension at least two through v are exactly the components
of size at least two. Their port sets are disjoint. Isolated ports can be
treated as maximal edges; they do not contribute triangular-face charges.

This also gives a purely combinatorial proof of unique maximal-simplex
extension of each triangular two-face. No Coxeter Gram computation is used
in this stronger argument. Both construction_audit and exact_hub_counts
independently read the argument and confirmed the three-face, connectivity
and simplex-completion steps within the internal agent workflow.

## Valid new charge columns

For each port p at v, the theorem gives

\[
 \sum_{A\ni v}\mathbf 1\{p\in B_A(v)\}\le1,
\]

where A ranges over the actual maximal simplex faces of dimension at least
two. For a full finite vertex forest F, group ports into orbits under its
labelled diagram automorphisms. For an orbit O, summing these inequalities
gives total incidence at most |O|. Give every port of O a nonnegative weight
u(F,O). A simplex corner with port block B receives

\[
 u(F,O)|B\cap O|,
\]

while the complete vertex budget increases by at most |O|u(F,O). This is
equivalent to an averaged budget-one charge, but its integer normalization
avoids denominators in the LP export. The charge counts simplex flags, not
triangle corners separately. Quadrilateral aggregate rows receive zero.

The implementation constructs complete one-marked-root forest codes for all
13 ports of each of the1,870 old vertex types. It then reconstructs all
10,185 flexible simplex stars and their entire port blocks. The export has
15,119 port-orbit columns, of which6,528 occur in a face row, and54,749 nonzero
row coefficients. Every row's coefficients sum to d(d+1), the number of
simplex port incidences. The old domain, source files and weights are unchanged.

## Exact separation of the old dual

For dual face masses alpha_f and vertex masses mu_F, each new column requires

\[
 \sum_f a_{f,F,O}\alpha_f\le |O|\mu_F.
\]

The saved exact dual of the
[simplex-packing repair](fresh_sqrt5_rank13_simplex_packing_repair_20261017.md)
violates282 such inequalities at136 forests. One short witness uses forest

`7 A1 + A2 + H4`.

Its isolated-root orbit has seven ports. The only positive contributing face
row in the saved dual is3266, with coefficient2 and mass275241/10000000000.
The vertex mass is91747/70000000000. Thus the port incidence requires six
times that saved vertex mass, even though the previous pair and selected
simplex-clique capacities pass.

Keeping every saved face mass fixed, the new port constraints require at least

`796340810567/2100000000000`

additional total vertex mass. Dividing the same face demand by this necessary
new mass gives at most

`50353882638360/5792681643743`, approximately8.69267219,

instead of the old dual's approximately11.98901961. This rejects that exact
dual distribution as an obstruction for the augmented model. It does not
bound the optimum of the augmented model: another dual distribution may
give a larger value. It does not construct a realizable incidence distribution.

The frozen full simplex/quadrilateral model has many more vertex forests;
this export supplies only the old flexible-simplex coefficients and original
1,870 forest budgets. Its columns can be remapped into the larger model, where
all quadrilateral coefficients are zero. The existing pair weights and every
face constraint must remain. Additional fixed-class stars and complete face
coverage are still required before a dimension theorem.
