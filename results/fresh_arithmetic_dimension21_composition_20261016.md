# Arithmetic dimension 21: the sharp proof candidate

The internally reviewed computer-assisted proof candidate states:
**a finite-volume arithmetic hyperbolic reflection group has dimension at
most 21**. Borcherds' established example attains dimension 21, so this
candidate is sharp for compact and noncompact arithmetic reflection groups
taken together. It does not determine the largest possible compact
arithmetic dimension. External mathematical validation and priority remain
open.

Generation and fresh saved replay of the final certificate both pass.

The [checker](../scripts/fresh_arithmetic_dimension21_composition_20261016.py)
and [certificate](fresh_arithmetic_dimension21_composition_20261016_bound21.json)
make the field composition explicit. Suppose a compact arithmetic chamber
had dimension above 21. Vinberg's compact theorem gives dimension at most
29; his field restriction applies at every remaining dimension 22–29.
The only possible fields are Q(√2), Q(√5), and Q(cos(2π/7)).
[Vinberg, Theorems 1–2](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=mmo&option_lang=rus&paperid=444&what=fullt).

The complete earlier Q(√2) and cubic-seven candidates give dimension at
most 20. The [complete new Q(√5) argument](fresh_sqrt5_h3_hub_coverage_20261016.md)
gives dimension at most 21. All **24 field/dimension branches** are therefore
excluded. The checker requires explicit complete-coverage metadata and
checks the recursive certificate manifests. The actual field proofs and
independent arithmetic reviews remain named mathematical dependencies.

For a noncompact arithmetic chamber, Esselmann's published bound already
gives dimension at most 21, with dimension 20 excluded. Borcherds' example
attains 21. The new result improves the compact arithmetic side; it does
not lower an already attained noncompact bound.
[Esselmann](https://doi.org/10.1006/jnth.1996.0141),
[Belolipetsky, §§1 and 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.03111).

Every arithmetic lattice generated by reflections is of simplest type, so
this already covers arithmetic first-type reflection groups. Arbitrary
first-type lattices, or infinite-volume reflection groups, are outside the
dimension claim. The unrestricted compact bound 29 and the separate
unrestricted finite-volume candidate 163 are unchanged.
[Vinberg's lemma, restated in Belolipetsky Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.03111).

This is an internal research proof composition, not formal proof-assistant
verification or an external referee decision. No new finite-volume example
is constructed. A lower bound than 21 for all arithmetic reflection groups
is impossible because of the established example; further compact-only or
nonarithmetic improvements remain meaningful.
