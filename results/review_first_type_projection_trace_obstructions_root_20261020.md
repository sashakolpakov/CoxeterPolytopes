For a label l, the intrinsic normal is (m+c*n)/sqrt(1-c^2), c=cos(pi/l).
Its height at the actual facet projection is
(t_m+c*t)/(sqrt(1+t^2)*sqrt(1-c^2)). The extra factor cot(pi/(2l))
cannot be omitted. In the compact (3,3,4) triangle the norm-two Gram
determinants are -2sqrt2 and +2sqrt2 at its two real embeddings, with
positive first two minors. Integrality and these signatures justify
the first-type arithmetic claim. The printed inverse calculation
gives t^2=1/(8+9sqrt2). At the specified tied maximum facet the new
height squared is (sqrt2-1)/3. Its ratio to t^2 is (10-sqrt2)/3>1.
This is a genuine failure of contraction, not an infeasible projection.

For the quadrilateral, the determinant signs -12-10sqrt2 and
-12+10sqrt2 are respectively negative and positive. The four given
root normals have the stated adjacent products, ultraparallel opposite
pairs and a strict feasible timelike vector. They form the compact
arithmetic quadrilateral claimed by the source.

Under the rational trace form, a_i and b_i=sqrt2*a_i have squares4
and8. For an integral K-product A+B*sqrt2, the rational reflection
coefficients are A and B respectively. Thus integrality survives the
lift, but finite volume does not. The vector z=a1+a3=b2+b4 is nonzero
trace-null. Its four zero walls a1,a3,b2,b4 have two mutually orthogonal
affine-A1 blocks of rank one each; its products with the other four
walls are strictly negative. In H5 the horosphere has dimension four,
while this Euclidean reflection system has rank two. Above the finitely
many hemispherical walls not incident to this cusp, its cross-section
is a bounded rectangle times R^2. It has infinite volume. The failure
is the missing cusp saturation, despite integral Coxeter walls.

No local Gram configuration here is advertised as a new compact
example in a larger dimension or as a completed finite-volume lift.
