# A rank-22 counterexample to the rational/projected-K3 outer routing

## Result

Let \(P_{\mathrm {Bor}}\subset\mathbb H^{21}\) be Borcherds' classical
210-facet chamber for

\[
                    L=U\oplus D_{20}(-1).
\]

Then all of the following hold.

1. \(P_{\mathrm {Bor}}\) is noncompact, finite-volume and arithmetic.  Its
   side group is the complete integral reflection group \(R(L)\).
2. The side group is **absolutely reflection-maximal**.  This is proved below
   by an exact classification of \(R(L)\)-stable lattices; Esselmann's global
   reflective-lattice classification is not needed.
3. No direct complex rational full-N\'eron--Severi/full-nef return exists.
4. The exact [weak-log rational compiler](rational_weak_log_finite_iff_compiler.md)
   is empty:

   \[
                         \operatorname {RLogComp}(P_{\mathrm {Bor}})
                         =\varnothing .
   \]

   This is not inferred merely from rank.  Every rational physical
   square-class and every adjunction colouring is exhausted.  The only two
   numerical colourings which survive force a Fano-plane incidence
   configuration of complex points and lines, which is impossible in
   characteristic zero.
5. No full-trace or projected-root **complex K3** eigenslice can return the
   chamber: exact chamber fidelity needs a rank-22 image, whereas a complex
   K3 surface has Picard rank at most 20.

Consequently the proposed implication

```text
raw maximal finite-volume arithmetic chamber
  + no full-trace/projected complex-K3 return
    => direct rational or RLogComp return
```

is false.  Raw rationality, cusps, maximality, canonical colouring, MMP and
section algebra do not make this disjunction exhaustive.  It therefore
cannot, by itself, prove the desired global \(n\leq25\) and finite-chamber
theorem.

This does **not** say that every algebro-geometric carrier is impossible.
Dolgachev--Kondō realize \(L\) as the Picard lattice of the supersingular
Artin-invariant-one K3 surface in characteristic two.  Its 42 short roots are
actual \((-2)\)-curves and its 168 long reflections are Cremona actions.  That
mixed divisor/action realization is a positive fourth-species calibration,
not a literal complex rational or projected-root K3 nef return.

## 1. Raw chamber and the three levels of maximality

The frozen wall packet has rank 22, determinant \(-4\), signature
\((1,21)\), and the primitive wall split

\[
  42\times(-2,\operatorname {div}1)
  \quad+\quad
  168\times(-4,\operatorname {div}2).
\]

Borcherds proves that these 210 walls bound a fundamental chamber for the
full group generated by all integral reflections of \(L\), and that this
group has finite index in \(O^+(L)\).  Thus this is not merely a declared
210-generator host: it is the complete root chamber, of finite arithmetic
covolume.  The archived maximal parabolics show that it has cusps.

It remains important to distinguish this from absolute maximality.  Here the
latter has a short, special-case proof.

### Proposition 1.1 (classification-free absolute maximality)

No discrete reflection group properly contains \(R(L)\).

#### Proof

Suppose

\[
                          R(L)\subseteq\Gamma
\]

with \(\Gamma\) a discrete reflection group.  A discrete overgroup of a
finite-covolume lattice has finite index.  Hence \(\Gamma\) is commensurable
with the arithmetic group \(R(L)\) and acts rationally on the same quadratic
space.  The standard finite-extension construction supplies a full integral
lattice \(M\) preserved by
\(\Gamma\): after passing to a normal finite-index subgroup, take the sum of
the finitely many translates of one invariant lattice.  A harmless common
homothety puts \(M\) in the same rational quadratic space.

The exact wall calculation now replaces any reflective-lattice
classification.

- The 42 short simple roots generate \(L\) integrally; their coordinate
  Smith diagonal is \(1^{22}\).
- Their odd Coxeter graph is connected.  Indeed, its edges are the
  square-minus-two pairs of inner product one, hence Coxeter label three.
  Therefore the 42 short root lines form one \(R(L)\)-conjugacy class.
- Rescale \(M\) so that \(M\cap\mathbf Qr=\mathbf Zr\) on one short-root
  line.  Conjugacy gives the same equality on every short-root line.  Since
  the short roots generate \(L\), this gives \(L\subseteq M\).
- For \(x\in M\) and a short root \(r\),

  \[
                      s_r(x)-x=(x,r)r
                      \in M\cap\mathbf Qr=\mathbf Zr.
  \]

  Thus \((x,r)\in\mathbf Z\) for all short roots.  They generate \(L\), so
  \(M\subseteq L^\vee\).

It remains to enumerate the finite interval

\[
                              L\subseteq M\subseteq L^\vee .       \tag{1.1}
\]

The discriminant module is \(A_L=L^\vee/L\simeq(\mathbf Z/2)^2\).  Exact
reduction of all 210 reflection matrices modulo \(L\) gives the full group

\[
                    \langle s_r:r\text{ a displayed wall}\rangle|_{A_L}
                    =GL(2,\mathbf F_2)\simeq S_3.                  \tag{1.2}
\]

The five subgroups of \(A_L\) have orders \(1,2,2,2,4\); only the trivial
and full subgroups are \(S_3\)-stable.  Therefore (1.1) leaves only
\(M=L\) or \(M=L^\vee\).

Finally, a rational isometry preserves \(L\) if and only if it preserves its
dual.  Hence

\[
                         O(L)=O(L^\vee),\qquad R(L)=R(L^\vee).     \tag{1.3}
\]

Every generating reflection of \(\Gamma\) preserves \(M\), so
\(\Gamma\subseteq R(M)=R(L)\).  The assumed inclusion is therefore equality.
\(\square\)

Esselmann's theorem independently identifies \(L\) and \(L^\vee\), up to
scale and isometry, as the rank-22 reflective endpoint.  Proposition 1.1
does not import that theorem; (1.2) is the complete special-case replacement.

## 2. The rational physical square classes are only \(1\) and \(2\)

Write \(G\) for the frozen rational form and choose the archived primitive
wall vectors \(r_i\).  Their norm magnitudes are 2 and 4.  In any rational
full-rank physical return, one common positive similarity \(c\) identifies
the rational quadratic spaces, while the physical class on the \(i\)-th
oriented line is

\[
                              C_i=\lambda_i r_i,
                              \qquad \lambda_i\in\mathbf Q_{>0}.
\]

Consequently

\[
                \frac{|C_i^2|}{|r_i^2|}=c\lambda_i^2,             \tag{2.1}
\]

so every quotient in (2.1) has the same class \([c]\) in
\(\mathbf Q^*/\mathbf Q^{*2}\).  The physical alphabet is
\(\{|C_i^2|:C_i^2=-1,-2,-4\}=\{1,2,4\}\).  Direct enumeration gives

| raw norm | physical norm | quotient square classes |
|---:|---:|---:|
| \(2\) | \(1,2,4\) | \(2,1,2\) |
| \(4\) | \(1,2,4\) | \(1,2,1\) |

Thus there are exactly two common classes.

### Square class \(1\)

Every one of the 42 short lines must have physical square \(-2\).  Those
short roots span the full rank-22 space, so adjunction

\[
                              K.C=-2-C^2                         \tag{2.2}
\]

forces \(K=0\).  A long line must have physical square \(-1\) or \(-4\),
but (2.2) then demands \(K.C=-1\) or \(2\), respectively.  Both contradict
\(K=0\).  This entire square class is empty before any MMP or nefness test.

### Square class \(2\)

Normalize the physical form to \(2G\).  Every long line then has square
\(-2\), represented by \(r/2\).  The 168 long roots span rank 21.  Their
orthogonal line has the primitive generator

```text
w = (-2,4,2,5,7,6,4,3,1,-5,-4,-7,-11,-9,-8,-6,-5,1,3,2,1,-3),
w^2_G = -6.
```

The 42 short roots split exactly as

\[
                 21\text{ with }(w,r)=-1,
                 \qquad
                 21\text{ with }(w,r)=1.                         \tag{2.3}
\]

Since all long curves are \((-2)\)-curves, (2.2) gives \(K=tw\).  On a
smooth rational surface of Picard rank 22,

\[
                         K^2=10-22=-12.
\]

But the physical form gives \(K^2=2G(tw,tw)=-12t^2\).  Therefore
\(t=\pm1\).  For \(t=1\), adjunction uniquely assigns

- the 21 roots with \((w,r)=-1\) to \((-1)\)-curves \(E_p=r/2\), and
- the 21 roots with \((w,r)=1\) to boundary \((-4)\)-curves \(B_l=r\).

The choice \(t=-1\) simply exchanges the two 21-element parts.  These are
the only two numerical colourings which survive (2.1), adjunction and the
canonical-square identity.

## 3. The surviving colourings force a characteristic-two plane

Within each 21-element part all raw pairings vanish.  Across the two parts
there are 105 pairings equal to one and 336 equal to zero.  In the physical
form,

\[
                         E_p.B_l=G(r_p,r_l)\in\{0,1\}.             \tag{3.1}
\]

Every \(E_p\) meets five \(B_l\)'s and conversely.  Every two \(E\)'s have
exactly one common \(B\), and every two \(B\)'s have exactly one common
\(E\).  This is the \(21_5\) point-line plane of order four; the frozen
incidence is the familiar \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_4)\).

Suppose the curves existed simultaneously on a complex rational surface
\(S\).  Contract the 21 pairwise-disjoint \((-1)\)-curves.  The Picard rank
drops from 22 to one, so the resulting smooth minimal rational surface is
\(\mathbf P^2\).  Each \(B_l\) meets five contracted curves once.  Its image
therefore has square

\[
                              -4+5=1,
\]

and remains an integral rational curve.  It is a projective line.  Thus the
21 exceptional images and 21 boundary images would realize the displayed
point-line configuration over \(\mathbf C\).

The certificate finds the following literal Fano subplane, with numbers
referring to the frozen one-based wall order:

```text
points: 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 22, 33
lines:
   5 : 2,4,6
   7 : 6,8,33
  31 : 4,22,33
  34 : 2,12,33
  40 : 6,12,22
  47 : 2,8,22
  51 : 4,8,12
```

For completeness, use the standard Fano frame

```text
A=2, B=4, C=8, D=6, E=22, F=12, G=33.
```

The seven lines are \(ABD,ACE,BCF,AFG,BEG,CDG,DEF\).  Put the three
noncollinear points \(A,B,C\) at the coordinate points.  The first six
incidences permit normalization

\[
               D=(1,1,0),\qquad E=(1,0,1),\qquad F=(0,1,1).
\]

The last Fano line would require \(D,E,F\) to be collinear, whereas

\[
  \det\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\1&0&1\\0&1&1\end{pmatrix}=-2\ne0
  \quad\text{over }\mathbf C.                                  \tag{3.2}
\]

This contradiction kills the \(t=1\) colouring.  Projective duality kills
the swapped \(t=-1\) colouring.  Therefore every `RLogComp` branch is empty.
Notice where the MMP enters: the obstruction is exactly the simultaneous
prime-divisor locus after the exhaustive contraction of the displayed
exceptional rays.  Base-point-freeness or section algebra cannot repair an
empty incidence locus.

## 4. The complex K3 and direct rational lanes

An exact full-trace or projected-root eigenslice returning a chamber in
\(\mathbb H^{21}\) must contain an injective rank-22 Lorentzian image.  For a
complex K3 surface \(X\),

\[
                   22\le\rho(X)\le20
\]

would be necessary, which is impossible.  In the nonrational full-trace
notation the stronger necessary inequality is

\[
                         [k:\mathbf Q]\,22\le20.
\]

Projection into a larger Picard lattice does not evade the obstruction:
the projected image itself has rank 22, so its ambient N\'eron--Severi
lattice must have rank at least 22.

The direct complex rational branch is already excluded by the proved
[direct full-NS bound](rational_direct_full_ns_n11_dimension_theorem.md)
\(n\le11\).  Its ordinary smooth-rational subcategory is also contained in
the square-class calculation above.  The broader weak-log branch allows
dimension 21 numerically, but Section 3 proves its actual constructible
return stack empty.

## 5. What the characteristic-two K3 does and does not show

The determinant \(-2\) in (3.2) vanishes in characteristic two.  This is not
an accident.  Dolgachev--Kondō construct the unique supersingular K3
surface of Artin invariant one in characteristic two with

\[
                         \operatorname {NS}(X)=U\oplus D_{20}(-1).
\]

Its 42 short roots are smooth rational \((-2)\)-curves in two 21-element
families with the point-line incidence of \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_4)\).  The
168 long square-minus-four reflections are realized by explicit involutive
Cremona transformations.  They are not 168 additional prime \((-2)\)-curve
walls of the K3 nef cone.

Thus the same raw polytope has a genuine positive-characteristic
algebro-geometric **mixed divisor/action** carrier.  The theorem above says
precisely that it lies outside the current complex rational and
full-trace/projected-root complex-K3 compiler species.  It does not assert

- nonexistence of \(P_{\mathrm {Bor}}\),
- nonexistence of every algebraic realization,
- failure of the carrier-relative \(n\le25\) bound, or
- failure of Nikulin's, Vinberg's, Esselmann's, or ABSW's theorems.

What fails is the proposed essential-surjectivity route into the listed
complex species.  Any truly exhaustive outer compiler must add a category
capable of retaining mixed divisor/reflection actions or prove a different
class-wide alternative.
