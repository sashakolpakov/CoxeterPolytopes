# Independent hostile audit: projected-K3 maximal abstract-chamber finiteness

**Verdict (2026-08-11): PASS, conditional and classification-importing.**
The owner theorem is correct in its declared carrier-relative scope.  For a
finite-sided finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chamber, a preserved native
arithmetic lattice is reflective; Nikulin's fixed-field/fixed-rank theorem
then leaves finitely many native lattices up to similarity; reflection
maximality identifies the facet group with the complete reflection group of
that lattice; and the chambers of the complete mirror arrangement form one
orbit.  Projected K3 scale, complement, denominator, and glue escape therefore
do not obstruct finiteness of **unmarked normalized abstract chambers**.

This does not prove a universal projected-K3 carrier theorem, does not cover a
chamber whose return omits the full native trace/admissibility package, and is
not an AG-only proof of ABSW: the global finiteness step is precisely Nikulin
1982, Theorem 5.2.1.

## 2. Exact K3 and field scope

The phrase “exact projected complex-K3 eigenslice return containing the full
native trace module” must be read in the strong sense used by the dependency
theorems:

1. a rank-\(m=n+1\) module over an order in the totally real native field;
2. an admissible field form, Lorentzian at one real place and definite at all
   others;
3. the full integral trace module embedded in the K3 Neron--Severi lattice;
4. the returned reflection operators and complete facet packet defined over
   that module.

Under those hypotheses the trace has integral rank \(dm\), so

\[
                         dm\le \rho(X)\le20.
\]

Consequently \(n\le19\); for \(d>1\), \(m\le10\) and \(n\le9\).  The exact
noncompact cusp theorem applies because the complete cusp packet acts on one
admissible module, and gives \(k=\mathbf Q\).  The compact full-trace theorem
applies because the exact facet operators preserve the order-module, and gives

\[
 |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|\le(32\cdot14^{10})^{30}.
\]

Thus the owner really obtains a finite set of pairs \((k,m)\).  A merely
semialgebraic eigenslice inside \(\operatorname {NS}(X)_\mathbf R\), with no
full native trace/admissibility or exact operator return, would not satisfy
this gate.  The theorem does not claim otherwise.  A rational proper
projector slice is harmless: \(d=1\), and the full native rank is still at
most twenty.

The physical projected-root estimate \(0\le\delta_i.\delta_j\le13\) is
correct whenever every selected eigenslice facet is exposed by an effective
irreducible K3 \((-2)\)-curve.  It is not used in the chamber-finiteness
implication.  In particular, the theorem remains valid if that local lemma is
deleted; the global completion comes from Nikulin's connected frame, not from
the projected physical star.

## 3. The reflective-lattice bridge

Fix a chamber \(P\).  Arithmeticity supplies a native \(k\)-space \((V,h)\)
and a lattice preserved by its facet reflections.  If the initial module is
over a suborder, its \(\mathcal O_k\)-span remains invariant because every
reflection is \(k\)-linear.  Multiplication of \(h\) by one positive rational
integer clears all remaining fractional ideals and changes no reflection.
The resulting finite torsion-free \(\mathcal O_k\)-module is therefore a
Nikulin lattice; freeness is unnecessary.

Let \(O^+(L)\) preserve the chosen cone and let \(R(L)\) be generated by all
integral native hyperplane reflections.  The primary source identifies these
geometric integral reflections with its lattice reflections, so the two
definitions of \(R(L)\) agree.  We have

\[
                         W(P)\le R(L)\le O^+(L).
\]

Both endpoint groups \(W(P)\) and \(O^+(L)\) are lattices in the same
hyperbolic isometry group.  The standard covolume/index formula gives

\[
 [O^+(L):W(P)]<\infty,
 \qquad [O^+(L):R(L)]<\infty.
\]

One small bridge is implicit in the owner report but is valid:
\(O^+(L)\) has index at most two in \(O(L)\), by its action on the two
components of the positive cone.  Hence

\[
 [O(L):R(L)]
 =[O(L):O^+(L)]\,[O^+(L):R(L)]<\infty,
\]

which is the literal hypothesis of Nikulin 5.2.1.  No assumption that a K3
root lies in \(L\) enters this step.

## 4. Similarity, maximality, and the chamber orbit

Nikulin now gives finitely many similarity classes \([L]\) for each fixed
\((k,m)\).  Similarity is the correct quotient.  Multiplying the form by a
common \(a\in k^\times\), positive at the distinguished place, cancels from

\[
                 s_r(x)=x-{2h(x,r)\over h(r,r)}r
\]

and from every normalized Coxeter Gram entry.  Module isomorphism conjugates
the same reflection representation.  Projective-module and order variation
has not been ignored: it is already finite inside Nikulin's similarity
classes.

The maximality step is also exact.  The group \(R(L)\) is a discrete
reflection overgroup of \(W(P)\).  If maximality is among all discrete
reflection groups, equality follows immediately.  If maximality is only
among arithmetic reflection groups, equality still follows because the
finite-index group \(R(L)\) is commensurable with \(O^+(L)\), hence
arithmetic.  Thus, in the hyperbolic isometry image,

\[
                              W(P)=R(L).
\]

Finally, the mirror arrangement of a discrete reflection group is locally
finite.  A generic compact path between regular points crosses finitely many
mirrors.  Reflection across each crossed mirror moves the current chamber to
the adjacent one, so a finite gallery carries any chamber to any other.
Therefore the complete arrangement has one \(R(L)\)-orbit of chambers.  A
native similarity host contributes at most one unmarked normalized abstract
chamber class.  The finite union over the finite set of \((k,m)\) proves the
owner theorem and its finite-pair corollary.

This is a chamber-orbit assertion, not a claim that all markings or all
ambient K3 embeddings are finite.  Central linear kernels and the choice of a
cone component do not create new projective hyperbolic chambers.

## 5. Hostile controls and the exact frontier

The golden \((2,5,5)\) family is the right countercontrol.  Its trace form is
\(sQ\), so all members have one native similarity host and one normalized
triangle, while its ambient discriminant and glue index diverge.  It refutes
ambient Neron--Severi-host finiteness, not abstract-chamber finiteness.

The redoubling family is the complementary control.  It keeps rank, field,
and divisor lattice fixed while producing chambers with \(N+12\) facets.
Those facet groups are proper finite-index reflection subgroups, so it shows
that reflection maximality cannot be removed from the finite-pair principle.

The connected-frame warning is also correct.  A minimal-aperture physical or
Cartan star can span the Lorentz space while its nonzero-pairing graph is
disconnected; the compact right-angled pentagon supplies the rank-three
example.  Its bounded local data do not control the relative arithmetic scale
of the orthogonal blocks.  Without Nikulin 5.2.1, the project has no proof that
finite \((k,n)\) plus that local packet yields finitely many complete native
similarity hosts.

Accordingly the logical endpoint is:

> finite exact full-trace K3 field/rank data plus raw reflection maximality,
> **using Nikulin's fixed-data theorem**, imply finitely many normalized
> chambers in the returned class.

It is not:

- a universal complex-K3 return for all maximal arithmetic chambers;
- a proof in dimensions above the complex-K3 ceiling \(19\);
- a proof that projected ambient K3 hosts are finite; or
- an AG-only replacement for Nikulin/ABSW.
