# Exact rational-surface carrier obstruction for P18 and P19

## Result

> **Theorem.** Let $n=18$ or $19$.  There is no smooth projective
> rational surface over a field of characteristic zero whose complete Mori
> cone is generated by irreducible curves having the facet intersection
> matrix of the classical odd-unimodular chamber $P_n$.

Equivalently, the Vinberg--Kaplinskaja $P_{18}$ and $P_{19}$ chambers do
not occur as complete nef chambers of smooth rational surfaces through
these divisor classes.

This is a **carrier nonexistence theorem**, not an abstract-polytope
nonexistence theorem.  Both finite-volume noncompact hyperbolic Coxeter
polytopes are classical and remain perfectly valid.  No new abstract
hyperbolic polytope is claimed here.

## Exact census

| chamber | facets | initial numerical $(-1)$ facets | rank-two states | $mathbb P^2$ endpoints | canonical classes | weighted-diagram group |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $P_{18}$ | 37 | 15 | $24\mathbb F_1+24\mathbb F_2$ | 24 | 24 | 24 |
| $P_{19}$ | 50 | 5 | $120\mathbb F_1+120\mathbb F_2$ | 120 | 120 | 120 |

The state counts, from the original Picard rank down to rank one, are

```text
P18: 1,15,6,12,12,24,24,24,24,48,24,24,24,24,24,24,24,48,24
P19: 1,5,75,30,60,60,120,120,120,120,240,120,120,120,120,120,120,120,240,120
```

The number of exceptional bases equals the number of states at every
level.  At the first step for $P_{18}$, the 15 numerical square-minus-one
facets give 15 formal branches, but 12 have no next square-minus-one image
and cannot be an MMP of a rational surface.  Only facet labels 18, 25, and
26 reach a minimal model, each through eight endpoints.  For $P_{19}$,
the five initial labels 19, 27, 31, 32, and 35 each reach 24 endpoints.

For both chambers, reconstructing $K_X$ at every $mathbb P^2$ endpoint,

\[
K_X=-3H+\sum e_j,
\]

and independently at every minimal $\mathbb F_2$ endpoint,

\[
K_X=-2s-4f+\sum e_j,
\]

gives exactly the same set.  The resulting 24 and 120 classes are,
respectively, the complete orbits of

\[
(-3,1^{18})\quad\text{and}\quad(-3,1^{19})
\]

under the full weighted Coxeter-diagram automorphism groups.  Every such
diagram automorphism is checked to lift to an integral unimodular isometry
of $I_{1,n}$ preserving the full ordered facet set.

## Why the finite contraction search is complete

Suppose the complete curve cone is

\[
\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)=\operatorname{Cone}(D_1,\ldots,D_m).
\]

On any nonminimal smooth rational surface, a $K_X$-negative extremal ray
is generated by a smooth rational $(-1)$-curve $e$.  Completeness of the
displayed cone puts at least one current nonzero image of a $D_i$ on that
ray.  It is actually the class $e$, not a proper multiple: if a distinct
irreducible image had class $q e$, then its intersection with $e$ would be
$-q<0$, impossible for distinct irreducible curves on a smooth surface.

After contracting $e$, each current class is represented in $e^\perp$ by

\[
f^*f_*D=D+(D\mathbin{\cdot}e)e.
\]

Consequently every possible rational-surface MMP is a branch of the exact
finite state enumeration.  Numerical square-minus-one images that are not
actually rational merely add harmless formal branches; a branch with no
next such image cannot represent a nonminimal rational surface.

At rank two, exact section--fiber decomposition shows that every surviving
cone is either $\mathbb F_1$ or $\mathbb F_2$.  The former continues to a
$\mathbb P^2$ endpoint and the latter is minimal.  Thus the endpoint list
misses neither a plane nor a ruled minimal model.

## From the canonical orbit to the Abel contradiction

Conjugate a putative marking by the certified diagram isometry so that
$K_X=(-3,1^n)$.  Adjunction then gives arithmetic genus zero to all $n$
facets in the standard $B_n$ chain

\[
E_n,\ E_{n-1}-E_n,\ldots,E_1-E_2.
\]

The corresponding irreducible curves are smooth rational.  Sequential
Castelnuovo contraction produces a smooth rational rank-one surface,
hence $\mathbb P^2$, and reversing the contractions recovers the standard
infinitely-near chain.

Four required facets now have plane classes

\[
L=H-E_1-E_2-E_3,
\qquad C=3H-E_1-\cdots-E_{11},
\]

\[
Q=4H-2E_1-E_2-\cdots-E_{15},
\qquad A=4H-E_1-\cdots-E_{18}.
\]

Their exact intersections are

\[
L\cdot C=C\cdot Q=Q\cdot A=0,
\qquad C\cdot A=1.
\]

Write $p$ for the first blow-up centre.  Since $L\cdot C=0$, plane
Bézout gives

\[
L|_C=3p,\qquad \mathcal O_C(1)=\mathcal O_C(3p).
\]

The common multiplicities of $C$ and $Q$ through the eleventh centre
already contribute $2+10=12=\deg C\deg Q$.  Hence the twelfth centre,
which lies on $Q$, is away from $C$.  The quartic $A$ follows the chain
and is smooth at $p$, so

\[
A|_C=11p+r
\]

for an effective Cartier divisor $r$ of degree one away from $p$.  On the
other hand,

\[
\mathcal O_C(A)=\mathcal O_C(4)=\mathcal O_C(12p),
\]

and therefore $\mathcal O_C(r)=\mathcal O_C(p)$.  The degree-one Abel map
of an integral plane cubic is injective on its smooth locus, forcing
$r=p$, a contradiction.
