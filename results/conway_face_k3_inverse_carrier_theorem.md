## Result

The Coxeter faces used in Allcock's construction admit a uniform
algebro-geometric carrier.  The input is entirely hyperbolic and
lattice-theoretic: a spherical face of Conway's chamber in
$II_{25,1}$.  The output is a positive-dimensional family of complex K3
surfaces except at the two rank-20 endpoints.

The underlying orthogonal-complement method is classical, not a new
invention here.  Conway--Sloane use root complements in $II_{25,1}$ to study
Lorentzian lattices, and Borcherds explicitly explains Kondō's use of such
complements as Picard lattices of K3 surfaces.  The contribution asserted in
this note is the uniform transfer to Allcock's complete face ladder, with an
exact primitive-embedding gate, a certified row-by-row census, the dimension
identity below, and simultaneous propagation to finite redoublings.  Any
worldwide novelty claim remains subject to a broader literature audit.

Let

\[
 L=II_{25,1}
\]

with the sign convention in which Conway roots have square $2$.  Let
$\sigma$ be a spherical subdiagram of Conway's simple-root diagram, let
$R_\sigma$ be the positive-definite root lattice generated by its nodes,
and put

\[
 S=(R_\sigma\otimes\mathbf Q)\cap L,
 \qquad K=S^\perp_L,
 \qquad N=K(-1).
 \tag{1}
\]

Write $s=\operatorname{rk}S$ and $n=25-s$.  Suppose:

1. the face $P_\sigma$ of Conway's chamber is a finite-sided Coxeter
   polyhedron in $\mathbb H^n$;
2. $6\le s\le23$, so $2\le n\le19$; and
3. either

   \[
    \ell(A_S)\le s-6,
    \tag{2}
   \]

   or there is an explicit even lattice $T$ of signature $(2,s-6)$ (hence
   rank $s-4$) with $q_T\cong-q_N$.

Then $N$ embeds primitively in the K3 lattice, and there is a projective
complex K3 surface $X$ with

\[
 \operatorname{NS}(X)\cong N.
 \tag{3}
\]

Every wall of $P_\sigma$ is the orthogonal hyperplane of an integral divisor
class of $X$ after positive primitive rescaling.  Moreover, the marking can
be chosen so that the imported Coxeter cone contains an ample class.  Thus
$P_\sigma$ is an exact **full-Picard ample-intersection divisor-hyperplane
carrier**, not merely an abstract Gram diagram.  The induced reflections are
rational isometries of $K_\mathbf Q$; unless integrality is checked
separately, they are not asserted to preserve $K$ or $\operatorname{NS}(X)$.

For every primitive-embedding orbit, the exact-Picard carrier locus has
complex dimension

\[
 20-\operatorname{rk}N=20-(26-s)=s-6=19-n.
 \tag{4}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{
 \dim_{\mathbf C}(\text{K3 carrier moduli})
 +\dim_{\mathbf R}(\text{hyperbolic polyhedron})=19.}
 \tag{5}
\]

Equation (5) is a rank identity, not a claim that the forgetful map from
periods to abstract polytopes is injective.  The entire period family carries
the same marked divisor arrangement.

## Proof of the K3 embedding

The lattice $K$ is primitive in $L$: if $mx\in K$ for $x\in L$, then
$(mx,S)=0$, hence $(x,S)=0$ and $x\in K$.  It has signature $(n,1)$, so
$N=K(-1)$ is even of signature $(1,n)$ and rank $n+1=26-s\le20$.

Because $S$ and $K$ are primitive orthogonal complements in the even
unimodular lattice $L$,

\[
 q_K\cong-q_S,
 \qquad q_N\cong q_S,
 \qquad \ell(A_N)=\ell(A_S).
 \tag{6}
\]

A primitive embedding $N\hookrightarrow\Lambda_{K3}$ needs a complement of
signature

\[
 (3,19)-(1,25-s)=(2,s-6)
 \tag{7}
\]

and rank $s-4$.  Under (2),

\[
 s-4\ge\ell(A_N)+2,
\]

so Nikulin's stable-range primitive-embedding theorem supplies the
embedding.  The alternative in item 3 is precisely the exact
discriminant-form gluing criterion outside stable range.  K3 period
surjectivity, with a period avoiding all extra Noether--Lefschetz
hyperplanes, gives (3) and the dimension formula (4).

## Why the walls are divisor hyperplanes

Let $r_A\in L$ be a Conway root whose wall cuts a facet of $P_\sigma$.
The normal to the induced face wall is the orthogonal projection

\[
 p_A=\operatorname{pr}_{K_\mathbf Q}(r_A).
 \tag{8}
\]

The projection is rational because $S$ is a nondegenerate integral lattice.
Also

\[
 (p_A,x)=(r_A,x)\in\mathbf Z
 \qquad(x\in K),
\]

so $p_A\in K^\vee$.  Multiplying by its exact denominator and then dividing
by the content gives a primitive vector $d_A\in K$ with
$d_A^\perp=p_A^\perp$.  After sign reversal and the marking (3), $d_A$ is an
integral algebraic divisor class.  Positive rescaling does not change the
oriented halfspace, so all wall incidences and Coxeter angles are literally
those of $P_\sigma$.

Choose a positive point $h$ in the interior of the imported cone which is
regular for the complete $(-2)$-root arrangement of $N$.  K3
Weyl-chamber transitivity allows the Neron--Severi marking to send the ample
chamber to the root chamber containing $h$.  An integral rational point
sufficiently near $h$ is then an ample divisor and remains strict on every
imported wall.  This proves ample intersection.

Nothing here proves that the $d_A$ are effective or irreducible curves, that
their rational reflections preserve the integral Picard lattice, or that the
imported cone is the full nef cone.  In particular, a projected wall of
square less than $-2$ is only an integral divisor hyperplane until those
additional gates are proved.

Here $S$ may be a proper even saturation of the displayed root span.  This

\[
 \ell(A_S)\le s-6.
\]

At the two $s=6$ endpoints there is no nonzero isotropic discriminant class,
so the displayed $E_6$ or $D_6$ root lattice is already primitive in $L$.
The exact positive-definite K3 complements are

\[
 T_{E_6}=A_2,
 \qquad T_{D_6}=A_1^2,
 \tag{9}
\]

whose discriminant forms are respectively $-q_{E_6}$ and $-q_{D_6}$.
Thus the rank-20 endpoints pass the non-stable local gate as well.

Indefinite-lattice uniqueness in this stable genus identifies their Picard
lattices explicitly:

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 N_{E_6}&\cong
 U\oplus E_8(-1)^2\oplus A_2(-1), &&|\det N_{E_6}|=3,\\
 N_{D_6}&\cong
 U\oplus E_8(-1)^2\oplus A_1(-1)^2, &&|\det N_{D_6}|=4.
 \end{aligned}

\]

These are not newly discovered K3 lattices.  Borcherds's Example 5.3
explicitly takes $J=E_6$ in the Conway diagram and $L=J^\perp$ to recover
Vinberg's determinant-three K3 Picard lattice; the following paragraph
performs the determinant-four calculation with the $D_6/D_7$ category.
Therefore the zero-dimensional $H^{19}$ endpoints of the ladder recover
Vinberg's two classical rank-20 K3 cases.  This makes the endpoint lineage
exact: the continuous carrier moduli shrink to the already known singular
K3 surfaces rather than to anonymous period points.

Allcock proves that the stated faces are finite-volume Coxeter polyhedra;
the $D_6+D_6+D_4$ row is the dimension-nine face used in the stronger
subtree-growth construction.  Therefore every published face in this ladder
has a K3 full-Picard ample-intersection divisor-hyperplane carrier.

## Redoubling remains on one K3 carrier

Suppose $P_\sigma$ or one of its finite doubles has a finite set of pairwise
disjoint doubling walls.  All initial face normals lie in $K_\mathbf Q$.
Reflection in a rational normal has a rational matrix on $K_\mathbf Q$, so
every boundary wall obtained from a finite subtree of the doubling-wall
Cayley graph again has a rational normal in $K_\mathbf Q$.  Primitive
integral rescaling puts every such normal in the same lattice $K$.

Translate a finite subtree to the congruent rooted representative containing
the original chamber.  Its union contains the chosen ample point $h$, and
Allcock's finite-subtree theorem says that the union is again a finite-volume
Coxeter polyhedron.  Consequently these rooted representatives of every
finite-subtree descendant are carried, simultaneously, by the same marked K3
period family.  This is the precise same-$X$ ample-intersection assertion; it
does not claim that the translating isometry acts on $X$.  No surface
automorphism or integral action of the reflection group is needed for this
weak divisor-hyperplane statement.

For the $D_6+D_6$ face in $\mathbb H^{13}$ the stronger integral result is
available: its complement is

\[
 K\cong U\oplus D_6\oplus D_6,
 \qquad
 N\cong U\oplus D_6(-1)\oplus D_6(-1),
\]

and all 21 facet reflections preserve $K$.  Hence Allcock's $Q$ and its
finite-subtree descendants share not only rational divisor hyperplanes but
one integral reflection lattice.  The exact 21-wall certificate and the
equation-level $2I_2^*+8I_1$ K3 handoff are recorded separately.

## Logical scope

This is a forward universality theorem for the stated Conway-face class:
every polyhedron in that class automatically has a K3 divisor carrier.  It
is also reversible for marked data: a K3 period with exact Picard lattice
$N$, the imported divisor normals, and the chosen ample orientation
reconstructs the same Coxeter halfspace system.

It does **not** imply that every abstract Coxeter polytope is a Conway face,
that every divisor wall is effective, or that carrier classification alone
decides finite volume.  Finite volume here comes from Allcock's independent
hyperbolic theorem.  Outside this class the literal Vinberg endpoint and
parabolic-neighborhood gates remain mandatory.
