## Quantifiers and branch composition

The main paper's `prop:inherited` and `sec:fields` concern compact
arithmetic reflection **lattices** and their actual cyclic ground fields.
They do not assume that a displayed entry overfield is the ground field.
Compactness supplies finite corners, simplicity, and exclusion of positive
affine principal sets throughout the companion arguments.

The published field restriction starting at dimension22 leaves the three
nonrational fields `Q(sqrt2)`, `Q(sqrt5)`, and `K7`; the rational possibility
is excluded separately. Thus the companion's finite branch domain really
is eight dimensions `22,...,29` times three nonrational fields: **24
branches**, not 24 fields or a list including the rational branch. The
published compact ceiling29 excludes all larger dimensions. The golden
rules have distinct checked payments and forest bounds at the different
dimensions; the rank22 rule is not extrapolated.

At dimension21 the main paper instead uses the thirteen-field list valid
from14. Its partition is one rational field, two prior nongolden fields
(`Q(sqrt2)`, `K7`), eight distinguished-bond fields, the exact biquadratic
field `Q(sqrt2,sqrt5)`, and the golden field. This gives twelve exclusions
before the paper's new golden step. The dimension22 restriction is not
applied at21. The published ranges/list were checked against Nikulin's
corrected paper, Introduction and Section3.2, formula(5):
[primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0708.3991v2).

For the rational branch, an arithmetic lattice generated by reflections is
of simplest type; this is an imported theorem, not an extra restriction
on the manuscript's chambers. Meyer isotropy and the arithmetic compactness
criterion then exclude the rational form in the ranks at issue. The
reflection-specific simplest-type statement is explicitly recorded in
[Belolipetsky, Lemma2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.03111).

by nonsingularity of that principal matrix, not a blanket assertion that
every conjugate full-star Gram is positive definite.
