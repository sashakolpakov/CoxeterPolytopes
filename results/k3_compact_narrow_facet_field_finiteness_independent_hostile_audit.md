## 1. The narrow star really spans

Choose a facet \(E\) of minimal visual aperture.  After scaling two wall
normals to square \(2\), write

\[
 b(F,G)=\frac{2|h(f,g)|}{\sqrt{h(f,f)h(g,g)}}.
\]

The elementary visual-cone formula and

\[
 \frac{\sin((a+b)/2)}{\sin(a/2)}<2
 \qquad(0\le b\le a<\pi)
\]

give \(b(F,G)<4\cdot2\cdot2-2=14\) whenever both closed
facets meet \(E\).  A wall meeting \(E\) itself has the sharper Coxeter
bound \(b\le2\).  Hence every pair in the star used below has absolute
normalized pairing below \(14\).

The spanning step is not an incidence slogan.  Let \(e\) be a normal to
\(E\), and project the other star normals to \(e^\perp\).  At a vertex
\(x\in E\), the \(m-2\) incident side normals span

\[
 x^\perp\cap e^\perp.
\]

Because \(E\) is compact and positive-dimensional, some side of \(E\) does
not contain \(x\).  Its projected normal pairs nontrivially with \(x\), so it
lies outside that \((m-2)\)-plane.  The projected side normals therefore span
all of \(e^\perp\), and adjoining \(e\) spans \(V\).  This includes the
\(n=2\) case: the two endpoints of the compact segment \(E\) supply the two
projected directions.  Real spanning by \(k\)-vectors is \(k\)-spanning, so
one may select \(m\) actual wall normals forming a \(k\)-basis.

The intrinsic facet \(E\) need only be a compact convex polytope.  No claim
that its projected side angles form another Coxeter chamber is used.

## 2. The Lorentz component inherits all of \(k\)

The Gram matrix of the selected basis is nonsingular of signature
\((m-1,1)\).  Splitting its nonzero-pairing graph into components makes the
Gram matrix block diagonal.  Exactly one block \(I\) is Lorentzian; every
other block is positive definite.

For \(i,j\in I\), put

\[
 a_{ij}=\frac{2h(r_i,r_j)}{h(r_i,r_i)},
\]

and let \(k_I\) be the field generated by all directed cyclic products in
this connected block.  Suppose \(k_I\subsetneq k\).  Total reality and
separability give a nonidentity real embedding

\[
 \sigma:k\hookrightarrow\mathbf R
\]

which fixes the distinguished copy of \(k_I\).  On every nonzero directed
edge define \(q_{ij}=\sigma(a_{ij})/a_{ij}\).  Fixed two-cycles give
\(q_{ij}q_{ji}=1\), and fixed longer cycles give trivial holonomy.  Choosing
a spanning tree therefore produces nonzero real \(\lambda_i\) such that

\[
 q_{ij}=\lambda_j/\lambda_i,
 \qquad
 A_I^\sigma=D^{-1}A_ID.
\tag{2.1}
\]

This argument also covers a tree block: its two-cycles are exactly what is
needed to extend the diagonal similarity along the tree.

At every real place the root squares are positive.  Thus \(A_I\) is similar
to the symmetric matrix obtained by positive normalization of its Gram
matrix, and the signs of its eigenvalues are the Gram inertia.  Equation
(2.1) would preserve the distinguished block's negative eigenvalue.  But
admissibility makes \(h^\sigma\) positive definite, so the conjugate block
has only positive eigenvalues.  This contradiction proves

\[
                              k_I=k.                     \tag{2.2}
\]

This is stronger than assuming that \(k\) was already named minimally: an
exact admissible \(k\)-form with the returned \(k\)-linear reflections
cannot be a gratuitous proper scalar extension of its cyclic field.

## 3. Integral cycles and their height

For a facet reflection \(s_i\), set \(T_i=1-s_i\).  It is a rank-one
endomorphism of the finite projective \(\mathcal O\)-module.  Rank-one
calculus gives

\[
 \operatorname {Tr}_{\mathcal O}
 (T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_\ell})
 =a_{i_1i_2}\cdots a_{i_\ell i_1}\in\mathcal O.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Hence no individual Cartan entry, primitive normal, or free-module basis is
needed for cyclic integrality.  Cutting a closed walk at repeated vertices
reduces the cyclic field to simple cycles; chord cutting may further reduce
to pair cycles and chordless simple cycles.  Every required cycle uses at
most \(|I|\le m\) basis nodes.

Around a cycle \(C\), the root-length factors cancel, so

\[
 |\tau(c(C))|
 =\prod_{(i,j)\in C}
 \frac{2|\tau h(r_i,r_j)|}
 {\sqrt{\tau h(r_i,r_i)\,\tau h(r_j,r_j)}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

At the distinguished place every factor is below \(14\); at a definite
place strict Cauchy--Schwarz bounds it by \(2\).  Therefore every integral
generator selected from the block satisfies

\[
                         |\tau(c)|<14^m                 \tag{3.3}
\]

at every real embedding.

## 4. Rank, primitive element, and discriminant

The primitive full trace has rank \(dm\) inside the K3 Picard lattice, so

\[
 dm\le20.
\]

For \(d>1\) and \(m\ge3\), this gives \(d\le6\) and \(m\le10\).  A minimal
field-generating sequence of the integral cycles has at most two elements:
three strict field adjunctions would have total degree at least \(2^3=8\).

If \(x,y\) are needed, each unordered pair of the at most six embeddings
forbids at most one coefficient \(q\) for which the two images of \(x+qy\)

\[
 |\tau(\theta)|\le16H,
 \qquad
 |\tau(\theta)-\tau'(\theta)|\le32H.

The Vandermonde formula and
\(\operatorname {Disc}k\mid\operatorname {Disc}\mathbf Z[\theta]\)
give (0.1).  This is a genuine discriminant bound, not merely a degree
bound, and hence yields a finite effective field set.
