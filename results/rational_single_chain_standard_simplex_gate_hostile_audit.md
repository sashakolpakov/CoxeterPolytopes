# Hostile audit of the marked minimal-base single-chain simplex gate

## Verdict

**PASS.**  The result in the
[single-chain gate](rational_single_chain_standard_simplex_gate.md) is an
exact no-go for one proposed depth argument, with the scope stated in the
note.  The marked standard simplex has finite volume exactly for
\(m\leq9\).  A full-Neron--Severi integral smooth rational \((-4)\)-wall
which cuts one of its old tail rays is necessarily doubled and has no later
exceptional support.  The numerical wall \(2v_{10}\) consequently cuts all
old tail rays, but the cone obtained by adding its mirror has finite volume
only for \(m=10,11\); at \(m=12\) the mixed ray \(v_1+2v_{12}\) is already
hyperideal.

The new \(\mathbb F_2\) companion also passes after one geometric-scope
repair.  Its visible threshold is \(m=8\); the numerical doubled wall
\(B_9=2u_9\) repairs exactly \(m=9,10\); and
\(f+2u_{11}\) is the first mixed hyperideal ray, of square \(-4\).  The
second blowup in the displayed physical frame must be the fibre-following
free infinitely-near point where the strict fibre meets the first
exceptional curve, rather than a generic free point away from the fibre.
It is not satellite: only one component through the centre is exceptional.
The producer report and certificate marking now say this explicitly.

This does not prove a chain-depth bound, produce an effective doubled wall,
or construct an unbounded family of geometric carriers.  The independent
hostile tests use only the diagonal plane intersection form; they do not
import the producer certifier or any root enumerator.

## 1. The standard-frame calculation is exact and properly scoped

The roots

\[
 \alpha_0=H-e_1-e_2-e_3,\qquad
 \alpha_i=e_i-e_{i+1},\qquad \alpha_m=e_m
\]

form an integral basis: recover the \(e_i\) backwards from \(e_m\), then
recover \(H\) from \(\alpha_0+e_1+e_2+e_3\).  Direct pairing with

\[
 v_0=H,\qquad
 v_i=\min(i,3)H-e_1-\cdots-e_i
\]

gives \(v_i.\alpha_j=\delta_{ij}\).  Therefore

\[
 v_0^2=1,\qquad v_i^2=\min(i,3)^2-i,
\]

and the inverse diagonal is

\[
                   1,0,2,6,5,4,3,2,1,0,-1,-2,\ldots .
\]

The chamber cone is generated by these dual rays.  They all have positive
\(H\)-coordinate, so every nonspacelike ray lies on the same closed sheet of
the positive cone.  A finite-sided pointed hyperbolic cone has finite volume
exactly when all of its projective vertices are finite or ideal.  Here that
is exactly \(v_i^2\geq0\) for all \(i\), hence exactly \(m\leq9\).

This is a theorem about the marked standard frame.  A general curvilinear or
satellite cluster need not contain the line wall \(\alpha_0\), and
reflection maximality does not manufacture it.  The producer note states
both restrictions explicitly.

## 2. The tail genus and physical adjunction identities survive

For \(j\geq10\),

\[
 v_j=3H-e_1-\cdots-e_j,\qquad
 v_j^2=9-j,\qquad K.v_j=j-9.
\]

Thus \(1+(v_j^2+K.v_j)/2=1\).  This is a numerical arithmetic-genus
identity; it does not assert that \(v_j\) is effective.

For a nonexceptional irreducible curve in the declared free-chain frame,
write \(C=dH-\sum_i\mu_i e_i\) with \(\mu_i\geq0\).  Since

\[
                     v_j=-K+\sum_{i>j}e_i,
\]

one has

\[
 C.v_j=(-K).C+\sum_{i>j}\mu_i.
\]

If \(C\) is smooth rational, adjunction changes this into

\[
                  C.v_j=C^2+2+\sum_{i>j}\mu_i.
\]

Consequently \((-1)\)- and \((-2)\)-curves cannot cut an old escape, and a
\((-4)\)-curve can do so only when \(\sum_{i>j}\mu_i\leq1\).  The
nonnegativity of the multiplicities is why the conclusion is limited to
plane strict transforms in the exact free-chain frame.

## 3. Full-NS doubling and later support are not optional

Let \(L=I_{1,m}\).  For \(C^2=-4\), reflection in its mirror is

\[
                         s_C(x)=x+{x.C\over2}C.
\]

Write \(C=dr\) with \(r\) primitive.  In a unimodular lattice a primitive
vector has divisibility one.  If \(d\) were odd, choose \(x\) with
\(x.r=1\); the coefficient \(d^2/2\) in
\(s_C(x)-x=(d^2/2)r\) would not be integral.  Hence \(d\) is even, and
\(C^2=-4\) forces

\[
                              C=2r,\qquad r^2=-1.
\]

Every plane multiplicity \(\mu_i\) is therefore even.  Combining evenness,
nonnegativity, and \(\sum_{i>j}\mu_i\leq1\) forces the tail sum to vanish.
It follows that \(C.v_{j'}=-2\) for every \(j'\geq j\).  This proves the
claimed support theorem and explains why checking the original rays one at
a time cannot bound depth.

On an adapted half-boundary host, unimodularity of \(L\) does not impose
this evenness on every physical wall.  Only the inequality
\(\sum_{i>j}\mu_i\leq1\) survives.  Even there a cutting wall has at most
one later simple base point, so it still pairs negatively with every later
\(v_{j'}\).  The producer correctly confines the doubling conclusion to the
direct full-NS lane.

## 4. The one-wall extreme rays are exhaustive

The numerical class

\[
                       B_{10}=2v_{10}
\]

has square \(-4\), canonical pairing \(2\), arithmetic genus zero, and
pairing \(-2\) with every \(v_j\), \(j\geq10\).  These are numerical tests,
not an effectivity theorem.

Write \(x=\sum_i t_i v_i\) with \(t_i\geq0\).  Adding the mirror
\(v_{10}^{\perp}\), oriented by \(x.v_{10}\geq0\), adds one inequality

\[
 \sum_i p_i t_i\geq0,
 \qquad
 (p_0,\ldots,p_m)=(3,2,4,6,5,4,3,2,1,0,-1,\ldots,-1).
\]

An extreme ray not on the new equality is a surviving coordinate ray with
\(p_i\geq0\).  On the equality, its support must contain exactly one
positive and one negative coefficient: support of size \(k\) leaves a
\((k-1)\)-dimensional face, so \(k=2\) is necessary and sufficient.
Because every negative coefficient is \(-1\), the remaining rays are
exactly

\[
                         v_i+p_i v_j
        \qquad(p_i>0,\ j\geq10).
\]

For these indices, \(v_i.v_j=p_i\), and therefore

\[
                    (v_i+p_i v_j)^2
                    =v_i^2+p_i^2(11-j).
\]

All surviving and mixed rays are nonspacelike for \(m=10,11\).  At
\(m=12\), take \(i=1\): since \(v_1^2=0\) and \(p_1=2\),

\[
                         (v_1+2v_{12})^2=-4.
\]

The same negative ray persists in every later rank.  Thus the augmented
cone has finite volume exactly for \(m=10,11\).  The old diagonal escape has
been removed, but a mixed escape immediately replaces it.

## 5. The \(\mathbb F_2\) companion is exact

Use the basis \((s,f,e_1,\ldots,e_m)\), with

\[
 s^2=-2,\qquad f^2=0,\qquad s.f=1,
\]

and the marked walls

\[
 s,\quad f-e_1-e_2,\quad e_i-e_{i+1}\ (1\leq i<m),
 \quad e_m.
\]

For both \(f-e_1-e_2\) and \(e_1-e_2\) to be actual \((-2)\)-curves, the
second centre must lie at the intersection of the strict fibre and
\(E_1\).  This point is proximate only to \(E_1\), hence is free rather than
satellite in the standard proximity terminology.  Later centres may be
generic free points of the newest exceptional component.  The
fibre-following position of the second point is the exact marked geometry;
a generic free chain whose second point avoids the strict fibre does not
supply the displayed wall system.

### 5.1 Dual basis and the visible threshold

In wall order, the dual rays are

\[
 u_s=f,\qquad u_0=s+2f,
\]

and

\[
 u_i=\min(i,2)(s+2f)-e_1-\cdots-e_i
 \qquad(1\leq i\leq m).
\]

Direct pairing gives the identity matrix, and their squares are

\[
 0,2,1,6,5,4,3,2,1,0,-1,-2,\ldots,8-m.
\]

All nonspacelike rays lie on the same closed positive-cone sheet.  Hence
the marked visible simplex has finite volume exactly for \(m\leq8\).
For \(j\geq9\),

\[
 u_j=2s+4f-e_1-\cdots-e_j,\quad
 u_j^2=8-j,\quad K.u_j=j-8,
\]

so \(p_a(u_j)=1\) and \(u_9=-K_{S_9}\).  The sign \(+u_9\) has canonical
pairing \(+1\) and is not asserted to be a physical \((-1)\)-curve.
Instead the numerical physical candidate is

\[
 B_9=2u_9,\qquad B_9^2=-4,\qquad K.B_9=2,
 \qquad p_a(B_9)=0.
\]

Unimodularity of \(\operatorname {NS}(\mathbb F_2)\perp
\langle-1\rangle^m\) makes the same doubling proof as in the plane case
valid.  Effectivity or irreducibility of \(B_9\) is not claimed.

### 5.2 The one-wall enumeration is exhaustive

In the old dual coordinates, the added \(u_9\)-mirror has coefficient
vector

\[
 (2,4,3,6,5,4,3,2,1,0,-1,-1,\ldots).
\]

Intersecting a nonnegative orthant with one homogeneous inequality produces
only two kinds of extreme ray: a surviving coordinate ray with nonnegative
coefficient, or an equality ray supported on one positive and one negative
coordinate.  Since every negative coefficient here is \(-1\), the latter
are exactly

\[
                           u_i+p_i u_j
             \qquad(p_i>0,\ j\geq9).
\]

There is no missing higher-support extreme type: support size \(k\) on the
new equality leaves a face of dimension \(k-1\), so extremality forces
\(k=2\).  For each mixed ray,

\[
              (u_i+p_i u_j)^2=u_i^2+p_i^2(10-j).
\]

Independent enumeration of every surviving and mixed ray through rank 40
shows that all squares are nonnegative exactly for \(m=9,10\).  At
\(m=11\), the choice \(u_s=f\), \(p_s=2\), gives

\[
                         (f+2u_{11})^2=-4.
\]

Thus the ruled calculation has the same deliberately narrow meaning as the
plane calculation: one numerical doubled mirror kills all old diagonal
escapes, but a mixed escape replaces them.  It proves neither effectivity
of the doubled class nor a complete-nef/maximal counterfamily.
