# A sharp AG bound for the anticanonical genus-one facet branch

**Status (2026-08-11): proved, characteristic-free rank bound; exact complex
`P10` and `P11` returns.**  This is the positive rerouting of the uncoloured
`P10` phenomenon.  It is separate from the twice-primitive `(-4)` decorated
no-go.

Let `S` be a smooth projective rational surface with `rho(S)>=3`.  Suppose
that

\[
                         F=-K_S                                      \tag{0.1}
\]

is itself a reduced irreducible negative curve whose orthogonal hyperplane
is a genuine facet of the complete nef cone, and suppose reflection in
`F^perp` is integral on `NS(S)`.  Then

\[
 F^2=K_S^2\in\{-1,-2\},\qquad
 \rho(S)\in\{11,12\},\qquad
 \dim\mathbf P(\operatorname{Nef}S)\in\{10,11\}.             \tag{0.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
                 \boxed{\dim\mathbf P(\operatorname{Nef}S)\le11}.    \tag{0.3}
\]

The bound is sharp: the flex-cubic surfaces `X_10` and `X_11` have complete
nef chambers exactly `P10` and `P11`; their smooth strict anticanonical
cubics have squares `-1` and `-2`, respectively.  Thus `P11`, not `P10`,
attains the upper endpoint.

## 1. Intrinsic theorem

### Theorem 1.1

Let `S` be a smooth projective rational surface over an algebraically closed
field.  Assume `rho(S)>=3` and that the divisor `F=-K_S` is a reduced
irreducible curve with `F^2<0`.  If

\[
                  s_F(x)=x-\frac{2(x.F)}{F^2}F                 \tag{1.1}
\]

preserves `NS(S)`, then (0.2) holds.  In particular the same conclusion
holds when `F^perp` is an integral-reflection facet of the literal full nef
cone.

The finite-volume and reflection-maximality hypotheses are unnecessary for
this bound.

### Proof

A smooth rational surface has unimodular Neron--Severi lattice and

\[
                         K_S^2+\rho(S)=10.                    \tag{1.2}
\]

Because `rho(S)>=3`, the surface is nonminimal: the minimal rational
surfaces have Picard rank one or two.  Hence `S` has an exceptional
`(-1)`-curve `E`.  Adjunction gives

\[
                              K_S.E=-1.                       \tag{1.3}
\]

Thus `K_S`, and therefore `F=-K_S`, is primitive in `NS(S)`.

Unimodularity and primitivity imply `div(F)=1`, so there is an `x` with
`x.F=1`.  Applying (1.1) to that `x`, integrality of `s_F(x)` forces

\[
                           \frac{2}{F^2}\in\mathbf Z.         \tag{1.4}
\]

Since `F^2<0`,

\[
                              F^2\in\{-1,-2\}.                \tag{1.5}
\]

Now `F^2=K_S^2`, and (1.2) gives `rho(S)=11` or `12`.  Projectivizing the
positive cone lowers dimension by one, proving (0.2)--(0.3).  \(\square\)

If negativity is not separately assumed but `F^perp` is declared a genuine
hyperbolic nef facet, it follows automatically.  A positive-square normal
has negative-definite orthogonal complement and misses hyperbolic space; an
isotropic normal is tangent to its ideal boundary rather than cutting a
codimension-one hyperbolic facet.

## 2. Why this is a genus-one branch

Adjunction is automatic:

\[
 2p_a(F)-2=F.(F+K_S)=(-K_S).0=0,                              \tag{2.1}
\]

so

\[
                               p_a(F)=1.                      \tag{2.2}
\]

The theorem only needs reduced irreducibility and arithmetic genus one.  In
the two sharp endpoint constructions below, `F` is a smooth elliptic curve.
If `F` is singular, its geometric genus can be zero; this does not change the
lattice bound.

This branch sits outside the ordinary smooth-rational alphabet.  Its
canonical pairings are

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
F^2&-1&-2\\ \hline
K_S.F&1&2,
\end{array}                                                   \tag{2.3}
\]

not `(-1,-1)` or `(-2,0)`.  Treating the `F^2=-1` mirror as a physical
twice-primitive boundary `B=2F` preserves the hyperplane but changes the
geometric demand: `2F` must then be a reduced smooth rational `(-4)` curve.
The adapted `P10` no-go proves that the change is not automatic.

## 3. Both allowed norms have exact full-nef carriers

Let `C` be a smooth plane cubic, let `p` be a flex, and resolve the pencil
generated by `C` and three times its flex line.  Its nine basepoints are
successive free points on the strict cubic.  Add a free chain of length one
or two along the selected cubic fibre.

### The `F^2=-1` endpoint

After ten blowups,

\[
 \operatorname{NS}(X_{10})=I_{1,10},\qquad
 F=3H-E_1-\cdots-E_{10}=-K_{X_{10}},\qquad F^2=-1.           \tag{3.1}
\]

The strict cubic is smooth and irreducible.  The complete nef cone is the
twelve-facet chamber `P10`, with exact ray count

\[
                         17\text{ finite}+2\text{ ideal}+0
                         \text{ hyperideal}.                 \tag{3.2}
\]

### The sharp `F^2=-2` endpoint

After eleven blowups,

\[
 \operatorname{NS}(X_{11})=I_{1,11},\qquad
 F=3H-E_1-\cdots-E_{11}=-K_{X_{11}},\qquad F^2=-2.           \tag{3.3}
\]

Again the strict cubic is smooth and irreducible.  The complete nef cone is
the thirteen-facet chamber `P11`, with

\[
                         26\text{ finite}+2\text{ ideal}+0
                         \text{ hyperideal}.                 \tag{3.4}
\]

Both cone equalities are already proved in
[`other_algebraic_reservoirs_audit.md`](other_algebraic_reservoirs_audit.md),
using the pencil-at-infinity cone theorem and an independent exact cone
replay.  The new certifier pins that archive and checks the two
anticanonical vectors, squares, canonical pairings, arithmetic genera,
integral reflections, and full-nef endpoint data.

Consequently every value allowed by Theorem 1.1 is attained, and `P11`
proves sharpness of (0.3).

## 4. Relation to the decorated `P10` obstruction

There is no contradiction between the two results.

- **Uncoloured positive return:** on `X_10`, the primitive mirror normal
  `F=-K` is the actual smooth genus-one `(-1)` facet curve.
- **Decorated negative return:** the same hyperplane, labelled instead by
  the physical class `B=2F`, asks for a reduced smooth rational `(-4)`
  curve.  No complex one-boundary strong-Coble full-nef carrier realizes
  that decoration.

Thus the polytope-to-surface functor should branch before effectivity:

\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{primitive anticanonical mirror }F\\
\swarrow\hspace{4em}\searrow\\
\text{irreducible genus-one }F
\qquad
\text{reduced rational boundary }B=2F.
\end{array}                                                   \tag{4.1}
\]

The left branch now has the intrinsic sharp bound `n<=11` and exact
`P10/P11` carriers.  The right branch still needs the simultaneous
reduced-irreducible boundary-effectivity gate isolated by the decorated
no-go.

## 5. Scope

The theorem does not assert that:

- every rational full-nef chamber contains an anticanonical facet;
- a curve merely proportional to `-K` satisfies the bound if its literal
  class is nonprimitive;
- a twice-primitive rational `(-4)` boundary is an anticanonical genus-one
  facet;
- `P10` and `P11` classify every chamber in their dimensions; or
- the broader rational property-4 problem is closed.

Primitivity is not cosmetic.  It is forced here by `F=-K_S` and
`rho(S)>=3`; without it, integrality of the primitive mirror only controls
the primitive normal's square, not the square of an arbitrary multiple.
