# Independent hostile audit: the adapted decorated (P_{10}) obstruction

**Verdict (2026-08-11): PASS, with the stated decorated complex scope.**
The twelve-wall chamber is finite-volume and relatively reflection-maximal
by a classification-free exact argument.  The terminal carrier no-go uses
the pinned complex one-boundary Coble classification.  It forbids the
specified reduced rational \((-4)\) decoration; it does not forbid the
uncoloured polytope or its genus-one rational carrier.

This audit does not import the owner certifier.  It recomputes all 66 active
sets of the dual cone, separately dualizes the ten-root Coble--Mukai
simplex, and checks the unbounded descent proof before running an independent
bounded regression.

## 1. The physical packet and its two meanings

On \(L=I_{1,10}\), put

\[
 F=(3,-1,\ldots,-1),\qquad K=-F,
\]

and take primitive mirror normals

\[
 E_{10},\quad E_i-E_{i+1},\quad H-E_1-E_2-E_3,\quad F.
\]

The physical label on the last mirror is \(B=2F\).  Direct calculation
gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
&E_{10}&E_i-E_{i+1},\ H-E_1-E_2-E_3&B\\ \hline
C^2&-1&-2&-4\\
K.C&-1&0&2.
\end{array}
\]

Because \(B/2=F\in L\), the adapted lattice is still \(L\); doubling the
form does not change its isometry group or any reflection formula.  Both
canonical orientations escape: \(K.E_{10}=-1\) and \(F.F=-1\).  Moreover

\[
              -\left(K+B/2\right)=0,
\]

while \(B\) is orthogonal to all ten \((-2)\) walls and meets \(E_{10}\)
in two.  Thus this is genuinely the favourable weak-incidence equality
case, not a local sign failure.

The mirror remembers only the primitive line \(\mathbf RF\).  The two
geometric readings are different:

- on the uncoloured flex carrier, \(F=-K\) is a smooth irreducible
  genus-one curve of square \(-1\);
- the decorated packet requires the divisible class \(B=2F\) itself to be
  a reduced irreducible smooth rational curve of square \(-4\).

Adjunction gives arithmetic genera one and zero respectively.  The same
hyperplane and reflection therefore do not imply the same effectivity or
reducedness statement.

## 2. Exact finite volume

The first eleven primitive normals, omitting \(F\), have determinant
\(-1\).  Hence the homogeneous twelve-halfspace cone is pointed.  In
dimension eleven every extreme ray lies on ten independent facets, so
enumerating the

\[
                         {12\choose10}=66
\]

active sets is exhaustive.  Independent rational row reduction again
finds exactly 19 rays:

\[
            17\text{ positive},\qquad2\text{ null},\qquad0\text{ negative}.
\]

Every ray has positive \(H\)-coordinate and every mutual Lorentz product is
nonnegative.  Their sum is

\[
 A=(137,-47,-45,-43,-41,-39,-37,-35,-33,-31,-30),
\]

with \(A^2=3940\) and strictly positive slacks

\[
 (30,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,1,2,30).
\]

The active rays on every facet span rank ten, so no displayed inequality is
redundant.  In the Klein model this is a finite Euclidean polytope whose
vertices lie inside or on the closed ball.  It triangulates into finitely
many finite/ideal hyperbolic simplices, each of finite volume in dimension
ten.  The two null rays are distinct and have positive mutual product, so
the ideal boundary consists of isolated vertices rather than an open ideal
face.  This proves finite volume without identifying the chamber in a
census.

The same data also prove

\[
                         x\in W\Longrightarrow x^2\ge0:
\]

the cone is generated by future causal rays with pairwise nonnegative
products.

For the later Coble criterion, the audit separately dualizes the chamber in
\(F^\perp\) cut out by the ten \((-2)\) roots.  Its dual squares are

\[
                    4,18,42,30,20,12,6,2,0,10.
\]

Thus this nine-dimensional root simplex itself has nine finite vertices,
one ideal vertex, and no hyperideal vertex.  Its reflection group has finite
covolume; this input is not inferred merely from the name \(P_{10}\).

## 3. The all-root descent is genuinely unbounded

Let \(u\) be a primitive integral reflector.  Odd unimodularity gives
divisibility one, so integrality of its reflection forces

\[
                              u^2=-q,\qquad q\in\{1,2\}.
\]

Signed exceptional permutations put

\[
 u=(a,-m_1,\ldots,-m_{10}),\qquad
 a\ge0,\quad m_1\ge\cdots\ge m_{10}\ge0,
\]

with \(\sum m_i^2=a^2+q\).  If \(a=0\), one reaches the simple
\((-1)\) or \((-2)\) exceptional root.  For \(a>0\), every \(m_i\le a\).

Write \(s=m_1+m_2+m_3\).  If \(s>a\), Cremona reflection changes the time
coordinate to \(2a-s\).  Unless \(s=3a\), its absolute value is strictly
smaller than \(a\); equality \(s=3a\) forces the simple root
\(H-E_1-E_2-E_3\).

Now suppose \(s\le a\), put \(T=\sum m_i\), and set \(d=T-3a\).  If
\(d\le0\), the negative vector \(u\) would satisfy every wall inequality,
contradicting the causal-cone calculation above.  Hence \(d\ge1\).
Reflection in \(F\) changes the time coordinate to

\[
                              a-6d.
\]

The only possible failure of strict descent would have \(d\ge a/3\).
Cauchy--Schwarz on the first three and last seven multiplicities gives

\[
 a^2+q\ge {s^2\over3}+{(T-s)^2\over7}
          \ge {a^2\over3}+{(2a+d)^2\over7}
          \ge {10a^2\over9}.
\]

Therefore \(a^2\le9q\le18\), so only \(a=1,2,3,4\) remain.  Exact
enumeration of these four mathematically forced cases leaves only

\[
                 a=3,quad q=1,quad m_1=\cdots=m_{10}=1,
\]

which is the simple root \(F\).  Every other reduced root has \(d<a/3\),
and hence \(|a-6d|<a\).  The positive integer \(|a|\) strictly descends.

This proves every integral reflection is conjugate to a side reflection.
The independent search through \(a=18\) tests more than ten thousand roots,
but is explicitly only regression coverage; the Cauchy bound and strict
integer descent prove the theorem for unbounded \(a\).

Since every reflection of the declared host already lies in the side group,
there is no proper reflection overgroup inside \(O^+(L)\).  Relative
maximality follows without Nikulin--Vinberg or a bounded root census.

## 4. The terminal decorated no-go

Assume an exact complex rational full-nef carrier with the physical
decoration.  Then \(B=-2K\) is a reduced irreducible rational curve.  If
\(-K\) were effective, its intersection \(-2\) with \(B\) would force
\(B\) as a component, while an ample degree contradicts
\([B]=2[-K]\).  Hence the carrier would be a complex one-boundary strong
Coble surface.

Its ten displayed \((-2)\) curves would be all effective irreducible
Coble--Mukai roots.  Indeed, a hidden irreducible root \(C\) is necessarily
a genuine nef facet: for ample \(H\),

\[
                   H+{H.C\over2}C
\]

has positive square, is orthogonal to \(C\), and is strictly positive on
every other irreducible curve.  Exact full-nef return therefore excludes a
hidden root.

The finite-volume ten-root chamber puts the surface in the finite-
automorphism lane.  The pinned complex one-boundary classification leaves
Types I and II, each with twelve effective irreducible Coble--Mukai roots,
not ten.  This is the contradiction.  The raw cone and maximality proof did
not use that classification; only this terminal complex AG step does.

## 5. Precise scope

The conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{\text{no exact complex rational full-nef carrier with the specified
reduced rational }B=2F\text{ decoration}.}
\]

It is not a nonexistence theorem for the uncoloured chamber.  The uncoloured
\(P_{10}\) has the pinned flex-cluster full-nef carrier, on which \(F\) is
the smooth genus-one \((-1)\) facet and \(2F\) is nonreduced.  No
positive-characteristic no-go, universal adapted-host dimension bound, or
