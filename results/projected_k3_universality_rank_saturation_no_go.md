# Projected K3 eigenslices: exact lattice gates, a block theorem, and a rank-saturated no-go

**Status (2026-08-11): proved in the exact-module category.**

There is no universal `POLYTOPE -> K3 eigen-nef slice` functor for all
K3-rank-eligible finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chambers.  The obstruction
already occurs at the endpoint of the Picard range.  Vinberg's finite-volume
36-wall chamber in \(\mathbb H^{19}\), on

\[
             L_{X_3}=U\oplus2E_8(-1)\oplus A_2(-1),
\]

has 24 primitive facets of square \(-2\) and 12 primitive facets of square
\(-6\), divisibility three.  An exact eigenslice returning a
19-dimensional chamber has eigen-rank 20.  A complex K3 has Picard rank at
most 20, so the eigenslice is the whole Picard space and there is no
projector complement in which to correct a facet norm.  One rational common
similarity and rational representatives on the two kinds of facet lines
would force

\[
                   c a^2=1,\qquad 3c b^2=1,
             \qquad (a/b)^2=3,
\]

which is impossible over \(\mathbb Q\).  Local finiteness of the K3 root
arrangement forces a square-\((-2)\) root to support a generic point of every
facet of a full nef cone, so this is an obstruction to eigen-**nef** return,
not merely to a preferred list of lifts.

The same example already has a K3 carrier in a weaker sense: the surface
\(X_3\) carries all 36 integral divisor cuts.  The 24 long cuts are curve
walls, while the 12 short cuts are non-root \((-6)\) divisor cuts inside the
nef cone.  Thus the example separates exact eigen-nef return from an
arbitrary mixed-divisor semialgebraic section.

The negative result does not make projected K3 carriers rare.  Sections 3--5
give a general block construction.  It proves, in particular:

> **Compact low-total-rank theorem.**  Let \(P\) be any compact arithmetic
> Coxeter chamber with admissible integral trace datum of rank \(R\) and
> \(m\) facets.  If \(R+m\le10\), then a sufficiently large direct-block
> scale produces an exact-module projective K3 eigen-nef carrier for \(P\).

There is also a noncompact rational version when \(P\) is the complete
chamber of all integral reflections of its pinned host.  For
\(10<R+m\le20\), the same construction works whenever its explicit
discriminant-form embedding gate passes.  Facets can sometimes be compressed
into fewer than \(m\) complement directions; Section 4 gives the exact
glue-theoretic lower bound.  Facet count alone is not a K3 obstruction.

## 1. The exact carrier contract

Let \(F\) be totally real of degree \(d\), let \(M\) be an
\(\mathcal O_F\)-module of rank \(n+1\), and let \(q\) be admissible:

\[
 \operatorname {sig}(q_{\sigma_0})=(1,n),\qquad
 q_\sigma<0\quad(\sigma\ne\sigma_0).
\]

After a totally positive codifferent normalization \(\beta\), put

\[
 T_0=(M_{\mathbb Z},B),\qquad
 B(x,y)=\operatorname {Tr}_{F/\mathbb Q}(\beta q(x,y)).
\]

Then

\[
       R:=\operatorname {rk}_{\mathbb Z}T_0=d(n+1),
       \qquad \operatorname {sig}(B)=(1,R-1).             \tag{1.1}
\]

The exact-module projected-K3 contract used here consists of:

1. an even lattice \(S\) of signature \((1,N-1)\), primitively embeddable
   in the K3 lattice, and a primitive rationally similar copy
   \(T\cong (T_0,sB)\subset S\);
2. the self-adjoint \(F\)-action on \(T_{\mathbb Q}\), extended by zero on
   \(T_{\mathbb Q}^{\perp}\), so that the distinguished real eigenspace
   \(E_{\sigma_0}\) is the raw Lorentzian space;
3. a regular point \(p\in E_{\sigma_0}\) and an ambient K3 root chamber
   \(C\) containing \(p\); and
4. exact equality

\[
       \mathbf P(C\cap E_{\sigma_0})=P.                  \tag{1.2}
\]

After Weyl framing, \(C\) is the ample chamber and its closure is the nef
cone.  This contract remembers the rational integral/order module up to one
common rational similarity.  If one forgets that structure and asks only for
an unlabelled real polytope, the square-class no-go in Section 6 does not
apply.

The trace rank gives the first necessary condition

\[
                       d(n+1)=R\le N\le20.                \tag{1.3}
\]

This is sharper than \(n\le19\) for nonrational input.  It bounds the degree,
but by itself it does not produce a finite set of number fields.

## 2. Necessary and sufficient lattice gates

Write \(\Delta(S)=\{\rho\in S:\rho^2=-2\}\), and let
\(\pi_0:S_{\mathbb R}\to E_{\sigma_0}\) be orthogonal projection.  At the
lattice level, an exact carrier exists if and only if one can supply the
following data.

### `EMB`: a K3 Picard lattice

The lattice \(S\) is even of signature \((1,N-1)\) and admits a primitive
embedding

\[
                     S\hookrightarrow\Lambda_{K3}
             =U^3\oplus E_8(-1)^2.                       \tag{2.1}
\]

Equivalently, there is an even complement of signature \((2,20-N)\) with
the discriminant form needed to glue to the unimodular K3 lattice.  A useful
sufficient criterion is

\[
                   22-N\ge \ell(A_S)+2.                  \tag{2.2}
\]

Rank alone is not sufficient near \(N=20\); the discriminant form is a real
gate.

### `ROOT`: physical projected facets

For every facet line \(\ell_i\subset E_{\sigma_0}\), some
\(\delta_i\in\Delta(S)\) has

\[
                         \pi_0(\delta_i)\in\ell_i\setminus\{0\}. \tag{2.3}
\]

This is necessary even if physical roots were not named in advance.  At a
generic positive-norm point of an exposed nef facet, local finiteness of the
root mirrors forces at least one ambient root hyperplane to give its support.

### `REG/HIDDEN`: exact chamber return

No root has zero projection, and, after orienting roots by \(p\),

\[
 P=\left\{[x]\in\mathbf P(E_{\sigma_0}):
       x^2>0,\ (x,\pi_0\rho)\ge0
       \text{ for every }\rho\in\Delta(S)\text{ positive at }p
       \right\}.                                        \tag{2.4}
\]

Thus every facet occurs and every other projected root inequality is
redundant on \(P\).  This is the exact hidden-root exhaustion gate.  It is
not implied by arithmetic reflection maximality unless one separately proves
that every visible projected root is an old raw reflection.

### `EIG/CORR`: the eigenspace is algebraic

Once \(T\subset S\) is present, this causes no additional lattice
obstruction.  Multiplication by a primitive element of \(F\) is self-adjoint
for the trace form.  Extend it by zero on \(T^\perp\) and clear denominators.
Using a divisor basis and its intersection-dual basis represents the result
by a transpose-invariant divisor-product correspondence on a K3 with
\(\operatorname {NS}(X)=S\).  This is a correspondence, not an assertion
that the operator comes from an automorphism.

The K3 period theorem supplies an exact-Picard surface after `EMB`; Weyl
framing and the K3 cone theorem turn `REG/HIDDEN` into (1.2).  Conversely,
any carrier satisfying (1.2) supplies all four gates.  This is the precise
necessary-and-sufficient lattice formulation; the difficult content is
constructing `ROOT` while retaining `EMB` and exhausting `HIDDEN`.

## 3. The direct facet-root block

Choose a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of \(T_0\), let \(G\) be the integral trace
Gram, and put the integral facet vectors in the columns of an
\(R\)-by-\(m\) matrix \(V\).  For a positive integer \(s\), define

\[
 A_s=
 \begin{pmatrix}
       sG&GV\\
       V^tG&-2I_m
 \end{pmatrix}.                                         \tag{3.1}
\]

The first \(R\) basis vectors form a primitive copy of \((T_0,sB)\).  The
rational matrix

\[
 e_s=\begin{pmatrix}I_R&V/s\\0&0\end{pmatrix}            \tag{3.2}
\]

satisfies

\[
 e_s^2=e_s,\qquad e_s^tA_s=A_se_s,
 \qquad e_s(\delta_i)=v_i/s,
 \qquad \delta_i^2=-2.                                  \tag{3.3}
\]

The Schur complement is

\[
                  K_s=-2I_m-{1\over s}V^tGV.             \tag{3.4}
\]

Hence \(S_s=(\mathbb Z^{R+m},A_s)\) has signature
\((1,R+m-1)\) for every sufficiently large \(s\).  Taking \(s\) even makes
\(S_s\) even, regardless of the parity of \(G\).

Every \(\rho=(x,a)\in S_s\) has projected trace normal

\[
                         y=sx+Va,                         \tag{3.5}
\]

and the exact root equation is

\[
 \rho^2=-2
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 G(y,y)=G(Va,Va)+2s(\lVert a\rVert^2-1).                 \tag{3.6}
\]

If the projected mirror meets the distinguished positive cone, admissibility
implies \(G(y,y)<0\).  Therefore

\[
                 a^t(2sI_m+V^tGV)a<2s.                  \tag{3.7}
\]

Let

\[
       b(V,G)=\max_i\sum_j |(V^tGV)_{ij}|.               \tag{3.8}
\]

If \(s>b(V,G)\), (3.7) leaves only

\[
                         a=0,\quad a=\pm e_i.             \tag{3.9}
\]

For integral \(G\) and \(s>2\), the case \(a=0\) is incompatible with
\(\rho^2=-2\).  Thus every visible root is on one of the exact residue
shells

\[
 \boxed{
   G(y,y)=G(v_i,v_i),\qquad y\equiv\pm v_i\pmod {sT_0}.} \tag{3.10}
\]

There is no root with zero distinguished projection: an element of \(M\)
which vanishes at one embedding is zero, and (3.6)--(3.9) then rule out
\(y=0\).

Equation (3.10) isolates the missing geometric property exactly.

> **Residue-shell completeness `RSC(s)`.**  Every vector on a shell (3.10)
> whose distinguished mirror meets the interior of \(P\) has a hyperplane
> already supporting or avoiding the raw chamber; equivalently, none gives a
> new cut of \(\operatorname {int}P\).

After the elementary size hypotheses above, `RSC(s)` is necessary and
sufficient for `HIDDEN` for this direct block.  This is weaker and more
precise than asking maximality to control arbitrary projected walls.

## 4. Facet compression and its exact lower bounds

The direct block spends one negative direction per listed facet, but this is
only an upper bound.  Let a general carrier have

\[
        T\subset S,\qquad K=S\cap T_{\mathbb Q}^{\perp},
        \qquad c=\operatorname {rk}K=N-R.                \tag{4.1}
\]

In the rational orthogonal decomposition write a chosen facet lift as

\[
                         \delta_i=t_i+u_i,
           \qquad t_i\in T^\vee,\quad u_i\in K^\vee.    \tag{4.2}
\]

Let \(D=((\delta_i,\delta_j))\) and \(C=((t_i,t_j))\).  Then

\[
  D\in\operatorname {Sym}_m(\mathbb Z),\quad D_{ii}=-2,
  \qquad U:=D-C=((u_i,u_j))\le0,
  \qquad \operatorname {rk}_{\mathbb Q}U\le c.          \tag{4.3}
\]

Conversely, such a negative Gram factorization, together with an even
discriminant-form glue containing the pairs \(([t_i],[u_i])\), is exactly
the lattice-theoretic datum needed to produce simultaneous root lifts.
Thus the sharp linear-algebra compression cost is

\[
 c_{\rm Gram}=min\operatorname {rk}_{\mathbb Q}(D-C),   \tag{4.4}
\]

where the minimum ranges over rational representatives on the facet lines,
integral symmetric \(D\) with diagonal \(-2\), negative factorizations, and
admissible glue.  The number \(m\) of facets does not itself lower-bound
\(c\).

There is also a discriminant-group lower bound.  The glue classes
\([t_i]\in A_T\) must inject into the discriminant group of \(K\).  Hence,
for every prime \(p\),

\[
        c\ge \ell_p\bigl(\langle[t_1],\ldots,[t_m]\rangle\bigr). \tag{4.5}
\]

For the uniform direct-block representatives \(t_i=v_i/s\), if \(p\mid s\)
then

\[
 c\ge\operatorname {rk}_{\mathbb F_p}(V\bmod p).         \tag{4.6}
\]

In particular, if the facet columns span the full trace lattice modulo
\(p\), that uniform-denominator strategy needs \(c\ge R\), hence
\(N\ge2R\).  This explains why the rank-six `Delta(2,4,6)` construction uses
three complement directions, but it is not a theorem that every construction
must do so: different denominators or shared negative Gram factorizations can
compress further.

## 5. When the block gives an exact K3 carrier

### 5.1 Compact chambers

For compact \(P\), `RSC(s)` can always be forced by increasing \(s\).  Fix a
facet \(v_i\).  The vectors

\[
 \mathcal Y_i=\{y\in M:\ B(y,y)=B(v_i,v_i),\quad
        y_{\sigma_0}^{\perp}\cap P\ne\varnothing\}       \tag{5.1}
\]

form a finite set.  At the distinguished place, a circumradius ellipsoid
bounds every spacelike normal of the fixed trace budget whose mirror meets
the compact chamber.  At every other embedding, negative definiteness and
the same trace budget give a positive-definite bound.  The Minkowski image of
\(M\) is a lattice, so the combined bounded set is finite.

Choose \(s\) larger than every nonzero coordinate of
\(y\mp v_i\), for all \(y\in\mathcal Y_i\), as well as the elementary block
bounds.  Then the congruences (3.10) leave only \(y=\pm v_i\).  This is the
general form of the finite ellipsoid/residue argument used in the
`Delta(2,4,6)` calibration.

If \(N=R+m\le10\), then \(\ell(A_{S_s})\le N\) and

\[
                  22-N\ge N+2\ge\ell(A_{S_s})+2,         \tag{5.2}
\]

so the stable K3 embedding criterion is automatic.  This proves the compact
low-total-rank theorem stated at the beginning.  For \(10<N\le20\), one
tests the actual Smith/discriminant form; whenever `EMB` passes, the same
proof gives the carrier.

### 5.2 Noncompact rational complete chambers

Suppose \(F=\mathbb Q\), every \(v_i\) is an integral reflection vector, and
\(P\) is a chamber for **all** integral reflections of the pinned host.
Choose \(s\) divisible by every \(|G(v_i,v_i)|\).  If \(y\) satisfies
(3.10), then for every \(z\in T_0\),

\[
 {2G(y,z)\over G(y,y)}=
 \pm {2G(v_i,z)\over G(v_i,v_i)}
       +{2sG(x,z)\over G(v_i,v_i)}\in\mathbb Z.          \tag{5.3}
\]

Thus \(y\) is itself an integral reflection vector.  Host completeness says
its mirror cannot cut the interior of \(P\).  Therefore `RSC(s)` holds even
at cusps.  Together with `EMB`, this proves the noncompact rational block
theorem.

For nonrational cusped chambers, equality of trace norms in (3.10) does not
imply equality of the field-valued norms, so (5.3) has no automatic analogue.
The compact ellipsoid is also unavailable globally at a cusp.  This is the
remaining exact outer gate: one must prove `RSC(s)` or use a different
construction.  Raw maximality alone has not been shown to do it.

## 6. The rank-saturated `X3` no-go

The exact Vinberg certificate reconstructs a 36-by-36 wall Gram with

\[
 \operatorname {rank}=20,\qquad
 \operatorname {inertia}=(1,19,16),\qquad
 \operatorname {SNF}_{\ne0}=1^{19},3.                   \tag{6.1}
\]

It is the full discriminant-three lattice
\(U\oplus2E_8(-1)\oplus A_2(-1)\).  Vinberg's completed algorithm, as
restated by Looijenga, says that the 36 roots are the complete simple system;
the exact endpoint/cusp audit gives 8,346 finite vertices, 107 cusps, and
finite volume.  The facet normals split as

\[
                 24\times(-2,\operatorname {div}1),
        \qquad 12\times(-6,\operatorname {div}3).        \tag{6.2}
\]

Assume an exact-module projected K3 carrier exists.  Since \(n=19\),

\[
             20=\dim E\le\rho(X)\le20.                  \tag{6.3}
\]

Hence \(E=\operatorname {NS}(X)_{\mathbb R}\), the projector is the
identity, and there is no negative complement.  Pull the intersection form
back through the rational marking; it is \(c(\ ,\ )\) for one
\(c\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\).  By the generic-facet argument in Section 2, a long
facet line and a short facet line contain K3 roots.  Rationality of the
marking makes them \(ar\) and \(bu\) for \(a,b\in\mathbb Q^\times\).  Their
squares give

\[
              -2c a^2=-2,\qquad -6c b^2=-2,
              \qquad (a/b)^2=3,                          \tag{6.4}
\]

contradicting the odd 3-adic valuation of 3.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{P_{X_3}\text{ has no exact-module projected K3 eigen-nef carrier}.} \tag{6.5}
\]

This is a counterexample **inside** the nominal rank-eligible range, not the
trivial \(n\ge20\) Picard obstruction.

The maximality scope is exact and deliberately conservative.  The Vinberg
statement makes this the complete reflection chamber of the pinned
discriminant-three integral host: no additional reflection of that host is
missing.  The present package does not prove that there is no proper
reflection overgroup on another commensurable host.  Consequently (6.5)
disproves a theorem quantified over every finite-volume arithmetic chamber,
but it does not by itself disprove a conjecture quantified only over
absolutely maximal arithmetic reflection groups.

## 7. Why mixed divisors do not restore the desired AG transfer

Vinberg's actual singular K3 surface \(X_3\) has

\[
             \operatorname {NS}(X_3)=L_{X_3}.            \tag{7.1}
\]

All 36 normals are integral divisor classes.  The 24 square-\((-2)\) classes
are rational-curve walls.  The 12 square-\((-6)\), divisibility-three
classes give extra divisor inequalities, not nef walls.  Thus

\[
 P_{X_3}=\mathbf P\bigl(\operatorname {Nef}(X_3)
       \cap\{x:(x,D_j)\ge0\text{ for the 12 short cuts}\}\bigr) \tag{7.2}
\]

after the appropriate orientation, but
\(P_{X_3}\ne\mathbf P(\operatorname {Nef}(X_3))\).

In fact, this weaker category has a broad functorial existence theorem.

> **Weak mixed-divisor theorem.**  Every admissible integral arithmetic
> Coxeter datum whose full trace rank satisfies \(R=d(n+1)\le10\) has a
> projective K3 weak eigendivisor carrier
> \((X,\Gamma;D_1,\ldots,D_m)\) with exact semialgebraic return
> \[
> P=\mathbf P\left(\operatorname {Nef}(X)\cap E_{\sigma_0}
>       \cap\bigcap_i\{x:(x,D_i)\ge0\}\right).           \tag{7.3}
> \]
> No compactness, root completeness, or reflection maximality is required.

Indeed, take \(S=(T_0,sB)\) for any even \(s>2\).  It is even of signature
\((1,R-1)\), and it has no square-\((-2)\) vector because every integral norm
is divisible by \(s\).  Since \(R\le10\),

\[
          22-R\ge R+2\ge\ell(A_S)+2,                    \tag{7.4}
\]

so \(S\) embeds primitively in the K3 lattice.  Choose a generic period with
exact Picard lattice \(S\).  The absence of roots makes the nef cone the
closure of a positive-cone component.  The integral classes
\(D_i=v_i\in S\), together with the self-adjoint field correspondence, then
give (7.3) directly.

This theorem confirms rather than repairs the loss of rigidity.  Arbitrary
integral divisor half-spaces need not be effective, extremal, contractible,
or visible in the Mori cone.  K3 geometry for any such exact-trace carrier
supplies at most

\[
                      d(n+1)\le20,                       \tag{7.5}
\]

not finite field sets, bounded discriminants, finite host lists, or a reason
that reflection maximality should be encoded in \(\operatorname {NS}\).
Those conclusions would again have to come from arithmetic reflection theory
or from an additional intrinsic condition on the divisor cuts.

The genuine geometric condition isolated by this audit is therefore not
“there are divisor classes on the facet rays.”  It is simultaneous
square-\((-2)\) lifting plus discriminant-compatible K3 embedding plus exact
hidden-root exhaustion.  In the direct block, the last condition is exactly
`RSC(s)`.

Exact scope:

- **proved false:** universal exact-module projected K3 eigen-nef return for
  every finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chamber with trace rank at most 20;
- **proved positive:** the direct-block theorem under `RSC(s)` and `EMB`,
  including the compact \(R+m\le10\) and rational host-complete
  \(R+m\le10\) corollaries, and the weak mixed-divisor theorem for every
  trace rank \(R\le10\);
- **not claimed:** absolute reflection maximality of the `X3` group,
  universality for an absolutely-maximal-only input class, universal
  compression below one complement direction per facet, or finite
  field/host control from mixed divisor cuts.
