## Result

Consider the golden field lattice

\[
M_{20}=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2\phi&1&0\\
1&2&1\\
0&1&2
\end{pmatrix}
\oplus E_8,
\qquad \phi^2-\phi-1=0.
\]

This is a genuine algebro-geometric carrier in hyperbolic dimension 10:

- its two real signatures are `(10,1)` and `(11,0)`;
- its even trace lattice has signature `(1,21)`, determinant `-20`, and
  discriminant group `Z/2 + Z/10`;
- an explicit order-20 gluing embeds that trace lattice **primitively in the
  actual OG10 Beauville lattice**
  \(3U\oplus2E_8(-1)\oplus A_2(-1)\), with positive complement
  \(\langle2\rangle\oplus\langle30\rangle\); and
- standard OG10 period surjectivity and the positive-square projectivity
  criterion therefore realize it as the full Neron--Severi lattice of a
  projective OG10-type irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifold.

It is not a new finite-volume norm-two Coxeter example.  A complete exact
projection argument proves that every norm-two mirror of `M20` meeting its
natural fixed `H9` restricts to an existing norm-two mirror of

\[
L_9=\langle-2\phi\rangle\oplus E_8\oplus\langle2\rangle.
\]

Consequently a natural invariant chamber has as its complete fixed slice an
`L9` norm-two chamber.  All such chambers are reflection-congruent to the
already certified infinite-volume chamber `P9`.  Uniform arithmeticity then
forces every `M20` norm-two chamber to have infinite hyperbolic volume.

This decision requires no Vinberg prefix computation.

## 1. Field and trace lattices

The determinant over `Z[phi]` is

\[
\det M_{20}=-2-6\phi,
\qquad
N_{\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)/\mathbf Q}(\det M_{20})=-20.
\]

At the identity embedding the form has signature `(10,1)`.  At the
conjugate embedding it is positive definite of rank 11.  With trace scalar

\[
\alpha=-\frac{\phi}{\sqrt5},
\qquad
B(v,w)=\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha M_{20}(v,w)),
\]

the restriction-of-scalars lattice `N20` is even and has signature `(1,21)`.
After a unimodular split of the golden `E8` coordinates,

\[
N_{20}\cong N_6\oplus E_8(-1)^2,
\]

where

\[
N_6=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&4&-1&-1&0&0\\

-1&-1&-2&-2&-1&-1\\
-1&-2&-2&-4&-1&-2\\
0&0&-1&-1&-2&-2\\

\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Exact Smith reduction gives

\[
\det N_{20}=-20,
\qquad
A_{N_{20}}\cong\mathbf Z/2\oplus\mathbf Z/10.
\]

Multiplication by `phi` is integral, self-adjoint for `B`, and satisfies
`tau^2-tau-1=0`.

## 2. An actual primitive embedding in the OG10 lattice

Take the positive complement

\[
K=\langle2\rangle\oplus\langle30\rangle.
\]

In coordinates for `N6 + K`, the discriminant gluing graph is generated by
the order-two and order-ten vectors

\[
g_2=
\left(-\frac92,3,3,-2,-\frac32,1;\frac12,\frac12\right),

\[
g_{10}=
\left(-\frac{33}{10},\frac{21}{10},\frac{11}{5},-\frac75,
      -\frac{11}{10},\frac7{10};\frac12,\frac25\right).
\]

They lie in the dual lattice and have exact total quadratic data

\[
g_2^2=g_{10}^2=(g_2,g_{10})=2.
\]

Thus their discriminant graph is isotropic.  A basis of the order-20

\[
R=

Q=R^t(N_6\oplus K)R=

The matrix `Q` is even, has signature `(3,5)`, determinant `-3`, and Smith

\[

The coordinate matrices of both `N6` and `K` in this overlattice have only

N_{20}\hookrightarrow

The residual class `(0,1/3)` in `A_K` has square

\[
\frac{10}{3}\equiv\frac43\pmod{2\mathbf Z},

as required for `A2(-1)`.

## 3. The mod-3 wall exclusion

Because `det(N20)=-20` is prime to 3, the form on `N20/3N20` is
nondegenerate.  The primitive embedding then excludes primitive Picard
classes of ambient divisibility three: such a class would reduce to the
radical of the OG10 Beauville form modulo 3, hence would be orthogonal to
`N20/3N20`; nondegeneracy forces it to be divisible by 3 already in `N20`,
contradicting primitivity.

This removes the numerical OG10 wall types of ambient divisibility three.
It is not, by itself, a complete audit of every Kähler-wall shell for this
new trace lattice.

## 4. The natural involution and the misleading 5-edge

Use coordinates `(x,y,u,z)` with `z` in `E8`.  The field isometry

\[
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1-\phi&0\\
0&-1&0\\
0&1&1
\end{pmatrix}
\oplus I_8
\]

is an involution.  It fixes `y=0`, whose lattice is `L9`, and negates the
orthogonal normal

\[
\nu=(1,2\phi,-\phi,0^8),
\qquad \nu^2=6+8\phi.
\]

For the norm-two root

\[
r=(0,1,0,0^8),
\]

one has

\[
Jr=(1-\phi,-1,1,0^8),
\qquad r^2=(Jr)^2=2,
\qquad (r,Jr)=-\phi.
\]

Thus this is a genuine Coxeter `5` pair, unlike the globally ultraparallel
pairs in the earlier orthogonal carrier.  This fact alone is not an escape
from the fixed-facet obstruction.  Its fixed projection is

\[
p=\frac{r+Jr}{2}
=\left(\frac{1-\phi}{2},0,\frac12,0^8\right),
\qquad p^2=\frac{2-\phi}{2}>0.
\]

But

\[
q=2\phi p=(-1,0,\phi,0^8)
\]

is an integral fixed norm-two root.  The common restriction `x+u=0` is
therefore already a wall of the fixed `L9` norm-two arrangement.  On the
Bugaenko subspace `y=u=0` it would require `x=0`, so it does not meet the
hyperbolic interior there.  The 5-edge neither proves a chamber subdivision
nor destroys the complete Bugaenko facet.

## 5. Complete projected-wall theorem

Let `s=(x,y,u,z)` be any norm-two root of `M20`, and put

\[
p_s=\frac{s+Js}{2}
=\left(x+\frac{1-\phi}{2}y,0,u+\frac y2,z\right).
\]

An exact calculation gives

\[
p_s^2=2-\frac{2+\phi}{2}y^2.
\tag{1}
\]

If the mirror meets the fixed hyperbolic space, then `p_s^2>0`, and hence

\[
y^2<\frac4{2+\phi}
=\frac{12}{5}-\frac45\phi.
\tag{2}
\]

At the conjugate embedding the entire form is positive definite.  Completing
squares in the `(x',y',u')` block gives

\[
2=(s')^2\geq\frac{3-\phi}{2}(y')^2,
\]

so

\[
(y')^2\leq\frac4{3-\phi}
=\frac85+\frac45\phi.
\tag{3}
\]

Both square bounds are strictly below `4`.  Writing `y=a+b phi`, equations
(2)--(3) give

\[
5b^2=(y-y')^2<16,
\]

and therefore `|b|<=1`.  Also

\[
|a|\leq |y|+|b|\phi<2+\phi<4,
\]

so `|a|<=3`.  Exhausting this proved finite rectangle gives exactly

\[
y\in\{-1,\,-1+\phi,\,0,\,1-\phi,\,1\}.
\tag{4}
\]

For `y=0`, the root is fixed.  If `y^2=1`, then

\[
q_s=2\phi p_s\in L_9,
\qquad q_s^2=2.
\]

If `y^2=2-phi`, then

\[
q_s=2(\phi-1)p_s\in L_9,
\qquad q_s^2=2.
\]

The multipliers remove both half-integral coefficients in `p_s`, so the
integrality assertion holds for arbitrary integral `(x,y,u,z)`, not only for
the displayed example.  Conversely every norm-two root of `L9` is already a
fixed norm-two root of `M20`.  Therefore

> the intersections with `H(L9)` of all `M20` norm-two mirrors are exactly
> the norm-two mirrors of `L9`.

There are no extra projected hyperplanes.

Choose a generic point `v` in the complement of the fixed arrangement.  The
projection theorem says that `v` avoids every ambient mirror.  Its unique
ambient chamber `C` is `J`-stable because `Jv=v`.  Paths inside the fixed
`L9` chamber encounter no ambient mirrors, so

\[
C\cap\mathbb H(L_9)=P,
\]

where `P` is the complete `L9` norm-two chamber containing `v`.  Every such
`P` is `W2(L9)`-congruent to the archived chamber `P9`.

## 6. Infinite-volume decision

The archived exact theorem proves

\[
\operatorname{vol}(P_9)=\infty.
\]

Suppose that the ambient chamber `C` had finite volume.  The norm-two Weyl
group is normal in the arithmetic group `O+(M20)`.  Since the conjugate form
is positive definite, `M20` is anisotropic over `Q(sqrt(5))`, so this
arithmetic group is uniform.  Finite covolume of the Weyl group would then
make it finite-index in a uniform lattice.  Its Coxeter chamber would be
compact.  The closed fixed slice would consequently be compact, contradicting
the infinite volume of `P9`.

Thus

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{vol}(C)=\infty}
\]

for the chosen invariant chamber, and hence for every norm-two chamber,
because the Weyl group is transitive on chambers.

This is a global decision for the norm-two Coxeter arrangement.  Finite
sidedness remains open.  A separate full OG10 mixed Kähler-wall audit could
study finer nonreflective subdivisions, but it cannot turn this norm-two
chamber itself into the desired compact or finite-volume Coxeter polytope.

## 7. Secondary symmetry diagnostics

The nontrivial Bugaenko diagram reversal `A8+1` has exactly two field-linear
extensions to `M20`, determined by `nu -> +nu` or `nu -> -nu`.  Both are
integral isometric involutions.  Their `e_y` columns begin respectively

\[
(-4-4\phi,1,0,-10-15\phi,-20-30\phi,\ldots)
\]

and

\[
(-3-5\phi,-1,1,-10-15\phi,-20-30\phi,\ldots).
\]

So the coupling does not obstruct the Bugaenko reversal.

The old intrinsic Pell-fold involution `g` of `L9` also has only two
field-linear extensions, but both are nonintegral.  Their `e_y` columns in
the first three coordinates are

\[
\left(\frac{-3+\phi}{2},1,-1-\frac\phi2\right),
\qquad
\left(-\frac{1+\phi}{2},-1,-\frac\phi2\right).
\]

This nonextension is a useful diagnostic, but it is not used in the volume
proof: the intrinsic infinite-volume theorem for the complete fixed chamber
already supplies the contradiction.

## 8. Comparison with the orthogonal carrier

The previous carrier was

\[
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-2\phi&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\oplus E_8\oplus\langle2\rangle.
\]

Its trace determinant was `-44`, with positive complement `<2>+<66>`.  Its
fixed-slice proof used the formula

\[
(r,Jr)=2-(3+\phi)y^2
\]

to make every nonfixed simple pair ultraparallel and redundant.

For `M20`, coupling the last line changes the arithmetic to determinant
`-20`, complement `<2>+<30>`, and pairing formula

\[
(r,Jr)=2-(2+\phi)y^2.
\]

The local ultraparallel argument genuinely fails at `y=1`, producing an
`m=5` pair.  The complete projection theorem shows why this is still
insufficient: every meeting fold rescales to a fixed norm-two root.  The
same infinite `L9` chamber survives as a complete slice, by a different
proof.

This supplies a reusable inverse-design exclusion rule: changing a coupling
is not enough if all projected roots land back in the fixed root system.
A viable next design must produce at least one projected wall that is not
proportional to a fixed norm-two root, and then prove that it cuts the
relevant selected chamber.
