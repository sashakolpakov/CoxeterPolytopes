# Dimension hypotheses and algorithm scope: bounded source check

Checked 15 October 2026. This note separates published theorems, a historical announcement, and the repository's internally reviewed research candidate. It is a bounded primary-source check, not an exhaustive search or a priority claim. Here dimension means the dimension of real hyperbolic space; a Coxeter polytope is finite-sided and has positive finite volume unless stated otherwise.

The noncompact arithmetic row uses the arithmetic reduction to an isotropic rational quadratic form, followed by the reflective integral-lattice classification. Esselmann's abstract states maximal lattice rank 22, corresponding to hyperbolic dimension 21. The dimension 20 gap requires the classification, not merely that rank ceiling. Neither a rational-form identification nor a bound for unimodular lattices proves a verdict for an arbitrary specified lattice. The reduction and the arithmetic finite-covolume setting are also stated in [Belolipetsky–Kapovich, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01282).

For clarity about the last row, the following is an elementary application of Borel–Harish-Chandra, not a reflection-group construction. Over `Q(√2)`, the form `−√2 x₀² + x₁² + … + xₙ²` has Lorentzian distinguished signature and positive-definite conjugate. It is anisotropic over that field, so its integral orthogonal group gives a cocompact arithmetic lattice. Over `Q`, the form `−x₀² + x₁² + … + xₙ²` is isotropic and gives a noncompact arithmetic lattice. These constructions work for every `n ≥ 2`; the Coxeter condition is essential to the finite dimension bounds.

Three different statuses must remain visible. Prokhorov's 995 is a published full-proof baseline. Nikulin's official ICM 1986 report, printed pp.654–659, explicitly discusses finite-covolume reflection groups without an arithmetic restriction and announces `dim H < 300` on p. 659. The inspected text outlines the narrow-facet method and an affine-A calculation; it does not itself supply all explicit affine-product and finite-facet casework used by the repository's later reconstruction. This neither refutes nor erases the announcement. The durable primary source is the [official IMU proceedings, volume 1](https://www.mathunion.org/fileadmin/ICM/Proceedings/ICM 1986.1/ICM 1986.1.ocr.pdf); PDF pages 756–773 correspond to printed pages654–671. The earlier [source audit](dimension_bound_prokhorov_20261015_proof_review.md) records that inspection.

The repository's `n ≤ 169` statement is a separate **internally reviewed research proof candidate**, not a published literature bound. The [four-thirds estimate](fresh_nikulin_unit_charge_20261015.md) has an [independent review](fresh_nikulin_unit_charge_20261015_review.md); the [combined 169 binder](fresh_nikulin_unit_charge_20261015_dimension169.md) records its dependency and verification status. Binder assembly was pending when this literature check was assigned. This note does not certify the binder's final freeze or replace its checks. External mathematical validation, priority, and current-best status remain unestablished. The candidate does not improve the stronger compact or arithmetic ceilings in the table.

The recent papers checked still describe compact examples only through dimension 8 and finite-volume examples through dimension 21. Guo–Ma–Zang–Zheng's July 2026 preprint constructs new noncompact families in dimensions 4,5,6,7,9; it continues to quote the29/995 bounds. Those new dimension 9 examples are noncompact. [Primary preprint, introduction and abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14715). Parkkonen–Paulin's 2026 paper explicitly lists known finite-volume dimensions as `n ≤ 19` or `n = 21`. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1474748026101662). Thus dimension 20 remains an existence gap in the sources checked: a noncompact example would necessarily be nonarithmetic, whereas the compact dimension 20 question is not excluded by Esselmann. This is a report of the inspected sources, not a proof that no later result exists.

Vinberg's geometric procedure applies to a specified discrete reflection group, provided every next-distance mirror can be found. An effective exhaustive root enumeration is an additional requirement; selected cap proposals do not provide it. Allcock's 2021 *An Alternative to Vinberg's Algorithm* explicitly works over `Z`: his lattice is a free abelian group with rational bilinear form, and roots must preserve that lattice. His edgewalking uses binary Lorentzian lattice algorithms to find the actual next corner, beginning at a known corner. It explores that corner's boundary component; Theorem 9.5 identifies finite volume with exhaustion of the complete unexplored-ray queue. These hypotheses are explicit in the introduction, §§2–3, and §9. [Allcock's primary text](https://web.ma.utexas.edu/users/allcock/research/edgewalking3.pdf), [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03784).

Accordingly, the repository's nonarithmetic endpoint constructions are geometric adaptations inspired by edgewalking, not instances of Allcock's lattice algorithm merely because they enumerate spherical endpoint profiles. They require their own exact compatibility, facet actuality, endpoint coverage and volume proofs. An exhaustive profile calculation for specified old edges does not enumerate every possible future mirror or justify a global termination claim. Conversely, a complete exact cone certificate can establish a particular chamber's geometry without invoking Allcock.

Belolipetsky–Kapovich supply an effective termination criterion for Vinberg's algorithm in the arithmetic setting; this is a more recent algorithmic result, but it does not supply the missing global enumeration for an unrestricted nonarithmetic cap search. [Primary paper, §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01282), [published version, São Paulo J. Math. Sci. 18 (2024), 558–568](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40863-022-00350-8).

The bounded check read Allcock and Belolipetsky–Kapovich directly, inspected the 2026 introductions, and revisited the cited historical records and cached official Nikulin excerpt. Some publisher full-text endpoints were unavailable on this pass; no fresh reproduction of Esselmann's complete classification or Vinberg's dimension proof is claimed. All pre-existing repository sources and reports were left unchanged.
