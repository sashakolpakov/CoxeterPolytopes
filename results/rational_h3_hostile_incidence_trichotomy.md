# The rational H3 escape: exact star trichotomy and the surviving tail gate

**Status (2026-08-10): rigorous carrier-relative reduction, not an absolute
rank theorem.**  This note treats only the H3 branch.  H1 (intersecting
square-minus-four curves) and H2 (square-minus-four/square-minus-two
incidence) are excluded from the hypotheses and are being handled
separately.  No Nikulin--Vinberg classification, ABSW theorem, Prasad
formula, or reflective-volume bound is used.

The conclusion is sharp about what the present algebraic-geometric method
does and does not prove.  Every H3 wall produces one of exactly three
packets:

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 Q&\text{packet}&F^2&\text{AG output}\\ \hline
 3&D_4\text{ spherical}&-4&\text{non-klt genus-one exceptional cycle}\\
 4&\widetilde D_4\text{ affine}&0&\text{nef genus-}2/3\text{ fibration if semiample}\\
 >4&\text{hyperbolic star}&>0&\text{nef-big marked curve on the quotient}
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{0.1}
\]

The first packet already contains its apparent extra root, the affine
packet has an uncontrolled abundance/vertical-tail gate, and the
hyperbolic packet has the wrong canonical sign for orbifold semipositivity.
Thus local contraction, adjunction, and Chern inequalities alone do not give
an absolute Picard bound.  What remains is a genuinely global lemma tying
reflection maximality and finite-volume full-nef geometry to the rank of
the exceptional or vertical tail.

## 1. Exact H3 hypotheses

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective rational surface over \(\mathbf C\).  Assume
that its closed Mori cone is rational polyhedral and is generated by the
complete set of smooth rational negative curves of squares

\[
                         -1,\quad -2,\quad -4.                \tag{1.1}
\]

Let \(B_1,\ldots,B_b\) be all square-minus-four generators and put
\(B=\sum B_i\).  In this note assume

\[
 B_i.B_j=0\ (i\ne j),\qquad B.R=0
 \quad\text{for every square-minus-two generator }R.          \tag{1.2}
\]

Thus neither H1 nor H2 occurs.  Fix a square-minus-one generator \(E\) for
which H3 occurs, and set

\[
 t_i=B_i.E,\qquad T=\sum_i t_i=B.E\geq3,\qquad
 Q=\sum_i t_i^2.                                               \tag{1.3}
\]

All \(t_i\) are nonnegative integers.  The standard wall host is

\[
 M=\left\langle\operatorname {NS}(S),B_1/2,\ldots,B_b/2\right\rangle,
 \qquad q_M=2q_{\operatorname {NS}(S)}.                        \tag{1.4}
\]

No maximality assumption is needed for the numerical trichotomy.  When
maximality is discussed below, additionally assume that
\(\mathbf P(\operatorname {Nef}S)\) is literally the supplied finite-volume
Coxeter chamber, that its facet reflections preserve \(M\), and that the
facet group has no proper discrete reflection overgroup in \(O^+(M)\).

## 2. The canonical star divisor

Define

\[
                         F=4E+\sum_i t_iB_i.                  \tag{2.1}
\]

Adjunction and (1.2) give the exact table

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F.B_i&=4t_i-4t_i=0,\\
 F.E&=Q-4,\\
 F^2&=4(Q-4),\\
 K_S.F&=-4+2T.                                                \tag{2.2}
 \end{aligned}
\]

For every other physical generator \(C\), distinct-curve intersections are
nonnegative.  More explicitly,

\[
 F.R=4E.R\geq0,\qquad
 F.E'=4E.E'+\sum_i t_iB_i.E'\geq0                             \tag{2.3}
\]

for every square-minus-two \(R\) and every square-minus-one
\(E'\ne E\).  Completeness of the Mori alphabet therefore proves:

### Proposition 2.1

If \(Q\geq4\), then \(F\) is nef.  It is isotropic for \(Q=4\), and nef and
big for \(Q>4\).

This is a full-Mori statement: checking only the displayed H3 star would
not prove nefness.

There is a simultaneous Coxeter interpretation which uses no root-system
classification.  Put

\[
 e=E,\qquad h_i=B_i/2\in M.
\]

On their span the host form is

\[
 e^2=h_i^2=-2,qquad e.h_i=t_i,qquad h_i.h_j=0\ (i\ne j),    \tag{2.4}
\]

and

\[
 r:=F/2=2e+\sum_i t_ih_i,qquad
 r.h_i=0,\quad r.e=Q-4,\quad r^2=2(Q-4).                    \tag{2.5}
\]

The Schur complement of the leaf block is \((Q-4)/2\).  Hence the star is
negative definite, parabolic of corank one, or Lorentzian according as
\(Q<4\), \(Q=4\), or \(Q>4\).  Since \(T\geq3\), integrality leaves exactly

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 Q=3&(t_i)=(1,1,1),\\
 Q=4&(t_i)=(1,1,1,1),\\
 Q>4&\text{all remaining H3 profiles}.
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{2.6}
\]

If every relevant \(B_i\) is twice primitive in
\(\operatorname {NS}(S)\), each \(t_i\) is even.  Thus the spherical and
affine packets necessarily use primitive square-minus-four classes; the
twice-primitive-only H3 lane starts in \(Q>4\).

## 3. The spherical packet \((T,Q)=(3,3)\)

Here three disjoint boundary curves meet \(E\) once each.  Their intersection
matrix is negative definite.  Its fundamental anti-nef cycle is

\[
                         Z=3E+B_1+B_2+B_3.                   \tag{3.1}
\]

Indeed

\[
 Z.E=0,qquad Z.B_i=-1,qquad Z^2=-3,qquad K_S.Z=3,
 \qquad p_a(Z)=1.                                             \tag{3.2}
\]

The smaller full-support cycle \(2E+\sum B_i\) has positive intersection
with \(E\), so (3.1) is minimal.  Artin's rationality criterion consequently
fails at equality: any contraction of this packet is not a rational, hence
not a quotient or klt, surface singularity.  When the contraction is
\(\mathbf Q\)-Gorenstein, solving the discrepancy equations gives

\[
 K_S=f^*K_Y-2E-B_1-B_2-B_3.                                  \tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the central discrepancy is \(-2\), while the leaves have discrepancy
\(-1\).  This is stronger than merely saying that the natural boundary is
not klt: the contracted surface is not log canonical.  Orbifold
\(\widehat c_2\) semipositivity for klt quotients is therefore unavailable.

Blowing down \(E\) gives an equivalent smooth model with three
square-minus-three curves through one triple point.  Their pairwise
intersections are one, and

\[
 \pi^*(K_{S'}+B_1'+B_2'+B_3')
       =K_S+2E+B_1+B_2+B_3.                                  \tag{3.4}
\]

The triple point, rather than the individual curve squares, records the
non-log-canonical discrepancy.

### 3.1 No automatic new reflection

In (2.4), the four roots \(e,h_1,h_2,h_3\) have the \(D_4\) star Gram
matrix.  The apparent additional root is precisely

\[
                         r=2e+h_1+h_2+h_3,qquad r^2=-2.      \tag{3.5}
\]

It is already generated by the four facet reflections.  Explicitly,
starting from \(h_1\), successive reflection in

\[
                         e,\ h_2,\ h_3,\ e
\]

sends \(h_1\) to \(r\).  Hence \(s_r\) is conjugate to a facet reflection
inside the local group.  Reflection maximality cannot exclude this packet
by adjoining \(s_r\).

The familiar chamber-halving leaf swap is also not automatic in the full
host.  For two leaves put \(v=h_i-h_j\), so \(v^2=-4\).  Its reflection is

\[
                         s_v(x)=x+\frac{(x,v)_M}{2}v.         \tag{3.6}
\]

All half-boundary tests are even.  On \(x\in\operatorname {NS}(S)\), however,

\[
                         (x,v)_M=(B_i-B_j).x.                 \tag{3.7}
\]

Unimodularity therefore gives the exact extension criterion

\[
 s_v\in O(M)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                  B_i\equiv B_j\pmod {2\operatorname {NS}(S)}.             \tag{3.8}
\]

The standard host does not impose (3.8).  Any proposed automatic
\(D_4\)-to-\(F_4\) enlargement must therefore supply a global discriminant
gluing lemma; the local star alone does not do it.

## 4. The affine packet \((T,Q)=(4,4)\)

Here four disjoint boundary curves meet \(E\) once each.  Equations (2.2)
give

\[
 F=4E+B_1+B_2+B_3+B_4,qquad F^2=0,qquad K_S.F=4.            \tag{4.1}
\]

Proposition 2.1 makes \(F\) nef, and (2.5) identifies \(F/2\) as the null
root of the affine \(D_4\) star.  Thus this H3 packet is literally an ideal
nef direction; finite volume does not by itself remove it.

Semiampleness is an additional abundance hypothesis, not a consequence of
the current carrier axioms.  If a multiple of \(F\) is base-point-free,
its Stein factorization is a fibration over \(\mathbf P^1\), since \(S\) is
rational.  Let \(G\) be the primitive general-fiber class and write
\(F\equiv mG\).  Adjunction gives

\[
             m(2g(G)-2)=K_S.F=4.                             \tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently \((m,g)=(1,3)\) or \((2,2)\).  The primitive case is a genus
three fibration; divisibility two gives genus two.

Let \(S_{\min}\) be its relatively minimal smooth model, obtained by
contracting \(k\) vertical square-minus-one curves.  Relative minimality in
genus at least two makes

\[
 K_f=K_{S_{\min}}-f^*K_{\mathbf P^1}=K_{S_{\min}}+2G
\]

nef.  Therefore

\[
 0\leq K_f^2=K_{S_{\min}}^2+8(g-1)
             =10-\rho(S_{\min})+8(g-1).                      \tag{4.3}
\]

This proves the classification-free core bounds

\[
 \rho(S_{\min})\leq18\quad(g=2),\qquad
 \rho(S_{\min})\leq26\quad(g=3).                            \tag{4.4}
\]

But

\[
                         \rho(S)=\rho(S_{\min})+k,            \tag{4.5}
\]

and the carrier axioms currently give no bound on \(k\).  The distinguished
affine star itself already contains the vertical \((-1)\)-curve \(E\), so
\(k\geq1\).  Chains of rational vertical blowups are exactly the sort of
negative-definite finite Coxeter tails which relative minimalization erases.
Local discrepancy and fibration Chern numbers see the core but not their
length.

If \(F\) is not semiample, the method stops one step earlier at the normal
bundle/abundance class of this effective nef isotropic divisor.  Neither
rational polyhedrality of the numerical cone nor the affine Coxeter Gram
matrix proves that this class is torsion.  Thus the affine branch has two
separate missing inputs:

1. semiampleness of the null class \(F\); and
2. an absolute bound on the number \(k\) of vertical exceptional tails.

## 5. The hyperbolic packet \(Q>4\)

Now \(F\) is nef and big.  The disjoint \((-4)\)-curves can still be
contracted projectively, but the weak-incidence positivity argument cannot be
imported verbatim: here

\[
 D=-\left(K_S+B/2\right),\qquad D.E=1-T/2<0.                \tag{5.1}
\]

Instead choose an ample Cartier divisor \(A\) and put

\[
 H=A+\sum_i\frac{A.B_i}{4}B_i.
\]

Then \(H\) is nef and big, \(H.B_i=0\), and \(H.C>0\) for every
irreducible curve \(C\) different from the \(B_i\).  After clearing
denominators write \(L=mH\).  Since the Mori cone has finitely many declared
generators, \(D.B_i=0\), and \(L\) is positive on every other generator, one
can choose \(a\gg0\) so that

\[
                         aL+D
   =aL-\left(K_S+B/2\right)
\]

is nef and big.  Applying the klt surface base-point-free theorem to
\((S,B/2)\) makes \(L\) semiample.  Its connected-fiber morphism contracts
exactly the \(B_i\), giving a projective birational contraction

\[
                         f:S\longrightarrow Y,
\]

so each image point is \(1/4(1,1)\) and

\[
                         K_S=f^*K_Y-B/2.                     \tag{5.2}
\]

If \(\bar E=f(E)\), the pullback identity

\[
                         f^*(4\bar E)=F                      \tag{5.3}
\]

gives

\[
 \bar E^2=-1+\frac Q4>0,qquad
 K_Y.\bar E=\frac T2-1>0.                                    \tag{5.4}
\]

Thus \(\bar E\) is nef and big, but the canonical sign is exactly wrong:

\[
                         (-K_Y).\bar E=1-\frac T2<0.          \tag{5.5}
\]

The IJZ anticanonical orbifold inequality cannot be invoked.  Adding any
effective boundary only increases \((K_Y+\Theta).\bar E\), so it cannot
repair (5.5).  On the smooth carrier the same conclusion follows from the
aggregate coefficient inequality.  Indeed, if \(a_i\) and \(a_E\) are the
boundary coefficients, testing \(B_i\) and \(E\), and dropping only
nonnegative intersections, gives

\[
 a_i\geq\frac12+\frac{t_i a_E}{4},\qquad
 a_E\geq\sum_i t_i a_i-1.
\]

Eliminating the \(a_i\) gives

\[
 \left(1-\frac Q4\right)a_E\geq\frac T2-1,                   \tag{5.6}
\]

which is impossible for \(Q>4\) and an effective coefficient
\(a_E\geq0\).  Hence no klt anticanonical pair on the same carrier can
recover orbifold semipositivity.

The positive class \(F\) also explains why Hodge index alone has the wrong
direction.  Since

\[
 D=-\left(K_S+B/2\right),\qquad D.F=4-2T<0,
\]

Hodge index gives only

\[
 D^2=10-\rho(S)+b
       \leq\frac{(D.F)^2}{F^2}
       =\frac{(T-2)^2}{Q-4}.                                 \tag{5.7}
\]

This is a lower, not an upper, constraint on \(\rho(S)\).

Moreover \(\operatorname {Null}(F)\) is negative definite by Hodge index.
It may contain arbitrarily long spherical root tails at the level of local
intersection theory.  Contracting that null locus lowers both Euler number
and Picard rank by the tail length and therefore erases precisely the datum
one needs to bound.  Since \(-K_Y\) is not nef, there is no compensating
orbifold \(c_2\) sign.

## 6. Exact remaining theorem

The H3 problem is therefore reduced to a global maximality statement, not a
different choice of log coefficient.

> **Missing H3 tail lemma.**  For a reflection-maximal finite-volume literal
> full-nef rational carrier with the standard host, bound absolutely the
> rank of the negative-definite exceptional/vertical tail orthogonal to the
> canonical H3 class \(F\); in the affine case also prove semiampleness of
> \(F\), or replace it by an equally bounded algebraic model.

The qualifications are essential.

* In the spherical packet the highest-root reflection is already in the
  facet group, while triality extensions depend on the mod-two gluing
  condition (3.8).
* In the affine packet ordinary fibration inequalities bound only the
  relatively minimal core, not the vertical blowup count.
* In the hyperbolic packet the marked curve is nef and big but has positive
  canonical degree, so both anticanonical orbifold semipositivity and
  effective-boundary repairs fail.

Accordingly, no universal H3 rank bound follows from the present local
contraction, adjunction, Hodge, or orbifold-Chern package.  Conversely this
note does not construct an unbounded reflection-maximal carrier family:
arbitrary birational tails can destroy the complete wall alphabet,
finite-volume property, or reflection maximality.  The missing lemma must
use those global hypotheses rather than another local surface inequality.
