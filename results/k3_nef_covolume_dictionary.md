# Algebraic meaning of finite divisor-chamber covolume

## Result

Let \(X\) be a complex projective K3 surface with
\(N=\operatorname{NS}(X)\) of rank \(\rho\geq3\).  Write

\[
\mathbb H_N=
\{x\in N_{\mathbb R}:x^2=1,\ x\text{ lies in the ample component}\}
\]

and let \(W_N\) be the group generated by the reflections in the
\((-2)\)-classes.  The normalized nef chamber is

\[
P_X=\operatorname{Nef}(X)\cap\mathbb H_N.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{hyp}}(P_X)<\infty
\iff [O^+(N):W_N]<\infty
\iff |\operatorname{Aut}(X)|<\infty
\iff \operatorname{Eff}(X)\text{ is rational polyhedral}
\iff \operatorname{Cox}(X)\text{ is finitely generated}.}
\]

Thus finite covolume of the full K3 divisor-reflection chamber is visible
in the algebra of the surface: it is precisely the Mori-dream, or
finitely-generated-Cox-ring, condition.

This statement concerns the full nef chamber.  It does **not** apply without
modification to the irrational real-multiplication eigenspace section used in
the Bugaenko construction.  Section 6 gives the appropriate enhanced-pair
formulation for that case.

## 1. From the Weyl chamber to finite automorphisms

The intersection form on \(N\) has signature \((1,\rho-1)\).  The standard
K3 Weyl-chamber theorem says that \(P_X\) is a fundamental chamber for
\(W_N\) on \(\mathbb H_N\).  On the other hand,
\(O^+(N)\) is an arithmetic lattice in \(O^+(1,\rho-1)\), hence

\[
\operatorname{vol}\bigl(O^+(N)\backslash\mathbb H_N\bigr)<\infty.
\]

For a subgroup of a lattice, finite covolume is equivalent to finite index.
Consequently

\[
\operatorname{vol}(P_X)
=[O^+(N):W_N]\,
\operatorname{vol}\bigl(O^+(N)\backslash\mathbb H_N\bigr),
\]

with the left side infinite when the index is infinite.

By global Torelli and Sterk's theorem, the natural map

\[
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\longrightarrow O(N)/W_N
\]

has finite kernel and image of finite index.  Therefore

\[
[O^+(N):W_N]<\infty
\iff |\operatorname{Aut}(X)|<\infty.
\]

References for these statements include Sterk, *Finiteness results for
algebraic K3 surfaces* (1985), and the modern formulation in Bright--Logan--
van Luijk, *Finiteness results for K3 surfaces over arbitrary fields*,
Theorem 1.1.

## 2. The Cox-ring formulation

The Cox ring is the divisor-graded section algebra

\[
\operatorname{Cox}(X)=
\bigoplus_{D\in\operatorname{NS}(X)}
H^0\bigl(X,\mathcal O_X(D)\bigr).
\]

For a K3 surface, Artebani--Hausen--Laface prove that this ring is finitely
generated if and only if the effective cone is rational polyhedral.  Sterk's
cone theorem gives

\[
\operatorname{Eff}(X)\text{ rational polyhedral}
\iff |\operatorname{Aut}(X)|<\infty.
\]

Combining these facts with Section 1 proves the boxed equivalence.  In
particular, finite hyperbolic covolume is not detected by the ordinary
single-polarization section ring alone; it is detected by the simultaneous
multigraded algebra of all divisor sections.

Primary reference: Artebani--Hausen--Laface, *On Cox rings of K3 surfaces*,
Compositio Mathematica 146 (2010), Theorems 2.7 and 2.11.

## 3. The hyperbolic measure comes from section growth

The invariant measure also has an intrinsic intersection-theoretic
description.  For a big and nef real divisor on a surface,

\[
\operatorname{vol}_X(D)
=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{2h^0(X,mD)}{m^2}
=D^2.
\]

On the cone of big and nef classes put

\[
\Phi(D)=-\log\operatorname{vol}_X(D)=-\log(D^2).
\]

At a point \(D^2=1\), tangent vectors to the normalized slice satisfy
\(D\cdot u=0\).  Direct differentiation gives

\[
\frac12\operatorname{Hess}_D(\Phi)(u,v)=-u\cdot v.
\]

The right side is the hyperbolic metric on the unit hyperboloid.  Hence its
volume form is the Hessian measure of the asymptotic section-growth function.
The finite-covolume statement can therefore be phrased without mentioning a
chosen Coxeter diagram:

> The space of unit-volume nef real polarizations on \(X\) has finite Hessian
> volume.

## 4. Quotient volume is a different, weaker statement

Two spaces must not be conflated:

\[
P_X
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
P_X/\operatorname{Aut}(X).
\]

The first is the root chamber itself.  It has finite volume exactly in the
boxed finite-generation situation.  The second is the space of normalized
polarizations up to automorphisms of the fixed surface.  For projective K3
surfaces it is, up to finite coverings, a piece of the finite-volume
arithmetic orbifold

\[
O^+(N)\backslash\mathbb H_N.
\]

Sterk's cone theorem gives it a rational polyhedral fundamental domain even
when \(P_X\) has infinite volume.  Thus an invariant finite measure after
dividing by automorphisms does not say that the reflection group itself has
finite covolume.  Amerik--Verbitsky give the analogous hyperbolic-orbifold
description for irreducible holomorphic symplectic varieties.

## 5. Walls, finite faces, and cusps

The hyperbolic boundary data have direct surface meanings:

| Hyperbolic feature | Algebro-geometric feature |
|---|---|
| wall | an irreducible \((-2)\)-curve |
| finite face | a nef and big class orthogonal to a negative-definite ADE configuration |
| finite vertex | a maximal such contraction configuration |
| ideal ray or cusp | a nonzero nef isotropic class \(F^2=0\) |

A primitive nef isotropic class on a K3 surface gives a genus-one fibration,
and a fibre class gives such a ray.  Under the finite-covolume hypotheses,

\[
P_X\text{ is compact}
\iff X\text{ has no genus-one fibration}.
\]

For \(\rho\geq5\), Meyer supplies a nonzero rational isotropic class and Weyl
reflections move it to the nef boundary.  This recovers the obstruction to an
ordinary compact divisor chamber in hyperbolic dimension at least four in
purely algebro-geometric language: a genus-one pencil creates a cusp.

## 6. The correct invariant for an eigen-nef section

Let \(\tau\) be the self-adjoint integral real-quadratic action used in the
golden construction, and let \(E_\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb R}\) be its
distinguished real eigenspace.  The relevant chamber is

\[
P_{X,\tau,\sigma}
=\{x\in E_\sigma:x^2=1,\ x\cdot C\geq0
\text{ for every irreducible curve }C\subset X\}.
\]

This is the space of unit-volume \(\tau\)-eigen-nef classes, equipped with
the restriction of the Hessian metric in Section 3.  It is an invariant of
the enhanced pair \((X,\tau)\), not of the unadorned surface.

The ordinary Cox ring still encodes it.  Its degree support is

\[
\operatorname{Supp}\operatorname{Cox}(X)
=\{D\in N:H^0(X,\mathcal O_X(D))\neq0\}.
\]

Restrict the corresponding intersection functionals to \(E_\sigma\) and
define the visible effective cone

\[
\operatorname{VisEff}_{\tau,\sigma}(X)
=\overline{\operatorname{cone}}
\{(D,\cdot)|_{E_\sigma}:D\in
\operatorname{Supp}\operatorname{Cox}(X)\}
\subset E_\sigma^*.
\]

Then \(\operatorname{Nef}(X)\cap E_\sigma\) is the dual of this cone inside
the positive component.  Finite generation of the whole Cox ring makes the
visible cone polyhedral, but is much stronger than necessary: a nonpolyhedral
effective cone can have a finite polyhedral shadow on an irrational
eigenspace.  The example track should therefore test the weaker conditions

\[
\operatorname{VisEff}_{\tau,\sigma}(X)
\text{ is finitely generated as a cone}
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{Hess}}(P_{X,\tau,\sigma})<\infty,
\]

followed separately by absence of visible isotropic rays for compactness.
These conditions are not asserted to be a standard named invariant or to
have a known finite-generation theorem.

Finally, the enhancement can itself be made algebro-geometric.  After
tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\), choose divisor bases dual for the intersection
pairing.  A sum of product cycles in
\(CH^2(X\times X)_{\mathbb Q}\) then acts as \(\tau\) on
\(\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\mathbb Q}\).  Thus one may regard the input as a K3
surface equipped with an algebraic correspondence, rather than as a bare
abstract lattice marking.

## Scope

- The boxed equivalence is specific to the full nef chamber of a projective
  K3 surface (and is stated here for \(\rho\geq3\)).
- Finite generation of the Cox ring is not claimed to characterize finite
  volume of an irrational eigen-nef section.
- A finite-volume quotient by automorphisms is not a finite-volume lifted
  root chamber.
- Chern numbers, the Hodge diamond, and \(\chi(\mathcal O_X)\) cannot detect
  this property: they are constant across complex K3 surfaces.  The full
  Neron--Severi intersection lattice and effective-degree data are essential.
