# One order-four K3 action carrying five `H10` Coxeter chambers

## Positive theorem

**TOOT.**  A very general K3 surface in the explicit family

\[
y^2=x^3+A(t^2)x+tC(t^2),
\qquad
\sigma(x,y,t)=(-x,iy,-t),
\]

with

\[
NS(X)=N=U\oplus E_8(-1)\oplus A_1(-1)^2
\]

has one order-four automorphism whose Picard involution carries five
full-rank divisor lattices.  The fixed sections of their effective root
chambers are the five certified rank-12 Coxeter polytopes

```text
H10/12, H10/15, H10/16, H10/38, H10/45.
```

All five are finite-volume noncompact polytopes in hyperbolic dimension ten.
The result adds new algebraic realizations, not new abstract diagrams.  In
particular, `H10/16` and `H10/45` were previously diagram-only slots in the
project; they now have genuine K3 divisor carriers.  The theorem also gives
new common-surface realizations of the other three known chambers.

generated by
[`certify_rank12_five_chamber_simultaneous_carrier.py`](../scripts/certify_rank12_five_chamber_simultaneous_carrier.py).

## The finite-index divisor lattice diamond

In the pinned Brandhorst--Hofmann row `0.4.4.1` marking, the five invariant
lattices are obtained by elementary parity conditions.  They form the
following inclusion pattern:

```text
                         N = U + E8 + A1^2              H10/12
                         /                 \
          U + D8 + A1^2 (C2a)       U + D8 + A1^2 (C2b)
                    H10/15                    H10/16
                    /     \                       /
       U + D4^2 + A1^2     U + D4^2 + A1^2
              H10/38              H10/45
```

The two copies at each lower lattice type are the same abstract lattice but
carry different inherited action/glue pairs.

| fixed section | parity coordinates in the pinned row-39 basis | index in \(N\) | fixed discriminant Smith factors |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `H10/12` | none | 1 | \(1^{10},4\) |
| `H10/15` | 12 even | 2 | \(1^8,2,2,4\) |
| `H10/16` | 10 even | 2 | \(1^8,2,2,4\) |
| `H10/38` | 7 and 12 even | 4 | \(1^6,2^4,4\) |
| `H10/45` | 10 and 12 even | 4 | \(1^6,2^4,4\) |

Every restriction is an integral involutive isometry with characteristic
polynomial

\[
(\lambda-1)^{11}(\lambda+1),
\]

and every algebraic coinvariant is the rootless rank-one lattice
\(\langle-4\rangle\).

## Why one actual automorphism suffices

The simultaneous assertion does not identify five actions merely by their
rank or determinant.  It uses the complete chamber-group censuses.

- The complete `U+E8+A1^2` chamber group has a unique rank-11 involution
  class, which gives `H10/12`.
- The complete `U+D8+A1^2` chamber group has exactly two rank-11 involution
  classes.  Their fixed Smith/\(q\)-histogram fingerprints are precisely the distinct
  `H10/15` and `H10/16` fingerprints.
- The complete `U+D4^2+A1^2` chamber group has exactly two rank-11
  involution classes, subgroup classes 4 and 12.  Their fixed
  Smith/\(q\)-histogram fingerprints are precisely the distinct `H10/38`
  and `H10/45` fingerprints.

Thus, after selecting a root chamber, the exact fixed-lattice fingerprint
forces the required archived chamber action; there is no third rank-11 class
with which it could be confused.  The dedicated `H10/16` and `H10/45`
certificates independently check the complete fixed/coinvariant/glue pair,
including every discriminant element.

Now let \(h_0\) be any ample class on \(X\) and average it over the finite
group generated by \(\sigma\).  The result is an invariant ample class \(h\).
Because the Picard coinvariant is rootless, the fixed positive cone is not
contained in any Picard-root hyperplane.  We may therefore choose \(h\)
generically so that it lies on no root wall.

For each of the five stable lattices \(M_i\), take the unique \(M_i\)-root
chamber containing the same class \(h\).  Since both \(M_i\) and \(h\) are
invariant, that chamber is invariant.  Transporting it to the archived
complete chamber conjugates the induced involution into the complete
chamber group, where the exhaustive uniqueness statement above forces the
desired class.

Finally, orient every square-minus-two wall \(r\) by \(h\cdot r>0\).  K3
Riemann--Roch says that one of \(r\) and \(-r\) is effective; ampleness of
\(h\) forces the positive choice to be effective.  Hence all walls of all
five chambers are effective divisor classes on the same surface.

This proves a reusable finite-index carrier criterion:

> An actual finite-order K3 automorphism, a stable finite-index divisor
> lattice, a rootless algebraic coinvariant, a complete chamber-group
> fingerprint, and a complete Vinberg audit together turn the corresponding
> invariant root chamber into a genuine algebraic Coxeter carrier.

## Scope and the independent monodromy realizations

On this row-39 surface, `H10/12` is the fixed section of the complete
Néron--Severi nef chamber.  The other four are effective finite-index
**divisor-subsystem** chambers: extra roots of \(N\) may refine them.  The
claim is not that all five are full nef chambers of this one surface.

There are also independent equation-to-monodromy realizations of the two
newly geometric slots:

- a `D8` enhancement meridian exchanges the two spinor components and gives
  `H10/16` on a full `U+D8+A1^2` Picard lattice;
- a `D4`-to-`E6` enhancement meridian exchanges two outer components and
  gives `H10/45` on a full `U+D4^2+A1^2` Picard lattice.

Those are parallel-transport actions in algebraic families, not regular
automorphisms of a returned single fiber.  See
[`r12_h10_16_h10_45_monodromy_carriers.md`](r12_h10_16_h10_45_monodromy_carriers.md).

Worldwide priority for these exact algebraic realizations has not been
established.  The abstract Coxeter polytopes and their arithmetic classes
were already present in the project ledger.
