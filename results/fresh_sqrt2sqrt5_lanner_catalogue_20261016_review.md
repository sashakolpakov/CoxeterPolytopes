## Independent arithmetic and catalogue

The reviewer uses the tower Q(√5)(√2), reversing the producer's tower. It
implements exact rational arithmetic, radical membership and signs in that
tower; inversion uses the product of the three other conjugates. Eighty
nonzero test elements spanning both quadratic generators check inversion and
recovery of positive square roots of squares. Those tests supplement the
algebraic formulas; exhaustive radical membership follows from solving the
trace/norm equations, not from these test elements.

The complete angle alphabet follows from φ(m) dividing eight. Prime powers
are generated from divisors of eight, rather than a label cutoff. Moduli
12 and 24 contain √3; the four rejected quartics have explicit elements of
order four in their Galois quotients. The surviving alphabet is
{2,3,4,5,6,8,10}. Every squared finite-angle product and its conjugates are
checked exactly.

All 84 unordered triangles are reconstructed. The cyclic ground-field degree
is computed as the dimension of the rational algebra generated by their
squared products and length-three cyclic product, using rational row
reduction. This differs from the primary Galois-stabilizer test. The complete
status counts, all stored cyclic products, determinants and conjugate signs
agree. Exactly (2,5,8) and (4,5,5) survive with exact ground field F.

The determinant test is legitimate even when individual normalized Gram
entries lie outside F: squared bonds, the triangle cycle, and the determinant
belong to F. All finite-cosine entry fields are totally real. At each
embedding, the positive proper minors together with positive determinant
give positive definiteness, irrespective of the individual off-diagonal
signs. Products of finite 2cos(π/m) values are algebraic integers; no rejection
depends on an assumed ring basis.

## Local geometry and scope

The reviewer generates 8,976 cases by starting with connected order-two or
order-three pieces and adding every cross-edge subset. The producer instead
tests 35,904 unrestricted binary graphs. The independent union-find cycle
test and short-path checks leave the same 16 supports. Both shared-white and
distinct-white configurations are included. No distinguished high edge is
assumed: the (4,5,5) triangle has none.

For a quadrilateral with no marked path of length at most two, minimal
opposite indefinite pieces cannot be orthogonal, cannot share a white node,
and cannot have cross contacts involving a black node. They are therefore
two triangles joined only by their white-white edge. The four opposite black
nodes form two orthogonal positive-definite rank-two blocks.

The reviewer independently evaluates every one of the five possible white
projection costs. Four are strictly greater than two. The fifth, the
white-centered (2,5,8), is impossible because its incident 8-bond together
with the compulsory white bridge gives a nonpositive three-node principal
minor inside a finite corner; the exact upper bound is strictly negative.

Eliminating the black blocks leaves two white diagonal entries strictly
below −1. The off-diagonal remains the original white-white pairing, whose
square is below one. The residual matrix is negative definite. Thus its
principal six-node Gram has two negative directions, contradicting Lorentz
signature. Additional whites cannot remove a negative plane of a principal
submatrix. This is an all-white argument, with no rank cutoff, missing
attachment queue, or assumption about the full star's corank.

A triangular face contains a minimal indefinite principal diagram on all
three black nodes, since every white-plus-two-black subset is positive
definite. With larger Lannér diagrams excluded, this is exactly one of the
two listed triangles. Its nonright bonds pay at least two units under the
distance-two rule. Quadrilaterals pay at least one; larger faces need only
the nonnegativity of weights.

## Counting and published inputs

For a connected finite Coxeter diagram of rank r, the short-pair count is
`(r−1) + sum binomial(degree(v),2)`. Finite diagrams are paths or
single-trivalent trees; this gives at most 2r−2 at every rank. Summing over
components gives a vertex bound of 2n−2. The 77 explicit path/fork checks
through rank 40 verify the formulas but are not an extrapolation premise.

At n=21, the paired h-vector coefficients in f₂/f₀ have minimum 50 and
extreme value 105. Symmetry, nonnegativity and h₀=h₂₁=1 make the average
strictly greater than 50. The required weight per vertex therefore exceeds
`5×50−binomial(21,2)=40`, while the vertex maximum is 40. The reviewer derives
the exact quadratic gaps for all higher odd and even dimensions; their
coefficients are nonnegative. Thus all n≥21 are excluded.

The primary-source inputs were reopened and checked during this review:
[Nikulin, §2 and §3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0708.3991) states that a hyperbolic
principal subset retains the arithmetic ground field and that arithmetic
Lannér diagrams of order at least four have fields only Q, Q(√2), Q(√5).
These are therefore absent for exact field F. The face correspondence,
compact simplicity and absence of affine subdiagrams are the geometric
inputs cited in [Vinberg's paper](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=mmo&option_lang=rus&paperid=444&what=fullt).
The same-field argument can also be checked directly: the nonzero Lannér
determinant is negative physically and positive at all other embeddings,
so no nonidentity ground-field embedding can fix it. It generates the full
ground field. The finite Coxeter classification and the symmetric
nonnegative h-vector theorem remain explicit published inputs.
