# A surviving simple-end B19/B20 quadrilateral over Q(sqrt(3))

19 October 2026. **New local obstruction, awaiting independent review.**

## 1. The proposed deletion and the local survivor

Use the eight-field charge from
`results/fresh_arithmetic_high_label_20261016.md`: one unit for marked
distance at most two, plus one at an entire marked I2(m) component for
m>=6. Its field list includes Q(sqrt(3)).

Let n be 19 or 20. The construction actually works for every n>=4.
Take an entire old B_n vertex, label its simple end A,B, and write
the remaining path as W=(R_3,...,R_n). The only 4-bond is at the far
end R_(n-1),R_n, so W is B_(n-2). All old nodes except the far short
root have squared norm 2; that short root has squared norm 1. Every
old consecutive inner product is -1. This is the usual integral B_n
Gram matrix, with a simple A-B bond.

Put r=sqrt(3), K=Q(r), and add two roots C,D as follows:

    C^2=2(3+r),       (A,C)=-(3+r),
    D^2=2+r,         (B,D)=-(2+r).

Every other product of C or D with an old root is zero, and (C,D)=0.
All root norms are totally positive. Every Gram entry belongs to
O_K=Z[r]. The cyclic order of the intended two-face is A,B,C,D.

After unit normalization, the opposite products are -x,-y, where

    x^2=(3+r)/4>1,       y^2=(2+r)/2>1.                 (1)

Thus both opposite pairs are ultraparallel. The other three black
adjacencies B-C,C-D,D-A are right. No finite bond of label at least
six is introduced at any of the four finite corners below.

## 2. Exact rank and arithmetic admissibility

The inverse of the raw B_(n-2) white matrix has entry 1 at its simple
end. Eliminating W therefore leaves A^2=2, B^2=1, and (A,B)=-1.
C,D retain the displayed norms and contacts.

Now eliminate the positive diagonal C,D block. The residual A,B matrix
is

    [ (1-r)/2     -1   ]
    [    -1      -1-r  ].                              (2)

Its determinant is zero because ((1-r)/2)(-1-r)=1, and its trace is
negative. Hence the full (n+2)-root Gram has inertia

    (n positive, 1 negative, 1 zero).

Under the nonidentity embedding r -> -r, the corresponding residual
matrix is

    [ (1+r)/2     -1   ]
    [    -1       r-1  ].                              (3)

It has positive diagonal, determinant zero, and rank one. The white
block and the C,D diagonals remain positive. Consequently the full
conjugate Gram is positive semidefinite of rank n+1.

There is also a particularly transparent full radical vector. Its
coefficient at A is 2, at C is 1, and at EVERY other old root and at
D is 1-r. Direct multiplication by the displayed Gram gives zero.
All coefficients are nonzero. After conjugation they are all positive.
Since the conjugate Gram is positive semidefinite with one-dimensional
radical, every proper principal conjugate matrix is positive definite:
a zero vector supported on a proper subset would have to be a multiple
of that full-support radical vector.

The unit cyclic products are integral:

    4x^2=3+r,       4y^2=4+2r,

and the old simple and double bonds have squared cyclic products 1
and 2. The entire diagram is a tree, so every closed-walk product is
generated by these squared edge products. The ground field generated
by the cyclic products is exactly K.

In fact the construction meets a stronger integral-reflector test.
For each nonorthogonal pair, both Cartan ratios 2(G_i,G_j)/G_i^2
are integral. The new ones, in the two possible directions, are

    A,C:  -(3+r), -1;
    B,D:  -(2+r), -2.

The old B_n ratios are -1 or -2. Thus the reflections preserve the
O_K module generated by these roots in the nondegenerate K-quotient
by the radical. The resulting K-form is Lorentz at the physical
embedding and positive definite at the other one. This is an explicit
integral local reflector model; no ambiguity about whether unit-normal
entries themselves lie in K is being suppressed.

These statements are necessary arithmetic and reflection-lattice
properties. They do not assert that this reflection subgroup has
finite index or finite covolume.

## 3. The four corners and the actual compact quad

The four consecutive white-plus-pair diagrams are precisely

| Black pair | Entire finite corner forest | Marked baseline |
|---|---|---:|
| A,B | B_n | 1 |
| B,C | B_(n-1)+A1 | 0 |
| C,D | B_(n-2)+A1+A1 | 0 |
| D,A | B_(n-2)+A1+A1 | 0 |

Each matrix is positive definite. For example C is isolated in the
second corner, and both black roots are isolated from W and one
another in the last two corners. None has a marked I2(m>=6) component.

This is more than a formal Gram pattern. Realize the physical Gram
in Lorentz space of signature (n,1). It is connected and all its
off-diagonal entries are nonpositive. An eigenvector for its negative
eigenvalue can be chosen strictly positive, by applying the Perron
argument to a sufficiently large scalar matrix minus the Gram.
Its root combination is a timelike strict interior point for all
n+2 halfspaces. Thus their intersection has nonempty hyperbolic
interior.

The standard all-wall acute projection argument applied to each of
the four positive-definite n-root corners gives an actual finite
vertex with all unselected wall inequalities strict. In particular
all n+2 walls are actual facets. The old white W cuts out an actual
two-face, since its positive-definite projection is feasible against
every other wall. In its Lorentz three-space the four remaining
halfplanes have the four finite consecutive intersections listed
above. The opposite pairs are ultraparallel by (1). These four
vertices and four sides therefore form the actual compact
quadrilateral; no additional inequality is present in this local
chamber to truncate it.

Its old A,B angle is pi/4: the projected raw norms are 2 and 1 and
their product is -1. The other three angles are pi/2. Hence it is
a compact Lambert quadrilateral. This intrinsic pi/4 angle should
not be confused with the old ambient A-B bond, which has label 3.

The surrounding n-dimensional chamber's volume is not determined
here. In particular the construction does not claim a compact or
finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter polytope in dimensions 19 or 20.
It gives an actual compact local two-face in a realizable acute
Coxeter halfspace system whose integral arithmetic data pass the
stated physical and nonphysical tests.

Thus a naked simple-end adjacent B_n refund cannot be justified by
finite-corner classification, absence of affine principal diagrams,

This identifies an inexpensive possible recipient, not a valid

## 5. A contrasting A19/A20 subcase that does close

For comparison, suppose the old whole diagram is A_n, again with
simple-end pair a,b, but BOTH added opposite walls c,d are
perpendicular to W=A_(n-2), and all other black adjacencies are right.
Put -(a,c)=x, -(b,d)=y after unit normalization. Let

    t=n/[2(n-1)],       z=4x^2,       w=4y^2.

The white residual old norms are 1,t. Opposite-side indefiniteness
and compactness give x>1 and y^2>t. The corank-one equation is

    (z-4)[(n-1)w-2n]=4(n-1).                          (4)

Cyclic integrality gives z,w in O_K. Equation (4), reduced modulo
(n-1), implies 2(z-4) belongs to (n-1)O_K. Therefore

    delta=z-4 belongs to D O_K,
    D=(n-1)/gcd(2,n-1), equal to 9 or 19.

The allowed nonright ambient cosine or dotted product y has
y^2>=cos^2(pi/5): labels 3 and 4 are too small for y^2>t>1/2.
Since t<=19/36 and cos^2(pi/5)=(3+sqrt(5))/8>23/36,

    0<delta=1/(y^2-t)<9.

At every nonphysical ground-field embedding, the old-A_n-plus-c
independent determinant ratio is positive. Its projection cost is
2n x^2/(n+1), so

    0<z'<2(n+1)/n<4, hence |delta'|<4.

All conjugates of the nonzero algebraic integer delta/D therefore
have absolute value strictly below one. Their product cannot be a
nonzero integer, a contradiction. This excludes the isolated/isolated
A_n subcase at n=19,20 without a field list. It does not exhaust other
white attachments or simultaneous endpoint deletions, and is not a
full A_n refund.
