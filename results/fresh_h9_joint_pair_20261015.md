# A joint finite corner in H9, plus an alternative polar cut

Two exact walls can be added together to the 30-wall chamber. Their mutual angle is π/3, and they create a finite vertex that uses both new walls. The resulting **32-wall chamber still has infinite volume**, certified by a new strict escape. An alternative single polar wall produces a different 31-wall chamber, also of infinite volume.

The joint construction supplies a concrete configuration beyond extending an existing finite corner along one edge. It does not establish a compact H9 polytope, a new finite-volume example, or a new dimension.

## Rank-seven proposals

The [scout](../scripts/fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_scout.py) works over seven old walls with positive-definite Gram matrix, representing a two-dimensional face. It prescribes a spherical contact profile for a new wall, chooses two more old finite-angle contacts, and solves the norm-two quadratic in the remaining three-dimensional Lorentz space. There is no requirement that the new wall by itself terminate an old edge.

The first phase uses the twelve positive-definite seven-wall subsets of the current escape's nine active walls. The old-anchor labels are sampled from `2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12,16,24,32,64`; unrestricted dihedral tails are sampled, not exhausted. A right-contact-only run saves 412 proposal records; the fuller contact-profile run saves 4,566. These are numerical banks and can contain geometrically duplicate records. All finite-angle fits use labels through 256 and a tolerance, so they are proposals rather than certificates. Numerical singular cases are explicitly counted.

Both scout generation and saved replay pass. The [pair screen](fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_pairs.json) finds 40 finite-angle fits in the fuller bank. Most need not be exact; two low-label records describe the same promising pair. The separate exact reconstruction below certifies that pair. The banks, recipes and numerical scope remain available in the [right-contact scout](fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_scout_escape_right.json), [full scout](fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_scout_escape.json), and [pair-screen script](../scripts/fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_pairs.py).

## Exact joint pair

The [two-cap certificate](fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_twocaps.json) and [checker](../scripts/fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_twocaps.py) use the existing quartic field

    K = Q(√5, √D),    D = 6 + (16/5)√5.

Both walls have square two. On the common independent contact set

    I = [0,1,2,3,5,6,7,10,29],

the first wall has label 5 at old wall 7 and label 3 at 29; the second has label 5 at old walls 3 and 7, and label 3 at 5. Every other contact in I is right. Each linear system leaves a single quadratic parameter. The required positive roots are exactly

    ((30471 + 7413√5)/18764)√D,
    ((7236 + 4059√5)/4691)√D.

The checker squares these roots, reconstructs both normals, verifies their norm and all 60 old-wall relations, and proves their mutual pairing is −1. Both cut the old strict escape. Strict-center projections provide actual facet witnesses including the other new wall. No extra coefficient-field extension is needed.

The [complete 32-wall certificate](fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_two_extension.json) is generated by the [global checker](../scripts/fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_two_extension.py). It checks all 496 pairs and all 32 actual facets, and reconstructs the complete cone:

| Quantity | Count |
|---|---:|
| Finite vertices | 90 |
| Ideal vertices | 0 |
| Hyperideal extreme rays | 882 |
| Finite edges | 250 |
| Open directions at finite vertices | 310 |

All 89 old finite vertices persist. The unique new finite vertex has incidence

    [0,1,2,5,6,10,29,30,31].

It requires both new walls. It has no finite edge to an old finite vertex: its nine directions remain open. Thus the construction creates a new finite corner separated from the old finite-edge graph, rather than simply extending an existing edge. The complete geometry and strict escape are subject to the [independent two-cut review](fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_two_review.md), which reconstructs the old cone and clips it twice using separate arithmetic.

## Alternative polar cut

The strongest right-contact proposal is the polar of the old escape's hyperideal ray, active at

    [0,1,2,3,5,6,7,10,29].

Its normal is kept at its exact positive square; no square-root rescaling is necessary. The [polar checker](../scripts/fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_polar.py) proves nine right contacts, 21 strictly disjoint hyperplanes, an actual facet, and a positive pairing with the old strict escape.

The [31-wall global certificate](fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_extension.json) and [independent review](fresh_h9_joint_pair_20261015_review.md) agree on 90 finite vertices, no ideal vertices, 858 hyperideal rays, 251 finite edges and 308 open directions. Its unique new vertex is `[0,1,2,3,5,6,7,10,30]`, closing one old edge. A new exact strict escape proves infinite volume.

This polar cannot be combined with the selected joint pair. The 31- and 32-wall results are alternative extensions of the same frozen 30-wall chamber. The fact that the polar closes an old edge also shows that the earlier bounded edge scouts were not exhaustive over all caps; their zero-cutter results remain bounded diagnostics.

## Next construction task

The joint pair realizes the intended new mechanism, but it does not reduce the global problem to a finite closed edge graph. The new corner's open directions, the saved escape, and changed retained geometry are explicit next targets. Extra facets or a larger finite-vertex count alone do not establish finite volume. Preserve the independent cone certificates and use a fresh namespace for subsequent extensions.
