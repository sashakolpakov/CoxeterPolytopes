# A P-first prime-binomial Coble pilot for the canonical-K3 rank bound

**Status (2026-08-10): `FAIL_NO_SHARED_RAW_RECOGNIZER`; exact strict-L4
target, four separately routed calibrations, and one isolated missing
lemma.  The current compilers do not prove that the four rows belong to one
invariant raw class.**  The terminal pushback would use only the canonical double
cover and the Picard-rank bound for a K3 surface.  It uses no
Nikulin--Vinberg dimension/classification list, no Kond\=o nine-type list,
and no arithmetic-volume theorem.

The main conclusion is negative but useful:

> The existing K3-first promotion theorem cannot give the requested rank
> pushback: primitive K3 embeddability and `rank <= 20` occur in its input
> gates, so the K3 rank conclusion is tautological there.  A genuine pilot
> must first construct a strong Coble surface from raw `P` and only then form
> its canonical K3 cover.

The broadest *candidate* uniform witness-free subclass presently visible is
the **raw prime-binomial contraction class** defined below.  Its proposed class definition
does not bound the boundary count, mention a surface, ask for a K3 embedding,
or supply a coordinate point.  Four distinct archived Coxeter inputs exhibit
prime-binomial branches only after separate routers have selected and
normalized them.  This does **not** prove that they pass one common raw
recognizer.  What is missing is one raw-to-atlas lemma making those branch
calculations uniform rather than four manually routed calibrations.

## 1. The raw class target

Start with a marked oriented integral finite-volume Coxeter datum `(P,h)`.
Apply the boundary de-scaling and all-contraction algorithms of
`D_CobVin`.  In particular, a raw branch recovers

\[
 N\simeq I_{1,9+b},\qquad B_i^2=-4,\qquad
 B_iB_j=0,\qquad K=-\frac12\sum_iB_i\in N,
 \tag{1.1}
\]

and the raw walls have the numerical `(-4),(-2),(-1)` alphabet.  No upper
bound on `b` is imposed.

For every numerical contraction branch to `P2` or `F_e`, fix the projective
gauge by a deterministic rule and construct, only from the Gram matrix,
wall coordinates and proximity table,

\[
 A_q=\mathbf Z[1/2N_q][t_1^{\pm1},\ldots,t_s^{\pm1}],\quad
 J_q\subset A_q,\quad \Delta_q\in A_q.                \tag{1.2}
\]

Here `J_q` is generated by the universal incidence, jet, kernel and final
anti-/bi-anticanonical rank equations; `Delta_q` is the product of all
collision, unwanted-incidence, reducibility, singularity and rank-drop
functions.  Both are compiler output.  No coordinates or surface equations
are supplied with `P`.

Call `q` **primitive-binomial regular** when the following finite symbolic
checks pass.

1. After saturation by `Delta_q`, one component has

   \[
       J_q^{\rm sat}=I_L+(p),                            \tag{1.3}
   \]

   where `p=0` or an odd prime and `I_L` is a translated lattice ideal whose
   exponent lattice `L` is saturated.  Smith normal form checks saturation
   and the circuit products check consistency of the translating character.
2. `Delta_q` is nonzero modulo `I_L+(p)`.  This is an exact normal-form
   calculation, not the assertion that a geometric point has been found.
3. The compiler-generated universal curve kernels have the prescribed rank,
   and the final two evaluation matrices have

   ```text
   h0(-K)=0,   h0(-2K)=1,
   ```

   identically on `D(Delta_q)`; their required and forbidden minors reduce
   respectively to nonzero characters and zero modulo (1.3).
4. The raw cone/root/cusp return certificate required by `D_CobVin` passes.
   This is a calculation on the original lattice and cone; it is not a
   surface label or a classified diagram.

Let `C_PB` denote the **target** raw Coxeter class for which the canonical all-contraction
compiler produces at least one branch whose **syntactic dependency grammar**
is primitive-binomial and whose exponent/minor data satisfy the four checks
above.  Membership uses one fixed algorithm on arbitrary raw input.  It may
not read a Kond\=o type, a source name, an archived output Gram, a supplied
coordinate point, or a claimed carrier.

This would be the largest structural subclass supported by the current
lattice-determinantal idea: arbitrary numbers of variables, primitive
circuits, characteristics and contraction lengths are allowed.  It is
strictly stronger than mere incidence-stack nonemptiness and is not defined
as the compiler-positive locus.

| `H17/48` | `lambda+1=0`, with `3=0` forced by the projectivity circuit | characteristic three | `8 / 17` |

They are not yet calibrations of one implemented grammar.  The current

The *proposed* class allows every boundary count `b>=1`; in particular neither

The two characteristic-zero rows show that a future common recognizer could
be non-singleton without crystalline input.  The two characteristic-three rows show that vertical

### Exact hostile counterexamples to the naive common criterion

“The selected component is a translated saturated prime-binomial variety”
is not a sufficient invariant criterion.

* In the `H10/30` calculation, `c=1` is a translated saturated
  prime-binomial point just as `c=-1` is, but it collides two raw-derived
  centers (`C=B`).
* In the `H11/26` calculation, `a=-b` is a translated saturated
  prime-binomial component just as `a=b` is, but its alleged boundary is a
  line times the square of a conic and is not reduced irreducible geometry.

Thus the exponent lattice and prime support do not distinguish the good
branches.  Collision, Jacobian, irreducibility and Hilbert bad factors must
be generated invariantly from raw P.  At present the candidate script reads
which factor is good from the row-specific upstream archives.  These two bad
components are rigorous false positives for the only common criterion that
has actually been frozen.

The repository does contain a genuine arbitrary-input Coble finite-geometry
recognizer.  It recognizes five rigid cores and includes the `H14/60` and
`H17/48` rows, but not `H10/30` or `H11/26`.  Its recognized boundary counts
are already `{1,2,5,8}` and its largest dimension is `17`; the K3 ceiling
`n<=19` removes no member.  It therefore supplies no substitute L4 class.

## 3. The conditional strict-L4 theorem (not presently instantiated)

Assume the Raw Prime-Binomial Atlas Lemma in Section 6.  Then every
`P in C_PB` has a geometric branch over an algebraically closed field of
the support characteristic.  Its iterated blowup is a smooth rational
surface `S`, the final Hilbert ranks give

\[
 |-K_S|=\varnothing,\qquad |-2K_S|=\{B_1+\cdots+B_b\}, \tag{3.1}
\]

and hence `S` is a strong Coble surface.  The raw return certificate and the
already proved contraction/root/cusp theorem give the literal equality

\[
            \mathbf P(\operatorname {Nef}S)=P            \tag{3.2}
\]

with the original Gram matrix, orientation, complete wall set and cusp
data.

Now, and only now, form the double cover

\[
             \pi:X\longrightarrow S
\]

branched over the reduced divisor in (3.1).  It is a smooth K3 surface and

\[
 \widetilde N(2)=
 2\left\langle\operatorname {Pic}(S),B_1/2,\ldots,B_b/2\right\rangle
 \hookrightarrow \operatorname {NS}(X),\qquad
 \operatorname {rk}\widetilde N=10+b.                  \tag{3.3}
\]

Because (3.2) is full-dimensional,

\[
              n=\dim P=\rho(S)-1=9+b.                  \tag{3.4}
\]

The K3 Picard bound therefore pushes back to the original raw polytope:

\[
 \boxed{\begin{array}{ll}
 \operatorname {char}k=0:& b\le10,\quad n\le19,\\
 \operatorname {char}k=p\ne2:& b\le12,\quad n\le21.
 \end{array}}                                           \tag{3.5}
\]

If `PB-Atlas` is proved, this delta will not be tautological.  Neither the target raw class nor the rational
surface construction contains a K3 lattice, a primitive K3 embedding, or a
rank ceiling.  Deleting the canonical-cover argument deletes (3.5).

The conclusion is weaker than the classified finite-automorphism Coble bound
`n<=17`, but it is methodologically cleaner: it is a P-first dimension
theorem derived from the canonical cover alone.  No finite list of surfaces
or polytopes is used.

## 4. Why the K3-first route cannot substitute for this route

The finite-root K3 promotion theorem asks at its first gate that the
unfolded lattice have rank at most `20` and admit a primitive equivariant K3
embedding.  Applying `rho(X)<=20` afterward merely repeats that input.

Likewise, defining a class by `the K3 period locus is nonempty`, by
`there is a complete K3 root chamber`, or by `the cover compiler passes`
would turn the desired conclusion into membership.  Such classes remain
valid recognizers but have zero P-side AG leverage.

The rational-surface order is indispensable:

```text
raw P
  -> automatically nonempty blowup/incidence branch
  -> strong Coble S
  -> exact same P = P(Nef S)
  -> canonical K3 cover X
  -> K3 rank
  -> bound on the original P.
```

inside the present claim.

## 6. The one exact missing lemma

> **Raw Prime-Binomial Atlas Lemma (`PB-Atlas`).**  There is a deterministic
> algorithm which, from the Gram matrix, integral wall coordinates, strict
> interior vector and one numerical all-contraction branch, constructs
> `(A_q,J_q,Delta_q)` in (1.2) and proves the following without a supplied
> geometric point.  If the branch passes the primitive-binomial regularity
> checks of Section 1, then
>
> \[
> D(\Delta_q)\cap V(J_q)\longrightarrow
> \mathcal C_{\rm CobVin}(P,h)_q                         \tag{6.1}
> \]
>
> is a nonempty open atlas onto the required incidence/Hilbert stratum.
> Every point of its geometric fibers yields all and only the prescribed
> centers and smooth irreducible wall curves, and the final boundary is
> reduced, disjoint and uniquely bi-anticanonical.

The lemma is stronger than “the stack is nonempty”: its hypotheses are a
fixed finite normal-form calculation on raw P-derived equations.  It is
weaker than a realization theorem for arbitrary incidence schemes, since it
claims only the prime-binomial grammar.

Once `PB-Atlas` is proved, no further existence, period or classification
lemma is needed for (3.5).

## 7. Concrete finite proof plan

1. **Canonical contraction schema.**  Canonically order every contraction
   sequence, quotient by lattice automorphisms fixing `(P,h)`, and emit the
   proximity DAG and divisor coefficient table.  Compare two independent
   orderings to ensure the output is invariant rather than archive-labelled.
2. **Universal coordinate atlas.**  Fix standard gauges on `P2` and `F_e`.
   Inductively express proper and infinitely-near centers and every raw wall
   section.  Prove by induction that the coordinate construction represents
   the corresponding Hilbert/proximity functor on `D(Delta_q)`.
3. **Exact ideal generation.**  Generate incidence, jet, kernel, Jacobian,
   `-K` and `-2K` matrices from the divisor table.  Saturate by `Delta_q` and
   compute a certified primary decomposition.  No hand-entered equation is
   permitted after the raw branch is loaded.
4. **Prime-binomial certificate.**  On every candidate component, use Smith
   normal form for the exponent lattice, circuit products for the translating
   character, and Groebner normal forms for all required minors and avoidance
   factors.  This proves that `V(I_L+(p))` is an irreducible translated torus
   and that its required open is nonempty.
5. **Geometric handoff.**  Prove smoothness, irreducibility, distinctness and
   the Hilbert ranks from the universal minors.  Reverse the blowups and
   identify the resulting marked Picard lattice coefficientwise with `N`.
   Invoke the existing exact-return theorem only after this step.
6. **Four positive and three hostile replays.**  Recover, without stored
   equations, the four components in Section 2.  Negative controls must
   include the discarded `c=1` collision component, the reducible `a=-b`
   component, and classical `P10`, whose raw local Coble tests must not be
   promoted accidentally.
7. **Rank pushback test.**  Run the same grammar with no boundary cap.  After
   construction, apply only (3.3) and the K3 Picard ceiling, and verify that
   the program rejects `b>=11` over characteristic zero and `b>=13` in
   positive characteristic for the AG reason, not by a P-side filter.

The work is finite: all contraction branches, polynomial matrices, lattice
ideals, Smith forms, saturations and bad factors are finite for fixed `P`.
The only theorem debt is the uniform correctness of the generated atlas,
not an unbounded surface search.
