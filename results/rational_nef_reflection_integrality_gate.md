# The rational-nef reflection-integrality gate

**Status (2026-08-10): proved classification-free theorem and hostile
counterexample.**  The conclusion is sharp at the level of the stated
hypotheses.  No Nikulin--Vinberg classification, Kond\=o classification,
ABSW, Prasad formula, or arithmetic-volume bound is used.

The answer is not that reflection maximality manufactures the Coble
alphabet.  On the actual N\'eron--Severi lattice, reflection integrality does
force a small *primitive-normal* alphabet.  A Coble-style half-boundary
extension retains only parity information.  The missing datum is the single
geometric equation

\[
                         K_S+\frac12B=0.                    \tag{0.1}
\]

Moreover, the apparent standard-coefficient generalization with boundary
squares `-2m` does not produce new disjoint rational branches: its canonical
adapted cover and Noether's formula force every `m` to equal `2`.

## 0. Scope and logical gates

The assertions below deliberately separate five hypotheses that a raw
compiler must not conflate.

1. **Full-nef completeness and effectivity.**  The local lattice sieve
   applies to each wall already proved to be the ray of a smooth rational
   negative curve.  The rank-nine conclusion additionally requires those
   curves to generate the *complete* Mori cone.  A Gram matrix alone proves
   neither effectivity nor completeness.
2. **Physical normalization.**  If \(r\) is the primitive integral vector
   on a wall ray, the physical curve can be \(C=d r\).  Reflection
   integrality concerns \(r\); adjunction is what constrains \(d\).
3. **Arithmetic module.**  Preservation of \(\operatorname {NS}(S)\),
   preservation of the specified half-boundary extension, and preservation
   of an arbitrary commensurable lattice have genuinely different
   consequences.  They are treated in Sections 1, 3, and 4, respectively.
4. **Reflection maximality.**  Maximal means no proper discrete reflection
   supergroup in the same hyperbolic isometry group.  The counterexample in
   Section 3 satisfies this strong, lattice-independent version.
5. **Characteristic and cover gate.**  Sections 1--4 use only intersection
   theory and surface Riemann--Roch.  Section 5 assumes that the
   characteristic is prime to the adapted-cover degree \(\ell\).  Its
   connectedness is proved by an explicit gcd calculation, its smoothness
   uses disjoint branch curves and tameness, and only then are the canonical
   and Noether formulas applied.

## 1. The unimodular gate

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective rational surface over an algebraically
closed field and put \(L=\operatorname {NS}(S)\).  Thus \(L\) is unimodular,
\(\operatorname {Pic}(S)=L\), and \(K_S\) is characteristic:

\[
                    K_S\mathbin\cdot x\equiv x^2\pmod2       \tag{1.1}
\]

for every \(x\in L\).  The last assertion is just surface Riemann--Roch, not
a lattice classification.

Suppose a nef facet is cut out by a smooth rational negative curve \(C\).
Write

\[
                  C=d r,\qquad d\geq1,                         \tag{1.2}
\]

where \(r\in L\) is the primitive integral vector on the same ray.  Assume
that the hyperplane reflection

\[
            s_r(x)=x-\frac{2(x,r)}{r^2}r                       \tag{1.3}
\]

preserves \(L\).

### Theorem 1.1 (sharp integral alphabet)

Exactly the following numerical possibilities survive:

| \(r^2\) | \(d\) | \(C^2\) | \(K_S.r\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `-1` | 1 | `-1` | `-1` |
| `-2` | 1 | `-2` | `0` |
| `-1` | 2 | `-4` | `1` |

In particular:

1. if every physical facet class is primitive in \(L\), reflection
   integrality on \(L\) proves the weak-anticanonical `(-1),(-2)` alphabet;
2. without physical primitivity, the only additional smooth-rational case
   is a twice-primitive `(-4)`-curve \(C=2r\);
3. the tempting case \(C=2r, r^2=-2, C^2=-8\) is impossible.

#### Proof

For a primitive vector in a unimodular lattice,
\(\operatorname {div}(r)=1\).  Integrality of (1.3) therefore says

\[
                         -r^2\mid2,                             \tag{1.4}
\]

so \(r^2=-1\) or \(-2\).  Adjunction gives

\[
                    d(K_S.r)+d^2r^2=-2.                         \tag{1.5}
\]

Thus \(d\mid2\).  For \(d=1\), (1.5) gives the first two rows.  For \(d=2\),
it gives \(K_S.r=1\) when \(r^2=-1\), and \(K_S.r=3\) when \(r^2=-2\).
The latter contradicts (1.1), because \(3\not\equiv-2\pmod2\).  This leaves
exactly the table. \(\square\)

Smoothness and rationality of the facet curve are doing real work here.  For
a singular curve, adjunction contains \(2p_a(C)-2\), and the divisibility
argument no longer gives \(d\mid2\).

### Corollary 1.2 (the direct rank-nine branch)

If the *complete* Mori cone is generated by the primitive physical facet
curves in Theorem 1.1, then every generator is a `(-1)`- or `(-2)`-curve.
Adjunction makes \(-K_S\) pair with them by `1` or `0`, respectively.  Hence
\(-K_S\) is nef, \(K_S^2\geq0\), and

\[
                       \rho(S)=10-K_S^2\leq10.                 \tag{1.6}
\]

Thus the projectivized full nef chamber has dimension at most `9`.  The
completeness qualification is essential: knowing only the negative
hyperbolic facets does not automatically control a hidden isotropic Mori
generator at a cusp.  More generally, an injected full trace block gives

\[
                         [k:\mathbf Q](n+1)\leq10.              \tag{1.7}
\]

## 2. What is still missing in the `(-4)` case

Let \(B_1,\ldots,B_b\) be all physical `(-4)` facet curves and put
\(B=\sum B_i\).  Assume every other physical facet is a `(-1)`- or
`(-2)`-curve and that the facet curves span numerical curve space.  Then the
following are equivalent:

1. \(K_S+B/2\equiv0\);
2. the boundary curves are pairwise disjoint and every other facet \(C\)
   obeys

   \[
   (C^2,B.C)=(-2,0)\quad\hbox{or}\quad(-1,2).                  \tag{2.1}
   \]

Indeed, adjunction says \(K_S.B_i=2\), \(K_S.C=0\) for a `(-2)`-curve, and
\(K_S.C=-1\) for a `(-1)`-curve.  Orthogonality of \(K_S+B/2\) to the
displayed spanning set is exactly (2.1); on a rational surface numerical
triviality is linear triviality.  Conversely, if \(B=-2K_S\), then

\[
 B.B_i=-2K_S.B_i=-4.
\]

Since \(B_i^2=-4\) and distinct curves have nonnegative intersection, all
other \(B_j.B_i\) vanish.  The same identity and adjunction give (2.1).

Thus the complete Coble incidence table is equivalent to one linear
log-Calabi--Yau certificate.  Reflection integrality by itself does not
prove that certificate.

### Proposition 2.2 (the coherent fixed-class reformulation)

Put \(L_{\log}=K_S+B/2\).  For any facet normal \(v\),

\[
                 s_v(L_{\log})=L_{\log}
                 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L_{\log}.v=0.  \tag{2.2}
\]

Because a physical facet curve and its primitive normal span the same ray,
adjunction translates (2.2) exactly as follows:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{facet species}&\text{condition for fixing }L_{\log}\\ \hline
B_i^2=-4&\displaystyle\sum_{j\ne i}B_i.B_j=0,\\[2mm]
R^2=-2&B.R=0,\\
E^2=-1&B.E=2.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{2.3}
\]

Nonnegative intersection of distinct curves makes the first row equivalent
to pairwise disjointness.  Hence the complete index-two Mori alphabet is
equivalent to coherent preservation of one log-canonical class by *all*
facet reflections.  If the primitive normals span
\(\operatorname {NS}(S)_{\mathbf Q}\), their common fixed vector
\(L_{\log}\) must be zero, recovering \(B=-2K_S\).

For one twice-primitive boundary \(B=2r\subset L\), this can be seen
directly.  Here \(r^2=-1\), \(K_S.r=1\), and

\[
 s_r(K_S+r)=K_S+r;
\]

a `(-2)` reflection fixes \(K_S+r\) exactly when \(B.R=0\), and a `(-1)`
reflection fixes it exactly when \(B.E=2\).  For a primitive physical
boundary, the identical statement lives on the half-boundary extension
with \(r=B/2\).  This supplies a useful compiler certificate, but it is an
extra common-invariant test; reflection maximality does not imply it.

## 3. The half-boundary extension gives parity, not incidence

Suppose now that a physical boundary \(B_i\) is primitive of square `-4`,
so its reflection is not integral on the unimodular lattice \(L\).  Form the
Coble-style module

\[
 \widetilde L=\left\langle L,\frac{B_1}{2},\ldots,
                    \frac{B_b}{2}\right\rangle,
 \qquad (\ ,\ )_{\widetilde L}=2(\ ,\ )_L.                    \tag{3.1}
\]

Writing \(h_i=B_i/2\), one has \(h_i^2=-2\) in the scaled form.  The exact
arithmetic consequences are much weaker than (2.1):

\[
 (h_i,h_j)_{\widetilde L}=\frac{B_i.B_j}{2},                  \tag{3.2}
\]

so integrality requires even boundary intersections, not zero boundary
intersections.  For a physical `(-2)`-curve \(R\),

\[
                  s_R(h_i)=h_i+\frac{B_i.R}{2}R,               \tag{3.3}
\]

which gives only a parity condition whenever no extra half of \(R\) is
present.  For a `(-1)`-curve \(E\),

\[
                  s_E(h_i)=h_i+(B_i.E)E,                       \tag{3.4}
\]

which imposes no new condition at all.  Coxeter angles also permit the
non-Coble values: two norm-`-2` roots may pair by `1`, and parallel or
ultraparallel walls permit still larger pairings.

### Theorem 3.1 (reflection-maximal full-nef counterexample)

Let \(S\to\mathbb F_4\) be the blow-up of a torus fixed point lying on the
positive section and off the negative section.  Let \(B\) be the negative
section, \(F\) the pulled-back fibre, \(E\) the exceptional curve, and
\(G=F-E\) the transformed fibre through the centre.  Then

\[
 \overline {NE}(S)=\mathbf R_{\geq0}B+
                    \mathbf R_{\geq0}E+
                    \mathbf R_{\geq0}G.                         \tag{3.5}
\]

The projectivized full nef cone is the finite-area Coxeter triangle

\[
                              (2,3,\infty).                      \tag{3.6}
\]

On the natural half-boundary lattice it is an arithmetic reflection
chamber, and its reflection group is maximal among discrete hyperbolic
reflection groups.  Nevertheless

\[
 B^2=-4,\quad E^2=G^2=-1,\qquad B.E=0,\quad B.G=1,              \tag{3.7}
\]

so neither `(-1)` wall has the required Coble intersection \(B.C=2\), and
\(B\neq-2K_S\).

#### Exact verification

In the basis \((B,F,E)\),

\[
 \operatorname {NS}(S)=
 \begin{pmatrix}-4&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad K_S=(-2,-6,1).                                        \tag{3.8}
\]

The torus-invariant curves generate the Mori cone.  The other fibre is
\(F=E+G\), and the transformed positive section is

\[
                    B+4F-E=B+3E+4G,
\]

which proves (3.5).  The physical wall Gram is

\[
 \operatorname {Gram}(B,E,G)=
 \begin{pmatrix}-4&0&1\\0&-1&1\\1&1&-1\end{pmatrix}.        \tag{3.9}
\]

The reflection in \(B\) is not integral on \(\operatorname {NS}(S)\), since
\(s_B(F)=F+B/2\).  Adjoin \(h=B/2\) and double the form.  In the basis
\((h,F,E)\),

\[
 \widetilde L=
 \begin{pmatrix}-2&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&-2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad |\det\widetilde L|=2.                                 \tag{3.10}
\]

It is therefore 2-elementary.  The three primitive roots \(h,E,G\) span
\(\widetilde L\), all have square `-2`, and have Gram matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-2&0&1\\0&-2&2\\1&2&-2\end{pmatrix}.        \tag{3.11}
\]

This gives angles \(\pi/2,\pi/3,0\) and area \(\pi/6\).  All three reflection
matrices are integral isometries of (3.10).

For maximality, any discrete reflection supergroup would contain this
finite-covolume nonuniform group with finite index, so its Coxeter polygon
would also be noncompact and would have area at most \(\pi/6\).  But a
noncompact Coxeter \(n\)-gon has area

\[
 \pi\left(n-2-\sum_{v\ \mathrm{finite}}\frac1{m_v}\right).       \tag{3.12}
\]

For \(n\geq4\) this is at least \(\pi/2\).  For a triangle with an ideal
vertex, the smallest positive value is \(\pi/6\), attained uniquely by
\((2,3,\infty)\).  Hence a proper reflection supergroup is impossible.
This makes maximality a proved red herring, not merely an untested
suspicion.

## 4. Arbitrary denominator changes destroy the alphabet completely

The ruled surfaces already give an infinite exact warning.  Let \(B_m\) be
the negative section of \(\mathbb F_{2m}\), so \(B_m^2=-2m\).  Put

\[
 M_m=\left\langle\operatorname {NS}(\mathbb F_{2m}),
                         \frac{B_m}{m}\right\rangle,
 \qquad (\ ,\ )_{M_m}=m(\ ,\ ).                               \tag{4.1}
\]

In the basis \(h=B_m/m,F\), every \(m\geq2\) gives the same integral lattice

\[
                         \begin{pmatrix}-2&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}, \tag{4.2}
\]

and the reflection in \(h\) is integral.  Thus an uncontrolled choice of a
commensurable arithmetic module can make smooth rational curves of
arbitrarily negative square into integral reflection walls.  Arithmetic
integrality constrains the chosen module; it does not recover the physical
curve square in \(\operatorname {NS}(S)\).

If arbitrary finite-index *sublattices* are also allowed, even the parity
disappears.  On \(\mathbb F_a\), with negative section \(C_a^2=-a\), take

\[
 N_a=\mathbf ZC_a+\mathbf Z(aF)\subset\operatorname {NS}(\mathbb F_a).
                                                                    \tag{4.3}
\]

In the displayed basis its Gram matrix and section reflection are

\[
 \operatorname {Gram}(N_a)=
 \begin{pmatrix}-a&a\\a&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 s_{C_a}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.               \tag{4.4}
\]

They are integral for every \(a\geq1\), including every odd \(a\).  This is
why a useful compiler must pin the arithmetic module to the geometric
N\'eron--Severi lattice or to one explicitly controlled boundary extension.

The surfaces in (4.1) are a local denominator counterfamily, not a claim of
finite covolume.  Finite covolume and even reflection maximality still fail
to recover the missing Coble incidence, as the exact full-nef example of
Theorem 3.1 shows.

## 5. The proposed standard-coefficient generalization collapses to Coble

There is a natural-looking generalization of (0.1).  Let
\(B_1,\ldots,B_b\) be pairwise disjoint smooth rational curves with

\[
                         B_i^2=-2m_i,\qquad m_i\geq2,            \tag{5.1}
\]

and set

\[
                 \Delta=\sum_i\left(1-\frac1{m_i}\right)B_i.   \tag{5.2}
\]

Adjunction gives \(K_S.B_i=2m_i-2\), so each boundary is annihilated by
\(K_S+\Delta\).  If every other spanning Mori generator satisfies the
corresponding weighted incidence equation, then

\[
                              K_S+\Delta\equiv0.                 \tag{5.3}
\]

On a rational surface this is a \(\mathbf Q\)-linear equality.

### Theorem 5.1 (adapted-cover obstruction)

Assume the characteristic does not divide
\(\ell=\operatorname {lcm}(m_1,\ldots,m_b)\).  If (5.3) holds and the
boundary is nonempty, then

\[
                              \ell=2,\qquad m_i=2\quad\text{for all }i. \tag{5.4}
\]

The canonical adapted cover is then the smooth connected K3 double cover
branched over \(B=\sum B_i=-2K_S\).  Consequently

\[
 \rho(S)\leq20\quad\text{over }\mathbf C,\qquad
 \rho(S)\leq22\quad\text{in tame positive characteristic}.    \tag{5.5}
\]

Accordingly, literal full-nef chambers have dimension at most `19` or `21`.
If instead a full restriction-of-scalars trace block injects into
\(\operatorname {NS}(S)_{\mathbf Q}\), the respective conclusions are
\([k:\mathbf Q](n+1)\leq20\) and \([k:\mathbf Q](n+1)\leq22\).

#### Proof

Multiplying (5.3) by \(\ell\) gives

\[
 D:=\sum_i\frac{\ell}{m_i}B_i
              =\ell\left(K_S+\sum_iB_i\right)\quad\text{in }\operatorname {Pic}(S).
                                                                    \tag{5.6}
\]

Thus, for \(M=\mathcal O_S(K_S+\sum_iB_i)\), one has
\(M^{\otimes\ell}\simeq\mathcal O_S(D)\).  Take the normalization
\(\pi:X\to S\) of the cyclic cover of degree \(\ell\) defined by an
\(\ell\)-th root of its canonical section with divisor \(D\).
It is connected because

\[
             \gcd\left(\ell,\frac\ell{m_1},\ldots,
                              \frac\ell{m_b}\right)=1:          \tag{5.7}
\]

for each prime dividing \(\ell\), at least one \(m_i\) contains its maximal
power in the least common multiple.  More explicitly, after a rational
trivialization of \(M\), the Kummer function has valuation
\(\ell/m_i\) along \(B_i\).  Its valuation vector therefore has exact order
\(\ell\) modulo \(\ell\), so its Kummer class has exact order \(\ell\).
This proves that the cover has degree \(\ell\) and is connected, without
hiding a line-bundle divisibility assumption.  Since the \(B_i\) are disjoint and the
cover is tame, its normalization is smooth: strictly locally above \(B_i\)
it is a disjoint union of \(\ell/m_i\) regular charts with ramification
index \(m_i\); after extracting a unit, each chart has equation
\(z^{m_i}=x\).  In particular the adapted cover has no singularities at all,
which is stronger than having only canonical singularities.

There is also an exact integral trivialization of the canonical bundle, not
merely numerical torsion.  Let \(Z\) be the reduced inverse image of the
branch.  The tautological root is a section of \(\pi^*M\), and
\(\pi^*D=\ell Z\), so

\[
                         \mathcal O_X(Z)\simeq\pi^*M.           \tag{5.8}
\]

The ramification divisor is
\(R_\pi=\pi^*(\sum_iB_i)-Z\).  Hence the ramification formula gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal O_X(K_X)
 &\simeq\pi^*\mathcal O_S\!\left(K_S+\sum_iB_i\right)
                         \otimes\mathcal O_X(-Z)\\
 &\simeq\pi^*M\otimes(\pi^*M)^{-1}
 \simeq\mathcal O_X.
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{5.9}
\]

For completeness, additivity of the tame Euler characteristic separates
the unramified complement from the reduced inverse images of the branch
curves:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 c_2(X)=e(X)
 &=\ell\,e\!\left(S\setminus\bigcup_iB_i\right)
       +\sum_i\frac\ell{m_i}e(B_i)\\
 &=\ell\bigl(c_2(S)-2b\bigr)+2\sum_i\frac\ell{m_i}\\
 &=\ell\left(c_2(S)-2b+2\sum_i\frac1{m_i}\right).
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{5.10}
\]

Here each \(B_i\simeq\mathbf P^1\) has Euler number `2`.  For a rational
surface, Noether on the base gives \(c_2(S)=12-K_S^2\).  Disjointness and
(5.3) give, step by step,

\[
 K_S^2=\Delta^2
 =\sum_i\left(1-\frac1{m_i}\right)^2B_i^2
 =-2\sum_i\frac{(m_i-1)^2}{m_i}.
\]

Substitution yields the useful exact form

\[
 c_2(X)=\ell\left(
       12+2\sum_i\frac{(m_i-1)(m_i-2)}{m_i}\right)\geq12\ell, \tag{5.11}
\]

with equality precisely when every \(m_i=2\).  On the other hand, \(X\) is
connected and \(K_X\simeq\mathcal O_X\), so Serre duality gives

\[
 \chi(\mathcal O_X)=2-q(X)\leq2.
\]

Noether's formula and (5.9) therefore give \(c_2(X)\leq24\).  Since the
branch is nonempty, \(\ell\geq2\); combining this with (5.11) forces
\(\ell=2\), equality throughout, and \(m_i=2\) for every \(i\).  It also
forces \(q(X)=0\), so \(X\) is K3.  Pullback injects
\(\operatorname {NS}(S)\) into \(\operatorname {NS}(X)\), proving (5.5).
\(\square\)

Thus the `-2m`, `1-1/m` construction is a useful *obstruction test*, but it
is not a new family beyond Coble in the disjoint smooth-rational setting.
Reflection extensions such as (4.1) allow every \(m\); the global weighted
Mori incidence equations eliminate every \(m>2\).

## 6. Exact conclusion for the compiler

For a rational full-nef carrier, the noncircular decision tree is now:

```text
facet curve smooth rational
  + primitive reflection integral on NS
      -> physical square -1 or -2, except C=2r gives square -4;

all physical facet classes primitive on NS
  + these are the complete Mori generators
      -> weak-anticanonical branch and n <= 9 immediately;

primitive -4 boundary handled on a half-boundary extension
      -> arithmetic parity only;
      -> must separately prove K + B/2 = 0;

attempted disjoint standard-coefficient branch (-2m, 1-1/m)
      -> weighted incidence + spanning -> adapted cover
      -> Noether forces m=2 everywhere;

arbitrary scaled/commensurable module
      -> no physical self-intersection alphabet at all.
```

Reflection maximality supplies none of the missing linear log-Calabi--Yau
equation.  The exact next raw-\(P\) gate is therefore geometric: construct
the physical facet curves and certify either their primitivity on
\(\operatorname {NS}(S)\), or the complete equation \(B=-2K_S\).  Searching
for a maximality-only substitute cannot work.
