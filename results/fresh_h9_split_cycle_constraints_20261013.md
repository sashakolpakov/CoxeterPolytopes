# An exact two-edge closure and its continuous continuation

One new norm-two wall closes **two** outgoing edges of the saved 72-wall split-seed chamber at once. It preserves all 72 old wall relations, creates two actual finite vertices, and completes a four-edge cycle in a two-dimensional face. The resulting 73-wall chamber still has an exact strict spacelike escape, so this is a local construction, not a finite-volume example.

A related continuous family has an actual all-right finite endpoint and strictly dotted relations to every wall outside its eight-wall edge block. Thus it illustrates what extra-finite-anchor sampling omits. However, one exact escape survives the entire family, and a positive-cone identity proves that every member with parameter greater than two is weaker than the already constructed cycle wall. Increasing that parameter cannot improve this chamber.

These are nine independent linear conditions on a ten-dimensional Lorentzian normal, followed by its norm equation. In the independent seed basis [0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,12], the linear solution is p=u+t v for the normal covector. The norm equation is At²+Bt+C0=0, and both real branches are tested exactly. A numerical proposal, number 2009 in the [optional-anchor scout](../data/enumerations/fresh_h9_split_endpoint_walk_optional_anchor_20261013_scout.json), suggested this small system. ## A continuous family without an extra finite anchor

Replace (r,D)=−1 by (r,D)=−lambda, while retaining the eight zero pairings to S∪{C}. Completing the norm quadratic gives

```
r(lambda) = lambda a + sqrt(kappa lambda² + delta) b,
kappa>0, delta>0,
(a,b)=0, a²+kappa b²=0, delta b²=2.
```

The formula applies equally to normal coefficients and covectors in the fixed basis. All coefficients and identities are saved in the [continuous-family archive](../data/enumerations/fresh_h9_split_cycle_constraints_20261013_continuous.json) and generated by [the exact family script](../scripts/fresh_h9_split_cycle_constraints_20261013_continuous.py).

The coefficient signs against the old walls are particularly simple. Both a and b are orthogonal to S∪{C}. Against D their pairings are −1 and 0. Against each of the other 63 old walls both pairings are strictly negative. At lambda=2, the relation to D is parallel and all other off-block relations are strictly dotted. Consequently, for **every real lambda>2**, all 64 off-block relations are strictly dotted. Both center coefficients are positive in the archive's convention, where point inequalities are (normal,point)>0.

Each such cap has an actual all-right finite endpoint on the edge S∪{C}. Here is an all-parameter proof, rather than a numerical incidence check. The eight old edge normals and r(lambda) have Gram matrix 2I9. Project the saved strict timelike center h orthogonally away from these nine normals. Its square becomes

```
h² − (1/2) sum_i (h,r_i)² < 0.
```

Every coefficient removed in this projection is positive. Each nonincident old normal has nonpositive pairing with every incident normal, so its inequality at the projected point is strictly positive. The archive checks these old-to-edge pairings and every center sign exactly. This proves the endpoint's actuality for the whole parameter range.

## Why this continuous family cannot improve the chamber

In the same ten-coordinate covector basis, the integer vector

```
y = [17553546,197944749,203785283,203785283,47178720,
     47178720,47178720,207849122,186231837,160670502]
```

is strict on all 72 old inequalities and has positive square

```
(2573443394755535281 − 1406249130371772101phi)/16.
```

Its time orientation agrees with the chamber center. Both (a,y) and (b,y) are strictly positive, so (r(lambda),y)>0 for every lambda>0 for which the formula is used. In particular, y is a strict spacelike escape for every lambda>2. The rational witness was proposed near the [remote numerical escape](../data/enumerations/fresh_h9_split_multiple_edge_caps_20261013_escape_scout.json), but all its geometric signs are checked exactly.

There is a stronger geometric explanation. Set z=U3+(phi/2)C, a positive combination of old wall normals. Exact calculation gives z=c a+d b with c>0 and d<0. For lambda>1,

```
r(lambda) = alpha r(1) + beta z,

alpha = [lambda d − c sqrt(kappa lambda²+delta)]
        / [d − c sqrt(kappa+delta)],

beta = [sqrt(kappa lambda²+delta) − lambda sqrt(kappa+delta)]
       / [d − c sqrt(kappa+delta)].
```

Both coefficients are positive. For beta, square the two positive radicals and use delta>0 and lambda>1; its numerator and denominator are both negative. For alpha, c>0 and d<0 make its numerator negative as well. Thus the inequality of every lambda>2 member is implied by the cycle wall r(1), U3 and C. Its halfspace contains the chamber already cut by the cycle wall.

This is a complete statement about the displayed continuous family, not about other profiles on the same edge, other edge blocks, or all completions of the split seed. It does not prove that no single cap can close three edges. The exact construction proves that closing two is possible; broader edge-coverage counts remain separate computations.
