# Hostile audit: intrinsic dessin field effectivity

**Audit target:** `results/dessin_native_field_effectivity_theorem.md`,
especially Sections 2 and 4, and the corresponding intrinsic-surface claims
in `GOAL.md`.

**Verdict:** **PASS after one substantive narrowing and several explicit
hypotheses.**  The zero-dimensional Gram-image theorem and the
carrier-descent theorem are sound.  The intrinsic argument proves finiteness
of the **geometric unit-normal Gram image** and of the Vinberg ground fields.
As written, it does not prove finiteness of arbitrary primitive-root/Cartan
coordinates, coefficient fields, or the broader `K_G` if those data are
included in `K_G`.  Unit rescaling gives a direct obstruction.  The compact
K3 endpoint is correct as a carrier-relative rank statement, but Picard rank
twenty alone gives zero-dimensional marked period strata, not a finite union
of all singular K3 surfaces.

## 1. Finite image versus finite carrier

The weak theorem is correct under the hypotheses already intended:

1. the target of the Gram map is a finite-type scheme (or algebraic space)
   over \(\mathbb Q\);
2. the scalar eigencharacter cover is an algebraic finite cover, obtained on
   the finite locally free separable coefficient-algebra locus; and
3. the scheme-theoretic image \(Z_\gamma\) is zero-dimensional.

Then \(Z_\gamma\) is finite over \(\mathbb Q\).  Its geometric points form a
finite Galois set, and the residue degree of a closed point is the size of
its geometric Galois orbit.  Positive-dimensional fibres of \(\gamma\) do
not affect this conclusion.  Thus no rigidity or descent theorem for the
surface carrier is needed to enumerate fields generated by the coordinates
actually retained by \(\gamma\).

The word **effective** additionally requires an explicit computable
presentation of the map and its image.  Zero-dimensionality as an abstract
existence statement does not itself supply a primary-decomposition
algorithm.  The report makes this qualification later, but it should be
attached to the weak theorem whenever that theorem is quoted.

The strong theorem is also correct.  A zero-dimensional separated
finite-type DM stack with trivial geometric inertia is a finite algebraic
space, hence in this situation a finite scheme.  For a geometric point,
trivial automorphisms make all stabilizer descent isomorphisms unique, so the
cocycle condition is automatic and stack descent is effective.  The field
of moduli is therefore a field of definition.  The real signature,
orientation, and Coxeter inequalities must still be applied as a real
selection on the finite algebraic Galois set; the real enhancement itself is
not a stack over \(\mathbb Q\).

Consequently the manuscript's distinction is valid:

\[
 \dim Z_\gamma=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{finite fields of retained Gram coordinates},
\]

whereas zero-dimensionality and rigidification of the full carrier locus are
needed for finite carrier fields and descent.

## 2. Scalar splitting cover

Choosing a scalar eigencharacter is essential.  A point retaining only an
abstract algebra, for example \(\mathbb Q[t]/(t^2-5)\), does not choose one of
its two scalar specializations and therefore does not determine the marked
scalar Gram point.  In characteristic zero, after requiring the universal
coefficient algebra to be finite locally free and separable, the character
or ordered splitting construction is finite etale and its geometric points
are permuted by \(G_{\mathbb Q}\).  This justifies the finite-Galois-set
argument.

If rank jumps, nilpotent coefficient algebras, or an unnormalized
\(\mathbb G_m\)-choice of root vectors are allowed, the claimed finite cover
need not be the stated object.  These cases must either be removed by a
locally closed condition or included in the full finite-type parameter
scheme and then separately shown to have zero-dimensional image.

## 3. Cyclotomic degree and finite angle alphabet

For every \(m\geq3\),

\[
 4\cos ^2(\pi/m)=2+\zeta_m+\zeta_m^{-1},
\]

so

\[
 \mathbb Q(4\cos ^2(\pi/m))
   =\mathbb Q(\zeta_m+\zeta_m^{-1}),\qquad
 [\mathbb Q(4\cos ^2(\pi/m)): \mathbb Q]=\varphi(m)/2.
\]

There are no exceptions for \(m\geq3\); the small cases \(m=3,4,6\) simply
have degree one.  The label \(m=2\) is genuinely separate: the cyclic product
is zero and its field is \(\mathbb Q\).  A parallel pair \(m=\infty\) has
product four, while an ultraparallel dotted pair has product greater than
four and is not controlled by the finite-angle alphabet.

If the minimal Vinberg field \(k\) contains these cyclic products and
\([k:\mathbb Q]\le d_{\max}\), then
\(\varphi(m)\le2d_{\max}\).  Hence

\[
 \{2\}\cup\{m\ge3:\varphi(m)\le2d_{\max}\}
\]

is finite and effective.  For a varying carrier class, a **uniform** Picard
bound \(\rho(S)\le R\) is needed, with
\(d_{\max}=\lfloor R/(n+1)\rfloor\).  An object-by-object value of
\(\rho(S)\) does not by itself give one finite alphabet for the union.

## 4. Neron--Severi rank inequality

The inequality

\[
 [k:\mathbb Q](n+1)\le\rho(S)
\]

is correct if the hypothesis means an injective map of the **entire
torsion-free \(\mathcal O_k\)-module** \(L\) of rank \(n+1\) into
\(\operatorname{NS}(S)\) as an additive lattice.  Indeed,
\(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}L=[k:\mathbb Q](n+1)\).  Merely having
\(n+1\) divisor roots whose pairings lie in \(k\), or an external
\(\mathcal O_k\)-local system, is not this hypothesis.

For compatibility with Hodge index one normally also wants the divisor
intersection form to be the appropriate integral trace form: after choosing
the sign convention with signature \((1,n)\) at the distinguished embedding,
the other real traces must contribute negative-definite summands.  This form
compatibility is not needed for the rank inequality alone, but it is needed
to call the embedding a realization of the arithmetic quadratic lattice in
the divisor intersection lattice.

## 5. Facet degree

The numerical implication \(f\le\Delta\) is correct when:

- \(H\) is an ample Cartier class (or there is a stated uniform denominator
  and hence a uniform positive lower degree);
- every facet sheet contributes a nonzero effective divisor \(D_i\);
- the total divisor is the labelled sum \(D_{\rm fac}=\sum_iD_i\), with every
  sheet counted once; and
- \(H^{s-1}\cdot D_{\rm fac}\le\Delta\) is an **absolute**, not scale-free,
  bound.

Then each \(H^{s-1}\cdot D_i\) is a positive integer.  Separation of sheets
is needed for exact recovery, though the elementary degree inequality itself
only needs every labelled contribution to occur in the sum.  For a
\(\mathbb Q\)-polarization, stacky degree, or a varying rescaling of \(H\),
one must instead state a uniform lower bound
\(H^{s-1}\cdot D_i\ge\varepsilon>0\), giving
\(f\le\Delta/\varepsilon\).

## 6. What Mostow--Prasad does and does not prove

Fix \(n\ge3\), a complete marked Coxeter matrix, and two faithful
finite-covolume reflection realizations.  The marked Coxeter presentation
identifies the two reflection groups.  It preserves the sign character, so
it identifies their orientation-preserving index-two lattices.  The
finite-volume Mostow--Prasad theorem conjugates these lattices, and the
marked reflections are then recovered from their conjugation action (the
centralizer of a finite-covolume hyperbolic lattice is trivial in the
relevant isometry group).  Thus there is at most one marked reflection
realization up to ambient isometry.

This argument includes:

- noncompact finite-volume lattices in dimension three;
- nonsimple Coxeter chambers; and
- pairs labelled \(\infty\), whether the realized walls are parallel or
  ultraparallel.

It does not apply in dimension two, and it does not turn an infinite-volume
representation into a rigid one.  Faithfulness, nonempty full-dimensional
chamber, and finite covolume are hypotheses, not consequences of the
abstract matrix.

The crucial limitation is that Mostow--Prasad fixes the **geometric
unit-normal Gram matrix**, not arbitrary arithmetic root vectors.  If
\(r_i\) is replaced by \(u r_i\), where \(u\) is an algebraic unit, then the
reflection and its wall are unchanged and a primitive root remains
primitive, but

\[
 a_{ij}\longmapsto u^{-1}a_{ij},\qquad
 a_{ji}\longmapsto u a_{ji},\qquad
 a_{ij}a_{ji}\longmapsto a_{ij}a_{ji}.
\]

More generally, independent root rescalings act by a diagonal torus on the
Cartan coordinates while fixing all cyclic products and the chamber.
Therefore a finite list of Coxeter matrices plus Mostow rigidity does **not**
give a finite image for a map that retains primitive-root scalings,
asymmetric Cartan entries, or arbitrary coefficient-order decorations.

The intrinsic conclusion should consequently be stated in one of the
following equivalent safe forms:

1. use the outward **unit-normal symmetric Gram matrix** as the geometric
   target and conclude that its image, its field, and the Vinberg fields are
   finite; or
2. quotient the Cartan/root target by the diagonal root-rescaling torus and
   retain cyclic products; or
3. add a genuinely canonical finite normalization of every root scaling and
   prove it is bounded by the surface data.

Without one of these changes, the statements that the intrinsic hypotheses
give a finite `K_G` image and a finite `K_G` field list are not justified by
Mostow.  The finite Vinberg-field conclusion **is** justified.  Likewise,
bounded degree of a coefficient field does not make arbitrary coefficient
fields finite; one must use the minimal field generated by the geometric
Gram cycles, or add a discriminant/ramification/height bound.

## 7. Effectivity and the finite-volume gate

Real quantifier elimination is a valid in-principle final step only after the
accepted realization locus has been written as a finite Boolean combination
of polynomial equalities and inequalities over explicit algebraic angle
fields.  At minimum the formula must impose:

- normalized outward normals, rank and Lorentzian signature;
- a nonempty full-dimensional chamber and irredundancy of every marked wall;
- the prescribed finite angles and the correct branches for every
  \(m=\infty\) pair; and
- the literal finite-volume/completeness condition, including every cusp.

Quantifier elimination does not infer finite volume merely from rank,
signature, or the Coxeter matrix.  One may encode the finite Vinberg
face/cusp criterion by enumerating active wall subsets, or give an equivalent
semialgebraic boundary-at-infinity predicate.  In a finite-volume polytope an
ideal face is an ideal **vertex**; a positive-dimensional ideal face signals
infinite volume.  For a nonsimple ideal vertex the full Euclidean cusp link,
not just one chosen affine subdiagram, must be certified complete.

Once the accepted unit-normal Gram set is a semialgebraic singleton for each
matrix, its coordinates are real algebraic and cylindrical algebraic
decomposition/root isolation can recover them exactly.  Mostow supplies
uniqueness; the explicit Vinberg/completeness predicate supplies existence
and finite volume.

## 8. Compact intrinsic K3 endpoint

For a complex K3 surface, \(\rho\le20\).  Under the full intrinsic
\(\mathcal O_k\)-lattice hypothesis, compactness in \(H^9\) gives

\[
 [k:\mathbb Q]10\le\rho\le20.
\]

For the minimal admissible Vinberg/quadratic field, \(k\ne\mathbb Q\): a
rational indefinite quadratic form of rank ten is isotropic by Meyer, so its
arithmetic lattice is nonuniform and cannot have a compact Coxeter
fundamental chamber.  Hence \([k:\mathbb Q]=2\) and \(\rho=20\).  The angle
alphabet is exactly

\[
 \{2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12\}.
\]

Also, \(2(n+1)>20\) for \(n\ge10\), so no degree-greater-than-one intrinsic
K3 carrier of this type exists there.  These conclusions are correct and
carrier-relative.

The phrase “zero-dimensional singular-K3 period” needs its usual marking
qualification.  For a **fixed primitive rank-twenty Neron--Severi lattice**
the period domain is zero-dimensional.  The union over all rank-twenty
lattices/discriminants is countably infinite, so \(\rho=20\) alone is not a
finite carrier census and does not by itself enumerate carrier fields.

## 9. Required manuscript corrections

1. Replace “finite Gram image and its \(k_V,K_G\) field lists” in the
   intrinsic bridge by “finite unit-normal geometric Gram image and finite
   geometric-Gram/Vinberg field lists,” unless `K_G` is explicitly redefined
   to exclude primitive-root scalings and coefficient-order coordinates.
2. Add a uniform Picard bound \(R\) for a varying surface class.
3. State that arbitrary coefficient fields are not made finite by their
   degree; identify \(k\) with the minimal Vinberg field for the field-list
   conclusion, or add independent arithmetic bounds.
4. Tie “effective” to explicit equations and an explicit finite-volume
   predicate.
5. Qualify the K3 period claim by fixing the primitive rank-twenty lattice;
   do not infer a finite singular-K3 carrier list from Picard rank alone.

With these corrections, there is no counterexample to the intended core
theorem: normalized absolute facet degree plus the intrinsic rank bound gives
finitely many marked Coxeter matrices, Mostow--Prasad gives at most one
finite-covolume geometric realization per matrix for \(n\ge3\), and the
Vinberg cyclic-product fields are therefore finite and effectively
recoverable once the exact realization/cusp predicate is implemented.
