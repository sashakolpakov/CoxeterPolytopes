# The canonical root-presentation gate for a 14-wall K3 design

## Result

A completed pair of integral wall matrices \((Q,B)\) already presents the
entire rank-20 trace lattice generated by the walls and their golden
translates.  There is no need to guess an ambient matrix for the golden
endomorphism.  The correct presentation matrix is

\[
\mathcal D(Q,B)=
\begin{pmatrix}
Q&B\\
B&Q+B
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For a 14-wall candidate in \(\mathbb H^9\), the first exact rank gate is

\[
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal D=20,
\qquad
\operatorname{inertia}\mathcal D=(1,19,8).
\]

Equivalently, the recovered golden Gram matrix has rank 10 and corank four,
with place inertias \((9,1,4)\) and \((10,0,4)\).  The saturated radical of
\(\mathcal D\), not an arbitrary denominator-cleared rational nullspace,
gives the eight integral relations in the 14-wall case.

The construction and its exact audits are implemented in
`coxeter_polytopes.geometric.k3_presentation`.

## 1. The finite labels and the dotted variables

Put \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), \(\mathcal O_F=\mathbb Z[\phi]\), and use the
trace scalar \(\alpha=-\phi/\sqrt5\).  Normalize each selected field root to
have square two.  If

\[
H_{ij}=a_{ij}+c_{ij}\phi,
\]

then the two integral matrices are

\[
Q_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha H_{ij})=-(a_{ij}+c_{ij}),
\qquad
B_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha\phi H_{ij})=-(a_{ij}+2c_{ij}),
\]

and hence

\[
H=(B-2Q)+(Q-B)\phi. \tag{1}
\]

The diagonal entries are \(Q_{ii}=B_{ii}=-2\).  The finite pairs are

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
m&2&3&5\\ \hline
(Q_{ij},B_{ij})&(0,0)&(1,1)&(1,2).
\end{array} \tag{2}
\]

### Why label 4 is absent

This exclusion is specific to the even golden K3 construction, rather than
to arbitrary Coxeter polytopes.  Evenness leaves only the wall-norm shells
\(2\) and \(4-2\phi\).  Multiplication by the unit \(\phi\), which is positive
at the distinguished embedding, takes a vector in the second shell to a
norm-two vector defining the same restricted mirror.  Thus every restricted
wall can be represented by a norm-two vector over \(F\).

For two norm-two walls with label 4, their field product would have to be
\(-2\cos(\pi/4)=-\sqrt2\).  But \(\sqrt2\notin\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\).
Consequently the example-track Boolean label alphabet is exactly
\(\{2,3,5\}\), even though the general 9/14 theorem track must retain label
4.

At a finite vertex, the graph obtained by replacing both 3- and 5-edges by a
single ordinary edge must be ADE.  The stronger labeled condition is that
each connected vertex component is one of

\[
A_n,D_n,E_6,E_7,E_8,H_3,H_4,I_2(5).
\]

Thus the ADE shadow is necessary but not sufficient: for example, a 5-edge
may occur only in an \(I_2(5)\), \(H_3\), or \(H_4\) component, in its
classified position.

### Exact dotted-edge fiber

The conjugate entry recovered from \((Q_{ij},B_{ij})=(q,b)\) is

\[
H'_{ij}=-q+(b-q)\phi=\phi b-\phi^2q.
\]

Positive definiteness at the conjugate place gives \(|H'_{ij}|<2\) for
distinct walls.  Hence a compact dotted pair must satisfy

\[
q\ge2,
\qquad
|b-\phi q|<\frac{2}{\phi}. \tag{3}
\]

Conversely, (3) implies that the identity-place entry is strictly less than
\(-2\).  For each fixed integer \(q\), the open interval in (3) contains only
two or three integers \(b\).  Thus a bounded search in curve intersection
\(q\le q_{\max}\) has a small, exactly generated dotted-edge domain.  Such a
bound defines a discovery census; it is not a non-existence theorem.

For \(q=0\), (3) produces no ultraparallel entry.  For \(q=1\), its only
possibilities are \(b=1,2\), precisely the 3- and 5-edges.  This proves the
stated lower bound \(q\ge2\) for a dotted edge.

## 2. The doubled trace-presentation theorem

Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) denote the formal wall generators over
\(\mathcal O_F\).  On the ordered integral generating set

\[
(e_1,\ldots,e_n,\phi e_1,\ldots,\phi e_n),
\]

the trace pairings are

\[
q(e_i,e_j)=Q_{ij},\qquad
q(e_i,\phi e_j)=B_{ij},\qquad
q(\phi e_i,\phi e_j)=Q_{ij}+B_{ij}.
\]

This proves the formula for \(\mathcal D(Q,B)\).  Multiplication by \(\phi\)
on the formal generators is

\[
S=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&I_n\\
I_n&I_n
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
S^2=S+I,
\qquad
S^t\mathcal D=\mathcal D S. \tag{4}
\]

Over the two real embeddings, restriction of scalars identifies
\(\mathcal D\), up to real congruence, with

\[
\alpha^{(0)}H^{(0)}\oplus \alpha^{(1)}H^{(1)}.
\]

Both embeddings of \(\alpha\) are negative.  Therefore place signatures
\((9,1,4)\) and \((10,0,4)\) give

\[
\operatorname{inertia}\mathcal D=(1,19,8). \tag{5}
\]

The rank equality is especially useful:

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\mathcal D
=2\operatorname{rank}_{F}H. \tag{6}
\]

This explains why imposing rank ten on \(Q\) is wrong.  The individual curve
classes may be independent over \(\mathbb Z\).  In the Bugaenko calibration,
for example, \(H\) has rank nine but the 11-by-11 matrix \(Q\) has full rank
eleven.

## 3. Saturation and the canonical root-span lattice

Let

\[
K=\ker(\mathcal D:\mathbb Z^{2n}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{2n})
  =\ker_{\mathbb Q}(\mathcal D)\cap\mathbb Z^{2n}. \tag{7}
\]

The equality in (7) is part of the definition of the integral kernel.  More
importantly, \(K\) is primitive: by the first isomorphism theorem,
\(\mathbb Z^{2n}/K\) is isomorphic to the image of the integer matrix
\(\mathcal D\), which is a subgroup of a free abelian group and hence
torsion-free.  Therefore

\[
N_0=\mathbb Z^{2n}/K \tag{8}
\]

is a free integral lattice.  The form descends nondegenerately to \(N_0\).
It is even because every diagonal entry of \(\mathcal D\) is even.

Equation (4) makes \(K\) invariant under \(S\), so \(S\) descends to a
self-adjoint endomorphism \(\tau\) of \(N_0\) satisfying
\(\tau^2=\tau+I\).  When (5) holds, \(N_0\) is an even lattice of signature
\((1,19)\), and its golden rank is ten.

The implementation obtains (7)--(8) from a Smith decomposition

\[
U\mathcal D V=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_{20},0,\ldots,0)
\]

with \(V\in\operatorname{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb Z)\).  The final columns of \(V\)
are a saturated integral radical basis, while the first columns descend to a
genuine basis of \(N_0\).  The matrix \(V\), its determinant \(\pm1\), the
radical columns, the quotient Gram matrix, and the descended golden action
are all returned for independent verification.

A common but incorrect shortcut would clear denominators in any rational
nullspace basis.  That produces some sublattice of \(K\), but need not produce
all of \(K\); the resulting quotient could contain artificial torsion and
have the wrong discriminant.

### Completeness of the ambient lattice step

Suppose an actual rank-20 Neron--Severi lattice \(N\) contains the proposed
walls and their golden span is all of \(N\otimes\mathbb Q\).  Then the map
from the formal generators identifies \(N_0\) with the sublattice generated
by the \(\delta_i\) and \(\tau\delta_i\).  Hence \(N_0\subset N\) has finite
index.

Conversely, all possible even ambient lattices are obtained by enumerating
isotropic subgroups

\[
C\subset (A_{N_0},q_{N_0})
\]

that are invariant under the endomorphism induced by \(\tau\).  The
corresponding even overlattice is the inverse image of \(C\) in \(N_0^*\).
This is the standard discriminant-form correspondence for even overlattices;
see Nikulin, *Integral symmetric bilinear forms and some of their
applications*, Proposition 1.4.1 and the primitive-embedding results in
Section 1.15
([primary source](https://doi.org/10.1070/IM1980v014n01ABEH001060)).

For each surviving rank-20 overlattice \(N\), primitive K3 embedding is an
exact rank-two problem.  It exists precisely when there is a positive
definite even binary lattice \(T\) with

\[
(A_T,q_T)\cong(A_N,-q_N). \tag{9}
\]

For a fixed determinant, reduced positive binary forms give a finite list of
possible \(T\).  Thus (8), invariant isotropic-subgroup enumeration, and (9)
form a complete finite lattice-realization gate for every completed
\((Q,B)\); only the search for the completed Gram data remains unbounded.

## 4. The vertex-anchored Schur theorem

The corank-four equations can be made much smaller than the collection of all
11-by-11 minors.

Let \(I\) be the nine facets through a finite simple vertex and choose any
facet \(j_0\notin I\).  The ten roots indexed by
\(S=I\cup\{j_0\}\) form a basis of the ambient Lorentzian space.  Indeed, if
\(r_{j_0}\) were in the span of the nine vertex roots, then the vertex vector,
which is orthogonal to those nine roots, would also lie on facet \(j_0\),
contrary to simplicity.

Write the full 14-by-14 field Gram matrix in this basis order as

\[
H=
\begin{pmatrix}
A&C\\
C^t&E
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad A=H_S,
\]

where \(E\) is 4-by-4.  Then

\[
\operatorname{rank}H=10
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
E-C^tA^{-1}C=0. \tag{10}
\]

Thus corank four is only ten equations over \(F\), equivalently twenty
rational polynomial equations.  Denominators can be removed by replacing
(10) with

\[
(\det A)E=C^t\operatorname{adj}(A)C. \tag{11}
\]

There is also a cheap oriented-matroid condition.  Express each of the four
remaining roots as

\[
r_k=\sum_{s\in S}x_{sk}r_s.
\]

If \(v_I\) is the vertex vector, then

\[
(v_I,r_k)=x_{j_0k}(v_I,r_{j_0}).
\]

All five facets outside \(I\) are strict on the same side of the vertex, so

\[
x_{j_0k}>0 \qquad(k\notin I\cup\{j_0\}). \tag{12}
\]

Equations (10) and (12), together with place inertias
\((9,1)\) and \((10,0)\) for \(A\), are the smallest exact realization gate
for a numerically completed 9/14 Gram matrix.  Checking every choice of
\((I,j_0)\) supplies redundant, independently verifiable certificates.

## 5. A practical finite-domain CSP

For a fixed 14-facet nerve, the following solver architecture avoids a large
determinantal ideal.

1. Give every intersecting pair a finite-domain label variable in
   \(\{2,3,5\}\).  Reject vertex components outside the classified spherical
   list above.
2. Give every disjoint pair a bounded integer \(q\in[2,q_{\max}]\), and
   precompute its two or three possible values of \(b\) from (3), using exact
   golden comparisons.
3. Choose one vertex-plus-anchor basis \(S\), preferably one minimizing the
   number of dotted entries incident with the anchor.
4. Introduce a field kernel matrix \(K=X+\phi Y\in F^{14\times4}\), with the
   four rows outside \(S\) fixed to \(I_4\).  If
   \(H=H_0+\phi H_1\), impose
   \[
   H_0X+H_1Y=0,
   \qquad
   H_0Y+H_1X+H_1Y=0. \tag{13}
   \]
   The gauge gives four independent kernel vectors.  Nonsingularity of the
   anchor block then proves that the rank is exactly ten.
5. Encode each Gram choice by a Boolean selector.  Conditional on those
   selectors, (13) is linear rational arithmetic in \(X,Y\).  This gives a
   practical SMT formulation rather than nonlinear determinant equations.
6. Lazily verify exact place inertias, every vertex, all Schur residuals and
   side signs, then build \(N_0\), enumerate invariant even overlattices, and
   apply the binary-complement test (9).

An exact verifier should consume the selected \((Q,B)\), a kernel or Schur
certificate, the unimodular saturation matrix, and the discriminant gluing
data independently of the SMT search state.

## 6. Implemented API and calibration

The module provides:

- `doubled_trace_gram(Q, B)`;
- `golden_action_matrix(n)`;
- `build_k3_root_presentation(Q, B)`;
- `audit_k3_root_presentation(Q, B, hyperbolic_dimension=...)`;
- `audit_vertex_anchor(Q, B, vertex, anchor)`; and
- `audit_all_vertex_anchors(Q, B, vertices)`.

On Bugaenko's 11-wall \(\mathbb H^8\) chamber the exact output is:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{field Gram rank and corank}&9,2\\
\text{field place inertias}&(8,1,2),(9,0,2)\\
\text{doubled rank and corank}&18,4\\
\text{nondegenerate trace inertia}&(1,17)\\
\det N_0&-4\\
A_{N_0}&(\mathbb Z/2)^2.
\end{array}
\]

All \(41\times3=123\) vertex-plus-outside-facet anchors have the expected
place inertias and identically zero Schur complement.  All 246 nontrivial
outer-anchor coordinates in (12) are strictly positive.  The regression tests
also alter one entry of \(B\) and verify that the target rank/signature gate
then fails.

## 7. What this gate does not prove

Passing this gate proves that the proposed walls generate, after a finite
and explicitly enumerable overlattice step, an admissible golden rank-20 K3
lattice.  It does not prove that the chosen roots are simultaneously
irreducible effective curves, that their chamber meets the distinguished
eigenspace, that no additional K3 roots cut the chamber, or that the chamber
is compact.  Those remain the nef-orientation and Vinberg-completeness gates.
