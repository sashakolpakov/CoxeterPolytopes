## Fiber collision

Direct factorization gives

```text
c4    = 144s^2t^4,
Delta = -432t^10(t-1)^2(t^2+(4s^3-2)t+1).
```

The two endpoints `t=0,infinity` have valuations `(4,5,4,10)` for
`(a4,a6,c4,Delta)`, hence two `II*` fibers.  At `t=1`, `c4=144s^2` and the
leading discriminant coefficient is `-1728s^3`, so the fiber is exactly
`I2`.  The residual quadratic has discriminant

```text
16s^3(s^3-1),
```

and therefore contributes two `I1` fibers on the stated open.  The Euler
sum is `10+10+2+1+1=24`.  The open is nonempty: `s=2` gives residual
quadratic `t^2+30t+1` of discriminant `896`.

Geometrically, this is the codimension-one collision inside the generic
Inose family where one pair of `I1` fibers becomes `I2`, adding exactly one
`A1(-1)` root to the divisor lattice.

## Exact very-general Néron--Severi lattice

The section, fiber, two `II*` component systems, and the nonidentity `I2`
component give

```text

```

This lattice has rank 19, signature `(1,18)`, and determinant `2`.  The
residual quadratic recovers the moduli parameter up to a finite cube-root
ambiguity:

```text
s^3=([t]Q+2)/4.
```

Because the unique `I2` fiber is at `1` and the two `II*` fibers are at
`0,infinity`, the remaining base symmetries are finite.  The family thus has
one-dimensional image, equal to the dimension of the `L`-polarized K3
period domain.  The Picard-rank-20 locus is zero-dimensional, so a very
general member has `rho=19`.

A proper same-rank overlattice would have index at least two and determinant
`2/4`, impossible for an integral lattice.  Therefore `NS=L` exactly;
Shioda--Tate also gives Mordell--Weil rank and torsion zero.
