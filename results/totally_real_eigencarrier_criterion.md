# The totally-real eigencarrier criterion

## Result

There is an exact reverse construction from an admissible arithmetic Coxeter
realization to an algebraic hyperkähler carrier.  It has three logically
separate gates.

1. **Weak full-Picard carrier.**  Fix the integral realization, not merely an
   abstract Coxeter diagram.  Its trace lattice is the full divisor lattice of
   a projective K3 or irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifold if and only
   if it admits an orientation-compatible primitive embedding in the relevant
   K3/Beauville lattice.
2. **Wall promotion.**  For each allowed negative divisor-wall square, all
   walls visible in the hyperbolic eigenspace lie in a finite, exactly
   enumerable set of totally-positive field-norm shells.  Equality with a
   nef, movable, or Kähler section is then a discrete complete-wall test.
3. **Volume.**  The totally-real signature condition makes the field form
   anisotropic.  Hence its arithmetic quotient is compact.  For any complete
   invariant integral reflection arrangement,
   
   \[
   \boxed{
   \operatorname{vol}(C)<\infty
   \iff \overline C\text{ is compact}
   \iff |\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(C)|<\infty .}
   \]

The last equivalence is a reusable sharp obstruction: an infinite complete-
chamber stabilizer proves infinite volume without completing a Vinberg wall
list.  It also proves that a finite-volume **noncompact** Coxeter chamber can
never be the full chamber of such an admissible invariant reflection
arrangement.  This does not exclude noncompact finite-volume sections coming
from rational forms, non-invariant wall subsystems, or non-admissible field
signatures.

## 1. Admissible trace data

Let `F` be a totally real field of degree `e >= 2`, with ring of integers
`O_F`, and choose a distinguished real embedding `sigma_0`.  Let `M` be a
projective `O_F`-module of rank

\[
m=n+1
\]

with a nondegenerate symmetric `O_F`-valued form `h`.  Assume

\[
\operatorname{sig}h^{\sigma_0}=(n,1),\qquad
\operatorname{sig}h^\sigma=(n+1,0)\quad(\sigma\ne\sigma_0).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Choose a totally negative codifferent scalar

\[
\alpha\in\mathfrak D_F^{-1},\qquad \sigma(\alpha)<0
\quad\text{for every }\sigma,
\]

and put

\[
N=N(M,h,\alpha),\qquad
q(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbf Q}(\alpha h(x,y)).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Require `q` to be even and integral.  The easy sufficient parity hypothesis
is `h(x,x) in 2 O_F` for every `x`, but it is not necessary: a saturated
trace lattice, such as the silver carrier, may be even even though its field
norms no longer lie in `2 O_F`.

Restriction of scalars decomposes

\[
N_{\mathbf R}=\bigoplus_{\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbf R}E_\sigma,
\qquad q|_{E_\sigma}=\sigma(\alpha)h^\sigma.
\]

Consequently

\[
\operatorname{rk}N=e(n+1),\qquad
\operatorname{sig}N=(1,e(n+1)-1),
\tag{1.3}
\]

and `E=E_{sigma_0}` has signature `(1,n)`.  Multiplication by `O_F` gives
integral `q`-self-adjoint endomorphisms of `N`.  No endomorphism is asserted
to be an isometry, a surface automorphism, or an action on the transcendental
Hodge structure.

The pair `(q,O_F-action)` recovers `h`.  Indeed, for fixed `x,y`, the values

\[
q(ax,y)=\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha a h(x,y)),\qquad a\in F,
\]

determine `h(x,y)` by nondegeneracy of the field trace.  Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Cent}_{O(N,q)}(O_F)=O(M,h),
\tag{1.4}
\]

where the left side means the centralizer of the full field action.  If
`O_F` is generated by one displayed endomorphism, centralizing that generator
is enough.

## 2. Exact weak-carrier equivalence

Fix a deformation type whose Beauville lattice is `Lambda`, of signature

\[
(3,b_2-3),
\]

and whose marked period map is surjective onto the relevant period-domain
component.  K3 surfaces and OG10-type manifolds satisfy this hypothesis.

### Theorem 2.1 — weak full-Picard eigencarrier

For a fixed oriented admissible trace datum `(M,h,alpha)`, the following are
equivalent.

1. There is a projective marked manifold `X` of the fixed deformation type
   with
   
   \[
   \operatorname{NS}(X)\simeq N(M,h,\alpha),
   \]
   
   carrying the prescribed self-adjoint `O_F`-action on its full divisor
   lattice.
2. There is an orientation-compatible primitive embedding
   
   \[
   j:N(M,h,\alpha)\hookrightarrow\Lambda.
   \tag{2.1}
   \]

When these conditions hold, the carrier period domain has complex dimension

\[
b_2-e(n+1)-2.
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

The forward implication is the primitive Néron--Severi embedding in integral
second cohomology.  Conversely, the orthogonal complement `T=j(N)^perp` has
signature

\[
(2,b_2-e(n+1)-2).
\]

Choose an oriented period in `T` avoiding every rational hyperplane that
would add an integral `(1,1)` class.  Period surjectivity realizes it, and
the genericity choice gives `NS(X)=N`.  Since `N` has signature
`(1,rk(N)-1)`, it contains a positive integral vector; the projectivity
criterion makes `X` projective.  If `T` has rank two, its oriented real plane
is already the period plane and primitivity gives `NS(X)=N` exactly.  Formula
(2.2) is the dimension of the type-IV period domain orthogonal to `N`.
`square`

The primitive embedding is a finite discriminant-form problem.  For K3 it is
the existence of an even complement of the required signature with quadratic
form `-q_N`; for other deformation types the residual discriminant form is
that of `Lambda`, rather than zero.  A stable-range shortcut is only
sufficient.  Outside it, the exact local gluing problem must be solved.

### Corollary 2.2 — the dimension ceiling

A full-Picard degree-`e` eigencarrier for `H^n` requires

\[
e(n+1)\le b_2-2,
\qquad
\boxed{n\le
\left\lfloor\frac{b_2-2}{e}\right\rfloor-1.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

For a real-quadratic action this gives `n <= 9` on a K3 (`b2=22`) and
`n <= 10` on OG10 (`b2=24`).  This explains, rather than merely observes,
the carrier ladder in the table above.  It is a full-divisor-lattice bound;
a lower-rank invariant sublattice or a non-full-Picard construction has
different quantifiers.

### Coxeter-to-variety direction

Let `P` be a crystallographic Coxeter chamber over `F`, with a fixed
admissible integral realization `(M,h)` and oriented wall halfspaces.  If an
`alpha` makes its trace lattice even and (2.1) holds, Theorem 2.1 makes `P` a
literal divisor-eigenspace arrangement on a projective variety.  This is the
desired reverse direction from polytope to variety at the weak level.

For an **abstract** Coxeter polytope one must quantify over all admissible
normal scalings and integral field lattices realizing its wall directions.
Failure for one scaling is not failure for the abstract polytope.  Nor does
an unoriented Gram isometry suffice: positive rescalings must preserve the
chosen halfspaces.

## 3. The finite visible-wall theorem

The continuous period problem ends after Theorem 2.1.  The apparent infinite
divisor-wall problem has a finite alphabet.

### Theorem 3.1 — finite field-norm shells

Fix a negative integer `s`.  Let `delta in M` satisfy

\[
q(\delta,\delta)=s,
\]

and suppose its hyperplane meets the positive cone of the distinguished
eigenspace `E`.  Then

\[
t=h(\delta,\delta)
\]

is totally positive and satisfies

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbf Q}(\alpha t)=s.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Writing `c_sigma=-sigma(alpha)>0`, one has the explicit bounds

\[
0<\sigma(t)<\frac{-s}{c_\sigma}
\quad\text{for every real embedding }\sigma.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence only finitely many `t` occur.  They are found exactly by enumerating
the Minkowski lattice of the fixed fractional ideal containing `h(M,M)` in
the bounded box (3.2), then imposing (3.1), the actual value-module/parity
condition, and any divisibility condition inherited from
`N hookrightarrow Lambda`.

#### Proof

At every non-distinguished embedding, `h^sigma` is positive definite, so
`sigma(t)>0`.  At the distinguished embedding, a hyperplane meets hyperbolic
space exactly when its projected normal is spacelike for `q|E`; because
`sigma_0(alpha)<0`, this is equivalent to `sigma_0(t)>0`.  Thus `t` is
totally positive.  Equation (3.1) is the definition of `q`, and it becomes

\[
\sum_\sigma c_\sigma\sigma(t)=-s.
\]

Every summand is positive, proving (3.2).  A fractional ideal is a discrete
lattice in its Minkowski embedding, so a bounded box contains finitely many
elements. `square`

For K3 surfaces the only wall square is `s=-2`.  For a fixed hyperkähler
deformation type, boundedness of MBM squares gives finitely many possible
negative `s`; when the numerical wall orbits are classified, square,
divisibility, and discriminant orbit give a completely explicit filter.
Thus a fixed deformation type always has finitely many **field-norm types**
to search.  This is not a claim that each shell contains finitely many roots.

Unit-square rescaling can identify two values with the same restricted
hyperplane, but only after checking that the unit takes the actual module and
divisor class to another allowed class.  It must not be imposed solely from
equality in `F*/F*2`.

### Golden replay

For

\[
F=\mathbf Q(\sqrt5),\qquad
\alpha=-\phi/\sqrt5,
\]

the equation is `Tr(alpha(a+b phi))=-(a+b)`.  Exact enumeration gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
q(\delta)&\text{totally-positive field values}\
\hline
-2&1+\phi,\ 2,\ 3-\phi,\ 4-2\phi,\ 5-3\phi.
\end{array}
\]

If the field form is even, only `2` and `4-2phi` remain; they are one
unit-square mirror orbit.  For OG10 square `-4`, the even values are

\[
2+2\phi,\ 4,\ 6-2\phi,\ 8-4\phi,\ 10-6\phi.
\]

Together with the square-minus-two shell they reduce to the three known
OG10 mirror orbits represented by

\[
2,\qquad4,\qquad6-2\phi.
\]

The executable audit reproduces these lists without floating-point
comparisons.

## 4. Exact promotion to a cone of the variety

Primitive embedding proves that every wall normal is an algebraic divisor
class.  It does not prove effectivity, MBM membership, or completeness.  The
following levels are exact and must remain separate.

### 4.1 K3: necessary and sufficient eigen-nef promotion

Let `Delta(N)={delta in N:delta^2=-2}` and restrict every root hyperplane to
`E`.  Choose a regular `p in E` in the selected positive cone, so
`q(p)>0`, and let `C_p` be its component in the complement of the restricted
mirrors inside that positive cone (equivalently, in its hyperbolic slice).
Let `D` be the ambient positive root chamber containing `p`.
Convexity and equality of sign patterns give

\[
D\cap E=C_p.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The K3 Weyl group is transitive on ambient root chambers.  On a K3 supplied
by Theorem 2.1, choose `w` taking `D` to its ample chamber and conjugate the
`O_F`-action by `w`.  Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Nef}(X)\cap wE=w\overline{C_p}.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus every component of the **complete restricted K3 root arrangement** is
an eigen-nef section after a Weyl-framed marking.  This is both necessary and
sufficient at the section level.  It does not say that the field action is a
surface automorphism.

An arbitrary projected root arrangement need not be Coxeter.  Coxeter
promotion additionally requires that the actual facet normals have the
prescribed normalized Coxeter Gram entries and that their field reflections
are integral.  In the golden even normalization the finite-shell collapse
makes the complete restricted arrangement the norm-two integral field-
reflection arrangement, so this additional gate is automatic.  In the
silver saturation it is not automatic: the new square-minus-two divisor has
field norm `3-sqrt(2)` and must enter wall closure.

### 4.2 Hyperkähler: the exact conditional criterion

For a fixed deformation type and primitive embedding `j`, form the complete
set `Delta_j(N)` of primitive algebraic MBM classes, with square,
divisibility in `Lambda`, and monodromy orbit all checked.  For the movable
cone use the prime-exceptional subset; for a Kähler cone use all MBM wall
classes.

A prescribed eigenspace cone `C` is realized exactly when:

1. `C` is a connected component of the complement of the restrictions of
   **all** hyperplanes in `Delta_j(N)` (or the prime-exceptional subset);
2. the corresponding ambient chamber is a Kähler-type or fundamental
   exceptional chamber realized by a marked model in the fixed period fiber;
3. the desired facet projections are nonzero and irredundant; and
4. their normalized projected Gram matrix passes the Coxeter-angle,
   reflection-integrality, and orientation tests.

These conditions are necessary and sufficient.  A complete numerical
MBM-orbit classification and Theorem 3.1 make the list of visible
**field-norm types** finite, but a type may still contain infinitely many
roots.  They become an algorithm only after adding a terminating Vinberg/root
enumeration, a complete chamber-stabilizer argument, or another independent
complete-wall certificate.  For the frozen OG10 carrier,
the modulo-three embedding calculation removes both divisibility-three wall
types, leaving square `-2` and `-4`; the prime-exceptional/movable arrangement
is precisely the norm-two field-reflection arrangement.  Markman's chamber
theory then realizes every selected component by a same-period marked model.

## 5. Uniform arithmeticity and the stabilizer criterion

### Theorem 5.1 — no cusps and the dual volume test

Assume `n >= 2` and the signatures (1.1).  Let

\[
\Gamma=O^+(M,h).
\]

Let `R` be a `Gamma`-invariant collection of integral reflection roots,
meaning $s_r\in\Gamma$ for every $r\in R$, whose mirrors are locally finite.
Assume that it is the full $W$-stable mirror arrangement generated by these
reflections (the completeness hypothesis used below).  Put

\[
W=\langle s_r:r\in R\rangle,
\]

and let `C` be a chamber of the full `R`-mirror arrangement.  Then

\[
\Gamma/W\simeq\Gamma_C:=\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(C),
\tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{vol}(C)<\infty
\iff [\Gamma:W]<\infty
\iff |\Gamma_C|<\infty
\iff \overline C\text{ is compact}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

The form `h` is anisotropic over `F`: an `F`-isotropic nonzero vector would
remain nonzero at every embedding and would be isotropic for a positive-
definite conjugate form, a contradiction.  Borel--Harish-Chandra
arithmeticity and the anisotropic compactness criterion therefore make
`Gamma` a uniform lattice in `Isom(H^n)`.

The root predicate is `Gamma`-invariant, so `W` is normal.  Reflection-
chamber theory makes `W` simply transitive on chambers.  Every `Gamma`
translate of `C` has a unique `W` translate back to `C`, proving (5.1).
Covering multiplicity gives

\[
\operatorname{vol}(C)=
[\Gamma:W]\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb H^n),
\]

with the left side infinite at infinite index.  Finite index in a uniform
lattice makes `W` uniform, so its closed Coxeter fundamental chamber is
compact.  Conversely compactness implies finite volume. `square`

Local finiteness is automatic for roots drawn from finitely many fixed
field-norm shells: a compact set in the distinguished hyperbolic space bounds
the root at that embedding, conjugate definiteness bounds it at every other
embedding, and the Minkowski lattice is discrete.

### Corollary 5.2 — a short exact obstruction

To prove infinite chamber volume it suffices to exhibit elements of
`Gamma_C` generating an infinite group.  In particular, two exact chamber
involutions whose product has infinite order close the carrier negatively.
An exact characteristic polynomial, a Salem eigenvalue, or the elementary
trace bound `|tr(g)|>n+1` certifies infinite order.

This is the common mechanism in the intrinsic silver field-norm-two and
golden OG10 norm-two failures.  The archived separator arguments prove that
the displayed involutions stabilize the **complete chambers of those
invariant reflection arrangements**, not merely finite wall prefixes.  Their products
have exact traces above the finite-order bound, so both chambers have
infinite volume by (5.2).

For silver, “complete” in the preceding paragraph must not be read as
complete for all K3 `(-2)` walls.  The saturation divisor of field norm
`3-sqrt(2)` is not a field-reflection wall, so the full restricted K3
eigen-nef arrangement remains open; the audited Alexeev local-star slices
fail the Coxeter-angle prerequisite.

### Algebraic dual, when available

Let `G(X,O_F)` be the automorphism or birational group centralizing the
divisor-lattice field action.  If

1. a finite-index subgroup of `Gamma` extends through the primitive embedding
   as Hodge monodromy;
2. the ambient nef/movable chamber containing a regular eigenspace point is
   unique; and
3. Torelli identifies its monodromy stabilizer with `G(X,O_F)` up to finite
   kernel,

then `G(X,O_F)` is commensurable with `Gamma_C`.  Under these explicitly
stated hypotheses,

\[
\operatorname{vol}(C)<\infty
\iff |G(X,O_F)|<\infty.
\tag{5.3}
\]

This geometric equivalence is proved for the frozen OG10 carrier.  It is not
automatic for an arbitrary deformation type or arbitrary divisor-lattice
endomorphism.

## 6. What the modular-form track can and cannot decide

The Borcherds/Whittaker calculations occur **after** the weak-carrier and
visible-shell gates.

- The ordinary signature-`(22,2)` extension of the golden OG10 lattice has no
  reflective Borcherds product by the cited classification.  This obstructs
  that construction method, not reflectivity of the real-quadratic chamber.
- The totally-real signature repair has a nonzero cusp obstruction for the
  pure norm-two principal part.  The complete globally minimal off-slice
  compensator shell also has no target-preserving kernel.  Larger compensator
  shells remain open.
- Even a successful Whittaker lift would produce an automorphic divisor, not
  a Vinberg terminal certificate.  On the compact totally-real arithmetic
  quotient there is no cusp/Weyl-vector argument that turns the product into
  finite reflection index.

The correct use is therefore as a **finite carrier filter**: use the shell
theorem to specify the desired mirror divisor, test its modular obstruction,
and, if it survives, return to complete-wall equality and Theorem 5.1.  It
cannot bypass those gates.

## 7. Search algorithm with a terminal path

For a proposed Coxeter polytope or a proposed variety, the exact bidirectional
search is now:

1. fix an oriented crystallographic field realization `(M,h)` and a totally
   negative codifferent scalar `alpha`;
2. certify trace integrality, evenness, signature, saturation, and every
   integral facet reflection;
3. solve the primitive embedding problem in the chosen Beauville lattice;
4. enumerate the finite visible field-norm shells for every allowed divisor-
   wall orbit;
5. prove equality with a restricted nef, movable, or Kähler chamber;
6. reduce to the actual irredundant projected facets and certify its Coxeter
   Gram and orientation;
7. decide volume either primally by the literal Vinberg endpoint/parabolic
   criterion or dually by the complete-chamber stabilizer in Theorem 5.1.

A successful step 3 gives a family of weak carriers of dimension (2.2), not a
single lucky surface.  A failure at step 3 excludes only that fixed integral
realization.  A failure at step 5 is a geometric wall obstruction.  A failure
at step 7 is a genuine reflection-volume theorem for the chosen complete
arrangement.
