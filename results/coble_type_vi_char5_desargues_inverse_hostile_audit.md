## Certified plane theorem

Let

\[
 V=\{(a_0,\ldots,a_4)\in\mathbf F_5^5:\textstyle\sum a_i=0\}/
   \langle(1,1,1,1,1)\rangle .
\]

In `P(V)`, put `p_ij=[e_i-e_j]`.  Gauge the fifth coordinate to zero and
write the remaining first three coordinates as `(x:y:z)`.  In lexicographic
edge order the ten points are

```text
01 (1:4:0)    02 (1:0:4)    03 (1:0:0)    04 (1:3:3)
12 (0:1:4)    13 (0:1:0)    14 (1:2:1)    23 (0:0:1)
24 (1:1:2)    34 (1:1:1).
```

There are exactly ten triangle lines, one through the three edge-points of
each triangle of `K5`, and ten smooth cut conics, one through the six
edge-points in `delta(A)` for each two-subset `A`.  Each linear evaluation
matrix has the required one-dimensional kernel; the audit also checks that
no line or conic contains an unintended one of the ten points.  The point
replications are exactly `3` for lines and `6` for conics.

The cubic evaluation matrix has rank 10.  The 30 first-derivative conditions
on plane sextics have rank 27 in the 28-dimensional vector space of sextics.
Their kernel is generated by

\[
\begin{aligned}
F={}&x^4y^2-x^4yz+x^4z^2+2x^3y^3+2x^3z^3\\
 &+x^2y^4+x^2y^2z^2+x^2z^4-xy^4z-xyz^4\\
 &+y^4z^2+2y^3z^3+y^2z^4 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Because `6` is nonzero in `F5`, Euler's identity makes the value conditions
consequences of the gradient conditions.  Adding all ten value rows leaves
the rank equal to 27.

Let `X` be the blowup of these ten points, after base change to an algebraic
closure of `F5`.  Then

\[
 K_X=-3H+\sum E_{ij},\qquad
 \widetilde F=6H-2\sum E_{ij}=-2K_X,
 \qquad \widetilde F^2=-4.
\]

The exact calculations below prove

\[
 |-K_X|=\varnothing,qquad |-2K_X|=\{\widetilde F\},
\]

and `F` is geometrically integral with exactly ten ordinary nodes.  Its
strict transform is therefore a smooth rational curve.  Hence `X` is a
strong Coble surface.

## Exact singular scheme and geometric integrality

This is not inferred from ten visibly singular points.  Over `F5`, Singular
computes

```text
S = sat(Jac(F), (x,y,z))
T = intersection of the ten explicit homogeneous point ideals

dim affine cone(S)   1
degree Proj(S)       10
degree radical(S)    10
S = radical(S)       yes, both ideal reductions are zero
S = T                yes, both ideal reductions are zero
minimal primes       10.
```

Thus the projective singular scheme is **literally the ten reduced listed
points**.  On affine charts the ten quadratic tangent cones have nonzero
discriminants, with histogram `2:9, 3:1`, so all ten singularities are
ordinary nodes.

Exact factorization over `F5` returns one nonunit irreducible factor with
exponent one.  To pass from arithmetic to geometric irreducibility, suppose
that the sextic had `r>=2` geometric components.  Irreducibility over `F5`
makes Frobenius transitive on them, so they have common degree `6/r`.  Every
`F5`-rational point on one component is fixed by Frobenius and consequently
lies on every component.  Two distinct components would therefore share the
ten listed points, whereas Bézout permits at most

\[
                         (6/r)^2\leq 9.
\]

This contradiction proves geometric integrality.

## Twenty roots and boundary disjointness

The triangle and cut curves have strict-transform classes

\[
 L_T=H-\sum_{e\subset T}E_e,
 \qquad
 Q_A=2H-\sum_{e\in\delta(A)}E_e.
\]

Both have square `-2` and pair trivially with `K_X`.  All ten conics have
nonzero symmetric-matrix determinant, hence are smooth and geometrically
integral.

At every assigned center the audit evaluates the boundary tangent cone on
the root-curve tangent direction.  All 90 values are nonzero:

```text
triangle lines   1:24, 4:6
cut conics       2:18, 3:42
total            1:24, 2:18, 3:42, 4:6.
```

Therefore no root direction equals either boundary branch direction.  More
strongly, the local intersection multiplicity is exactly two.  The three
line centers contribute `3*2=6`, and the six conic centers contribute
`6*2=12`; these equal the global Bézout products with the sextic.  There are
no residual intersections away from the centers, so all twenty strict
transforms are disjoint from the boundary.

## Classification and lineage boundary

[Kondō's classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00591) gives a unique
one-boundary Type-VI strong Coble surface in characteristic five, with twenty
effective roots of graph `Gamma_VI` and automorphism group `S5`.  Its printed
constructions use the Hessian/Cremona quotient and a `P1 x P1` double-cover
model.  The pinned source inspected for this audit does not print the plane
quotient and sextic above.  That absence is not a worldwide novelty proof;
the classical Coble-sextic literature is much larger.

The twenty-root reflection graph here is exactly the marked `Gamma_VI`
graph.  Kondō's Vinberg finite-index result therefore puts this explicit
surface in the classified finite-automorphism case, and uniqueness identifies
it with the known Type-VI surface.  Combining that primary theorem with the
project's frozen full-nef theorem justifies importing the 57 effective
`(-1)`-curves and exact cone completeness.

The defensible positive claim is:

> A project-new, explicit `P -> surface -> same marked P` realization of the
> known Type-VI `H10/78` datum, at inverse level L3.

It is not yet the requested L4 leverage theorem for a variable class of
arithmetic Coxeter polytopes.  What it does provide is a complete template
whose obligations are now sharp:

```text
intrinsic contraction frame
 -> finite incidence realization
 -> anti-bicanonical existence and uniqueness
 -> exact singularity/geometric-integrality gates
 -> effective-root/Vinberg classification
 -> full negative-curve exhaustion
 -> exact marked return.
```

A class-wide theorem must replace each datum-specific arrow by an invariant
criterion and prove exhaustion of alternative carriers.  No such theorem,
new dimension, or improved general dimension bound is claimed here.
