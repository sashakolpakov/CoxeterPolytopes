# Deep finite-distance-eight payments: completed interfaces and next obligations

This is a bounded proof-development note, not a complete new weight rule
or a dimension theorem. The unrestricted ceiling remains65. Its proposed
finite change is to halve a unit at distance8 in a whole finite A/B/D
component when both marked roots have distance at least a chosen
threshold t=17 or20 from every endpoint, branch and endpoint of a
nonsimple bond. All existing ideal
weights and separate-component coupons would stay unchanged.

## Completed geometry and local payment interfaces

The frozen [full A43 high-domain theorem](fresh_full_a43_corridor_high_domain_20261017.md)
covers all26,812 corners of all6,703 threshold-seven high-overlap contexts
at depth20. Any such deep finite pair forces an opposite-black path of length at
most7 in the **full supporting star**. It is not an unconditional
geometric exclusion of a deep pair. The explicit full-star qualification
is essential; a short path may use new supporting roots. At depth17,
the corresponding A39 successor must be verified and bound separately.
Its authoritative proof and checks are outside this note and the cycle
interface; depth17 high-domain coverage is not inferred from the
depth20 theorem.

A finite corner at graph distance at most7 retains its full unit. The
current rank16/17 deletions and halves preserve every finite short8
pair, and the large-D half change concerns distance9 only. The proposed
new change also leaves every short7 pair intact. The frozen
[finite7 triangle theorem](fresh_finite7_ideal5_triangle_payments_20261017.md)
therefore preserves every triangle's two-unit payment without a new
triangle census.

An ideal corner at distance6 or7 need not have a unit: affine cycle
pairs at those distances can have weight zero, and large classical
trees far from their features use universal replacements. Thus one
cannot identify the A43 short-path alternative directly with a unit
at that same ideal corner. The inherited actual-corner extraction and
factor-fiber theorem must be followed by its cycle/tree replacement.

The new [deep finite8 cycle interface](fresh_deep_finite8_cycle_interface_20261017.md)
closes this particular dependence on finite8 payments. A single endpoint3
attachment along an arc of length8 has its other mark at distance
complementary_arc-1 from a fixed whole-component leaf. Therefore the
apparently problematic cycle14 arcs(6,8) and cycle15 arcs(7,8) are
protected at distances5 and6 from a leaf; they are not deep pairs.
All replacement outputs needed for a short6/7 ideal alternative retain
their old payments. Endpoint4 and second-node3 outputs have a feature
at distance0 or1 from the new mark and cannot be deep either.

The actual universal-cycle dependence is arcs P=8,Q>=t+1 with endpoint3
on the8-arc. The new mark can be deep only if it has a spectator
neighbor. The inherited unequal-height coordinate Cauchy bound then
changes one fixed profile constant from1 to1/2. The exact successor
checks all36 ordered pairs and144 rectangle vertices separately at
Q=18 and Q=21, with strict minima29/144 and73/336 respectively. The
residual six-profile list and height bounds are constant on either
tail; monotonicity in1/Q covers every Q above its endpoint.
The small cycle equalities, whole-leaf9 outputs, tree finite paths of
length at most7, and their separate coupons remain unchanged.

The relevant implementation interfaces are:

* `fresh_finite8_cycle_replacement_20261017.classification` determines
  the old finite8, whole-leaf9 and spectator-contact cases.
* Its `rectangles` uses one fixed Cauchy side and does not add two
  coordinate lower bounds.
* `fresh_distance8_ideal_corner_replacement_20261017.cycle_profiles`
  supplies the complete weak heights and positions in the all-rank
  cycle classification.
* `fresh_simple_cusp_affine_a_quadrilateral_20261017.value` supplies the
  normalized determinant formula.
* The new `fresh_deep_finite8_cycle_interface_20261017.cycle_tail`
  admits `end3_0` at P=8,Q>=t+1, subtracts1/2 from its norm constant on
  one fixed side, and records every exact rectangle value.

## What a complete payment successor still has to prove

First compose the full-star short7 extraction with the retained finite,
basic ideal and replaced ideal payments. A proof should track the whole
quadrilateral's total and the actual factor/component roles. It should
not count a transferred ideal pair as an additional literal unit, or
add the original deep half twice.

Next cover the **low-core and high-disjoint branches at threshold7**.
The old distance8 packages prove their statements under absence of a
path of length at most8. That hypothesis fails precisely when a new
deep distance8 unit is being reduced. Their earlier whole-component
bound12 and disjoint exclusions cannot simply be cited with threshold7
substituted. The complete high-overlap A43 theorem does not cover these
two branches. Either extend the forced-corridor Gram obstruction to
their full all-label domains, or establish retained alternative payments
there with complete supporting-root control.

Then audit every use of the former generic finite-short8 branch in
later local refinements. Most literal alternatives are sealed whole
components of order at most17, whole-leaf9 pairs, or separate coupons,
so neither a depth17 nor20 pair can occur in those explicit outputs. However,
an arbitrary-support argument can first discharge a newly created
short8 path before reaching its sealed case. Its short8 branch must
be routed through the completed new short7/high/low/disjoint theorem,
or checked directly; the small size of the subsequent sealed case
does not alone justify it. This is a provenance/composition task,
not another repetition of the4983 A43 template values.

Finally encode the simultaneous finite pair rule and recompute the
complete typed finite-forest budget, retaining every current coupon.
No new coupon is introduced by the cycle successor. A new ambient
bound would additionally need a full local-to-global binder using the
[already positive ambient65 ideal margins](fresh_ambient65_ideal_margin_closure_20261017.md)
and the exact finite maximum.
