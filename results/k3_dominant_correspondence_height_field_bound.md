# A one-intersection AG height gate for full-trace fields

**Status (2026-08-11): proved, scoped conditional field-finiteness
theorem.**  In an
admissible full restriction-of-scalars surface carrier, one positive
intersection number

\[
                 H\cdot \tau_\theta(H)
\tag{0.1}
\]

bounds the distinguished conjugate of every totally positive field
coordinate whose distinguished conjugate dominates the others.  A bounded
field-generating packet of such coordinates therefore gives an explicit
field-discriminant bound.  For Coxeter coordinates, the hypothesis is
especially concrete:
the two-wall cyclic Cartan products have every non-distinguished conjugate
at most \(4\), so a bounded mixed correspondence degree against one
big-and-nef divisor bounds every conjugate.

This supplies the missing **shape** of a project-native field-height gate.
It does not prove that reflection maximality supplies the bound in (0.1).
It also does not construct, or rule out, a nonorthogonally coupled rank-six
counterfamily allowed to have unbounded correspondence degree.

## 1. The signed-place identity

Let \(k\) be totally real of degree \(d\), with distinguished embedding
\(\sigma _0\).  Let \(N\) be the full trace lattice of an admissible
rank-\(n+1\) \(k\)-module.  Thus its real intersection space has an
orthogonal decomposition

\[
 N_{\mathbf R}=E_{\sigma _0}\oplus
       \bigoplus_{\sigma\ne\sigma _0}E_\sigma,
\tag{1.1}
\]

where the intersection form \(q\) has signature \((1,n)\) on
\(E_{\sigma _0}\) and is negative definite on every other \(E_\sigma\).
Multiplication by an algebraic integer \(\theta\in k\) is the integral
self-adjoint operator \(\tau _\theta\), acting on \(E_\sigma\) by
\(\sigma(\theta)\).

Here “full trace” means that the intersection space under discussion is
exactly \(N_{\mathbf R}\).  If \(N\) is only a proper summand of a larger
Néron--Severi lattice, Theorem 1.1 applies to a positive class lying in that
invariant summand.  It does not apply to an arbitrary ample class having an
uncontrolled component in the complement.

### Theorem 1.1 (dominant spectral height)

Assume

\[
 0<\sigma(\theta)\le\sigma _0(\theta)
             \qquad\text{for every }\sigma:k\hookrightarrow\mathbf R.
\tag{1.2}
\]

For every \(H\in N\) with \(H^2=q(H,H)>0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 H\cdot\tau _\theta(H)
       \ \ge\ \sigma _0(\theta)H^2.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

More exactly, if \(H=\sum_\sigma H_\sigma\) and
\(w_\sigma=q(H_\sigma,H_\sigma)\), then

\[
 H\cdot\tau _\theta(H)-\sigma _0(\theta)H^2
 =\sum_{\sigma\ne\sigma _0}
   \bigl(\sigma(\theta)-\sigma _0(\theta)\bigr)w_\sigma
 \ge0.
\tag{1.4}
\]

### Proof

Every \(w_\sigma\) with \(\sigma\ne\sigma _0\) is nonpositive.  Orthogonality
and the scalar action give

\[
 H^2=\sum_\sigma w_\sigma,
 \qquad
 H\cdot\tau _\theta(H)=
             \sum_\sigma\sigma(\theta)w_\sigma.
\]

Subtracting \(\sigma _0(\theta)H^2\) gives (1.4).  Each factor in each
summand is nonpositive, so every summand is nonnegative.  This proves
(1.3).  Notice that \(H_{\sigma _0}^2>0\) automatically: all other
summands are nonpositive while \(H^2>0\).  \(\square\)

The inequality uses the one-positive-place Hodge signature.  It is not an
operator-norm estimate in a chosen basis and is unchanged by re-marking the
integral lattice.

### Corollary 1.2 (one scalar gives a spectral envelope)

If

\[
                  H^2\ge s_0>0,
 \qquad H\cdot\tau _\theta(H)\le C,
\tag{1.5}
\]

then every conjugate of \(\theta\) belongs to

\[
                  0<\sigma(\theta)\le {C\over s_0}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

If \(\theta\) generates \(k\), its monic minimal polynomial

\[
 x^d+c_{d-1}x^{d-1}+\cdots+c_0
\]

satisfies

\[
 |c_{d-j}|\le {d\choose j}(C/s_0)^j,
 \qquad
 |\operatorname {Disc}k|
 \le |\operatorname {disc}(\theta)|
 \le (C/s_0)^{d(d-1)}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

### Proof

Equation (1.6) is Theorem 1.1.  The coefficient estimate is the elementary
symmetric-polynomial bound.  The polynomial discriminant is the square of
the Vandermonde product; every difference of two roots is smaller in
absolute value than \(C/s_0\).  Finally

\[
 \operatorname {disc}(\theta)
 =\operatorname {Disc}(k)
   [\mathcal O_k:\mathbf Z[\theta]]^2.
\]

No number-field finiteness theorem is needed: the integral coefficient
boxes in (1.7) are already a finite, exactly enumerable list.  Every
irreducible polynomial in those boxes defines at most one field up to
isomorphism, so this proves a finite field set, not merely bounded field
degree.  \(\square\)

## 2. Joint generators and an explicit primitive element

The ground field need not be generated by one displayed coordinate.  The
following elementary lemma keeps the result effective.

### Lemma 2.1 (positive primitive combination)

Suppose the algebraic integers
\(\theta _1,\ldots,\theta _r\) jointly generate a totally real field \(k\)
of degree \(d\).  Put

\[
                 M_d={d(d-1)\over2}+1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

There are integers \(1\le c_j\le M_d\) for which

\[
                       \theta=\sum_{j=1}^r c_j\theta_j
\tag{2.2}
\]

generates \(k\).

### Proof

For each unordered pair of distinct embeddings \(\sigma,\rho\), the bad
coefficient vectors satisfy the proper real hyperplane

\[
 \sum_j c_j\bigl(\sigma(\theta_j)-\rho(\theta_j)\bigr)=0.
\]

It is proper because the joint generators separate embeddings.  Each such
hyperplane contains at most \(M_d^{r-1}\) points of
\(\{1,\ldots,M_d\}^r\).  There are \(d(d-1)/2\) hyperplanes, and their union
therefore contains strictly fewer than \(M_d^r\) grid points.  At a point
outside the union, (2.2) has \(d\) distinct conjugates and hence generates
\(k\).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (bounded dominant packet)

Assume each \(\theta_j\) is totally positive and dominant as in (1.2), and

\[
 H^2\ge s_0,
 \qquad H\cdot\tau_{\theta_j}(H)\le C.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then the primitive element (2.2) has all conjugates in

\[
        0<\sigma(\theta)\le
        R:=rM_d{C\over s_0},
\tag{2.4}
\]

and consequently

\[
             |\operatorname {Disc}k|\le R^{d(d-1)}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Thus bounded degree, a bounded number of returned coordinates, and the one
mixed intersection (2.3) per coordinate give a finite field set.  This is
strictly weaker input than a bounded full operator matrix or a finite
integral Gram image.

## 3. The Coxeter pair-spectral specialization

Let \(M\) be the returned order-module, let \(e_i,e_j\in M\) be two facet
normals, and assume their reflections \(s_i,s_j\) preserve \(M\).  Write the
Cartan coefficients

\[
 a_{ij}={2h(e_i,e_j)\over h(e_i,e_i)},\qquad
 a_{ji}={2h(e_j,e_i)\over h(e_j,e_j)}
\]

in the fraction field.  For nonprimitive displayed normals, reflection
preservation need not put the individual \(a_{ij}\) in the returned order.
The scale-invariant product is nevertheless integral.  Indeed, on the
rank-\((n+1)\) field space,

\[
 \operatorname {tr}_k(s_is_j)=n-3+a_{ij}a_{ji},
\]

so preservation of the order-module gives

\[
 \xi_{ij}=a_{ij}a_{ji}
 ={4h(e_i,e_j)^2\over h(e_i,e_i)h(e_j,e_j)}
 =\operatorname {tr}_k(s_is_j)-(n-3)\in\mathcal O_k.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently, a returned primitive crystallographic normalization may
certify both Cartan entries directly.  This is the exact integrality
premise: algebraic integrality of \(\xi_{ij}\) is not inferred from the
abstract Coxeter diagram alone.  At
every non-distinguished embedding, the field form is positive definite.
Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives

\[
                       0\le\sigma(\xi_{ij})\le4.
\tag{3.2}
\]

At the distinguished place:

- intersecting or asymptotic facet hyperplanes have
  \(0\le\sigma _0(\xi_{ij})\le4\);
- ultraparallel facet hyperplanes have
  \(\sigma _0(\xi_{ij})>4\), so \(\xi_{ij}\) is totally positive and its
  distinguished conjugate dominates all the others.

If \(\xi_{ij}\ne0\), none of its conjugates can vanish: an embedding of a
field is injective.  Thus every nonzero coordinate in (3.2) is strictly
positive at every place.  The zero coordinate is rational and may be
discarded.  Hence Theorem 1.1
gives the following result without a reflective-lattice classification.

### Theorem 3.1 (pair-generated K3 field gate)

Consider **exact full-Picard trace** K3 eigen-nef carriers of hyperbolic
dimension \(n\ge2\), so

\[
                  \operatorname {NS}(X)=N
\tag{3.3a}
\]

with no extra Néron--Severi complement.  Assume:

1. the ground field is generated by nonzero two-facet coordinates
   \(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_r\) from (3.1), the returned coefficient order
   contains them, and their multiplication operators act integrally on
   \(N\);
2. the carrier returns one integral big-and-nef class \(H\) with
   \(H\in N=\operatorname {NS}(X)\) and \(H^2\ge s_0\); and
3. for every ultraparallel generator, its multiplication correspondence
   satisfies
   
   \[
                 H\cdot\tau_{\xi_j}(H)\le C.
   \tag{3.3}
   \]

Then every conjugate of every \(\xi_j\) is at most

\[
                         R_0=\max\{4,C/s_0\}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Since the full trace lattice has rank \(d(n+1)\le20\), one has

\[
                         d\le6.
\tag{3.5}
\]

If \(d=1\), the field is \(\mathbf Q\) and the finite-field conclusion is
immediate.  Suppose henceforth that \(d\ge2\).

From any pair-generating set one can choose at most
\(\lfloor\log _2d\rfloor\le2\) generators: adjoining a genuinely new
coordinate multiplies the current subfield degree by at least two.  This
selection uses pair coordinates only; it does not replace arbitrary field
generators by dominant elements.  With

\[
 D=6,\qquad M_D=16,\qquad r\le2,
\tag{3.6}
\]

Lemma 2.1 gives a primitive integral generator whose conjugates are bounded
by

\[
                         R=32R_0.
\tag{3.7}
\]

In particular

\[
 \boxed{
 |\operatorname {Disc}k|
 \le \bigl(32\max\{4,C/s_0\}\bigr)^{30}.}
\tag{3.8}
\]

For a K3 divisor lattice \(H^2\) is a positive even integer, so one may take
\(s_0=2\).  The coefficient bounds in (1.7) give a literal finite search
over monic integer polynomials of degrees at most six.  The exponent \(30\)
in (3.8) is \(D(D-1)\): a degree-\(D\) Vandermonde has
\(D(D-1)/2\) differences and the discriminant squares their product.

The pair-generation hypothesis is real content.  General Vinberg fields
are generated by cyclic products, and a longer cycle can carry a quadratic
sign extension invisible in the pair squares.  A more general compiler may
replace item 1 by a bounded packet of signed cyclic coordinates.  It must
then return either a uniform conjugate envelope for those coordinates or a
totally positive dominant affine normalization to which Theorem 1.1
applies.  Merely embedding the abstract Gram lattice in \(\operatorname{NS}\)
does neither.

## 4. Why (0.1) is genuinely geometric

Let \(X\) be the carrier surface and let
\(Z_\theta\in CH^2(X\times X)_{\mathbf Q}\) act as
\(\tau_\theta\) on \(\operatorname {NS}(X)\).  The projection formula gives

\[
 \boxed{
 H\cdot\tau_\theta(H)
 =\deg\bigl(Z_\theta\cdot p_1^*H\cdot p_2^*H\bigr).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus (1.5) is the bidegree of a marked correspondence against a marked
polarization.  It is constant in a connected bounded family carrying that
marked cohomological action.  An effective correspondence of bounded
\((H,H)\)-degree supplies it directly; a signed return grammar supplies it
when the positive and negative cycle degrees are bounded.  Only this single
mixed degree is used.  The theorem does not assume bounded Picard
discriminant, bounded entries of \(\tau_\theta\), or bounded full Chow data.

This identifies a precise enhancement of the proposed

\[
 P\longmapsto
 (X,\operatorname {NS}X,\operatorname {Nef}X,
     \text{field action},\text{canonical/MMP data}).
\]

For the non-rational branch, the compiler must additionally return a
canonical positive class \(H_P\) and bound the scalar bidegrees (4.1) for a
field-generating spectral packet.  If the packet is pair-generated, (3.8)
then turns the K3 rank ceiling into a finite field set.

## 5. Exact sharpness against the rank-four family

The exact maximal eigen-interval family has

\[
 k_a=\mathbf Q\bigl(a+\sqrt{a^2-1}\bigr),
 \qquad H_a^2=14,
\tag{5.1}
\]

but, for the dominant totally positive generator
\(t_a=a+\sqrt{a^2-1}\),

\[
                   H_a\cdot\tau_{t_a}(H_a)=32a-10.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Theorem 1.1 reads

\[
                   32a-10\ge14t_a.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The executable certificate checks this inequality exactly for the frozen
family and records

\[
 \operatorname {disc}(x^2-2ax+1)=4(a^2-1)
       <\left({32a-10\over14}\right)^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently a fixed polarization square, exact eigen-nef return, complete
restricted saturation, and declared-host reflection maximality can coexist
with infinitely many fields only by letting the mixed correspondence height
escape.  The new hypothesis cuts exactly that known escape direction.

This does **not** show it is logically necessary in every conceivable
carrier: a different bounded primitive spectral envelope would also suffice.
It shows that (4.1) is a minimal scalar AG replacement for the much stronger
bounded-operator-packet condition.

If a proposed carrier has \(N\subsetneq\operatorname {NS}(X)\), an ample or
big-and-nef class need not lie in \(N\).  One must then either return a
positive \(H\in N\) explicitly, extend the field action to the complement
with a signed-place decomposition proving (1.4), or use a different height
argument.  The theorem makes no automatic-complement claim.

## 6. Exact conclusion and remaining \(n\ge2\) problem

The current field picture is therefore:

1. the orthogonal rank-six stabilization closes the eigenslice but fails
   algebraic integrality of its mixed facet reflection;
2. any orthogonal or nonorthogonal full-trace family with a bounded
   **field-generating** pair-spectral correspondence packet has bounded
   field discriminant by (3.8); and
3. a possible coupled infinite-field counterfamily must evade the theorem
   by having unbounded mixed bidegree, by requiring unbounded/long cyclic
   generators, or by failing to return a canonical positive class.

The still-open global implication is

\[
 \text{reflection maximality + exact eigen-nef carrier}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{bounded canonical mixed degree (4.1)}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Proving (6.1), or proving a bounded signed-cycle substitute, would give the
non-rational field-set part of the AG route.  No dimension or field
finiteness theorem for reflection groups is imported here.
