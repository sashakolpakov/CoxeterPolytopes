# Maximal simplex incidence behind the triangle-only obstruction

The [literal deletion census](../scripts/fresh_sqrt5_rank13_triangle_simplex_flags_20261017.py) checks every one-root deletion of each fourteen-root star in the [exact triangle dual](fresh_sqrt5_rank13_active_triangle_dual_20261017.md), recognizing a spherical forest only through the complete finite-diagram catalogue. The [saved records](fresh_sqrt5_rank13_triangle_simplex_flags_20261017.json) find **409 maximal triangle stars, 743 tetrahedral stars, and 2,497 four-dimensional simplex stars**. Every additional spherical deletion, resulting whole forest, and triangular root triple is explicit. Generation and fresh saved reconstruction check the same full deletion list.

Here is the geometric reason these deletions impose incidence rather than merely providing more unrelated local rows. Let P be a compact acute-angled hyperbolic polytope, H a strict interior timelike point, and J a spherical set of its walls. Its positive definite Gram matrix has nonpositive off-diagonal entries and nonnegative inverse. Orthogonal projection of H onto the common-wall subspace subtracts nonpositive coefficients of the normals, hence adds a nonnegative normal combination. Its products with every other wall remain strictly negative, and its norm is more negative. Thus a spherical set of n walls in dimension n gives an actual finite vertex of P.

For a genuine compact triangular two-face, its n+1 wall normals are independent. Eliminate the positive definite block of its n-2 containing walls. The remaining Schur block is the Gram matrix of the three side normals in the triangle's Lorentzian three-space. Three distinct sides of a nondegenerate compact hyperbolic triangle are independent (otherwise the three projective side lines would be concurrent). Hence the Schur block, and therefore the full Gram matrix, is nonsingular.

Let S be the indices whose one-root deletion is spherical, with m=|S|. Each such deletion gives an actual future finite vertex. Put W equal to the other n+1-m indices. On the common W subspace, invertibility of the full Gram identifies the remaining m wall products with coordinates; the cone satisfying these m inequalities is precisely the nonnegative cone on the m finite dual rays. Its projectivization is a compact (m-1)-simplex. Every other wall of P is strict at all m vertices, so no additional wall cuts this simplex. Its triangles are exactly the root triples in S.

This simplex extension is unique and maximal among simplex faces containing the original triangle. Any simplex face containing that triangle has all its defining walls among the same n+1 walls: its boundary walls either contain the triangle or supply one of its three sides. Every vertex of that simplex is consequently one of the spherical deletions already in S. Thus all triangular two-faces of P are partitioned by these maximal simplex extensions; different extensions cannot share a triangle.

This permits a different payment rule. Require total corner payment at least two times the number of triangles in each maximal simplex, rather than two at every individual triangle. The requirements are two for a triangle, eight for a tetrahedron, and twenty for a four-dimensional simplex. Summing over the unique partition still pays all triangles globally, while allowing compensation between triangles forced to occur together. Quadrilateral payments and complete vertex budgets remain separate requirements.

The census alone does not establish a feasible weighting or complete face coverage. Its observed simplex dimensions two through four are not asserted as a global upper bound: a projected face need not itself be Coxeter. The [fresh partial aggregate model](../scripts/fresh_sqrt5_rank13_simplex_aggregate_lp_20261017.py) tests only the maximal simplex stars extending the saved positive dual rows. All omitted simplex types, quadrilaterals, and absent whole forests remain outstanding before a dimension argument could be attempted.
