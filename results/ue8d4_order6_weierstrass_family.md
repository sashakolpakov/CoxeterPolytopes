# Explicit Weierstrass realization of the `D4`-triality Coxeter section

## Positive theorem

Let `omega` be a primitive cube root of unity.  For parameters `a,b,c` such
that `0,1,a,b,c` are pairwise distinct, let `X_(a,b,c)` be the minimal
resolution of

```text
y^2 = x^3 + t^3 (t-1)(t-a)(t-b)(t-c),
```

and define

```text
zeta(x,y,t) = (omega*x, -y, t).
```

Then `zeta` is a primitive non-symplectic automorphism of order six.  For a
very general parameter triple,

```text
NS(X_(a,b,c)) = U + E8(-1) + D4(-1),
```

and `zeta` is the identity on `U+E8(-1)` and the outer order-three triality on
`D4(-1)`.  Consequently this family is an explicit algebro-geometric model
for Brandhorst--Hofmann row `0.6.3.1`, hence for the previously certified
`R14_UE8D4_C3_S3` fixed-nef section and its finite-volume noncompact
`H11/13` Coxeter polytope.

This is a new explicit realization in this project.  The abstract Coxeter
polytope and its arithmetic lattice are known, and no worldwide priority for
the equation itself is claimed.

## The geometric construction

In homogeneous base coordinates `[U:V]`, the Weierstrass coefficient is the
degree-twelve form

```text
A_12 = U^3 (U-V)(U-aV)(U-bV)(U-cV) V^5.
```

Thus the displayed affine equation is a global elliptic K3 Weierstrass model.
Its discriminant is `-432 A_12^2`, and the usual characteristic-zero Kodaira
valuation table gives

| place | `ord(A_12)` | fiber | Euler number | fiber root lattice |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| `t=0` | 3 | `I0*` | 6 | `D4(-1)` |
| `t=1,a,b,c` | 1 | four `II` fibers | `4*2` | none |
| `t=infinity` | 5 | `II*` | 10 | `E8(-1)` |

The Euler numbers sum to 24.  The fiber, zero section, and nonidentity
components of the two reducible fibers therefore give

```text
U + D4(-1) + E8(-1)  subset NS(X),
```

of rank 14 and determinant `-4`.

The two-form is `dt wedge dx/y`.  Its pullback under `zeta` is
`-omega*(dt wedge dx/y)`, a primitive sixth-root multiple, so `zeta` is
primitive non-symplectic of order six.

At the `I0*` point, the Tate-resolution cubic has three roots corresponding
to the three nonidentity outer components.  Multiplication of `x` by `omega`
cycles these roots; `y -> -y` acts trivially on this order-two component
group.  Hence `zeta` fixes the central `D4` node and cycles its three outer
nodes.  The `II*` diagram has no nontrivial graph automorphism, so all its
component classes are fixed.  The resulting Picard characteristic polynomial
is exactly

```text
(lambda-1)^12 (lambda^2+lambda+1).
```

The fixed and coinvariant lattices are

```text
NS^zeta       = U + E8(-1) + A2(-1),  rank 12, determinant -3,
(NS^zeta)^perp = A2(-2),               Gram [[-4,2],[2,-4]].
```

The coinvariant is rootless.  Entry by entry, this is the same `D4` action as
the pinned Brandhorst--Hofmann `0.6.3.1` certificate.

## Why the generic Picard lattice is exact

The displayed divisors initially give a lower bound of rank 14.  It is exact
for the geometric generic, equivalently a very general complex, member.

The unordered positions of the four type-`II` fibers, after fixing the
distinct `I0*` and `II*` fibers at zero and infinity and quotienting by
`t -> lambda*t`, form a three-dimensional parameter space.  Forgetting the
marked fibration has discrete fibers: elliptic fibrations and sections on a
fixed K3 surface are represented by integral Neron--Severi classes.  Hence
the family of K3/automorphism pairs also has dimension three.

For a primitive order-six automorphism, the transcendental lattice is a
module over `Q(zeta_6)`, so both its rank and the Picard rank
`22-rank(T)` are even.  Since the displayed divisors already give Picard rank
at least 14, any strict jump would therefore have Picard rank at least 16.
The transcendental rank would then be at most six and the relevant
primitive-eigenvalue period space would have projective dimension at most
two.  Local Torelli would contradict the three-dimensional family.  Thus the
very general Picard rank is 14.

There is also no same-rank saturation ambiguity.  The only possible proper
overlattice would have index two and determinant one.  It would be an even
unimodular lattice of signature `(1,13)`, which cannot exist because
`1-13` is not divisible by eight.  Therefore the rank-14 sublattice is all of
`NS`.

This proves exact generic `NS`; it does **not** certify a particular rational
triple `(a,b,c)`.  Producing a named number-field member and proving its
Picard rank by good reductions remains an optional arithmetic refinement,
not a gap in the family theorem.

## The missing lineage: a Noether--Lefschetz collision

[Dillies's order-six classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.5228) proves
that the relevant elliptic models have the form `y^2=x^3+p_12(t)` and that
the fiberwise order-six lift is `(x,y,t) -> (omega*x,-y,t)`.  Its table 1,
row 11, gives the pre-collision fixed-locus data for `zeta` and `zeta^2`
(not the complete fixed-locus data of every power) and has generic
root-multiplicity pattern

```text
5, 2, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1,
```

corresponding to `II* + IV + 5 II`.  The present family imposes the single
collision

```text
II + IV  -->  I0*,
```

so the pattern becomes

```text
5, 3, 1, 1, 1, 1.
```

This is the decisive Noether--Lefschetz specialization: the generic `A2`
fiber root lattice is replaced by `D4`, increasing Picard rank by two, and
the order-three action becomes the three-arm triality required by the
Coxeter construction.  It also explains why a search only through generic
rows of the elliptic classification misses the explicit `U+E8+D4` model.

The collision does change the fixed locus of `zeta^3`.  Its non-section fixed
curve is the normalization of `x^3=-p_12(t)`.  Before collision this cyclic
triple cover has seven fully ramified branch points and genus 5; afterwards
it has five and genus 3, by `2g-2=-6+2r`.  The detailed fixed-locus
fingerprint of the identified
[Brandhorst--Hofmann row](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2023.50) is:

| power | fixed locus | Euler characteristic |
|---|---|---:|
| `zeta` | 5 points of type `(2,5)`, 8 of type `(3,4)`, 2 rational curves | 17 |
| `zeta^2` | 5 points, 3 rational curves, one genus-2 curve | 9 |
| `zeta^3` | 6 rational curves and one genus-3 curve | 8 |

The certificate directly checks the generic transcendental characteristic
polynomial `Phi_6^4`, the displayed Picard action, and hence the `H^2` traces
`15,7,6`; topological Lefschetz gives the checked Euler characteristics
`17,9,8`.  The finer point/curve decomposition in the table is the BH-row
fingerprint.  For `zeta` and `zeta^2` it agrees with Dillies's local
classification across the collision.  For `zeta^3`, the genus-three curve is
the cyclic cover just computed, while the six rational curves also follow
from the non-symplectic-involution fixed-locus formula for the 2-elementary
invariant lattice `U+E8(-1)+D4(-1)` with `(r,a)=(14,2)`.

## Coxeter consequence

The equation supplies the algebro-geometric carrier and the exact triality
action.  Finite volume is still supplied by the independent complete Vinberg
audit already pinned for `R14_UE8D4_C3_S3`: the fixed lattice has hyperbolic
dimension 11, its chamber has 13 facets, 27 finite vertices, one cusp, 162
elliptic edges, and zero failed edge or parabolic-neighborhood audits.

Therefore the output is an explicit K3/Weierstrass realization of a known
finite-volume noncompact Coxeter polytope, not merely a lattice-period
existence statement.
