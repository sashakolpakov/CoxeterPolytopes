# Direct full-nef surface carriers cannot reach compact dimension four

**Status (2026-08-09): proved return theorem for the stated carrier class.**

This is a `polytope -> surface -> theorem on the same polytope` result.  It
does not begin with a chosen surface: it applies whenever a `P`-first compiler
claims that the complete nef cone of a smooth projective surface is the given
integral-reflection Coxeter chamber.

## 1. General theorem

Let `X` be a smooth complex projective surface, put

```text
N = NS(X)/torsion,   rho = rank N,   n = rho-1,
```

and suppose `rho>=3`.  Assume that the projectivization of the entire nef cone
is a finite-sided hyperbolic Coxeter polyhedron `P` and that reflection in
every facet preserves `N`.  Then

\[
 \boxed{P\text{ compact}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad n\le3.}
 \tag{1.1}
\]

More precisely, whenever `P` has finite volume,

\[
 P\text{ compact}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 N_{\mathbf Q}\text{ is anisotropic}.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

If the complete nef cone is rational polyhedral, finite volume is automatic
in `n>=2` by the convex--Hodge nef-volume theorem, so no extra volume
hypothesis is needed in that common setting.

### Proof

Hodge index gives signature `(1,n)` on `N`.  Let `W` be generated by the
facet reflections.  Coxeter's reflection theorem makes `P` a fundamental
polyhedron for `W`, and the integrality hypothesis gives

```text
W <= O^+(N).
```

The arithmetic group `O^+(N)` has finite covolume.  If `P` has finite volume,
then `W` is also a lattice; a lattice subgroup of a lattice has finite index,
because a fundamental domain for `W` is the disjoint union, modulo boundary,
of `[O^+(N):W]` arithmetic fundamental domains.  Hence

\[
 [O^+(N):W]<\infty.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

The arithmetic quotient of an orthogonal group of signature `(1,n)` is
compact exactly when its rational quadratic space is anisotropic.  Uniformity
is invariant under finite index, so (1.2) follows from (1.3).

Meyer's theorem says that every indefinite rational quadratic form in at
least five variables represents zero.  Thus `rho>=5`, equivalently `n>=4`,
makes `N_Q` isotropic and forces every finite-volume integral-reflection
chamber to be noncompact.  This proves (1.1).

The bound is sharp inside the algebraic carrier world.  The
Gritsenko--Nikulin rational-Weyl-vector K3 classification contains four
anisotropic rank-three carriers and the anisotropic rank-four carrier
`S_(4,28)`.  The latter returns a compact complete full-nef Coxeter chamber
in `H3`.  Thus the same `P -> K3 -> classification -> P` mechanism supplies
both the obstruction above `H3` and a positive survivor on its boundary; see
[`polytope_to_k3_weyl_vector_transfer.md`](polytope_to_k3_weyl_vector_transfer.md).

## 2. Stronger rational-surface theorem

If `X` is rational and `rho(X)>=2`, its complete nef projectivization is
never compact, even without a Coxeter or reflection hypothesis.

Indeed run the smooth rational-surface minimal model program.  If the minimal
model is a Hirzebruch surface, compose the contraction with its ruling.  If
the minimal model is `P^2` and `X` is not `P^2`, choose a blowdown sequence;
the surface immediately before `P^2` is `Bl_p(P^2)=F_1`, so the sequence
again factors through a ruling.  In either case there is a morphism

\[
 f:X\longrightarrow\mathbf P^1.
\]

The pullback `F` of a point is a nonzero nef divisor with `F^2=0`.  Its ray is
an ideal point of the projectivized nef cone.  Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 X\text{ rational},\ \rho(X)\ge2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathbf P(\operatorname{Nef}X)\text{ is noncompact}.}
 \tag{2.1}
\]

The primitive-rational-wall theorem remains useful for finite-volume rank:
under its extra primitivity, smooth-rationality, and integral-reflection
hypotheses, adjunction gives `rho<=10`, hence `n<=9`.  Equation (2.1)
supersedes its earlier `n<=8` compact corollary.

## 3. Exact polytope return

Let `C` be any surface-free class of compact Coxeter polytopes for which a
`P`-first compiler proves that every `P in C` is exactly the whole nef cone of
a smooth projective surface and every facet reflection is integral on its
Neron--Severi lattice.  Then the theorem transfers without further choices:

```text
for every P in C, dim(P)<=3.
```

If the compiler is specifically universal into rational surfaces, then `C`
is empty in every positive hyperbolic dimension covered by `rho>=2`.

This sharply changes the design target.  A compact example in dimension at
least four cannot be obtained as the entire integral Neron--Severi nef cone
of a surface.  It must use a proper totally-real eigenspace, invariant/folded
section without a rational integral reflection lattice on the section,
automorphic arrangement, or another non-direct carrier.  Bugaenko's compact
`H8` eigen-nef K3 realization is consistent with the theorem: its golden
eigenspace is irrational inside the rank-18 integral Picard lattice and is
not the full nef hyperbolic space.

## 4. Scope

The theorem does not exclude:

- finite-volume noncompact full-nef surface chambers in high rank;
- compact totally-real eigen-nef sections whose field form is anisotropic;
- geometric Coxeter sections whose reflected section lattice is not an
  integral rational lattice; or
- compact polytopes with no proved surface carrier.

Thus (1.1) is already a universal theorem for the **direct full-nef carrier
class**, but it is not a universal nonexistence theorem for compact arithmetic
Coxeter polytopes.
