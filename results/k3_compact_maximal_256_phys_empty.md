# A raw-maximal compact arithmetic chamber with empty K3 `PHYS`

**Status (2026-08-11): proved, conditional only on the published absolute
finite-maximality classification of the \((2,5,6)\) triangle group.**  All
Gram, arithmetic-order, codifferent-shell, inert-prime and square-class
computations are exact and executable.

This gives a negative answer to the compact full-Picard K3 Property-4 gate:

\[
 P\text{ raw maximal arithmetic}
 \quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname {K3Comp}(P)\ne\varnothing .             \tag{0.1}
\]

The counterexample is the compact triangle with angles

\[
                 (\pi/2,\pi/5,\pi/6).                  \tag{0.2}
\]

Its field is \(k=\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)\), its trace rank is only \(2\cdot3=6\),
and its raw side group is an absolutely maximal hyperbolic reflection group.
Nevertheless its two facets meeting at angle \(\pi/6\) have field norm
classes differing by \(3\), while no two totally negative codifferent
elements of trace \(-2\) differ by \(3\) times a square.  Hence the first
`PHYS` gate is empty before primitive K3 embedding or hidden roots are even
considered.

This is a counterexample to the **primitive full-trace, full-Picard,
same-vector** K3 compiler.  It does not exclude a projected-root return in a
larger Picard lattice or a carrier in another AG category.

## 1. The exact raw arithmetic triangle

Put

\[
 \phi={1+\sqrt5\over2},\qquad
 k=\mathbf Q(\sqrt5),\qquad \mathcal O_k=\mathbf Z[\phi].
\]

On \(V=k r_1\oplus k r_2\oplus k r_3\), take

\[
 G=\bigl(g(r_i,r_j)\bigr)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -6&0&3\\
 0&-2&\phi\\
 3&\phi&-2
 \end{pmatrix}.                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

The leading principal minors are

\[
                    -6,\qquad 12,\qquad 6\phi.        \tag{1.2}
\]

Thus \(G\) has signature \((1,2)\) at the distinguished embedding and is
negative definite at the conjugate embedding, where
\(\phi'=(1-\sqrt5)/2<0\).  Its normalized products are

\[
 {g_{12}^2\over g_{11}g_{22}}=0,qquad
 {g_{23}^2\over g_{22}g_{33}}={\phi^2\over4}
       =\cos^2{\pi\over5},\qquad
 {g_{31}^2\over g_{33}g_{11}}={3\over4}
       =\cos^2{\pi\over6}.                            \tag{1.3}
\]

So its Coxeter type is exactly \((2,5,6)\).  It is compact because

\[
                   {1\over2}+{1\over5}+{1\over6}
                         ={13\over15}<1.               \tag{1.4}
\]

The nonzero Cartan coefficients are

\[
 C_{13}=-1,\quad C_{31}=-3,\quad
 C_{23}=C_{32}=-\phi.                                 \tag{1.5}
\]

Consequently all three side reflections preserve
\(M_0=\bigoplus_i\mathcal O_k r_i\).  Together with the admissible signatures
and compact fundamental triangle, this realizes an arithmetic reflection
group.  Its Cartan cycle products are \(3\) and \(\phi^2\), and

\[
             \mathbf Z[3,\phi^2]=\mathbf Z[\phi]
                         =\mathcal O_k.                 \tag{1.6}
\]

In particular, the cycle order \(\mathcal O_0\) used by the finite K3
compiler is already the maximal order.  There is no overorder lane that
could evade the calculation below.

## 2. Why this is raw reflection-maximal

Singerman proved that a finite-index Fuchsian supergroup of a triangle group
is again a triangle group and classified every triangle-group inclusion
([primary source](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s2-6.1.29)).  Maclachlan's
arithmetic table places \((2,5,6)\) over \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)\) and explicitly
lists it as the maximal triangle group in its commensurability class
([Table 3 and Table 4](https://msp.org/pjm/1996/176-1/pjm-v176-n1-p10-p.pdf)).
Since every finite-covolume supergroup is commensurable, the
orientation-preserving group

\[
                         \Delta(2,5,6)                  \tag{2.1}
\]

is finitely maximal among all Fuchsian groups.

Let \(W\) be the full group generated by the three side reflections.  If
\(W\) were properly contained in a discrete reflection group \(W'\), then

\[
                       W^+=\Delta(2,5,6)\subseteq W'^+ \tag{2.2}
\]

would be a finite-index Fuchsian extension.  Maximality gives \(W^+=W'^+\).
Both full reflection groups have index two over this same orientation
subgroup, so \(W=W'\), a contradiction.  Therefore \(W\) is absolutely
maximal as a hyperbolic reflection group, not merely maximal in the chosen
integral module.

## 3. Complete codifferent energy shell

For \(k=\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)\),

\[
                  \mathcal O_k^\vee={1\over\sqrt5}\mathcal O_k. \tag{3.1}
\]

Write an element as \((a+b\phi)/\sqrt5\).  Its field trace is \(b\).
Therefore trace \(-2\) forces \(b=-2\), and, after putting \(c=a-1\), every
candidate is

\[
             u_c={a-2\phi\over\sqrt5}
                 =-1+{c\over\sqrt5}.                  \tag{3.2}
\]

The two real values are \(-1\pm c/\sqrt5\).  Total negativity is exactly

\[
                          |c|<\sqrt5.                  \tag{3.3}
\]

Hence the entire diagonal energy shell is

\[
 \mathscr E_{-2}(\mathcal O_k)=
       \{u_{-2},u_{-1},u_0,u_1,u_2\}.                 \tag{3.4}
\]

This is an exhaustive shell, not a bounded sample.

### Energy-square-class coherence

There is a useful intrinsic formulation of the first diagonal part of
`PHYS`.  In the square-class group \(k^\times/k^{\times2}\), put

\[
 {\cal C}_a=
 \left\{[u/h_a]:u\in\mathscr E_{-2}(\mathcal O_0)\right\}       \tag{3.5}
\]

for every raw facet-line norm \(h_a\).  Changing a line representative
multiplies \(h_a\) by a square, so \({\cal C}_a\) is intrinsic to the pinned
raw line.  A simultaneous diagonal trace-\((-2)\) normalization exists
exactly when

\[
                       \bigcap_a{\cal C}_a\ne\varnothing.       \tag{3.6}
\]

Indeed, the common class in (3.6) is the form scale \([\lambda]\), and
\(u_a/(\lambda h_a)\) supplies the required root-rescaling square.  This is
the weakest square-class precondition; evenness, a common integral module
and the nonbasis facets remain additional parts of full `PHYS`.

Raw reflection maximality contains no information about (3.6).  The example
below makes its intersection empty using only one adjacent pair.

## 4. The inert-prime `PHYS` obstruction

The facets \(r_1,r_3\) meet at angle \(\pi/6\), and their field norms in
(1.1) satisfy

\[
                         {h_1\over h_3}=3.             \tag{4.1}
\]

Suppose a full-trace K3 return existed.  After replacing \(g\) by a common
similar form \(\lambda g\), literal physical roots on those two raw lines
would be

\[
                     \delta_i=a_i r_i,qquad a_i\in k^\times.
\]

Their field energies \(u_i=g(\delta_i,\delta_i)\) would lie in (3.4), because
the trace lattice is integral and

\[
                         \operatorname {Tr}_{k/\mathbf Q}(u_i)=-2.
\]

But the common form scale cancels in their ratio, giving the necessary
square-class equation

\[
                         {u_1\over u_3}
                         =3\left({a_1\over a_3}\right)^2. \tag{4.2}
\]

This equation has no solution in the shell.  Indeed,
\(x^2-x-1\) has no root modulo \(3\), so \(3\) is inert in
\(\mathcal O_k\).  Let \(\mathfrak p=(3)\).  For every
\(c\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\),

\[
              u_c={c-\sqrt5\over\sqrt5},qquad
              N(c-\sqrt5)=c^2-5\in\{-5,-4,-1\}.       \tag{4.3}
\]

Neither numerator nor denominator is divisible by \(\mathfrak p\); hence

\[
                           v_{\mathfrak p}(u_c)=0.      \tag{4.4}
\]

The left side of (4.2) has even valuation zero, whereas the right side has

\[
                  v_{\mathfrak p}(3z^2)=1+2v_{\mathfrak p}(z), \tag{4.5}
\]

which is odd.  This contradiction proves

\[
 \boxed{
   \text{no simultaneous trace-}(-2)\text{ normalization of the facets,}
   \qquad \mathrm{PHYS}(P)=\varnothing .}              \tag{4.6}
\]

The executable certificate independently checks all \(5^2=25\) quotients
\(u_c/(3u_e)\) by an exact square test in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)\); none is a
square.

### Theorem 4.1 (maximal compact K3 compiler counterexample)

The raw compact arithmetic \((2,5,6)\) triangle is absolutely
reflection-maximal, has \(d(n+1)=2\cdot3=6\le20\), but

\[
                    \operatorname {K3Comp}(P)=\varnothing. \tag{4.7}
\]

The failure occurs at `PHYS`; `EMB` and `ROOT` are not reached.  Therefore
raw arithmetic reflection maximality cannot prove nonemptiness of the finite
full-Picard K3 compiler.

This is not a disguised high-rank embedding failure.  If an even hyperbolic
rank-six `PHYS` host \(N\) existed, then

\[
 \operatorname {rk}(N^\perp)=16,\qquad
 \ell(A_N)\le6,\qquad 16\ge\ell(A_N)+2.                \tag{4.8}
\]

The stable primitive-embedding theorem would embed \(N\) in the K3 lattice
automatically.  Thus, in this example, `PHYS` is the only lattice-existence
obstruction before the chamber test.

## 5. The sharp way maximality *can* be used

The counterexample shows that any positive theorem must at least supply
`PHYS` independently.  Primitive K3 embeddability (`EMB`) is likewise still
an explicit discriminant-form premise or gate.  Once those are available,
maximality can remove precisely one part of `ROOT` under a much weaker
condition than global root saturation.

Let \((M,g)\) be a surviving full-trace even module with physical facet
roots, let \(N=M_{\mathbf Z}\) embed primitively in the K3 lattice, and let
\(P\) be raw reflection-maximal.  Impose:

1. no square-\(-2\) root of \(N\) has zero distinguished projection; and
2. for every square-\(-2\) root \(\delta\in N\) whose nonzero restricted
   mirror meets \(P^\circ\), its **native** field reflection
   \[
    s_\delta^g(x)=x-{2g(x,\delta)\over g(\delta,\delta)}\delta
   \]
   preserves the same module \(M\).

Call (2) **wall-selective native saturation**.  It is needed only for actual
interior cutters, not for all K3 roots.

If a hidden root cut \(P^\circ\), its native reflection would lie in the
discrete arithmetic group \(O(M,g)\).  Adjoining it to the raw side group
would produce a strictly larger discrete reflection group: it cannot already
belong to the side group, because a reflection mirror of a Coxeter group does
not cross the interior of its fundamental chamber.  Raw maximality forbids
this.  Thus there is no interior root cut.  Physical facet witnesses prevent
extension through a facet, so \(P^\circ\) is the complete restricted root
chamber.  The period theorem, Weyl framing and the K3 cone theorem then give
the exact eigen-nef return; no further ample or scheme-theoretic gate remains.

Consequently the sharp conditional implication is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathrm{PHYS}+\mathrm{EMB}+\mathrm{ZERO}+\mathrm{NATIVE}_{P}
 +\text{ raw maximality}
 \Longrightarrow \operatorname {K3Comp}(P)\ne\varnothing .} \tag{5.1}
\]

This does not make Property 4 automatic: the \((2,5,6)\) packet proves that
the first premise cannot be deleted, and checking native saturation may use
the same finite root window as the direct `ROOT` test.  What (5.1) identifies
is the weakest intrinsic same-host condition under which maximality itself
has genuine force.
