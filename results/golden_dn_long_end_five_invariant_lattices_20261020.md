# Whole D_n long-end five: every invariant lattice

20 October 2026. Own written proof candidate; outside-author review pending.
No enumeration, scientific job, TeX or Lean execution. This excludes one
actual arithmetic vertex profile, not every D_n vertex or a new dimension
ceiling. The literal golden cutoff is a published input.

**Theorem.** A compact arithmetic first-type Coxeter n-polytope, n>=8,
cannot have a whole D_n vertex with an actual outside wall whose only
contact with that vertex is a label5 at the end of its long arm.
Every compatible invariant lattice is covered, including odd n.

## 1. The full-rank model and the published input

Put O=Z[phi], phi^2=phi+1, u=phi-1=phi^(-1), and

    L=O e0 + ... + O en,     f=<-u>+I_n.

The old D_n roots may be taken as -e_i+e_(i+1) (1<=i<n) and
-e_(n-1)-e_n. All have norm2. The extra root r=e0+phi e1
also has norm2, has product -phi with the long-end root, and is
orthogonal to every other old simple root. Thus its unit bond is
-phi/2=-cos(pi/5). These n+1 roots span the full Lorentz space.
Their Gram determines this model up to similarity.

The reflection R_i in e0+phi e_i, obtained by old spatial permutations,
has its only nonidentity block on coordinates (0,i):

    [[phi, -phi], [1, -phi]].                         (1)

The arithmetic ground field is exactly Q(sqrt5). It contains this
field from the label5 square. A proper totally real extension would
have another embedding fixing phi, at which this full-rank Lorentz
Gram remains indefinite, contrary to admissibility. After similarity,
the arithmetic reflection group preserves an O-lattice Lambda in this
model. The usual stable-lattice justification is to sum finitely many
translates of a lattice invariant under a finite-index arithmetic
subgroup. Hence Lambda is invariant under all old even spatial sign
changes, spatial permutations, and every R_i.

We import the published literal cutoff: O(<-phi>+I_n,O) is reflective
only for n<=7. A primary account is Kellerhals, *Hyperbolic Orbifolds
of Minimal Volume* (2014), printed p.474, equation(4.3) and the following
paragraph, attributing the result to Bugaenko(1984):
https://homeweb.unifr.ch/kellerha/pub/CMFT.pdf .
The time-coordinate unit change t -> u*t identifies <-u>+I_n
with <-phi>+I_n. The argument below supplies the missing all-lattice
bridge; similarity alone would not supply it.

## 2. A fourfold matrix-unit sandwich

Let A be the O-algebra generated by those lattice-preserving maps.
For distinct spatial i,j,k, let d_ij negate coordinates i,j. Then

    (1-d_ij)(1-d_ik)=4 E_ii.

Spatial permutations give every 4 E_ij. Equation(1), and the fact
that phi is a unit, then give 4 E_i0, 4 E_0i and 4 E_00:

    4 E_ii R_i = 4 E_i0 - 4phi E_ii,
    R_i 4 E_ii = -4phi E_0i - 4phi E_ii,
    R_i 4 E_i0 = -4phi E_00 - 4phi E_i0.

Consequently 4 End_O(L) is contained in A. The golden ring is a PID
(the standard norm-Euclidean quadratic ring). Normalize the fractional
ideal generated by all coordinates of Lambda to O. Applying these
matrix units proves

    4L subset Lambda subset L.                       (2)

This uses fourfold, not twofold, matrix units; odd-dimensional deep
lattices must not be omitted.

## 3. The complete residue flag

The prime2 is inert: O/2O=F4 and phi^2+phi+1=0 there. In V=L/2L put

    ell(t,x)=phi*t+sum x_i,    H=ker ell,
    v=(phi^2,1,...,1).

The only subspaces invariant under the spatial permutations and R_i
are 0, <v>, H, V. Indeed, a vector with unequal spatial coordinates
gives e_i+e_j by a transposition difference; their permutation orbit
spans the spatial sum-zero space. Applying R_1 to e1+e2 supplies one
more independent vector, so the invariant subspace contains H. If
every spatial coordinate of every vector is equal, invariance under
R_1 forces t=phi^2*x_1, leaving only the line <v>.
Here v is in H exactly when n is odd.

Define the invariant residue subspaces

    W1=(Lambda+2L)/2L,
    W2=((Lambda intersect 2L)/2)/2L.

They satisfy W1 subset W2. The coordinate-content normalization makes
W1 nonzero. If W1=V, then Lambda=L by Nakayama (or two direct lifts
using(2)). If W1=H, choose a lift of e_i+e_k with j distinct from i,k.
The difference under d_ij, divided by2, has residue e_i. It lies in
W2. Its permutation and R_i orbit spans V, so W2=V. Therefore Lambda
is the inverse image of H in this case.

If W1=<v>, the same differences give e_i+e_j in W2, and their R_i
orbit spans H. Thus W2 contains H and <v>. For even n it follows
that W2=V. For odd n the only possibilities are H and V.

Write E for the inverse image of H. The complete resulting list is

    L; E; O v+2L;
    O(v+2z)+2E, z in L, only when n is odd.           (3)

The last line may be indexed by ell(z) in F4; no classification of
those indices or equality of their orthogonal groups is needed.
This is exhaustive from(2), not a sampled flag catalogue.

## 4. Recovering the standard lattice intrinsically

The lattice E is precisely {x in L : f(x) belongs to 2O}. Its f-dual is

    E^*=L+O(v/2),    f(v)=n-1-2phi.                  (4)

This follows because f(v,x) modulo2 is ell(x). The valuation at2 of
f(v) is zero for even n and exactly one for odd n: in the latter case
f(v)/2=(n-1)/2-phi is nonzero in F4. Hence in either parity

    {x in E^* : f(x) in O}=L.                       (5)

To check this, write x=y+a v/2. If a is not divisible by2, its final
norm term a^2*f(v)/4 has negative2-valuation; the other norm terms
are integral and cannot cancel it. Otherwise x lies in L.
There is no denominator away from2 in E^*/L.
Thus every isometry of E preserves L. Since O v+2L=2E^*, its
isometries preserve E and then L as well.

For a deep lattice in(3), put lambda=v+2z. Odd n gives
f(lambda)=f(v)+4f(v,z)+4f(z), still of valuation one. Moreover

    {x in (Lambda/2) : f(x) in O}=E,                (6)

by writing x=a lambda/2+e, e in E, and the same negative-valuation
test. Therefore its isometries preserve E, hence L. Equations(5)--(6)
prove, in every case of(3),

    O(f,Lambda) subset O(f,L).                      (7)

No individual spatial sign reflection was assumed integral on an
arbitrary Lambda. No commensurability-class conclusion is asserted.

## 5. Actual-polytope conclusion and scope

Every actual facet reflection preserves Lambda, hence L by(7).
The compact polytope's reflection lattice would therefore be a
subgroup of the full integral reflection group of L. For n>=8 the
published cutoff says that latter group has infinite covolume; it
cannot contain a finite-covolume subgroup. This contradiction proves
the theorem.

The proof covers a WHOLE D_n old vertex and an isolated long-end5
contact. Spectator components, other contact labels and nonarithmetic
polytopes are not included. It is support for the compact arithmetic
bound programme, with no new uniform ceiling, example, manuscript or
Lean module.
