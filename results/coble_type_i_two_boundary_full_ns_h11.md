## Exhausting the negative curves

Write

\[
 B=B_1+B_2\in|-2K_S|,
 \qquad h_i=B_i/2.
\]

Kondō proves that `CM(S)=E10`, that his Type-I diagram is the complete list
of effective irreducible Coble--Mukai roots, and that its chamber has nine
primitive isotropic cusp rays.  In the two-boundary case the twelve roots
are ten actual `(-2)`-curves and two `(-1)`-roots

\[
 \alpha_j=2E_j+h_1+h_2.
\]

The latter give exactly two `(-1)`-curves meeting both boundaries once.

For any other `(-1)`-curve, adjunction gives

\[
 B_1.E+B_2.E=2.
\]

It therefore meets one boundary twice and the other not at all.  Then

\[
 f=E+h_i
\]

is a primitive nef isotropic Coble--Mukai vector.  Conversely, Kondō's
genus-one-fibration correspondence and Halphen blow-up theorem turn a cusp
whose boundaries lie in distinct blown fibres into the two exceptional
curves `E=f-h_1` and `E=f-h_2`.

The remaining issue is integral glue, and here the calculation is forced.
The lattice generated by the two boundaries, the ten `(-2)`-curves, the two
crossing `(-1)`-curves, and `K_S` has determinant four.  A direct
discriminant enumeration finds **exactly one** integral index-two
overlattice.  It is the full rational-surface lattice

\[
 \operatorname{NS}(S)\cong I_{1,11}.
\]

In that unique lattice, exactly six of the nine CM cusps are boundary-split;
each gives two further `(-1)`-curves.  The other three cusps keep the
boundaries in one blown fibre.  Hence the complete list is

```text
2 boundaries + 10 (-2)-curves + 2 crossing (-1)-curves
             + 6*2 split-fibre (-1)-curves = 26.
```

Kondō also proves that the only negative curves on a Coble surface are the
boundaries, `(-2)`-curves, and `(-1)`-curves.  This exhausts the full nef
wall list.  The exact cone calculation independently verifies that all 26
walls are irredundant.

## The arithmetic denominator is not `NS(S)`

There is an important correction to the naive arithmetic claim.  Reflection
in a boundary is not integral on `NS(S)`: since `B_i^2=-4` and a crossing
exceptional curve has `E.B_i=1`,

\[
 s_{B_i}(E)=E+\frac12B_i\notin\operatorname{NS}(S).
\]

The correct invariant module is the extended Picard lattice

\[
 \widetilde{\operatorname{Pic}}(S)
 =\langle\operatorname{NS}(S),B_1/2,B_2/2\rangle,
\]

with its form multiplied by two.  It is the even 2-elementary lattice

\[
 2\widetilde{\operatorname{Pic}}(S)
 \cong U+E_8(-2)+\langle-2\rangle^2,
 \qquad (r,a,\delta)=(12,10,1).
\]

Its determinant is `-1024` and its Smith diagonal is `1^2 2^10`.  Use
`h_i`, the `(-1)`-curves, and the `(-2)`-curves as primitive normals.  Their
scaled norm/divisibility split is

```text
4*(-2, div 1) + 12*(-2, div 2) + 10*(-4, div 2).
```

All 26 reflections are integral involutive isometries of this lattice, and
the normals span it with Smith diagonal `1^12`.  This identifies the known
Alexeev `(12,10,1)` arithmetic lattice; his full reflection chamber has 14
walls.  Our 26-wall group has finite index because both chambers have finite
hyperbolic covolume.  The numerical index remains open: in odd hyperbolic
dimension both Coxeter Euler characteristics vanish, so their ratio is
`0/0`.  See Valery Alexeev,
[*Reflective hyperbolic 2-elementary lattices, K3 surfaces and hyperkähler
manifolds*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09110).
