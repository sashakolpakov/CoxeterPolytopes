# Literature and algorithm gate, checked 14 October 2026

**Keep noncompact arithmetic searches out of H20 and H22+. Do not apply the dimension-21 bound to compact arithmetic polytopes.** The current intrinsic H20 lane must seek a nonarithmetic finite-volume completion; its exact infinite-volume intermediate chambers are not counterexamples to any dimension bound.

| category | verified restriction | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| noncompact arithmetic reflection lattices | hyperbolic dimension `n≤19` or `n=21` | H20 and every H22+ arithmetic target are excluded |
| arithmetic reflection lattices in general | `n≤29` | dimension 21 is not the general arithmetic ceiling |
| compact Coxeter polytopes, arithmetic or not | `n≤29` | compact H9 is not ruled out by these theorems |

Esselmann's original [1996 paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022314X96901419) treats integral Lorentzian lattices of signature `(n,1)` and gives maximal **lattice rank** `n+1=22`, hence hyperbolic dimension 21. The stronger gap at hyperbolic dimension 20 belongs to the same reflective-lattice classification, not merely to that numerical ceiling; Borcherds explicitly records that essentially the sole reflective lattice of rank at least 21 is his rank-22 example in the [corrected thesis, §6.8](https://math.berkeley.edu/~reb/papers/thesis/thesis.pdf). For a noncompact arithmetic reflection lattice, the isotropic quadratic-form model has ground field `Q`, and after scaling yields the integral lattice to which this gate applies. This argument does not apply to arbitrary nonarithmetic Coxeter groups.

Vinberg's general arithmetic and compact exclusions begin at hyperbolic dimension 30: [original 1981 article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01082285), expanded in the 1984/1985 paper. The [existing repository note](arithmetic_dimension_bounds_and_effective_high_dimension_search.md) records the detailed field and category distinctions. I found **no newer general upper bound replacing 29** in the primary research sources located in this recheck. That statement reports the sources checked; it is not a proof that every possible recent announcement has been found.

For the existence frontier, Guo–Ma–Zang–Zheng's [16 July 2026 primary preprint, introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14715v1) still records known compact examples only through dimension 8 and noncompact finite-volume examples only through dimension 21. Its new infinite families are in dimensions 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9; these do not raise either record. Thus the careful present wording is **“compact 8 and noncompact 21 are the standard known frontiers in the current sources checked.”** This does not imply existence in every dimension below 21. The separately documented missing H20 case remains meaningful; see the [earlier source check](fresh_h9_makarov_face_extension_20261013.md).

Recent restrictions on facet budget are not new universal dimension bounds. [Burcroff's primary classification](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.03437v2) combines affine Gale diagrams, missing-face restrictions and exact Gram equations, and gives stronger bounds when the number of facets is constrained. The [3 August 2026 McLeod preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02894v1) reports the remaining six-dimensional, ten-facet classification and thereby a completion of the `d+4`-facet family. Its stated assumptions and computational certification are explicit in §8. This is a recent restricted classification, not a compact H9 construction or a lowering of the universal bound 29; no reliance on its new census is needed for the present construction.

**Allcock's published edgewalking is also arithmetic in its input.** In [*An alternative to Vinberg's algorithm*, introduction and §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.03784v1), the lattice is a free abelian group with a rational-valued bilinear form; the paper expressly restricts its method to lattices over `Z`. It starts at a chamber corner, solves the associated binary Lorentzian lattice problems to find the actual next corners, and traverses the starting boundary component. Theorem 9.5 equates finite volume with exhaustion of its complete unexplored-ray queue for that specified lattice chamber. This is not a theorem that arbitrary abstract spherical diagram extensions, a truncated unit search, or a selected set of cap profiles exhaust a nonarithmetic chamber.

A genuine alternative is **combinatorial incidence enumeration followed by metric realization**: affine Gale diagrams or missing-face data, finite and affine Coxeter-subdiagram constraints, exact rank/signature equations including dotted distances, and a complete compactness or volume check. Burcroff and the July 2026 paper provide primary examples. This framework permits nonarithmetic diagrams, but an abstract nerve or a closed-looking local edge graph alone supplies no hyperbolic realization. Our intrinsic cap construction likewise needs exact global covering and all cusp links; pair compatibility and local endpoint closure are intermediate certificates only.

The precise strategy remains: exclude impossible arithmetic categories first; use lattice algorithms only on admissible, explicitly specified hosts; use genuine diagram-and-metric or intrinsic geometric searches for nonarithmetic H20/H22; and retain an explicit global volume certificate as the success condition.
