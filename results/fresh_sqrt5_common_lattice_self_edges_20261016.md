# Same-type local edges in one fixed admissible lattice

Independent local edge existence, even with a common ambient quadratic
space and lattice prescribed, cannot exclude the rank21 finite vertex
forests under discussion. The fixed-parameter
[squareclass diagnostic](fresh_sqrt5_vertex_common_space_20261016.md)
is correct for its particular duplicated contacts and `lambda=phi^8`,
but variable binary translations evade it. The argument below also
repairs the orientation problem in simply translating the old framing
root: use the opposition element of the common finite reflection group
before translating.

This is a proof note, not a numerical search or an independently reviewed
new dimension bound. It does not assert that the proposed endpoint is
the first mirror of the full lattice reflection group, or that the
different endpoint choices close the two-faces of a compact chamber.

## Precise hypotheses

Let `k` be totally real of degree `e>=2`. Let `(V,Q)` have signature
`(n,1)` at one distinguished real embedding and be positive definite at
every other embedding. Fix an O_k lattice L in V. Suppose
`r_1,...,r_n` in L are independent spherical simple roots, and their
reflections preserve L. They need not have equal norms. Let H in V(k)
span their common orthogonal line, with H future timelike at the
distinguished embedding. Put `H^2=-d`.

These hypotheses hold for the literal finite diagrams in the
[universal self-edge package](fresh_sqrt5_universal_self_edges_20261016.md)
inside the common form `A orthogonal-sum <-phi>` over `Q(phi)`:
the saved integral symmetrizations make the finite reflections integral,
and A is positive definite at both embeddings. The same lattice,
generated by the finite-root lattice and H, works for all root ports.

Fix i, and write

```
J = {r_j : j != i},
W = span_k(J),
B = W^perp,
r_i = v+w,       v in W, w in B,
s = w^2 > 0.
```

The binary space B has form `<s,-d>` in the basis `(w,H)`. It is
Lorentzian at the physical place and positive definite at every other
place. In particular `D=d/s` is positive at the physical place and
negative at every other place. It is not a square in k.

## Integral binary translations really exist

Identify B with `E=k(sqrt(D))`, with form `s*Norm(E/k)`. The field E has
two real embeddings and `e-1` pairs of complex embeddings. Its unit rank
is e, whereas the unit rank of k is `e-1`. The norm image contains the
squares of the units of k, so it has rank exactly `e-1`. Consequently the
relative norm-one unit group has rank exactly one. This uses only
Dirichlet's unit theorem; see Milne, *Algebraic Number Theory*,
[Theorem 5.1 and the proof in Section 5](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf),
or Sutherland's [Lecture 15](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-785-number-theory-i-fall-2021/mit18_785f21_lec15.pdf).

Choose an infinite-order relative unit. An even power is positive at
both physical real embeddings, so multiplication is a nontrivial
future-preserving hyperbolic translation of B. Extend it by the identity
on W. Passing to a further power makes it preserve the **original**
lattice L, not merely a new commensurable lattice. Here is the explicit
finite-index justification.

Choose any O_k lattice in W and the O_E lattice in B under the preceding
identification. Their direct sum L' is preserved by the translation.
Since L and L' are full lattices in the same k-space, a nonzero integer
`a` can be chosen with

```
a L'  subset  L  subset  a^(-1) L'.
```

The translation acts invertibly on the finite group
`a^(-1)L'/aL'`. A power acts identically there and therefore preserves
L and its inverse also preserves L. This retains infinite order. Denote
such a power by T. No finite-index subgroup of the rank-one relative
unit group can become finite.

## The opposition correction gives the correct outward wall

Let `w_J` be the longest element of the finite reflection group generated
by J. It sends the simple root rays of J to their negatives, permuted by
the opposition automorphism. It fixes B pointwise. Set

```
b_i = -w_J(r_i),             r_i(m) = T^m b_i.
```

The reflection in `b_i` is `w_J s_(r_i) w_J^(-1)`; the reflection in
`r_i(m)` is its conjugate by `T^m`. Both preserve L. Thus no additional
integrality assertion about a rational sheet-flipping reflection is
needed. The harmless minus sign chooses the outward normal and does not
change its reflection.

For each `r_j` in J, there is a permutation pi of J and a scalar
`c_j>0` at the physical embedding with
`w_J^(-1)r_j=-c_j*r_pi(j)`, hence

```
(r_i(m), r_j) = c_j*(r_i, r_pi(j)).
```

Because `c_j^2*r_pi(j)^2=r_j^2`, the normalized Gram of
`J + r_i(m)` is the original normalized finite Gram with its common-root
labels permuted. Thus the endpoint Coxeter type agrees, and the deleted
port's norm is unchanged. Coherent standard finite-root normalizations,
as in the literal package, permit `c_j=1`; the general statement does
not require that normalization. The common walls are geometrically the
same walls. Their contact list is allowed this opposition permutation;
the construction does not claim to duplicate every old contact at the
same labelled node.

The essential sign is that the B-component of `b_i` is `-w`, whereas
the B-component of `r_i` is `w`. Directly applying a future translation
to `r_i` would keep the wrong spacelike sheet.

## There is an actual finite edge

Use normalized physical vectors `e_0=H/sqrt(d)` and `e_1=w/sqrt(s)`.
Replace T by its inverse if necessary so that, for positive m and
`ell=m*ell_0>0`,

```
T^m e_0 = cosh(ell)e_0 - sinh(ell)e_1,
T^m e_1 = cosh(ell)e_1 - sinh(ell)e_0.
```

Put `v'=-w_J(v)`. Then

```
r_i(m) = v' - sqrt(s)cosh(ell)e_1
              + sqrt(s)sinh(ell)e_0,
(H,r_i(m)) = -sqrt(d*s)sinh(ell) < 0,
(T^m H,r_i) = -sqrt(d*s)sinh(ell) < 0.
```

Both H and `T^m H` are future timelike. Every root in J is orthogonal
to both. The new root is orthogonal to `T^m H`; the old root is
orthogonal to H. The positive segment between the two endpoints
therefore lies inside all these halfspaces, with exactly J active in
its interior. Both endpoint normal Grams are positive definite and have
the same finite type. A small inward perturbation at either endpoint
provides a strict common interior, and the endpoint sectors also prove
that all the indicated walls are actual facets.

Finally,

```
(r_i,r_i(m)) = (v,v') - s*cosh(ell)  ->  -infinity.
```

For sufficiently large m the framing pair is dotted. Every other pair
has the original finite Coxeter angle. Thus this is an actual local
Coxeter edge chamber in the prescribed quadratic space and lattice.
Conjugate definiteness comes from the ambient form itself. The argument
can be applied separately at every port without changing L.

## What remains a real obstruction

This defeats independent per-port feasibility tests based only on the
common quadratic space, integral reflection, finite endpoint type or
unbounded dotted distance. The required powers can be large and depend
on the port. No length or narrowness bound is obtained.

An endpoint produced this way need not be the first wall encountered in
the chamber of the **full** lattice reflection group. Intermediate
mirrors can exist. Nor does selecting these endpoints separately supply
the mutual contacts, full-rank two-face stars, compact two-face closures
or a finite-volume chamber. Those simultaneous conditions are the
remaining research target. The frozen compact dimension21 result and
the newly enumerated triangle obligations are unaffected.

The fixed-uniform diagnostic is retained only as an exact example of
why independently chosen *specific* edge witnesses cannot automatically
be placed in one space. It gives no exclusion of any of the306 maximizing
vertex forests after variable endpoint choices are allowed.
