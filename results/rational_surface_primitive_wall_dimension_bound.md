# Primitive rational-wall bound for full-nef rational-surface carriers

**Status:** proved project criterion (2026-08-08).

## Result

Let `X` be a smooth projective rational surface over an algebraically closed
field, let `rho=rank NS(X)>=3`, and suppose its complete nef cone is rational
polyhedral.  Assume every extremal ray of the Mori cone dual to a nef facet is
generated by a smooth rational curve `C_i` whose numerical class is primitive,
and that reflection in `C_i^perp` preserves `NS(X)`.

Then

```text
C_i^2 is -1 or -2 for every i,
-K_X is nef,
rho <= 10.
```

Consequently, if the complete nef cone is an integral Coxeter chamber under
these primitive rational-wall hypotheses, its hyperbolic dimension satisfies

```text
dim H = rho-1 <= 9.
```

In fact the complete nef projectivization of a rational surface of Picard
rank at least two is never compact:

```text
compact full-nef rational-surface carriers: none.
```

Thus a strong full-nef rational-surface carrier in `H10` or above must violate
at least one of the following: primitive prime wall classes, rationality of
the wall curves, full-nef rather than invariant/eigencone extraction, or
integrality of the facet reflections.

This is a polytope-to-surface obstruction: once an arithmetic Coxeter
polytope is proposed as a full-nef rational carrier with primitive rational
curve normals, its dimension alone rules out the association above `H9`.

## Proof

Each facet normal is negative.  Indeed, by Hodge index a positive vector has
negative-definite perpendicular and hence no nonzero nef class on its
hyperplane.  If the normal were isotropic, its perpendicular would meet the
closed future cone only in that one isotropic ray, too small to support a nef
facet of dimension `rho-1>=2`.  Thus `C_i^2<0`.

The Neron--Severi intersection lattice of a smooth rational surface is
unimodular.  In a unimodular lattice every primitive vector has divisibility
one: if all pairings of a primitive `v` were divisible by `d>1`, then
`v/d` would lie in the dual lattice, which equals the lattice itself.

For a primitive negative vector `C_i`, integrality of

```text
s_i(x)=x-2(x,C_i)/C_i^2 C_i
```

therefore forces `C_i^2` to divide `2`.  Since it is negative,

```text
C_i^2 in {-1,-2}.
```

Adjunction for the smooth rational curve `C_i` gives

```text
(K_X+C_i).C_i=-2,
-K_X.C_i=2+C_i^2 in {1,0}.
```

The stated curves generate all extremal rays of the complete polyhedral Mori
cone.  Hence `-K_X` is nonnegative on the entire Mori cone and is nef.  Every
nef divisor on a surface has nonnegative square, so `K_X^2>=0`.

For a smooth rational surface,

```text
K_X^2 + rho = 10;
```

this is immediate on `P^2` and the Hirzebruch surfaces and is preserved by a
blow-up, which decreases `K^2` by one and increases `rho` by one.  Therefore
`rho<=10`, proving the finite-volume bound.

If equality `rho=10` holds, then `K_X^2=0`.  The class `K_X` is nonzero:
in the odd unimodular rational-surface lattice it is characteristic, whereas
zero is not characteristic (a vector of odd square supplies an immediate
test).  Since `-K_X` is nef, it is therefore a nonzero nef isotropic class.
Its projective ray is an ideal boundary point of the hyperbolic nef chamber.
Consequently the chamber cannot be compact when `rho=10`.  The following
argument removes compactness in every remaining positive rank as well.

Run the smooth rational-surface minimal model program.  A Hirzebruch minimal
model has its ruling.  If the minimal model is `P2` and `X` is not `P2`, a
blowdown sequence factors at its last step through `Bl_p(P2)=F1`, again with
a ruling.  Pulling a fibre back to `X` gives a nonzero nef class `F` with
`F^2=0`.  Its projective ray is an ideal boundary point of the complete nef
cone.  Thus no rational surface with `rho>=2` has compact projectivized full
nef cone.  This stronger conclusion does not require the primitive-wall or
integral-reflection hypotheses.

## Toric corollary

Every extremal Mori ray of a smooth projective toric surface is generated by
a torus-invariant rational curve.  In a smooth fan an invariant prime curve
meets an adjacent invariant prime curve once, so its numerical class is
primitive.  Therefore any full-nef **integral** Coxeter chamber coming from a
smooth projective toric surface satisfies

```text
dim H <= 9.
```

No positive-dimensional toric full-nef chamber is compact, since a smooth
projective toric rational surface has the nef isotropic ruling class above.

The remaining toric problem is finite in spirit: the proof forces `-K_X` nef,
so one can enumerate smooth toric weak-del-Pezzo fans and apply the exact
Coxeter-angle gate.  This can yield a classification and positive
realizations in dimensions at most nine, but it cannot produce the desired
new higher-dimensional examples.

## Canonical-vector test derived from a Coxeter polytope

The proof gives a stronger finite arithmetic prefilter.  Let an oriented
integral Coxeter realization have lattice `N` of rank `rho=n+1` and primitive
candidate wall classes `v_i`, each intended to be a smooth rational curve on
a marked rational surface.  A strong rational carrier necessarily supplies a
canonical vector `K in N` satisfying

```text
K.x = x^2 mod 2       for every x in N       (K characteristic),
K^2 = 10-rho = 9-n,
K.v_i = -2-v_i^2      for every wall curve v_i.
```

It also requires the rational-surface lattice type: for `rho>=3`, the marked
intersection lattice is the odd unimodular lattice `I_(1,rho-1)`.  When the
wall classes span `N_Q`, the adjunction equations determine `K` uniquely over
`Q`; integrality, characteristic parity, and the required square are then an
exact immediate accept/reject calculation on the polytope data.

Passing this canonical-vector test is not sufficient by itself.  The
remaining algebraic moduli problem must produce a rational surface on which
all `v_i` are effective irreducible rational curves and generate the complete
Mori cone in the chosen orientation.  But failure is a rigorous obstruction
to the proposed rational-surface association, and success identifies the
precise bounded Hilbert/blow-up problem that must be solved.

## Why Coble and eigensurface constructions survive

The theorem deliberately assumes that each effective wall curve itself has a
primitive numerical class and is smooth rational.  Higher-rank rational
surfaces can escape through a nonprimitive effective divisor whose hyperplane
has a primitive reflective normal.  On a Coble surface, for example, the
bi-anticanonical divisor in `|-2K_X|` can be a multiple of the primitive
canonical direction.  Singular rational prime curves (failure of smoothness)
and invariant/eigencone sections with reflective orbit-sum normals also fall
outside the theorem's hypotheses.

Therefore the bound does not kill the intended `H10` Coble lane.  It explains
why that lane, nonprimitive wall divisors, or genuine eigenspace sections are
the correct rational-surface designs above dimension nine, while ordinary
primitive toric walls are not.

## Role in the association program

The resulting rational-surface compiler begins from the polytope, not the
surface:

1. decide the unimodular lattice and canonical-vector equations;
2. reject the primitive rational-wall class immediately in dimension above
   nine, and reject every compact direct full-nef rational carrier;
3. enumerate the permitted escape mechanisms—nonprimitive effective walls,
   walls which are not smooth rational, or invariant/eigencone orbit walls;
4. construct the corresponding bounded surface moduli problem; and
5. prove effectivity and complete Mori/nef equality before extracting the
   original labelled chamber.

This supplies a genuine necessary condition from classical surface algebra
and narrows the search for the missing middle association class.

The broader arithmetic proof that every direct full-nef integral-reflection
surface carrier is noncompact in dimension at least four is frozen in
