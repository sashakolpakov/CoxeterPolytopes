# Hostile audit: two K3-carried redoubling rays in `H10`

## Bottom line

There are two exact infinite path-doubling rays based on the 13-wall full
reflection chambers of

\[
 F=U+E_8(-2)+\langle-2\rangle,
 \qquad
 S=U+E_8(-2)+\langle-4\rangle.
\]

For every integer `N >= 1`, each selected pair produces a genuine
finite-volume noncompact Coxeter polyhedron in `H10` with

```text
seed copies       N
reflection index  N
volume            N * volume(seed)
facets             N + 12.
```

The `F` ray is the positive discovery: its `14`, `15`, and `16`-facet
descendants have no exact relation-diagram match in the two pinned CoxIter
lanes.  This is a bounded project-level result, not a worldwide priority
theorem.

The `S` ray is an important control, not a new abstract family.  Its seed is
the classical `f_2^10` chamber for
`-2*x0^2+x1^2+...+x10^2`, and the two `14`- and two `16`-facet descendants
are exactly the corresponding records in CoxIter's pinned index-two-doubling
archive.  Its new content here is the common nine-dimensional K3
period-carrier interpretation.

## Why Allcock's theorem applies

Allcock calls a wall doubling when every wall it meets has an even Coxeter
label.  Two doubling walls make a polyhedron redoublable when they do not
meet inside hyperbolic space; **parallel walls qualify**, even though their
closures share an ideal point.  Lemma 1.3, Theorem 4.1, and Corollary 4.2 of
[*Infinitely many hyperbolic Coxeter groups through dimension
19*](https://doi.org/10.2140/gt.2006.10.737) then apply to every finite
subtree of the Cayley tree generated by their reflections.

In both cases below the selected walls are distinct and parallel.  Their
reflections generate the infinite dihedral group, whose Cayley tree is a
line.  Every finite `N`-vertex subtree is therefore a path.  Its chamber
interiors are disjoint, so index and volume are exactly `N`, not merely
bounded by `N`.

For a selected wall, ten other seed walls are orthogonal to it.  Gluing
across one internal path edge removes the two seam panels and merges ten
orthogonal panel pairs, for gluing cost `12`.  A path has `N-1` internal
edges, hence

\[
 13N-12(N-1)=N+12.
\]

The certificate also proves this count without relying only on panel
bookkeeping.  Nine seed roots are fixed by both selected reflections, while
the remaining boundary roots are given by two explicit parabolic root
orbits and two endpoint roots.  Their polynomial recurrences are checked
symbolically, their closed boundary lists are compared with literal chamber
unions, and every resulting root is primitive with an integral lattice
reflection.

## The `F` ray

The only seed doubling walls are one-based walls `10` and `13`.  Both have
type `(-2,div2)` and

```text
(r10,r13)       = 2
(r10,r13)^2     = 4
r10^2*r13^2     = 4
normalized ratio = 1.
```

Thus `delta=r10+r13` is nonzero isotropic and orthogonal to both roots.  If
`A` means that wall `10` is the first internal seam and `B` means wall `13`,
the complete CoxIter-label histograms are as follows.  Label `0` means
parallel and label `1` means ultraparallel; they are never conflated.

| path | `0` | `1` | `2` | `3` | `4` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| odd `N` | `(N+3)/2` | `(N-1)(N+2)/2` | `(19N+111)/2` | `N+8` | `2` |
| even `N`, `A` | `(N+4)/2` | `(N-1)(N+2)/2` | `(19N+112)/2` | `N+8` | `1` |
| even `N`, `B` | `(N+2)/2` | `(N-1)(N+2)/2` | `(19N+110)/2` | `N+8` | `3` |

The primitive mirror types are

| path | `(-4,div2)` | `(-2,div1)` | `(-2,div2)` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| odd `N` | `(N+17)/2` | `(N+3)/2` | `2` |

Every boundary wall set spans `F` with index one.  For odd `N`, the two
phase choices are congruent by a power of the parabolic translation.  For
even `N`, their different `m=4` counts prove that they are nonisometric.
Different values of `N` already have different facet counts and volumes.

The full 13-wall seed is finite-volume and nonuniform.  Allcock's theorem
therefore proves finite volume for every path union independently of a new
Vinberg enumeration, and finite-index subgroups of the nonuniform seed group
remain nonuniform.  These descendants are finite-volume **and noncompact**.

## The classical `S` control ray

The chosen one-based walls `10` and `11` have types `(-4,div4)` and
`(-4,div2)`.  Their inner product is `4`, so again the raw square is `16` and
the normalized ratio is exactly `1`.  Each has gluing cost `12`.

There is one correction to a tempting summary: these are not the only
doubling walls.  The exact zero-based inventory is

```text
0, 9, 10.
```

Wall `0` meets both selected walls, so it does not invalidate the selected
parallel pair or the path theorem.  It merely means that the seed has extra
doubling structure.

The relation-label formulas are

| path | `0` | `1` | `2` | `3` | `4` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| odd `N` | `(N+3)/2` | `(N-1)(N+2)/2` | `(19N+111)/2` | `(N+17)/2` | `(N+3)/2` |
| even `N`, `A` | `(N+2)/2` | `(N-1)(N+2)/2` | `(19N+110)/2` | `(N+18)/2` | `(N+4)/2` |
| even `N`, `B` | `(N+4)/2` | `(N-1)(N+2)/2` | `(19N+112)/2` | `(N+16)/2` | `(N+2)/2` |

The primitive mirror types are

| path | `(-4,div2)` | `(-4,div4)` | `(-2,div1)` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|

These walls span an index-two sublattice of `S`, not all of `S`.  They are
nevertheless primitive integral Picard classes, so the common `S`-polarized
period locus is a full-Picard carrier in the sense that a very general
surface has `NS=S`; “full-Picard” does not mean that the displayed walls
generate `S`.

## The rays are genuinely different

The two seeds have the same coarse global label histogram

```text
0:2, 2:65, 3:9, 4:2,
```

but their complete labelled relation graphs are not isomorphic.  A still
cheaper obstruction is the multiset of node-wise label profiles: the `F`
seed has three nodes of profile `{2:10,3:1,4:1}`, while the `S` seed has one
such node and instead has a node of profile `{2:10,4:2}`.

The closed formulas distinguish every pair of descendants across the two
rays except two coarse-histogram coincidences: the seeds (`N=1`) and
`F`-phase-`B` versus `S`-phase-`A` at `N=2`.  Exact node-profile comparison
separates both exceptions.  Thus the second carrier ray is not a disguised
copy of the first.

The sole misleading survivor is important.  `F,N=2,B` and `S,N=2,A` have


complete edge-labelled graph test agrees.  A histogram-only lineage claim
would therefore have been false even though the final bounded nonmatch is
true.

## Algebraic-carrier scope

Every boundary reflection is conjugate, by the two selected integral lattice
reflections, to a seed reflection.  Hence square, divisibility, primitivity,
and integrality are preserved for all `N`.

- The `F` ray is weakly carried by the same explicit nine-parameter K3
  equation family with very-general `NS=F`.
- The `S` ray is weakly carried by the same nine-dimensional K3 period locus
  with very-general `NS=S`; the current theorem is period-level, not
  equation-level.

Neither statement identifies a full nef cone, produces an effective or
irreducible representative of every wall, or realizes the reflections as
surface automorphisms.  The bounded CoxIter absence does not settle worldwide
priority for the `F` descendants.  The `S` descendants are explicitly old as
abstract Coxeter polyhedra.
