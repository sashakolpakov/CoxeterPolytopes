# Polytope-first golden K3 leverage: the strongest noncircular theorem

**Status (2026-08-08).**  This report separates an unconditional
`polytope -> K3 surface -> the same polytope` theorem from the universality
statement that would be needed to turn it into a new no-go theorem for all
compact arithmetic Coxeter polytopes.  The input class below is defined only
by an oriented Coxeter realization and exact arithmetic of its Gram lattice.
It is not defined by saying that a K3 carrier exists.

Throughout this report, an unqualified **carrier** means an `O`-stable,
integral, full-trace golden K3 carrier extending the fixed normalized root
span.  It does not mean an arbitrary K3 divisor sublattice, a fractional field
form, or a carrier obtained after an unenumerated change of root scaling.

The principal conclusions are these.

1. For a fixed compact, even, norm-two golden realization there is a finite,
   exhaustive compiler of every `O`-stable even trace-lattice overclosure,
   every K3 embedding package, and every hidden K3 wall.  When a package
   survives, period theory and the K3 Weyl theorem return the **same** complete
   Coxeter chamber as an eigen-nef section.
2. K3 existence is automatic in hyperbolic dimensions at most four.  More
   generally it is automatic in dimension `n <= 8` under the surface-free
   discriminant-length inequality

   \[
   \ell(A_N)\le 18-2n.
   \]

   Bugaenko's compact `H8` chamber is the top audited survivor: its trace
   discriminant length is two, so equality holds.
3. An `O`-stable integral full-trace quadratic K3 carrier is impossible for
   `n >= 10`.  In
   dimension nine its existence is an exact finite positive-binary-complement
   problem, and every survivor is a singular K3 surface.  This is a genuine
   carrier nonexistence/finiteness theorem, but it is **not** a nonexistence
   theorem for the underlying Coxeter polytopes.
4. Bugaenko is not an isolated positive point in the theorem's class.  Compact
   golden Lanner seeds in dimensions two, three, and four give compact complete
   norm-two chambers and hence strong K3 eigen-nef carriers.  What remains
   isolated is the **high-dimensional** positive result in the audited
   golden/silver full-trace ladder: Bugaenko survives, while the silver `H9`
   complete **intrinsic field-norm-two** chamber has infinite volume.  The
   full restricted K3 `(-2)` arrangement is not closed by that result.
5. Meyer's theorem does not lower the compact dimension bound for eigen-nef
   sections.  Every one of these K3 Picard lattices is rationally isotropic,
   while Bugaenko's eigen-nef section is nevertheless compact.  The isotropic
   class need not lie in the distinguished irrational eigenspace.

Thus this is a genuine non-singleton inverse theorem and a useful search
boundary, but not yet the requested universal compact-polytope theorem.

## 1. The surface-free input class

Put

\[
F=\mathbf Q(\sqrt5),\qquad O=\mathbf Z[\phi],\qquad
\phi^2=\phi+1,
\]

and fix the identity real place.  A **compact golden norm-two package** is
finite arithmetic data

\[
\mathcal P=(P;M,h;r_1,\ldots,r_s)
\]

with the following properties.

1. `M` is a free rank-`n+1` `O`-module with an even integral symmetric form
   `h`; even means `h(x,x) in 2O`.
2. The identity form has signature `(n,1)` and the conjugate form is positive
   definite.
3. The primitive vectors `r_i` have `h(r_i,r_i)=2`; their oriented
   halfspaces cut out a compact Coxeter polytope `P` in `H^n`; and all field
   reflections

   \[
   s_r(x)=x-h(x,r)r
   \]

   preserve `M`.
4. The wall vectors span `M_F`.  The package records the actual integral
   realization, not only an unscaled abstract diagram.

Call the package **root-complete** when `P` is a chamber of the arrangement
of every vector

\[
R_2(M)=\{r\in M:h(r,r)=2\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

All of these predicates concern the Coxeter halfspaces, the field Gram form,
and an integral lattice.  Root-completeness is a reflection-arithmetic
condition, not a reference to a surface.  For compact `P` it is finitely
decidable by Theorem 2.1 below.

The equal-norm hypothesis is deliberately restrictive.  An abstract diagram
with unequal crystallographic root lengths can have other positive scalings,
and failure of this compiler says nothing about those realizations.

## 2. A terminating compiler for compact input

### Theorem 2.1 -- finite polytope-first compiler

For a fixed compact golden norm-two package, the following computation is
finite and exhaustive.

1. Let `R` be the `O`-lattice generated by the walls and let `N_R` be its
   trace lattice.  Enumerate all `O`-stable, reflection-stable, even integral
   trace overclosures

   \[
   N_R\subseteq N'\subseteq N_R^\vee.
   \tag{2.1}
   \]

   Recover the field form on each `N'` from the trace pairings, and retain the
   required integral field packages `M'`.
2. For each `M'`, record its even integral trace lattice

   \[
   N(M')=\left(M',q\right),\qquad
   q(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\mathbf Q}
   \left(-\frac{\phi}{\sqrt5}h(x,y)\right).
   \tag{2.2}
   \]

3. Compute the discriminant form of `N(M')`.  Use Nikulin's local
   discriminant-form criterion to decide existence and the possible
   complement genera.  To enumerate actual
   `O(Lambda_K3)`-embedding orbits, enumerate the isometry classes of even
   complements `T` with the required signature and `q_T=-q_N`, and then the
   relevant finite anti-isometry double cosets

   \[
   \overline{O(T)}\backslash
   \operatorname{Iso}^{-}(q_N,q_T)/\overline G.
   \tag{2.2a}
   \]

   Here `G=O(N)` for unmarked lattice packages, while for a marked oriented
   Coxeter package `G` is the subgroup preserving that package.  Orientation
   is imposed as a final finite refinement.  Local forms alone decide neither
   the complement class nor the embedding orbit.
4. Enumerate every square-minus-two vector of `N(M')` whose hyperplane meets
   the closed distinguished slice over `P`, and decide whether any such
   hyperplane cuts the interior without being one of the prescribed walls.

Consequently, for a fixed normalized Gram realization, this compiler decides
whether `P` itself has an `O`-stable integral full-trace golden K3 eigen-nef
carrier in this species.  It does not merely fold `P` to an unrelated surface
lattice.

#### Why the enumeration is finite

The quotient `N_R^vee/N_R` is finite, so (2.1) is an enumeration of
`O`-stable subgroups of a finite discriminant module, followed by exact
parity, field-integrality, primitivity, and reflection-stability tests.  This
also includes trace-even saturations whose recovered field form is not even;
silently discarding those would not be exhaustive.  This is the fixed-Gram
integral-normalization step that is missing if one tests only a favorite
ambient lattice.

At the K3-wall step, the golden shell theorem gives the finite list

\[
h(\delta,\delta)\in
\{1+\phi,2,3-\phi,4-2\phi,5-3\phi\}
\tag{2.3}
\]

for a visible trace root `delta^2=-2`.  The actual value module, parity, and
divisibility tests delete impossible shells.  If the selected field lattice
is even, only `2` and `4-2phi` survive, and multiplication by the unit
`phi-1` identifies their restricted hyperplanes.  In that even case it
suffices to enumerate (1.1); the general trace-even overclosure requires all
five finite shells.

There is also a direct compactness bound.  For a trace root `delta`, put
`t=h(delta,delta)`.  By (2.3), `t` ranges over only five values, and both real
embeddings of every surviving value are fixed positive constants.  If
`delta^perp` meets the compact set `P`, then at the identity place `delta`
lies in the positive-definite orthogonal complement of some point of `P` and
has norm `sigma_0(t)`.  Compactness and the finite list of constants give one
uniform coordinate bound.  At the conjugate place `h^sigma` is fixed
positive-definite and `h^sigma(delta,delta)=sigma(t)`, giving a second uniform
bound over the same finite list.  The Minkowski image of `M'` is discrete, so
only finitely many `delta` occur.  The bounds are effective from the algebraic
vertices, the shell list, and the two exact Gram matrices.  Thus hidden-wall
equality for a compact chamber is a terminating calculation, not an
open-ended Vinberg prefix.

For an abstract diagram one must additionally enumerate every admissible
normal scaling.  That enumeration is not claimed here.  The theorem is exact
for the normalized arithmetic realization included in the input.

## 3. The exact return to a K3 nef section

### Theorem 3.1 -- polytope to K3 to the same polytope

Let \(\widetilde M=M\), or let \(\widetilde M\) be one of the trace-even
compiler outputs.  Require the finite test in Theorem 2.1 to prove that the
restriction of the complete `(-2)`-root arrangement of
\(N(\widetilde M)\) has `P` itself as a chamber.  (For the original
field-even package this is precisely root-completeness.)  Suppose there is a
primitive, orientation-compatible embedding

\[
N(\widetilde M)\hookrightarrow\Lambda_{K3}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then there is a projective marked K3 surface `X` with

\[
\operatorname{NS}(X)\simeq N(\widetilde M)
\]

and a self-adjoint integral endomorphism `tau` satisfying
`tau^2=tau+1`, such that, after a K3 Weyl change of marking,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf P\bigl(\operatorname{Nef}(X)\cap E_\phi^+\bigr)
\simeq \overline P .
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

For the field-even norm-two package, every irredundant facet is supported by
the paired K3 roots `r` and `(phi-1)r`; after Weyl framing they are classes of
two disjoint smooth rational curves.  The complete paired intersection matrix
retains the difference between Coxeter labels three and five.

#### Proof

The primitive embedding and the K3 period theorem produce a projective K3
surface whose exact Picard lattice is `N(\widetilde M)`.  By the explicit
hypothesis of the theorem, the finite complete-arrangement test has already
proved that its restricted `(-2)`-mirror arrangement has `P` as the selected
component.  If `D` is the ambient root chamber containing an interior point
of `P`, equality of sign patterns gives \(D\cap E_\phi=P\).  The K3 Weyl group
is transitive on positive-root chambers, so a Weyl-framed marking moves `D`
to the ample chamber and proves (3.2).

Only for the original field-even package does the shell collapse further
identify the complete restricted K3 arrangement with the norm-two field
arrangement.  In that case, at a generic point of a restricted facet the
shell theorem leaves exactly the four signed roots
`+/-r,+/-(phi-1)r`.  The two unsigned roots are orthogonal, so the local
ambient root system is `A1+A1`; the chamber chooses one sign of each as a
simple wall.  After Weyl framing, the standard K3 root/curve theorem makes
both simple roots irreducible smooth rational curves, rather than merely
effective reducible divisors.  This is the theorem in
`k3_eigen_nef_factory_theorem.md`, now preceded by the finite P-first
normalization and compact hidden-wall compilers.  No surface automorphism is
asserted: `tau` is a divisor-lattice endomorphism, represented rationally by
an algebraic correspondence.

## 4. Automatic and exact K3 gates

The trace lattice has

\[
\operatorname{rk}N=2(n+1),\qquad
\operatorname{sig}N=(1,2n+1),
\tag{4.1}
\]

and the desired K3 complement has signature

\[
(2,18-2n),\qquad \operatorname{rk}N^\perp=20-2n.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Write `ell(A_N)` for the minimum number of generators of the discriminant
group.

### Corollary 4.1 -- automatic low-dimensional inverse theorem

Every root-complete compact golden norm-two package in dimension
`n <= 4` has a K3 eigen-nef realization (3.2).

Indeed `ell(A_N) <= rk(N)=2n+2`, while for `n <= 4`

\[
20-2n\ge (2n+2)+2\ge\ell(A_N)+2.
\]

Nikulin's stable primitive-embedding theorem applies.  This is an automatic
surface theorem for a class defined without a K3 condition.

### Corollary 4.2 -- the stable-discriminant range through `H8`

For `5 <= n <= 8`, the surface-free arithmetic inequality

\[
\boxed{\ell(A_N)\le18-2n}
\tag{4.3}
\]

is sufficient for the same conclusion.  Bugaenko has

\[
n=8,\qquad
N\simeq\langle2\rangle\oplus\langle-2\rangle
\oplus E_8(-1)^2,qquad
A_N\simeq(\mathbf Z/2)^2,
\]

so (4.3) is an equality.  Its complete Vinberg certificate proves that its
actual eleven-wall polytope, not a refinement, is returned in (3.2).

Condition (4.3) is not disguised K3 existence: it is a Smith-normal-form
inequality computed directly from the trace Gram.  It is sufficient rather
than necessary.

### Corollary 4.3 -- the rank-twenty boundary

For `n=9`, K3 association is equivalent to the existence of an even
positive-definite binary lattice `T` and a discriminant anti-isometry

\[
q_T\simeq-q_N.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For a fixed `N`, reduced positive binary forms of determinant `|det N|` and
the finite anti-isometry sets give a finite exhaustive list.  Every survivor
is a singular K3 surface, classified by the oriented complement `T`; the
period locus is zero-dimensional.  The silver rank-twenty package is one
survivor of this carrier gate, with `T=<2>+<2>`, but its complete norm-two
field-reflection chamber has an infinite stabilizer and therefore infinite
volume.  This does not decide the full K3 restricted-root arrangement:
saturation adds a `(-2)` divisor of field norm `3-sqrt(2)` whose reflection
is not `Z[sqrt(2)]`-integral.

### Corollary 4.4 -- genuine carrier nonexistence

An `O`-stable integral full-trace quadratic K3 eigen-nef carrier cannot exist
for `n >= 10`, since then `rk N=2(n+1)>20\ge\rho(X)`.  For an
`O_F`-stable integral full-trace carrier in degree `e`, the identical argument
is

\[
\boxed{e(n+1)\le20,
\qquad n\le\left\lfloor\frac{20}{e}\right\rfloor-1.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The period dimension of a surviving exact-Picard carrier is

\[
20-e(n+1).
\tag{4.6}
\]

These are genuine theorems about the proposed polytope-to-surface mechanism.
They are not yet theorems that the polytopes themselves do not exist.

## 5. A non-singleton positive class

The automatic theorem is not vacuous.  In addition to Bugaenko, take the
equal-root Coxeter Gram lattices of the following compact golden Lanner
simplices:

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
n&\text{Coxeter labels}\\
\hline
2&(2,5,5),(3,3,5),(3,5,5),(5,5,5),\\
3&[3,5,3],\ [5,3,5],\ D_4(5,3,3),\
   C_4(\text{one }5),\ C_4(\text{opposite }5\text{s}),\\
4&[5,3,3,3],\ [5,3,3,5],\ D_5(\text{terminal }5).
\end{array}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Here a bracket is a linear Coxeter diagram and omitted edges have label two.
Their field Gram matrices have identity signature `(n,1)`, positive-definite
conjugates, and positive-definite proper principal subdiagrams.  Thus the
displayed simplices are compact.  The diagonal `2` and off-diagonal entries
`0,-1,-phi` make their `O`-root lattices even and every simple reflection
integral.

For reference, the full determinants of the four higher-dimensional chains
are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
[3,5,3]&5-4\phi&(1+4\phi)\text{ at the conjugate place},\\
[5,3,5]&6-5\phi&(1+5\phi)\text{ at the conjugate place},\\
[5,3,3,3]&6-4\phi&(2+4\phi)\text{ at the conjugate place},\\
[5,3,3,5]&8-6\phi&(2+6\phi)\text{ at the conjugate place}.
\end{array}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Each identity determinant is negative and each displayed conjugate is
positive.  The finite proper subdiagrams give the remaining Sylvester tests.

The subsequent exact permutation and two-place census has now both proved
that this list is exhaustive in the equal-root golden Lanner class and
removed the provisional subchamber qualification.  It enumerates every
norm-two mirror meeting each closed simplex.  The boundary-contact counts are

\[
9,8,10,12;\quad25,27,26,28,44;\quad64,114,108,
\]

in the order (5.1), and every row has zero mixed-sign/interior-cutting root.
Consequently each original Lanner simplex is itself the chamber `C` of the
complete norm-two arrangement.  Literal deleted-wall Gram tests make every
vertex finite.  Corollary 4.1 therefore gives K3 eigen-nef sections equal to
these same twelve compact simplices, with family dimensions `14,12,10` for
`n=2,3,4` respectively.  Lanner's maximum-order-five theorem proves that
there is no member in `H5+`.  The full enumeration and replay are frozen in
[`golden_lanner_exhaustive_census.md`](golden_lanner_exhaustive_census.md).

This proves a non-singleton explicit positive inverse class without assuming
carrier success.  Worldwide novelty is not claimed for the classical
low-dimensional abstract Lanner polytopes; the new project result is their
complete-root and inverse-K3 realization.

At high dimension the current ledger is sharper and less cheerful:

| package | K3 gate | complete chamber | conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bugaenko golden `H8` | rank 18, stable, period dimension 2 | 11 facets, compact | strong positive |
| silver `H9` | rank 20, binary complement `<2>+<2>` | intrinsic field-norm-two chamber has infinite stabilizer; full K3 `(-2)` arrangement open | no output from the intrinsic arrangement; tested Alexeev local-star slices are non-Coxeter |

The golden `H10` package lives on OG10 rather than a surface and its audited
complete chamber also has infinite volume.  Thus Bugaenko plus the Lanner
completions form a non-singleton theorem class, but there is still no second
audited strong example in dimension at least eight.

## 6. What classical algebraic geometry does and does not return

### Period and Picard theory: real leverage

Period surjectivity converts every primitive trace embedding into an actual
family of projective K3 surfaces, while the Picard bound gives (4.5).  In
rank twenty, Shioda--Inose theory converts the finite binary-complement
calculation into explicit singular K3 isomorphism classes.  These are genuine
surface-theoretic consequences extracted after starting with `P`.

### Meyer: no compact eigen-slice obstruction

For `n >= 2`, the trace lattice has indefinite rational rank at least six.
Meyer's theorem therefore supplies a nonzero rational isotropic class, and
after the usual K3 Weyl/Riemann--Roch arguments the surface has a genus-one
fibration.  But that isotropic vector need not belong to `E_phi`, nor need
its nef Weyl frame be compatible with the frame used in (3.2).  Consequently

\[
X\text{ has an isotropic nef ray}
\centernot\Longrightarrow
P\text{ has an ideal vertex}.
\]

Bugaenko is an explicit counterexample to the implication: its rank-18 trace
lattice is isotropic, while its `H8` eigen-nef section is compact.  Thus Meyer
cannot lower the compact boundary in this eigenslice class.

### Existing K3 nef-cone classifications: wrong quantifier

The Nikulin--Vinberg/Gritsenko--Nikulin classifications concern the complete
root chamber in the full real Picard space.  Here `E_phi` has only half the
real dimension of `N_R`.  Finite volume, rational polyhedrality, finite
automorphism group, and finite Cox ring for the **full** nef cone therefore
do not classify the eigen-nef sections.  Treating them as if they did would
reverse an invalid implication.

The divisor-lattice action does not repair this: multiplication by `phi` is
self-adjoint but is not an isometry, a Hodge action, or a surface
automorphism.  Hence classifications of K3 automorphisms do not apply to it.

## 7. The exact missing universality lemma

Let `A_e` denote a proposed broader class of compact arithmetic Coxeter
polytopes over totally real fields of degree `e`, defined independently of
surfaces.  To turn (4.5) into a polytope nonexistence theorem one needs the
following implication, proved uniformly from the Coxeter data.

### Missing Lemma U -- exhaustive strong trace-carrier universality

For every `P in A_e`, an exhaustive enumeration of positive root scalings and
integral reflection-stable saturations produces at least one package
`(M,h,alpha)` such that

1. the trace form is even integral and embeds primitively in the K3 lattice;
2. every K3 `(-2)` wall visible in the distinguished eigenspace belongs to a
   finite list of field-norm shells determined from `P`;
3. the complete visible arrangement has `P` itself as one chamber, with no
   hidden interior wall; and
4. every allowed arithmetic realization of `P` is represented in the
   enumeration, so failure of all packages excludes `P`, not merely one
   favorite normalization.

If Lemma U held, Picard theory would immediately give

\[
n\le\left\lfloor20/e\right\rfloor-1;
\]

in particular, no real-quadratic member of `A_2` could exist above `H9`.
To sharpen the boundary to `H8`, one would additionally need a rank-twenty
classification theorem saying that no K3-compatible real-quadratic form has
a finite-volume **complete geometric restricted-root** chamber.  The
negative intrinsic silver field-norm-two arrangement is one data point, not
that theorem; it does not include the extra saturation `(-2)` wall.

Lemma U is not presently known, and its first clause cannot be inserted into
the definition of `A_e` without making the claimed leverage circular.  In
fact its primitive-embedding clause already contains the prospective
high-dimensional nonexistence: above the Picard ceiling no such output can
exist.  What is proved here is the finite compiler and its automatic/stable
subranges, not this universal success assertion.

For compact equal-norm golden input with a **fixed normalized Gram**, clause 2
is solved and clause 3 is finitely decidable: the shell collapse is uniform
and compactness makes hidden-wall enumeration finite.  The saturation part of
clause 4 is also finite through the trace discriminant module.  A subsequent
theorem now closes the normal-scaling part of clause 4 on the independently
defined class `C_35^conn`: connected `m=3,5` crystallographic symmetrizable
systems have one equal-norm class modulo vertex units and common homothety.
For Bugaenko, rank equations also force its dotted entry and unique integral
saturation.  See
[`golden_normal_scaling_universality.md`](golden_normal_scaling_universality.md)
and its
[`hostile audit`](golden_normal_scaling_universality_hostile_audit.md).

This is not the existential content of Lemma U.  The remaining obstacles are
universal success of the primitive-embedding and hidden-root clauses,
enumeration of distinct dotted products when the realization is not already
fixed or rank-rigid, a jointly exhaustive carrier branch for failures, and an
arithmetic classification of the rank-twenty survivors.

## 8. Why other surface classes do not yet improve the bound

- Enriques and abelian surfaces have smaller divisor ranks, so they only
  lower the dimensions accessible to a full quadratic eigenspace.
- Rational surfaces give the existing universal weak carrier because their
  Picard ranks are unbounded under blow-up.  That same flexibility prevents a
  dimension bound unless the compiler also controls the canonical class,
  effectivity, and the complete nef cone.
- General-type surfaces likewise have no useful uniform Picard ceiling
  without a polytope-derived bound on Chern numbers.  Hodge index alone gives
  the desired Lorentzian signature but no finiteness.
- Higher-dimensional hyperkähler varieties raise the Betti ceiling (OG10
  reaches the golden `H10` weak carrier), but they are not algebraic surfaces
  and the known `H10` complete chamber is infinite-volume.

Thus K3 surfaces currently give the strongest honest combination of a broad
inverse period theorem and a rigid numerical ceiling.  Any stronger surface
route needs a canonical construction whose classical invariants are bounded
from the Coxeter data; the universal blow-up carrier does not have that
property.

## 9. The next theorem-shaped tasks

1. **Fixed-Gram implementation.**  Turn (2.1) into a general stable-isotropic
   subgroup enumerator for compact golden wall spans, and emit the complete
   discriminant-form/K3 decision for every saturation.
2. **`H9` boundary classification.**  Enumerate rank-ten golden/silver field
   forms whose rank-twenty trace discriminant form has a positive binary
   complement, then use the compact stabilizer criterion to decide reflection
   index.  This is the shortest route to either a second high-dimensional
   positive example or a scoped `H9` nonexistence theorem.
3. **Carrier-success universality.**  Use the solved normal-scaling theorem on
   `C_35^conn`, enumerate any unfixed dotted Gram products, and prove that at
   least one finite K3 package survives primitive embedding and every hidden-
   wall test--or route every failure through a jointly exhaustive alternative
   carrier.  Until this is proved, a failed K3 list cannot exclude an abstract
   polytope.
4. **Do not spend Meyer twice.**  Genus-one fibrations of the ambient K3 do
   not threaten compact eigen-nef sections; searches should instead target
   visible isotropic rays in the field eigenspace or infinite exact chamber
   stabilizers.
