# Allcock's `H13` finite-subtree family has one full-Picard K3 carrier

## Outcome

There is a concrete positive answer to the divisor-lattice programme.
Allcock's published finite-volume 21-wall Coxeter polytope $F\subset H^{13}$,
its 25-wall double $Q$, and **every finite-subtree redoubling descendant**
can be carried by one Néron--Severi lattice

\[
N\cong U\oplus D_6(-1)\oplus D_6(-1).
\]

More precisely, there is a six-dimensional family of projective complex K3
surfaces with exact Picard lattice $N$.  The primitive wall vectors of $F$
span all of $N$, all redoubling reflections are integral lattice isometries,
and every finite-subtree descendant again has full wall span $N$.  Thus this
is not merely a lattice containing a diagram: it is a **common full-Picard
divisor-lattice carrier** for Allcock's infinite family.

There is also an effective-divisor strengthening.  One can choose a marking
and ample class so that all 19 square-$-2$ walls of $F$ are simultaneously
effective.  With the natural inward orientation, every square-$-2$ boundary
wall of every rooted finite-subtree descendant is effective on the same K3.

The replayable certificate is
[`allcock_h13_d6d6_k3_carrier.json`](../data/enumerations/allcock_h13_d6d6_k3_carrier.json),
generated by
[`certify_allcock_h13_d6d6_k3_carrier.py`](../scripts/certify_allcock_h13_d6d6_k3_carrier.py).

This is a new linkage in the project, not a claim that either endpoint was
previously unknown.  Allcock's polytopes are published, and the elliptic-K3
stratum with root type $2D_6$ and trivial torsion already occurs in
Shimada's classification.  Worldwide publication priority for the linkage
has not yet been established.

## The corrected 21-wall lattice

Let $L=II_{25,1}$, let $R=D_6\oplus D_6$ be the spherical root system
whose Conway-chamber face is $F$, and put $K=R^\perp\subset L$.  We use
the positive-root convention, so $K$ has signature $(13,1)$ and the K3
lattice is $N=-K$.

Allcock's Figure 3 has 21 nodes `A` through `U`.  Its exact integral wall
types are

```text
15 walls: norm 2, divisibility 1
 4 walls: norm 2, divisibility 2   (D,E,H,I)
 2 walls: norm 4, divisibility 2   (B,J)
```

The integral basis

```text
A B C D F G H I K L P Q R T
```

has determinant `-16`, Smith diagonal `1^10 2^4`, and inertia `(13,1)`.
The certificate stores all 21 primitive coordinate columns.  Their Gram
matrix reproduces Figure 3 and every reflection

\[
s_r(x)=x-\frac{2(x,r)}{r^2}r
\]

is integral and preserves $K$.

There is a noncircular containment step here.  Projecting each Figure 3
extension root to $K\otimes\mathbf Q$ gives a normal $p\in K^\vee$.
For a $D_6A_1$ extension, $p\in K$ and $p^2=2$; for a $D_7$ extension,
$2p\in K$ and $(2p)^2=4$; and for an $E_7$ extension, $2p\in K$ and
$(2p)^2=2$.  Therefore the normalized 21-wall span is genuinely a sublattice
$W\subseteq K$.  The 14 displayed basis walls give
$\det W=16=\det K$, so $W=K$.

### Why the dashed products really equal `-4`

The old project transcript made two related mistakes: it omitted the heavy
bonds `H-F` and `I-G`, and it treated dashed edges as though the drawing fixed
their metric distance.  The corrected argument does not infer a distance
from the drawing.

Allcock's left--right `(01)` symmetry and his additional
$Co_\infty$-symmetry exchanging the two $D_6$ components put the dashed
edges in the two orbits

```text
{DE, HI},  {DI, EH}.
```

Write their negative Gram products as $x,y$.  Since the chamber lies in
$H^{13}$, every 15-by-15 minor of the 21-wall Gram vanishes.  Using the
14-node basis above and adjoining respectively `J,N,O,S,U` gives, after
dividing by `80,40,8,16,40`,

```text
21x²-24xy-86x+ 9y²+32y+120
13x²- 8xy-96x+ 7y²-12y+240
 2x²+ 2xy-28x+11y²-92y+240
 9x²- 6xy-66x+ 7y²-20y+184
10x²-10xy-60x+ 7y²- 4y+144.
```

Their exact lexicographic Gröbner basis is

```text
x-4, y-4.
```

Thus all four dashed products are exactly `-4`.  With `H-F` and `I-G`
restored, the full Gram has rank 14 and inertia `(13,1,7)`; the previous
omitted-edge matrix had the impossible rank/inertia `(18;15,3,3)`.

## Primitivity and lattice identification

The root lattice $R=D_6\oplus D_6$ is primitive in $II_{25,1}$.  Here is
the short obstruction argument.

Its discriminant form is

\[
q_R=\langle 3/2\rangle^4,
\]

whose only nonzero isotropic class is the diagonal class $(v,v)$.  The
corresponding index-two even overclosure would contain a new norm-two vector,
represented by $(e_1,e_1)$.  If that vector belonged to $L$, its root
reflection would fix a hyperbolic point in the relative interior of $F$.
The Tits-cone stabilizer theorem says that this stabilizer is exactly
$W(D_6)^2$; every reflection in it has its root in $R$.  That contradicts
the new vector lying outside $R$.

Consequently

```text
signature(K) = (13,1)
det(K)       = 16
q_K          = <1/2>^4.
```

On the other hand $U\oplus D_6\oplus D_6$ has discriminant form
$\langle3/2\rangle^4$.  Over $\mathbf F_2$, the invertible matrix $J-I$
gives an explicit isometry between these two forms.  The indefinite-genus
uniqueness theorem applies because

\[
14\ge \ell(A_K)+2=6.
\]

Therefore

\[
K\cong U\oplus D_6\oplus D_6,
\qquad
N=-K\cong U\oplus D_6(-1)^2.
\]

This also corrects an initially tempting but false guess
$U\oplus E_8(-1)\oplus D_4(-1)$.

## A primitive K3 embedding

Use the coordinate model

\[
E_8=D_8\cup(\tfrac12^8+D_8).
\]

The $D_6$ on the first six coordinates is primitive: intersecting its real
span with $E_8$ gives exactly the integral vectors of even coordinate sum,
namely $D_6$.  Its orthogonal complement is the $A_1^2$ generated by
$e_7-e_8,e_7+e_8$.  Embedding the two $D_6(-1)$'s into the two
$E_8(-1)$'s and $U$ into one hyperbolic plane gives

\[
N\hookrightarrow \Lambda_{K3}=U^3\oplus E_8(-1)^2,
\qquad
N^\perp\cong U^2\oplus A_1(-1)^4.
\]

The marked-period theorem therefore gives a nonempty exact-Picard family of
dimension $20-14=6$.

## One explicit six-parameter elliptic K3 family

Let $[s:t]$ be coordinates on the base, put $h=st$, and let $P,R,S$ be
binary quadratics.  Consider

\[
\begin{aligned}
f&=s^2t^2(-3P^2+hR),\\
g&=s^3t^3(2P^3-hPR+h^2S),\\
y^2z&=x^3+fxz^2+gz^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

The discriminant is

\[
\Delta=-16s^8t^8\Xi,
\]

where

\[
\Xi=-9P^2R^2+108P^3S+h(4R^3-54PRS)+27h^2S^2.
\]

On the open set where the endpoint coefficients do not vanish and $\Xi$
has eight simple roots away from $st=0$, this is a globally minimal elliptic
K3 with fibers

```text
2 I_2^* + 8 I_1.
```

Its trivial lattice is $U\oplus D_6(-1)^2$.  The following one-parameter
gauge leaves $f,g$ unchanged:

\[
P\mapsto P+ch,
\quad R\mapsto R+6cP+3c^2h,
\quad S\mapsto S+cR+3c^2P+c^3h.
\]

Starting from nine coefficients, quotienting this gauge, Weierstrass scaling,
and the base $\mathbf G_m$ leaves six parameters.  The coefficient map has
rank eight (a frozen minor equals `108`), while the four endpoint
$I_2^*$-conditions have independent Jacobian.  Hence the family dominates
the six-dimensional $N$-polarized K3 moduli space, so its very general
member has exact Picard lattice $N$.

An explicit open-locus witness in the affine chart $u=t/s$ is

```text
P=u²+u+1,  R=3u²+2u+1,  S=u²-u+2.
```

It gives

```text
F=-3u⁴-3u³-7u²-5u-3
G= 2u⁶+3u⁵+8u⁴+7u³+11u²+5u+2
Xi=27u⁸-108u⁷+126u⁶-126u⁵+473u⁴+210u³+753u²+382u+207.
```

The exact discriminant of `Xi` is

```text
6482406901924477836136627545047040000,
```

and its endpoint coefficients are `207` and `27`.  A monic cubic-root
argument reduces possible 2-torsion to a quadratic polynomial; the exact
coefficient ideal has Gröbner basis `[1]`.  Thus this member has no 2-torsion.
Together with the component groups/height constraint this rules out torsion;
Shimada's independent table records the same `2D6`, trivial-torsion stratum.

## Why every finite subtree has the same full Picard carrier

Doubling $F$ across wall `D` gives Allcock's 25-wall chamber $Q$.  Direct
Smith calculations give index one for the spans of both wall sets.  More is
true:

- deleting any doubling wall of $F$ leaves a set spanning $K$;
- deleting any doubling wall of $Q$ leaves a set spanning $K$; and
- every doubling reflection is an integral isometry of $K$.

Take a leaf in any finite subtree of chamber copies.  That leaf contributes
all its boundary walls except the one used for gluing, and those surviving
walls already span $K$.  Removing the leaf and iterating proves that every
finite-subtree boundary has exact span $K$, not merely containment in it.
Allcock's theorem supplies the Coxeter and finite-volume conclusions; a
finite subtree is a finite union of finite-volume copies.

## Ample-intersection and effectivity framing

In the displayed $K$-basis, take

```text
h=(-6,1,6,3,6,12,19,10,-4,2,6,2,0,2).
```

Then $h^2=-1252$ in $K$, hence $h^2=1252$ in $N=-K$, and its
intersections with the 21 oriented wall divisors are

```text
48,2,12,48,114,34,2,2,104,58,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,4.
```

An exact saturated kernel calculation gives $\det(h^\perp)=5008$.  After a
frozen unimodular reduction, exact LDL enumeration finds no nonzero vector of
square at most two and 388 vectors of square at most four.  Thus
$h^\perp$ has minimum four: $h$ is not orthogonal to any K3 root.

Choose the K3 Weyl chamber containing $h$ as the ample chamber.  For every
oriented square-$-2$ wall $d$, Riemann--Roch says that one of $d,-d$ is
effective, while positivity against $h$ rules out $-d$.  This proves the
simultaneous effectivity statement.  It does **not** prove that the classes
are irreducible, that the 21 walls form the full nef cone, that the two
square-$-4$ classes are effective, or that any reflection is induced by a
surface automorphism.

## Claim boundary and next theorem

This example suggests the precise inverse-carrier question:

> Given a finite-volume Coxeter polytope whose primitive integral wall
> lattice $N$ has signature $(1,n)$, when does $N$ admit a primitive K3
> embedding, an exact-Picard period, and a root-regular positive class meeting
> every oriented wall positively?

These are substantially softer tests than constructing the polytope:
discriminant-form length controls the primitive K3 embedding, the period map
supplies the surface, and a finite exact short-vector calculation supplies an
ample-intersection framing.  The Allcock example proves that this converse
programme is nonempty and can carry an infinite positive family.  Equality
with a nef cone is a stronger second-stage condition, not part of the carrier
criterion.
