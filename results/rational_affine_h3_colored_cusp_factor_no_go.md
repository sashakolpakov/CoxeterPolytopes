# Canonically colored affine cusp factors: an exact local no-go

**Status (2026-08-10): proved at the marked root-packet level; no global
surface family or reflection-maximal counterfamily is claimed.**  This note
tests whether the affine-H3 genus-three fibre, the positive affine marks,
and the three adjunction colors can by themselves bound the other
irreducible factors in its cusp link.

They cannot.  Every factor satisfies one exact weighted-color identity, but
for every even \(n\geq4\) there is a primitive physical
\(\widetilde D_n\) packet of affine rank \(n\) satisfying that identity,
with only smooth-rational \((-4)\)- and \((-1)\)-colors and with no H1 or
H2 incidence.  Thus even a separate bound on every zero-color
\((-2)\)-chain would not bound the affine-factor rank.

There are two indispensable qualifications.  The displayed
\(\widetilde D_n\) chamber is not reflection-maximal on its split root
lattice: coordinate mirrors enlarge \(D_n\) to the signed-permutation
arrangement.  Whether those mirrors preserve the **full adapted host** is a
parity/glue question not determined by the affine Gram matrix.  Moreover,
the packet has an exact ordinary-blowup proximity model, but this note does
not construct, for arbitrary \(n\), a projective rational genus-three
pencil realizing it.  Those are precisely the two global gates which a
genuine dimension theorem may still exploit.

No Nikulin--Vinberg list, affine-diagram census, or rational-surface census
is used.

## 1. The identity obeyed by every cusp factor

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective rational surface carrying the affine-H3
fibre

\[
                    F=4E+B_1+B_2+B_3+B_4,
 \qquad F^2=0,\qquad K_S.F=4,                         \tag{1.1}
\]

and assume that the index-one abundance conclusion has made \(F\) the
primitive connected fibre class.

Use the doubled adapted host

\[
 M=\left\langle\operatorname {NS}(S),B_\alpha/2\right\rangle,
 \qquad q_M=2q_{\operatorname {NS}(S)}.               \tag{1.2}
\]

The three physical roots and their canonical colors are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{curve}&r&q_M(K_S,r)\\ \hline
B^2=-4&B/2&+2\\
R^2=-2&R&0\\
E^2=-1&E&-2.
\end{array}                                             \tag{1.3}
\]

Let \(\Gamma\) be any irreducible affine factor in the full wall
neighborhood of the cusp \([F]\).  Write its primitive positive mark
relation as

\[
                 u_\Gamma=\sum_{i\in\Gamma}a_i r_i,
 \qquad a_i\in\mathbf Z_{>0},\qquad
                 u_\Gamma=\lambda {F\over2}.           \tag{1.4}
\]

Here \(\lambda>0\).  Since

\[
 D_\Gamma:=2u_\Gamma
   =\sum_{i\in B}a_iB_i
     +2\sum_{i\in R}a_iR_i
     +2\sum_{i\in E}a_iE_i                             \tag{1.5}
\]

is integral, primitivity of \(F\) makes \(\lambda\) a positive integer.
Pairing (1.4) with \(K_S\) gives the universal equation

\[
 \boxed{\quad
   2\sum_{i\in B}a_i-2\sum_{i\in E}a_i=4\lambda,
   \qquad
   \sum_{i\in B}a_i-\sum_{i\in E}a_i=2\lambda.
 \quad}                                                  \tag{1.6}
\]

The zero-color marks do not occur in (1.6).  More importantly, (1.6)
controls only a **difference** of the positive and negative marked masses;
it controls neither mass separately.  Primitivity only says that the
physical coefficient vector in (1.5) has the appropriate gcd.  It supplies
no component count.

Equation (1.6) applies to every irreducible affine factor, independently of
whether the full cusp has one factor or several.  It is therefore the
strongest conclusion available from the null relation and canonical
coloring alone.

## 2. An arbitrary-rank primitive colored packet

Fix an even integer \(n\geq4\).  Use vertices

\[
                         0,1,2,\ldots,n                 \tag{2.1}
\]

and edges

\[
 0-2,\quad1-2,\quad2-3-\cdots-(n-2),\quad
 (n-2)-(n-1),\quad(n-2)-n.                              \tag{2.2}
\]

Give the symmetric root Gram matrix diagonal \(-2\) and entry \(+1\) on
the edges (2.2), with all other entries zero.  Direct row reduction gives
rank \(n\) and one-dimensional radical generated by the positive marks

\[
 a_0=a_1=a_{n-1}=a_n=1,
 \qquad a_i=2\quad(2\leq i\leq n-2).                    \tag{2.3}
\]

This is the affine \(D_n\) packet, but (2.2)--(2.3), rather than a table,
are all that will be used.

Color the four leaves and every odd internal vertex by \(B/2\), and color
every even internal vertex by \(E\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal B&=\{0,1,n-1,n\}\cup
              \{3,5,\ldots,n-3\},\\
 \mathcal E&=\{2,4,\ldots,n-2\}.                        \tag{2.4}
\end{aligned}
\]

Every edge joins unlike colors.  Therefore (2.2) is exactly the doubled
host Gram matrix of a putative physical configuration

\[
 B_i^2=-4,\qquad E_j^2=-1,
 \qquad B_i.E_j=1\ \text{on (2.2)},                     \tag{2.5}
\]

with every other intersection zero.  In particular the \((-4)\)-curves
are pairwise disjoint, there is no \((-4),(-2)\) incidence, and hence the
packet has neither H1 nor H2.

Put

\[
 F_n=\sum_{i\in\mathcal B}a_iB_i
       +2\sum_{j\in\mathcal E}a_jE_j.                   \tag{2.6}
\]

Thus the four leaf \(B\)'s occur with multiplicity one, the internal
\(B\)'s with multiplicity two, and every \(E\) with multiplicity four.
The equations at the vertices are immediate:

* a leaf \(B\) sees one multiplicity-four \(E\);
* an internal \(B\) sees two multiplicity-four \(E\)'s; and
* an end \(E\) sees \(1+1+2=4\), while an internal \(E\) sees
  \(2+2=4\).

Consequently

\[
                   F_n.C=0\quad(C\subset F_n),
 \qquad F_n^2=0.                                       \tag{2.7}
\]

Take the physical component group to be free on the displayed curve
symbols.  The coefficient one on every leaf then makes \(F_n\) primitive
in that marked group.  This is not merely a gcd heuristic: one may adjoin
a class \(t\) with \(t.F_n=1\), extending the semidefinite component form
to a nondegenerate Lorentzian form in which the embedding and \(F_n\) are
primitive.  An eventual surface embedding could add further glue, and is
part of the separate realization gate below.

The marked positive and negative masses are

\[
              \sum_{\mathcal B}a_i=n,
 \qquad       \sum_{\mathcal E}a_i=n-2.                 \tag{2.8}
\]

Hence (1.6) holds with \(\lambda=1\), and adjunction gives

\[
 K_S.F_n=2n-2(n-2)=4,
 \qquad p_a(F_n)=1+{F_n^2+K_S.F_n\over2}=3.             \tag{2.9}
\]

This proves the exact local no-go:

> Positive affine marks, primitive fibre class, the physical norms,
> H1/H2 avoidance, and the three canonical colors admit irreducible affine
> packets of every even rank \(n\geq4\).  The family uses no zero-color
> vertex at all.  Thus bounding zero-color chains, even perfectly, cannot
> by itself bound the full cusp-factor rank.

The construction is a marked intersection packet.  If the curves (2.5)
occur effectively on a smooth surface, then (2.7) makes \(F_n\) nef
automatically: a distinct irreducible curve has nonnegative intersection
with every effective component of \(F_n\).

## 3. Exact proximity compatibility, and its limit

The \((-1)\)-vertices in (2.4) are pairwise disjoint.  Contract them.  A
leaf \(B\) then has square \(-3\), while an internal \(B\) has square
\(-2\).  At either end, the three neighboring \(B\)-branches acquire one
ordinary common point; at every internal contracted \(E\), the two
neighboring \(B\)-branches acquire an ordinary double point.  Conversely,
blowing up those ordinary points once recovers (2.5), and the exceptional
multiplicity is the sum of the incident branch multiplicities:

\[
                         1+1+2=4,
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad 2+2=4.                            \tag{3.1}
\]

For \(n=4\) the two end vertices coincide: the unique \(E\) meets all four
leaf branches, and the corresponding equality is instead

\[
                         1+1+1+1=4.                       \tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the marks satisfy the exact point-blowup proximity rule in every even
rank; no fractional or negative multiplicity is hidden in the packet.

This is **not** an existence theorem for a global pencil.  One must still
construct, for arbitrary \(n\), a projective rational surface carrying the
downstairs rational branches with precisely these ordinary clusters and a
second member of the same genus-three pencil.  Neither the Gram matrix,
adjunction, nor the proximity equalities prove that smoothing/global
linear-system statement.  Accordingly this note does not call (2.5) an
unbounded family of rational-surface fibres.

## 4. Why reflection maximality is not encoded by the packet

There is an equally sharp lattice warning.  Realize the finite \(D_n\)
root lattice as

\[
 D_n=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\mathbf Z^n:
                         \sum x_i\equiv0\pmod2\}.         \tag{4.1}
\]

Its norm-two roots are \(\pm e_i\pm e_j\).  The vector

\[
                              v=2e_n                    \tag{4.2}
\]

is primitive in \(D_n\), has norm four, and its reflection changes the
sign of the last coordinate.  It preserves \(D_n\) but is not generated
by the even-sign-change Weyl group \(W(D_n)\).  Adjoining it gives the
signed-permutation group.  In the finite simple roots it is

\[
                         v=\alpha_n-\alpha_{n-1}.          \tag{4.3}
\]

Both terminal leaves have color \(+2\), so (4.3) gives
\(q_M(K_S,v)=0\).  Its mirror swaps the two terminal leaf walls and bisects
the standard affine \(D_n\) cusp cell.  Explicitly, that cell may be
written

\[
 x_1>x_2>\cdots>x_{n-1}>|x_n|,
 \qquad x_1+x_2<1                                      \tag{4.4}
\]

in its interior; it contains points with either sign of \(x_n\), and
\(v^\perp=\{x_n=0\}\).  On the split root lattice, therefore, the
\(\widetilde D_n\) chamber has a proper reflection refinement by a
genuinely zero-color norm-four mirror.

This does not by itself contradict maximality on the adapted host \(M\).
With the negative sign convention, a primitive norm-minus-four vector has

\[
                         s_v(x)=x+{q_M(x,v)\over2}v.       \tag{4.5}
\]

Thus this coordinate reflection preserves \(M\) only if

\[
                              q_M(M,v)\subset2\mathbf Z.  \tag{4.6}
\]

The affine factor proves (4.6) against its own root sublattice, but says
nothing about pairings with the ambient glue or with walls outside the
cusp factor.  In the physical basis, (4.3) is

\[
                         v={B_n-B_{n-1}\over2}.             \tag{4.7}
\]

There is nevertheless an exact ambient test.  The Neron--Severi lattice
of a smooth rational surface is unimodular.  Moreover the square-minus-four
boundaries in the affine-H3 lane are pairwise disjoint, so pairing \(v\)
with their half-classes contributes only \(0\) or \(\pm2\).  Pairing with
an arbitrary \(x\in\operatorname {NS}(S)\) gives

\[
                         q_M(x,v)=x.(B_n-B_{n-1}).           \tag{4.8}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 q_M(M,v)\subset2\mathbf Z
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B_n-B_{n-1}\in2\operatorname {NS}(S).}
                                                               \tag{4.9}
\]

The host \(M\) is even, while \(q_M(v,v)=-4\), so \(v\) is primitive in
the full host as well as in the split \(D_n\) sublattice.  Thus (4.9) is
not merely sufficient: if an ambient class pairs oddly with \(v\), the
half-integral shift in (4.5) cannot belong to \(M\).  The affine Gram
matrix does not determine the congruence in (4.9).

Conversely, if (4.6) is proved for the full host, the coordinate mirror
subdivides the Euclidean \(\widetilde D_n\) cusp cell.  A chamber whose
facet group equals the full integral reflection group cannot retain the
unrefined factor.  This is a genuine possible use of reflection maximality,
but it requires the missing ambient-parity theorem.

## 5. Exact conclusion

The local AG data give the identity (1.6), not a rank inequality.  The
packets (2.2)--(2.9) rule out every argument of the form

\[
 \text{primitive }F+K.F=4+\text{positive marks/canonical colors}
                         \Longrightarrow\text{bounded affine rank}.     \tag{5.1}
\]

They also show that a theorem devoted only to zero-color chains is
insufficient: the total rank is already unbounded with no zero-color node.
What remains genuinely global is one of the following.

1. Prove that the coordinate refinements (or another bounded-index family
   of refinements) are integral on the **full adapted host**, and then use
   reflection maximality.
2. Prove that arbitrary-rank packets (2.5) cannot be smoothed inside a
   rational genus-three pencil, or bound their global marked degree.
3. Control the total number and ranks of rational singular-fibre factors by
   a relatively minimal fibration invariant which survives all vertical
   blowups.

Until one of these is supplied, the cusp coloring is a precise diagnostic,
not the missing vertical-tail theorem.
