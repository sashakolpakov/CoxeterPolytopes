# Enhanced returns and the finite-quotient obstruction for the H11 K3 corridor

**Status.** Exact theorem for the two observed return galleries and a
mathematically total centralizer-decision dichotomy.  The former one-witness
radius-five and radius-eight "complete ball" claims are withdrawn: one
restricted facet can have a nontrivial induced-chamber subdivision on its
opposite side.  The replacement exact **ambient carrier** is now implemented
and passes that counterexample.  Its complete radius-eight ball has now been
enumerated: it
contains exactly the enhanced returns \(I,A\), while the known \(B_+,B_-\)
occur only at distance at least nine.  The queue remains open, so no global
centralizer or finite-volume decision is claimed.  The return symmetry itself
remains completely identified.

## 1. The two returns are the same marked automorphism

The first simultaneous auxiliary-wall crossing is

\[
D_{68}\longrightarrow D_{10}\longrightarrow D_{10}
\longrightarrow D_{69}\longrightarrow D_{68}.
\]

Let \(A\) be its cumulative integral row transport.  A second simultaneous
crossing appears at the physical \(D_{27}\):

\[
D_{27}\longrightarrow D_{11}\longrightarrow D_{11}
\longrightarrow D_{74}\longrightarrow D_{27}.
\]

If the entering physical \(D_{27}\) has transport \(g\), and the returned
copy has transport \(g'\), exact matrix multiplication gives

\[
\boxed{g'=gA,\qquad g^{-1}g'=A.}                                    \tag{1}
\]

Thus the \(D_{27}\) gallery supplies no second automorphism.  It is another
fundamental-domain realization of the same marked transformation already
seen at \(D_{68}\).

The distinction between \(g^{-1}g'\) and \(g'g^{-1}\) matters.  Shimada's
states use cumulative row transports.  The first expression is the global
right action on the fixed marked lattice and centralizes the golden action;
the latter is merely a conjugated local left transport and does not.

The matrix \(A\) has SHA-256

```text
1944304c8501f25118eaee6304aaf4038e690199a6e1a19847b2258645a65b4d
```

and satisfies, entrywise,

\[
A^2=I,\qquad AGA^{\mathsf T}=G,\qquad
A^{\mathsf T}\tau=\tau A^{\mathsf T},qquad \det A=1.                \tag{2}
\]

Its characteristic polynomial on the rank-20 Neron--Severi lattice is

\[
(t-1)^{18}(t+1)^2.                                                   \tag{3}
\]

On the cyclic discriminant group \(A_{\operatorname{NS}}\cong\mathbb Z/11\),
\(A\) acts as \(-1\), not as the identity.  It therefore extends to the K3
lattice by \(-I\) on the positive oriented transcendental lattice
\(T_{11}\).  This extension is a Hodge isometry: \(-I\) preserves the
orientation of the rank-two positive plane and acts on the holomorphic
two-form by a scalar.  Since \(A\) takes an ample class to another ample
class, global Torelli realizes it as an involutive K3 automorphism.  Equation
(2) makes it an automorphism of the enhanced K3 data with the golden
correspondence.

## 2. Its action is one hyperbolic reflection

In the transported golden eigenbasis the action collapses to

\[
E=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
1-\phi&-1&0\\
0&0&I_8
\end{pmatrix}.                                                       \tag{4}
\]

For a row of coefficients \(c\), this says

\[
c_0' = c_0+(1-\phi)c_1,qquad c_1'=-c_1,qquad c_i'=c_i\ (i\ge2).
\]

Hence \(E-I\) has rank one, \(E+I\) has rank nine, and the fixed hyperplane
is

\[
\boxed{c_1=0.}                                                       \tag{5}
\]

The common oriented functional is

\[
f=(0,-1,0^8).
\]

At \(D_{68}\), walls \((9,0),(16,0)\) restrict to \(f,\phi f\).  At the
physical \(D_{27}\), walls \((19,0),(31,0)\) restrict to the same pair
\(f,\phi f\).  Exact evaluation gives \(Ef=-f\).  Thus the two four-step
galleries cross the same enhanced-automorphism mirror.

Consequently the subgroup generated by the \(D_{68}\) and \(D_{27}\)
returns is not a rank-two dihedral group:

\[
\boxed{\langle A_{68},A_{27}\rangle=\langle A\rangle
\cong W(A_1)\cong C_2.}                                              \tag{6}
\]

## 3. The Pell isometry folds to this same involution

The independent H11 Pell seed is an infinite-order field-lattice isometry,
so it was a natural candidate for an infinite chamber stabilizer.  Exact
K3-nef folding gives the opposite conclusion.

For the first power, its image crosses four auxiliary induced chambers,
reaches one genuine root wall, and one norm-two reflection folds it into a
physical \(D_{68}\).  The residual field action is exactly \(E\) in (4):

\[
P\,s_r=E.                                                            \tag{7}
\]

For the second power, two root reflections leave the identity.  More
generally the exact power audit gives \(k\) root reflections and residual
\(E^k\).  Since the full norm-two reflection group is normal in the lattice
isometry group, equation (7) already proves that the Pell class modulo root
reflections has order at most two.  The infinite Pell stream therefore
supplies neither an infinite stabilizer nor a second enhanced generator.

The factorization is generated independently by
`scripts/fold_h11_seed_via_shimada.py` and cross-checked in the enhanced
quotient verifier.

## 4. A total exact centralizer-decision procedure

The finite Shimada atlas by itself is not a finite-state centralizer
calculation.  Its 1098 entries are representatives modulo the **full** K3
automorphism group.  A physical induced chamber carries a cumulative matrix
label

\[
                       (i,g)\quad\leftrightarrow\quad D_i g,
\]

and infinitely many different labels can have the same representative index
\(i\).  Commutation with the fixed golden operator depends on \(g\), not just
on \(i\).  Discarding this decoration is therefore not a completeness
argument.

There is nevertheless a terminating exact algorithm, but its graph must be
defined at the level of the ambient Shimada arrangement.  Let \(E\) be the
golden eigenspace and let \(\overline D\) denote a decorated physical Shimada
chamber.  Define the carrier component \({\cal K}_E\) by

\[
 \operatorname{Vert}({\cal K}_E)=
 \left\{D:\overline D\cap E\text{ contains a future-timelike point}\right\}.
                                                                    \tag{8}
\]

Two vertices are joined when their **ambient** chambers share one auxiliary
wall member.  Every member of every finite wall orbit is crossed; a
\((-2)\)-wall is a boundary of the nef cone and is never crossed.  Closed
intersection in (8), rather than open intersection, is essential: it retains
lower-dimensional incident cells and prevents a tangency or a multiple-wall
intersection from disconnecting the combinatorial carrier.

This graph is locally finite because each Shimada chamber has finitely many
ambient wall members.  It is connected by the standard carrier argument:
join two points of the convex eigensection by a segment, use local finiteness
to obtain the finite sequence of ambient cells met by that compact segment,
and, at a multiple intersection, include all closed incident cells.  Adjacent
cells across root walls are omitted because those walls are boundary faces of
the nef cone, not internal transitions.

Membership in (8) is decided without numerical tolerances.  Fix a future
timelike reference \(h\in E\), let \(q\) be the Lorentzian form, and let the
restricted ambient walls of \(D\) be the rows \(a_i\).  Homogeneity gives the
equivalence

\[
 \overline D\cap E\text{ contains a future-timelike ray}
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \left\{x:a_i x\ge0,\ B(h,x)=q(h)\right\}
 \text{ contains a point with }q(x)>0.                              \tag{9}
\]

An exact Bland phase-I calculation first decides feasibility of this closed
normalized polyhedron.  If its basic point is not timelike, maximize \(q\)
there.  On the affine time hyperplane \(q\) is strictly concave and coercive,
so a global maximum exists.  The normal cone of a polyhedron is generated by
its active rows without any Slater assumption.  Conic Caratheodory therefore
provides an independent active support of size at most \(\dim(E)-1\); finite
exact KKT enumeration finds it.  A positive maximum retains the chamber and
a nonpositive maximum rejects it.  This proves totality even for
lower-dimensional carrier cells.

Take \(D_{68}\) as base chamber and write

\[
 H_{68}=\operatorname{Aut}_{G_X}(D_{68}),\qquad |H_{68}|=2.
\]

Suppose a traversed physical state of representative type 68 is stored as
\((68,g)\).  An automorphism \(\gamma\) takes the base chamber to that state
exactly when

\[
 D_{68}\gamma=D_{68}g
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\gamma=a^{-1}g\text{ for some }a\in H_{68}}.               \tag{10}
\]

Consequently every enhanced automorphism in a traversed carrier ball is
recovered by enumerating the two elements of \(H_{68}\) and testing
\((a^{-1}g)^{\mathsf T}\tau=\tau(a^{-1}g)^{\mathsf T}\) entrywise.  The
stabilizer correction in (10) is necessary: testing only the stored transport
\(g\) can miss a return.

The algorithm has two rigorous stopping rules.

1. If the exact ambient-carrier queue closes, (10) gives the complete
   centralizer as a finite list.  Indeed, only finitely many physical images of \(D_{68}\)
   remain and every induced-chamber stabilizer is finite.
2. If (10) produces an infinite-order integral matrix commuting with \(\tau\),
   the enhanced centralizer is infinite.

One of these rules must occur after finitely many breadth-first layers.  If
the enhanced centralizer is finite, the enhanced-covolume theorem makes
\(\overline C\) compact; a locally finite arrangement has only finitely many
cells meeting a compact set, so the queue closes.  If the centralizer is
infinite, use \(\Gamma_C\cong\Gamma_\tau/W_F\).  The arithmetic group
\(\Gamma_\tau\) is finitely generated, hence so is its quotient
\(\Gamma_C\); the enhanced centralizer is commensurable with \(\Gamma_C\),
so it too is a finitely generated linear group.  Schur's theorem says that a
finitely generated torsion linear group is finite.  The infinite centralizer
therefore contains an infinite-order element.  The image of \(D_{68}\) under
that element is a vertex of \({\cal K}_E\) at finite distance, where (10)
detects it.

Finite order is also decided exactly.  In rank 20 every torsion integral
matrix has eigenvalue orders among the 41 integers \(n\) with
\(\varphi(n)\leq20\), whose least common multiple is

\[
                            6\,983\,776\,800.
\]

The verifier first rejects a non-cyclotomic characteristic factor.  Only in
the remaining cyclotomic case does it test this universal power, which also
detects a nontrivial unipotent part, and then divides the exponent to recover
the exact order.

This gives the precise additional certificate missing from the finite atlas:

\[
\boxed{\text{decorated-state queue closure, or an infinite-order decorated
return.}}                                                           \tag{11}
\]

The carrier traversal and its closed feasibility gate are implemented in
`scripts/explore_shimada_disc11_ambient_carrier.py`.  This replaces facet-star
sampling: ambient subdivision walls are graph edges from the outset.  The
mathematical dichotomy (11) is therefore executable, although the currently
archived radius-eight run has not reached either stopping rule.  Its exact
carrier layers are

\[
                         1,4,7,12,17,23,28,36,47,
\]

for 175 states through radius eight and 65 queued radius-nine states.  It
tests 4,572 auxiliary ambient wall members, retaining 874 crossings and
rejecting 3,698; it also records 3,486 \((-2)\)-wall members without crossing
them.

## 6. What the order-two quotient does to the 50-state corridor

For comparison with the earlier root-cone quotient calculation, the
one-witness retained sample has

\[
50\text{ processed physical states},\qquad
26\text{ queued states},\qquad
208\text{ certified internal crossings}.
\]

The hard-cutoff set of 76 states omits six order-two partners.  Closing it
under \(A\) gives exactly

\[
82\text{ physical states}=41\text{ free }C_2\text{-orbits}.         \tag{13}
\]

There are no fixed physical chambers.  The processed part meets 27 quotient
orbits, while 14 quotient orbits remain wholly in the frontier.  Thus the
quotient halves the repeated copies but does not close the traversal.

This conclusion does not depend only on representative indices.  Among the
50 processed states there are 32 pairs sharing a Shimada representative.
Exactly 23 relative transports centralize \(\tau\), and every one of those
23 matrices is the same \(A\).  No second centralizing relative transport
appears.  In particular, representatives \(D_9\) and \(D_{18}\), each seen
four times, split into two different \(A\)-orbits; their longitudinal copies
are not identified by an enhanced automorphism found in this corridor.

This was the precise obstruction visible inside the historical sample:

\[
\boxed{C_2\text{ removes the transverse doubling but supplies no
longitudinal folding generator.}}                                  \tag{14}
\]

The independent Bugaenko fixed-slice construction has since produced the two
additional finite-order elements \(B_+,B_-\).  Neither occurs in the exact
50-state sample that was inspected.  The corrected ambient carrier must
eventually reach their decorated D68 returns, providing a useful calibration
for its breadth-first layers; whether still later returns produce an
infinite-order element remains open.

## 7. Quotient of the eleven accumulated root walls

The accumulated outer wall system has eleven oriented root rays.  The
involution \(A\) fixes nine rays and exchanges exactly one pair.  Because the
rows were normalized by their pairing with a fixed ample point, the exchanged
rows acquire reciprocal positive scales; after including those exact scales,
the wall Gram matrix is invariant.  Hence this is an exact projective Coxeter
diagram automorphism.

The wall system itself has rank 10 and inertia \((9,1,1)\), with pair counts

\[
44\,m_2,\qquad9\,m_3,\qquad1\,m_5,\qquad1\text{ ultraparallel pair}.
\]

Its exact outer cone has six finite vertices, no cusps, and twelve hyperideal
extreme rays.  Under \(A\):

\[
6\text{ finite vertices}\longrightarrow3\text{ orbits},
\qquad
12\text{ hyperideal rays}\longrightarrow6\text{ orbits}.           \tag{15}
\]

No extreme ray is fixed.  In particular, the finite order-two quotient still
has six hyperideal directions.  A finite quotient cannot turn this current
outer cone into a finite-volume polytope.

Therefore the accumulated eleven root walls are not the desired completed
finite-volume eigen-nef chamber.  The two exact decision routes are:

1. further effective root walls cut every escape direction and eventually
   give a compact finite outer cone; or
2. a later infinite-order enhanced return proves that this K3 candidate has
   infinite volume.

Additional finite symmetries can accelerate the first route by enlarging
certified wall orbits, but do not alone decide finiteness of the full group.

The D68/D27 involution and the Pell stream do neither.  The later
\(B_+,B_-\) symmetries do enlarge finite wall orbits; their action on the
97 certified curve facets is audited separately and does not rely on this
withdrawn one-witness traversal.
