## 1. Trace induction really isolates the directed full cycle

Let \(M\) be finite projective of rank \(m\) over the returned order \(A\).
Every returned reflection \(s_i\) is \(A\)-linear.  Therefore any reflection
word is an endomorphism of \(M\), and its projective-module trace lies in
\(A\); after base change it is the ordinary \(k\)-linear trace.

Over \(k\), write

\[
        s_i=I-P_i,\qquad P_i=e_i\alpha_i,\qquad
        \alpha_i(e_j)=a_{ij}.
\]

For positions \(j_1<\cdots<j_r\), rank-one multiplication gives exactly

\[
\operatorname {tr}
 (P_{i_{j_1}}\cdots P_{i_{j_r}})
=a_{i_{j_1}i_{j_2}}\cdots
 a_{i_{j_r}i_{j_1}}.
\]

Expanding a reflection word therefore produces one cyclic product for each
ordered positional subsequence.  The unique full subset has coefficient
\((-1)^\ell\); all other subsets have smaller length.  Induction over all
ordered words, not merely graph cycles, covers repetitions and non-induced
subsequences.  The base \(c(i)=a_{ii}=2\) is integral, so every full cyclic
product lies in \(A\).

The independent executable tests \(1{,}367\) exact reflection words,
including repeated words.  Its displayed normals have six genuinely
fractional individual Cartan entries while every reflection matrix is
integral.  The subset-trace identity and integrality of every full cyclic
product both hold.  Thus the proof does not quietly restore primitive
normal scaling.

This argument needs finite projectivity.  For a nonprojective order-lattice,
the owner theorem correctly demands an explicit acting-order trace and
multiplication certificate instead.

## 2. Chord reduction respects both directions

Orient a simple perimeter cycle \(C\).  A chord \(ij\) cuts it into two
oriented cycles:

- the first follows one perimeter path and closes along \(j\to i\);
- the second follows the complementary path and closes along \(i\to j\).

Their product contains every directed perimeter factor exactly once and the
two chord factors exactly once.  Hence

\[
       c(C_1)c(C_2)=c(C)\,a_{ij}a_{ji}.
\]

The chord is an edge of the nonzero Cartan graph, so the pair factor is
nonzero and division is legitimate in \(k\).  A repeated vertex splits a
closed walk multiplicatively into shorter closed walks.  Induction therefore
reduces the field generated by all nonzero cyclic products to pair products
and chordless simple cycles.  It does not claim equality of the generated
orders.

The independent checker verifies \(1{,}092\) rotated directed chord
decompositions through length \(16\).  Unlike the owner's undirected
edge-exponent control, this test retains the ordered factors
\((i,j)\) and \((j,i)\) separately.

## 3. The \(m+2\) rank bound has an explicit nonzero minor

Assume \(d=[k:\mathbf Q]\ge2\).  At a non-distinguished embedding the
form \(h\) is positive definite, so the principal Gram matrix \(A_C\) on an
induced cycle is positive semidefinite and has rank at most \(m\).
Chordlessness makes it cyclic tridiagonal, and every adjacent entry is
nonzero.

There is a direct rank witness stronger than a sample recurrence.  Number
the vertices \(1,\ldots,\ell\).  Take rows \(2,\ldots,\ell-1\) and columns
\(3,\ldots,\ell\).  The resulting \((\ell-2)\)-square matrix is triangular,
with diagonal

\[
                 A_{2,3},A_{3,4},\ldots,A_{\ell-1,\ell}.
\]

Its determinant is their nonzero product.  Therefore

\[
       \operatorname {rank}A_C\ge\ell-2,\qquad
       \ell-2\le m,\qquad
       \ell\le m+2=n+3.
\]

The independent checker replays this exact minor through length \(30\).
No full facet-Gram rank or facet-count bound is assumed.  When \(d=1\),
the field is already \(\mathbf Q\), so the absent definite conjugate place
causes no gap in field finiteness.

## 4. Sign, trace form, and total positivity

The repaired setup takes a totally negative codifferent scalar \(\alpha\)
and

\[
                    q=\operatorname {Tr}_{k/\mathbf Q}(\alpha h).
\]

Thus the distinguished \((m-1,1)\) form \(h\) becomes the unique
\((1,m-1)\) summand of \(q\), while every positive-definite conjugate
\(h\)-summand becomes negative definite.  Multiplication by a field element
is \(q\)-self-adjoint.  This is precisely the decomposition required by the
dominant spectral-height identity.

At a definite embedding, Cauchy--Schwarz and cancellation of root-length
denominators give

\[
                         |\sigma(c(C))|\le2^\ell.
\]

At the distinguished acute chamber, every perimeter pairing is
nonpositive, so

\[
                         \beta_C=(-1)^\ell c(C)
\]

has nonnegative distinguished value.  With \(U_C=2^\ell\), put

\[
                         \theta_C=\beta_C+U_C.
\]

Every non-distinguished conjugate belongs to \([0,2U_C]\), and the
distinguished one is at least \(U_C\).  A zero conjugate is impossible:
an embedding of a field is injective, so it would force
\(\theta_C=0\), contradicting the distinguished value.  Hence
\(\theta_C\) is totally positive.

If \(\sigma_0(\beta_C)\le U_C\), every conjugate is at most \(2U_C\).  If
\(\sigma_0(\beta_C)>U_C\), then

\[
       0<\sigma(\theta_C)\le2U_C<\sigma_0(\theta_C),
\]

so it is dominant.  The affine change is reversible over \(\mathbf Q\) and
preserves algebraic integrality.  The independent checker tests \(240\)
exact boundary and large-place packets and retains the weak upper inequality
at the Cauchy boundary.

## 5. The one-intersection height step

Let \(X/\mathbf C\) be the returned complex projective K3 with full trace
lattice \(N=\operatorname {NS}(X)\).  Decompose \(H=\sum_\sigma H_\sigma\)
according to the real places.  The non-distinguished values
\(q(H_\sigma,H_\sigma)\) are nonpositive.  For a totally positive dominant
\(\theta\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 H\cdot\tau_\theta(H)-\sigma_0(\theta)H^2
  =\sum_{\sigma\ne\sigma_0}
   \bigl(\sigma(\theta)-\sigma_0(\theta)\bigr)
   q(H_\sigma,H_\sigma)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently the returned bounds

\[
             H^2\ge s_0,\qquad
             H\cdot\tau_{\theta_C}(H)\le C
\]

give \(\sigma_0(\theta_C)\le C/s_0\) for every selected large coordinate.
Dominance controls its remaining conjugates.  Small coordinates already
have the \(2U_C\) envelope.

The theorem assumes this mixed-height bound.  Reflection maximality,
finite volume, and bounded \(H^2\) do not prove it.  The rank-four family
with fixed \(H^2=14\) and
\(H\cdot\tau_t(H)=32a-10\) is the correct sharpness control.
In particular, the height bound is not derived from reflection maximality.

## 6. Generator selection and constants

For a complex K3 full trace carrier,

\[
        d(n+1)=\rho(X)\le20,\qquad
        D=\left\lfloor{20\over n+1}\right\rfloor.
\]

Pair and chordless cycles of length at most \(L=n+3\) generate the field.
After the affine shift, every selected coordinate has all conjugates in

\[
        (0,R_0],\qquad R_0=\max\{2^{L+1},C/s_0\}.
\]

Successively adjoining a coordinate outside the current subfield multiplies
degree by at least two.  Thus at most

\[
                       r=\lfloor\log_2D\rfloor
\]

coordinates are needed.  The independent checker exhausts all multiplicative
degree towers through \(D=64\) and recovers this bound exactly.

There are at most \(D(D-1)/2\) unordered embedding pairs.  Avoiding their
proper equality hyperplanes on the positive grid gives coefficients at most

\[
                       M_D={D(D-1)\over2}+1.
\]

A positive primitive combination therefore has all conjugates in
\((0,R]\), where \(R=rM_DR_0\).  Positivity makes every conjugate difference
at most \(R\), not \(2R\), so

\[
                |\operatorname {Disc}k|
                \le R^{D(D-1)}.
\]

For \(n=2\), the independent calculation gives

\[
 D=6,\quad L=5,\quad U=32,\quad r=2,\quad M_D=16,
\]

and hence exactly

\[
 |\operatorname {Disc}k|
 \le
 \left(32\max\{64,C/s_0\}\right)^{30}.
\]

The monic minimal-polynomial coefficient boxes then prove a finite field
set directly; no number-field discriminant finiteness theorem is imported.
