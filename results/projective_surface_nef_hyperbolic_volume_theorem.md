# TOOT: rational-polyhedral nef cones have finite hyperbolic volume

**Status:** proved project theorem; independently hostile-audited
(2026-08-08).

Independent verification:
[`projective_surface_nef_hyperbolic_volume_hostile_audit.md`](projective_surface_nef_hyperbolic_volume_hostile_audit.md).

## The theorem

Let `X` be a smooth projective surface over an algebraically closed field of
arbitrary characteristic, let

```text
rho = rank N^1(X) >= 3,
n   = rho - 1,
V   = N^1(X)_R,
q(D)=D^2,
```

and choose an ample class `H`.  The component of `q>0` containing `H`, with
the unit hyperboloid metric, is hyperbolic `n`-space.  If `Nef(X)` is a
rational-polyhedral cone, then

```text
P_X = P(Nef(X)) intersect H^n
```

has finite hyperbolic volume.  Moreover,

```text
P_X is compact
  <=> every nonzero extremal nef ray has positive square
  <=> Nef(X) has no isotropic extremal ray.
```

The rationality is useful for algebraic marking but is stronger than the
convex-geometric proof needs: finite polyhedral generation by real nef rays
already implies the volume statement.

This is a **nef-volume theorem**, not by itself a Coxeter theorem.  If the
complete nef facets are additionally proved to have the prescribed Coxeter
dihedral angles, their hyperbolic section is automatically a finite-volume
geometric Coxeter polytope.  If its primitive facet reflections also preserve
`N^1(X)`, it has the corresponding integral arithmetic realization.  A
separate Vinberg edge-endpoint and full parabolic-zero-neighbourhood replay,
including reducible parabolics as whole cusps, is then an independent
certificate of the extracted finite wall list, not the logical source of
finite volume.

## Proof

The Hodge index theorem gives `q` signature `(1,n)`.  Every nef class is a
limit of ample classes, hence

```text
D in Nef(X)  =>  D^2 >= 0.
```

If `D` is nonzero and nef, then `H.D>0`: otherwise `D` lies in `H^perp`, on
which Hodge index is negative definite, contradicting `D^2>=0`.  Consequently
the nef cone is full-dimensional, pointed, and contained in the closure of
the `H`-positive Lorentz cone.

Use the affine Klein chart

```text
A_H = {D in V : H.D=1},
B_H = {D in A_H : D^2>0}.
```

Here `B_H` is the Klein ball and its boundary is the projectivized light
cone.  Write

```text
Nef(X)=cone(v_1,...,v_s)
```

with its extremal generators and normalize
`p_i=v_i/(H.v_i)`.  Then

```text
K = Nef(X) intersect A_H = conv(p_1,...,p_s)
```

is a compact Euclidean polytope contained in the closed Klein ball.  A vertex
`p_i` lies in the open ball exactly when `v_i^2>0`, and on its sphere exactly
when `v_i^2=0`.  Thus `K` has no hyperideal vertices.

Choose a finite pulling triangulation of `K`.  Each resulting hyperbolic
simplex has only finite or ideal vertices.  Near an ideal vertex `p`, write
points of one simplex as `x=(1-t)p+t y`, where `y` lies in its compact
opposite face.  The tangent hyperplane to the strictly convex Klein sphere at
`p` meets the closed ball only at `p`, so compact separation of the opposite
face gives a uniform constant `c>0` with

```text
1-|x|^2 >= c t.
```

The Euclidean shell is `O(t^(n-1) dt)` and the Klein volume density is at most
`O(t^(-(n+1)/2))`; hence the local volume is bounded by

```text
integral_0^epsilon t^((n-3)/2) dt,
```

which converges precisely for `n>1`.  Finitely many simplices give finite
total volume.

Finally, the future Lorentz cone is strictly convex projectively: a
nontrivial convex combination of two distinct future causal rays is timelike.
Hence `K` meets the Klein sphere exactly when one of its extremal rays is
isotropic.  Avoiding the sphere is equivalent to compactness in hyperbolic
space, proving the compactness statement.  The same strict convexity shows
that non-simple ideal vertices cause no problem and that there is no
positive-dimensional face lying in the sphere.

## The sharp low-rank exception

The hypothesis `rho>=3` is the sharp threshold for the unconditional
polyhedral-nef implication.  For
`X=P^1 x P^1`, the Picard rank is two and the nef cone is generated by the
two isotropic fibre classes.  Its projectivization is the full hyperbolic
line between two ideal endpoints, which has infinite length.  Some rank-two
polyhedral nef cones with two timelike endpoints are compact and have finite
length; the example shows that rational polyhedrality alone no longer forces
finite volume in hyperbolic dimension one.

## What becomes algebraic

For a full-nef carrier with `rho>=3`, the volume gate can now be stated in
classical surface language:

```text
rational-polyhedral complete Nef(X)
  => finite hyperbolic volume;

no isotropic extremal nef ray
  <=> compactness.
```

An isotropic extremal ray is always an ideal vertex.  Calling it a fibration
requires an additional semiampleness theorem.  This holds in the complex
K3/Enriques and Mori-dream settings used in the project, but not for an
arbitrary surface merely from nefness.

Finite Cox-ring generation implies the theorem whenever it supplies the
complete rational-polyhedral nef cone (for example on a Mori-dream surface).
The converse implications—finite volume to finite Cox ring, finite
automorphism group, or a particular fibration classification—are
surface-class-specific and are not asserted here.

## Gates that remain indispensable

The theorem does **not** prove any of the following:

- that a proposed finite divisor list is the complete nef wall list;
- effectivity, irreducibility, or extremality of its facet divisors;
- reflection integrality, acuteness, or Coxeter angles;
- finite volume for a weak divisor subsystem whose inequality cone is larger
  than `Nef(X)`;
- finite volume for a Mori cone itself, whose curve rays may have negative
  square; or
- a fibration from an isotropic ray without semiampleness.

It applies to an invariant or eigenspace section only after proving that the
restricted form has signature `(1,k)` with `k>=2`, the section contains a
relative-interior ample class, and the displayed cone equals the complete
intersection with `Nef(X)`.  Hidden ambient or orbit walls remain a terminal
obstruction.  One-dimensional sections retain the infinite-length exception.

Accordingly the reverse-carrier volume obligation has three exact routes:

```text
V_angle : exact Lorentz signature, orientation, irredundancy,
          integral reflections, and Coxeter angles;

V_volume: (a) rho>=3 + complete rational-polyhedral nef equality; or
          (b) a literal Vinberg edge-endpoint and full parabolic-zero-
              neighbourhood certificate; or
          (c) a complete exact projective extreme-ray enumeration proving
              full dimension, pointedness, the chosen time sheet, and that
              every extreme ray is timelike or lightlike—none hyperideal.
```

Route (a) is the sought direct translation from the algebra of the surface to
finite hyperbolic volume.  Once the separate reflection gate identifies the
polytope as a fundamental chamber, this is also its finite covolume statement.

## Consequences for the search program

1. **Full-nef K3 and Enriques chambers.**  Their existing Cox-ring and
   finite-automorphism dictionaries are instances of this general convex-
   Hodge mechanism; the separate literal Vinberg audits remain valuable
   independent checks.
2. **Toric and Mori-dream surfaces.**  Once the full nef cone is extracted,
   finite volume is free.  The real filters are negative-curve completeness,
   integral facet reflections, and Coxeter angles.
3. **Looijenga pairs and bounded rational families.**  Period or Cox-ring
   descriptions can be used to construct complete polyhedral nef strata;
   every successful Coxeter stratum then yields a finite-volume example.
4. **Coble surfaces.**  Full-`NS` finite-automorphism families give concrete
   `H10`, `H11`, `H14`, and `H17` fishing lanes.  All nine classified
   templates now have exact full-`NS` certificates.  Type MI realizes the
   known Fermat `H11/206` chamber as a full nef cone, while Type V supplies a
   project-new literal `H11/36` realization with `6,356+432+0` rays.  Working
   only in the rank-ten Coble--Mukai lattice would miss both the intended
   dimensions and the cusp-lift facets.
5. **Weak carriers.**  No conclusion changes.  A finite list of divisor
   halfspaces may still have spacelike extreme rays and infinite volume until
   full-nef equality, a literal Vinberg certificate, or a complete exact
   no-hyperideal extreme-ray certificate is proved.

This theorem therefore strengthens the inverse-carrier program without
making it tautological: algebraic polyhedrality closes volume only after the
hard geometric cone-completeness gate, while the Coxeter gates remain exact
and discrete.
