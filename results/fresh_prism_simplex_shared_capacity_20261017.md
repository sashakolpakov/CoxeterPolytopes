# Prism bases share one vertex capacity across all dimensions

Let P be a simple compact convex n-polytope. Select its maximal faces that
are combinatorial prisms `Delta1 x Delta(k−1)`, with k at least 2. For k at
least 3, count the two simplex bases; for a square, count all four edges as
bases. Fix a maximal simplex face S of dimension d. Then

\[
 \sum_{k\ge2}\sum_Q k\,m(Q,S,k)
 \ \le\ (d+1)(n-d),
\]

where m counts the bases of Q contained in S. After summing over actual
faces of any specified simplex type, the right side is multiplied by their
number. Thus bases of different prism dimensions share one capacity.

For the repository model, the selected objects are the unique maximal
prisms of the [dotted full-wall-star aggregates](fresh_quad_product_aggregation_20261017.md).
The theorem applies to these actual prism faces. An all-finite full-star
aggregate that groups several overlapping products is not automatically
one object of this theorem; its incidence would need a separate definition.

## Three-dimensional completion

Suppose a simple convex three-polytope has the triangle abc and the squares
abBA and acCA. These two squares share the edge aA. The six named vertices
are distinct. A coincidence with a vertex of abc would make a square and
the triangle share more than one edge. If B=C, the two square facets would
share both aA and AB. Either contradicts the fact that the intersection of
two distinct facets is a face.

The three neighbors of a, b, c and A are already fixed by these faces:
respectively `{b,c,A}`, `{a,c,B}`, `{a,b,C}` and `{a,B,C}`. Removing B and C
therefore separates these four vertices from any additional vertex. By
[Balinski's three-connectivity theorem, printed p.432](https://msp.org/pjm/1961/11-2/pjm-v11-n2-p03-s.pdf),
there is no additional vertex. The missing degrees at B and C force the
edge BC. The remaining facet cycles are the triangle ABC and the square
bcCB. The whole three-polytope is a triangular prism.

## Completion from ports

Fix a vertex v, a simplex face through v, a nonempty subset C of its edge
directions, and an edge direction x outside that simplex. Suppose every
two-face generated by x and one member of C is a quadrilateral. Put
r=|C|. These r+1 directions generate a unique (r+1)-face F, because P is
simple. Write v_i for the neighbors in the directions C, w for the neighbor
in direction x, and w_i for the fourth vertex of the square v,v_i,w_i,w.

For every pair i,j, the three-face generated by x,i,j has a triangular base
v,v_i,v_j and the two stated squares. The preceding lemma makes it a
triangular prism. In particular, w_i and w_j are distinct and joined by an
edge. They lie outside the original simplex: each square intersects that
simplex precisely in the edge v,v_i. Consequently the displayed vertices
form two disjoint complete graphs on r+1 vertices, joined by the matching
v--w and v_i--w_i.

Every displayed vertex already has degree r+1 in this graph. The face F is
simple of dimension r+1, so none can have an edge to another vertex of F.
Connectivity forces the displayed vertices to exhaust F. For r=1 the
conclusion is the assumed square. For larger r, the top triangles from the
three-faces imply that the top clique spans a simplex facet T, by the same
simplex-completion argument as in the disjoint-port theorem. The bottom
simplex is another facet B. For every ridge of B, let H be its other incident
facet of F. At each vertex of that ridge, H contains the one edge leaving B,
so H contains the matched top vertex too. Its vertices are exactly the two
copies of that ridge: containing either missing vertex would force H to
contain all of B or all of T. These r+1 side facets, together with B and T,
already supply all r+1 incident facets at every vertex. There are no other
facets. This explicitly gives the face structure of `Delta1 x Delta_r`,
without assuming that an arbitrary polytope is determined by its graph.

## Disjoint bases inside a fixed coface

Each base F of a selected prism Q lies in a unique maximal simplex S, by
the [disjoint simplex-port theorem](fresh_simplex_disjoint_ports_20261017.md).
The intersection Q with S is exactly F: it is a simplex face containing
the simplex facet F of Q, and cannot be all of Q. This also holds for a
square with any of its edge bases.

At any vertex v of F, Q has exactly one direction x outside S. The join of
S and Q in the face interval at v is the (d+1)-face U generated by the d
directions of S and x. Thus Q determines an immediate coface U of S,
independent of the chosen vertex. There are exactly n−d such cofaces.

Suppose two distinct selected prisms have bases F1,F2 inside S and the
same U, and suppose those bases share v. Their external direction at v is
then the same x. Take the union of their base-direction sets at v. All
cross-pairs with x are quadrilateral faces, so the completion lemma produces
one prism containing both. This contradicts maximality of the selected
prisms. Thus their bases have disjoint vertex sets.

For completeness, the same conclusion holds within one selected prism.
Its two bases are disjoint when k is at least 3. Two edge bases of a square
cannot share a vertex while both lying in S: their pair of directions
would generate both a square and a triangle. Therefore all counted base
vertex sets inside a fixed pair (S,U) are disjoint. A base of a k-prism has
k vertices, and S has d+1 vertices. Summing first over these bases and then
over the n−d choices of U proves the inequality.

In the dotted-star application, maximality follows from full-star
aggregation. Any non-simplex prism subface of a larger prism has the same
complete set of n+2 incident wall normals as the larger prism. The dotted
star's complete positive-definite two-deletion census gives its unique
whole prism, so two distinct such aggregates cannot merge into a larger
prism with that same star.

## Use in the exact model

For a simplex type S with dimension d, add a nonnegative signed transfer
variable with zero vertex cost. Its coefficient on a prism row of dimension
k is k times that row's number of bases of type S; its coefficient on S's
own row is `−(d+1)(n−d)`. The global incidence inequality proves that this
transfer does not increase the total available charge. A dual must obey

\[
 \sum_{k,Q} k\,m(Q,S,k)\alpha_Q
 \le (d+1)(n-d)\alpha_S.
\]

For merged rows, coefficient-wise minima over every source provenance give
valid weaker constraints, just as in the
[separate-dimension base diagnostic](fresh_sqrt5_prism_simplex_base_incidence_20261017.md).
Missing base types retain unknown masses and must not be assigned zero.

This is a written combinatorial proof and a recipe for valid columns.
Construction_audit independently checked all three steps, the square
convention, and the dotted full-star restriction. Exact_hub_counts
independently confirmed the three-dimensional completion argument. No
numerical optimum, new dimension bound, external review or formal proof
assistant validation is asserted by this note.
