# A null separator at a complete nine-corner

Date: 2026-10-18. New written local geometry, with tiny exact supporting
checks. This packet does not exclude a complete K9 corner or improve a
universal dimension bound.

## Precise separator lemma

Let F be a finite-volume right-angled nine-polytope with a finite vertex q
whose bad-triangle corner graph is K9. Its nine outward normals n_i are
orthonormal, q.q=-1, and all 36 walls m_ij=q-n_i-n_j are actual. Put

    h=3q-sum_{i=1}^9 n_i.

Then h is future null. If an additional outward unit facet normal r
satisfies h.r>=0, it is disjoint in hyperbolic space from all 45 initial
walls n_i,m_ij. More precisely,

    r.n_i<=-1,       r.m_ij<=-1       for every i,j.

The condition h.r>=0 includes walls that cut off h and walls passing
through h. Disjoint here permits parallel walls meeting at infinity;
it means that there is no hyperbolic ridge intersection.

### Proof

Write r=a q-sum c_i n_i. Since r is not incident at q, a>0.
The normal gap and acuteness give

    each c_i is zero or >=1,
    c_i+c_j<=a,
    a-c_i-c_j is zero or >=1,
    sum c_i^2=a^2+1.

Also h.r>=0 means sum c_i>=3a. First a>=2: otherwise there can be at
most one positive c_i, and the inequalities c_i<=a would contradict the
unit-normal equation.

Let m be the number of positive coefficients. If m<=7, Cauchy gives
9a^2 <=m(a^2+1), hence a^2<=m/(9-m)<=7/2<4, a contradiction. If m=8,
the same inequality gives a^2<=8, so a<3. Any two positive coefficients
have sum at least two. Consequently their nonnegative gap
a-c_i-c_j is less than one, so is zero. All eight positive coefficients
are a/2, and the norm equation gives 2a^2=a^2+1, again contradicting
a>=2. Thus all nine c_i>=1, which proves r.n_i<=-1.

Suppose r were orthogonal to one m_ij, so c_i+c_j=a. The other seven
coefficients sum to at least 2a. Cauchy separately on the pair and its
complement gives

    a^2+1 >= a^2/2+4a^2/7=(15/14)a^2,

so a^2<=14. Let b=min(c_i,c_j)<2 and write the larger coefficient as
a-b. For any of the other seven coefficients c_k, acuteness gives
c_k<=b, while the gap b-c_k is zero or at least one. Since c_k>=1 and
b<2, it must be zero. The nine coefficients are therefore one a-b and
eight b. Their sum condition gives a<=7b/2; their norm equation gives

    1=9b^2-2ab >=2b^2>=2,

a contradiction. Hence r is not orthogonal to any m_ij; the normal gap
makes all these products at most -1. This proves the lemma.

## The finite-volume dichotomy and its limit

Finite volume forces either a wall with h.r>0, which cuts off h, or an
actual cusp at h. Indeed, if every wall product were strictly negative,
an open neighborhood of h in the ideal sphere would remain inside all
halfspaces, giving infinite volume. If there is no positive product,
h belongs to the ideal boundary of F and is an ideal vertex. Its cusp
has sixteen walls, all with h.r=0. Thus every cutoff facet, or every
facet through this cusp, has no hyperbolic ridge with the initial 45
walls. Any finite-volume completion needs a system of intervening walls.

This is a separation condition, not a contradiction. The explicit unit
normal

    r=sqrt(35)q-2sum n_i

has h.r=18-3sqrt(35)>0, products -2 with n_i, and products
4-sqrt(35)<-1 with m_ij. It obeys every initial normal gap and cuts off h.
The lemma alone therefore cannot prohibit a cutoff wall. The unresolved
step is compatibility of the extra walls required to connect its facet
to a finite-volume right-angled completion.

## The fixed horosphere and its finite root configuration

The invariant h is unchanged by each actual P3 opposition. Since h is
null and h.q=-3, an entire finite opposition component lies on one
horosphere. Project h-perp onto the Euclidean quotient h-perp/Rh.
For a triple S, the displacement q_S-q=q-sum_S n_i projects to

    beta_S=(1/3)sum_i n_i-sum_{i in S}n_i.

These 84 vectors have square two and sum zero. Together with their
negatives and the 72 vectors n_i-n_j they form a 240-vector,
eight-dimensional, equal-length reflection configuration. The checker
verifies closure under all 57,600 pairwise reflections with integers after
scaling coordinates by three. This is an explicit model of the usual E8
root configuration; its formulas and closure are supplied independently
of that name.

The old frame permutations and every formal P3 reflection preserve this
finite configuration in the Euclidean quotient. Hence actual opposition
displacements in a component lie in the lattice generated by it. At a
complete K9 corner all 84 triple neighbors are actual and their Euclidean
average is the corner itself. Their directions span eight dimensions, so
such a corner is strictly inside the convex hull of its actual neighbors.
In particular every Euclidean convex-hull extreme vertex of a finite
component is incomplete. This refines the earlier maximum principle but
does not give a uniform fraction of incomplete corners.

## Application to the surviving multipartite pattern

The LP's nonempty pattern has six singleton parts and three doubled parts.
Its eight nine-cliques select the singleton directions and one direction
from each doubled part. Each determines an intrinsic nine-face with a K9
corner, so the separator lemma applies independently in every one of these
nine-faces. The previous propagation obstruction also forces each such
nine-face to encounter a noncanonical five-face with a complete corner.

These statements do not yet compare the eight completions or force their
separating walls to be distinct. That overlap/attachment question is the
clearest remaining geometric obstruction in this lane. It is stronger and
more specific than treating the nine-clique merely as a graph count, but
it is presently unresolved. No optimizer sweep or Lean work is warranted
for this unproved overlap step.
