# Higher-dimensional Coxeter-polytope lineage ledger

Last updated: 2026-08-09.

## Purpose and claim levels

This ledger separates six assertions which must never again be collapsed into
the word "new":

1. **Construction:** the archived inequalities define a genuine hyperbolic
   Coxeter polytope.
2. **Volume:** the complete polytope is compact, finite-volume noncompact, or
   infinite-volume, as decided by the literal Vinberg endpoint/parabolic gate
   and, where available, exact cone enumeration.
3. **Lattice:** the intrinsic integral Lorentzian lattice has or has not
   appeared before.
4. **Chamber:** the weighted primitive-mirror Gram matrix has or has not
   appeared before, up to facet permutation.
5. **Reflection group / commensurability:** equality, finite-index relation,
   or commensurability has actually been proved.  Matching dimension or facet
   count is not enough.
6. **Algebraic lineage:** the particular extraction from a divisor-wall stock
   may be new even when the abstract Coxeter chamber is old.

"Apparently new" below always means absence from a stated bounded comparison,
not a worldwide priority theorem.

There is also no priority claim for the **folding mechanism itself**.  If a
group of automorphisms preserves the simple generators of a Coxeter system,
its fixed subgroup is again a Coxeter group with canonical generators; see
Geck--Iancu, [*Coxeter groups and automorphisms*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5428).
What is being tested here is more specific: an explicit complete divisor-wall
stock, the saturated fixed divisor lattice, irredundancy of the restricted
facets, the literal finite-volume criterion, the resulting weighted chamber,
and its integral/full-reflection and algebro-geometric lineage.

## The dimensions were already populated

None of the present `H10` through `H17` catches is
the first finite-volume Coxeter polytope in its dimension.

There is a stronger historical obstruction to any such claim.  Allcock's
2006 theorem [*Infinitely many hyperbolic Coxeter groups through dimension
19*](https://doi.org/10.2140/gt.2006.10.737) constructs infinitely many
isometry classes of finite-volume Coxeter polytopes in every `H^n` with
`n <= 19`.  In particular, the `H10`, `H11`, and `H13` catches below enter
dimensions that already contain infinite populations.  Failure to find their literal
normalized Gram matrices in a pinned corpus can support only a bounded
diagram-lineage statement, never first-in-dimension or worldwide priority.

## Compact `H6` control: the known `P_{6,10}` double

The known compact arithmetic ten-facet chamber `P_{6,10}` is now frozen as an
exact control. It is not a new abstract example: Burcroff's Figure 5 draws it
and attributes it to Bugaenko. The pinned CoxIter index-two corpus proves a
more useful lineage statement: `P_{6,10}` is the double of Bugaenko's compact
nine-facet `H6` chamber across one even wall, hence its reflection group has
index two in the Bugaenko group. Its exact unit Gram has inertia `(6,1,3)`,
`f=(31,93,125,95,42,10,1)`, and `chi=-67/288000`.

After rescaling two normals, the same chamber has a crystallographic
`Z[phi]` Gram with eight norm-two and two norm-four roots. This is a live
mixed-root eigen/folded K3-carrier target, but the deleted parent wall remains
a potential hidden divisor cut and must be eliminated by a complete root
audit.

The uniqueness and completed `d+4` classification claimed in
arXiv:2608.02894v1 are not imported: its reduction assumes every facet of a
compact Coxeter polytope is Coxeter, contrary to Burcroff's stated face
criterion and the explicit `[5,3,5]` tetrahedron facet counterexample. The
exact existence/compactness and the classification claim are kept separate in
the [`P_{6,10}` lineage and audit](p6_10_bugaenko_double_lineage_and_classification_audit.md).
