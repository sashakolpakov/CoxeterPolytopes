# Bounded chordless cycles reduce the K3 field gate to one-intersection heights

**Status (2026-08-11): proved conditional finite-field theorem.**  The
pair-generation restriction in the first
[dominant-height theorem](k3_dominant_correspondence_height_field_bound.md)
can be removed.  In field rank \(m=n+1\), the ground field is generated by
pair cycles and chordless Cartan cycles of length at most

\[
                              m+2=n+3.
\tag{0.1}
\]

The bound comes from a positive-definite conjugate Gram matrix: the kernel
of a cyclic tridiagonal matrix injects into two coordinates.  After a
canonical sign and integer shift, every such cyclic coordinate is totally
positive.  It is either uniformly small at every place or dominant, in
which case one mixed surface intersection

\[
                         H\cdot\tau_\theta(H)
\tag{0.2}
\]

bounds it.  Thus bounded mixed degrees for the finitely many
field-generating large cycles give an explicit finite field set.

This is an internal Gram-graph/Hodge argument.  It does not use a
classification of reflective lattices.  Its unresolved premise is that the
exact POLYTOPE-to-SURFACE functor must bound (0.2) for a canonical positive
class.

## 1. Cyclic Cartan coordinates

Let \(M\) be a finite projective rank-\(m\) module over a returned order
\(A\) in a totally real field \(k\).  Let \(h\) have Lorentzian signature
\((m-1,1)\) at the
distinguished embedding \(\sigma_0\) and be positive definite at every
other embedding.  Choose a totally negative codifferent scalar \(\alpha\)
and let the integral trace form be

\[
                         q(x,y)=\operatorname {Tr}_{k/\mathbf Q}
                         \bigl(\alpha h(x,y)\bigr).
\tag{1.0}
\]

Thus \(q\) has exactly one positive direction: its distinguished summand
has signature \((1,m-1)\), while every other real-place summand is negative
definite.  Multiplication by a field element is \(q\)-self-adjoint.  Suppose
facet normals \(e_i\in M\) have positive \(h\)-norm and
their reflections preserve \(M\).  Over the fraction field write

\[
             a_{ij}={2h(e_i,e_j)\over h(e_i,e_i)}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

For nonprimitive displayed normals, preservation of \(M\) need not put an
individual \(a_{ij}\) in \(A\).  The cyclic products are nevertheless
integral.

### Lemma 1.1 (trace induction gives cyclic integrality)

Every ordered cyclic Cartan product (repeated indices allowed)

\[
 c(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)
 =a_{i_1i_2}a_{i_2i_3}\cdots a_{i_\ell i_1}
\tag{1.2}
\]

belongs to \(A\).

### Proof

Over \(k\), write

\[
 s_i=I-P_i,\qquad P_i=e_i\alpha_i,\qquad
 \alpha_i(e_j)=a_{ij},\qquad\alpha_i(e_i)=2.
\]

For \(J=\{j_1<\cdots<j_r\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\ell\}\), rank-one
calculus gives

\[
 \operatorname {tr}_k
 (P_{i_{j_1}}\cdots P_{i_{j_r}})
 =c(i_{j_1},\ldots,i_{j_r}).
\]

Consequently

\[
 \operatorname {tr}_k(s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_\ell})
 =m+\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\ell\}}
 (-1)^{|J|}c(i_{j_1},\ldots,i_{j_r}).
\tag{1.3}
\]

The left side belongs to \(A\), because the product is an endomorphism of
the finite projective returned order-module, whose module trace lies in
\(A\).  The induction is over ordered words, not induced graph cycles, so
repeated and non-induced proper subsequences are covered.  Its base is
\(c(i)=a_{ii}=2\); at length two it gives the pair product
\(a_{ij}a_{ji}\).  Inducting on \(\ell\), every proper-subsequence term
belongs to \(A\), so the unique length-\(\ell\) term belongs to \(A\) as
well.  \(\square\)

For a closed walk
\(C=(i_1,i_2,\ldots,i_\ell,i_1)\) in the nonzero Cartan graph, put

\[
                   c(C)=c(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)\in A.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Assume, as part of the exact returned arithmetic host, that \(k\) is the
field generated by these cyclic products.  This is the usual ground-field
packet, but here it is a stated carrier premise.  Lemma 1.1 obtains
integrality from the finite projective returned order-module and its
integral reflections, not from primitive normal scaling or an abstract
diagram.  For a nonprojective order-lattice, cyclic trace integrality in the
actual acting order and preservation by multiplication must instead be
certified explicitly.

Let \(G\) be the graph whose edges are the nonzero \(h(e_i,e_j)\).
Nonzeroness is independent of the embedding because every field embedding
is injective.

### Lemma 1.2 (cycle reduction)

The field generated by all closed-walk products (1.4) is generated by:

1. the length-two products \(a_{ij}a_{ji}\); and
2. products attached to chordless simple cycles of \(G\).

### Proof

A closed walk with a repeated vertex splits into shorter closed walks.
It therefore suffices to consider simple cycles.  If a simple cycle \(C\)
has a chord \(ij\), the chord cuts it into two shorter cycles \(C_1,C_2\),
and direct cancellation gives

\[
              c(C_1)c(C_2)
              =c(C)\,(a_{ij}a_{ji}).
\tag{1.5}
\]

The pair product is nonzero, so
\(c(C)=c(C_1)c(C_2)/(a_{ij}a_{ji})\).  Induction on length reduces to
pair products and chordless cycles.  \(\square\)

No facet-count bound is used in this reduction.

## 2. Rank bounds the chordless cycle length

Assume \(d=[k:\mathbf Q]\ge2\), so a non-distinguished embedding exists.
For \(d=1\) the field is already \(\mathbf Q\) and no cycle-length argument
is needed for field finiteness.

### Theorem 2.1 (the \(m+2\) cycle theorem)

Every chordless cycle in \(G\) has length

\[
                              \ell\le m+2.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

Choose any non-distinguished embedding \(\sigma\).  The Gram matrix of the
\(\ell\) normals on a chordless cycle is positive semidefinite and has rank
at most \(m\).  Because the cycle is induced, this principal matrix has
nonzero entries only on the diagonal and the two cyclic adjacent
off-diagonals.  Every adjacent entry is nonzero.

If \(Ax=0\), the row recurrence determines \(x_3\) from \(x_1,x_2\), then
\(x_4\), and so on.  More invariantly, the map

\[
                         \ker A\longrightarrow\mathbf R^2,
                         \qquad x\longmapsto(x_1,x_2)
\tag{2.2}
\]

is injective: if \(x_1=x_2=0\), the second row and the nonzero \(2,3\)
entry force \(x_3=0\), and induction forces \(x=0\).  Hence

\[
             \dim\ker A\le2,\qquad
             \operatorname {rank}A\ge\ell-2.
\]

Since \(\operatorname {rank}A\le m\), equation (2.1) follows.
\(\square\)

This is the nontrivial polytope property encoded by the trace lattice:
although the chamber may have many facets, no field-generating induced
cycle can be longer than \(n+3\).

## 3. A canonical totally positive affine coordinate

At a positive-definite embedding, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\sigma(c(C))|
 &=2^\ell
   \prod_{r=1}^{\ell}
   {|\sigma h(e_{i_r},e_{i_{r+1}})|\over
    \sqrt{\sigma h(e_{i_r},e_{i_r})
          \sigma h(e_{i_{r+1}},e_{i_{r+1}})}}\\
 &\le2^\ell.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The square-root factors cancel around the cycle.  At the distinguished
place, the outward normals of an acute Coxeter chamber satisfy
\(h(e_i,e_j)\le0\).  Therefore

\[
                    \beta_C:=(-1)^\ell c(C)
\tag{3.2}
\]

has \(\sigma_0(\beta_C)\ge0\).  Put

\[
             U_C=2^\ell,\qquad
             \theta_C=\beta_C+U_C.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The affine change is integral and reversible, so

\[
                         \mathbf Q(\theta_C)=\mathbf Q(c(C)).
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (small-or-dominant dichotomy)

The element \(\theta_C\) is totally positive.  Moreover exactly the
following bound is available:

1. if \(\sigma_0(\beta_C)\le U_C\), then every conjugate of
   \(\theta_C\) lies in \((0,2U_C]\);
2. if \(\sigma_0(\beta_C)>U_C\), then \(\theta_C\) is dominant:
   \[
       0<\sigma(\theta_C)\le2U_C
       <\sigma_0(\theta_C)
       \quad(\sigma\ne\sigma_0).
   \tag{3.5}
   \]

### Proof

Equation (3.1) puts every non-distinguished
\(\sigma(\beta_C)\) in \([-U_C,U_C]\), so
\(\sigma(\theta_C)\in[0,2U_C]\).  It cannot be zero: if an embedding sent
\(\theta_C\) to zero, injectivity would give \(\theta_C=0\) in \(k\), while
its distinguished value is at least \(U_C>0\).  Thus it is strictly
positive.  At the distinguished place, (3.2) gives
\(\sigma_0(\theta_C)\ge U_C>0\).  The two alternatives now follow directly.
\(\square\)

The shift in (3.3) is important.  A raw longer cyclic product can have
mixed signs at the definite places and is not an admissible input to the
dominant-height inequality.

## 4. The full cyclic field theorem

Let \(X/\mathbf C\) be an exact full-Picard **complex projective** K3 trace
carrier:

\[
                  \operatorname {NS}(X)=N,\qquad
                  \operatorname {rank}N=d(n+1).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let \(H\in N\) be integral, big and nef, with \(H^2\ge s_0>0\).  For every
returned \(\theta_C\), multiplication is an integral self-adjoint operator
\(\tau_{\theta_C}\).  If \(\theta_C\) is in the large case of Lemma 3.1,
the one-positive-place identity from the dominant-height theorem gives

\[
 H\cdot\tau_{\theta_C}(H)
       \ge\sigma_0(\theta_C)H^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (bounded cyclic correspondence field gate)

Fix \(n\ge2\) and uniform constants \(s_0,C>0\).  Consider a class of exact
carriers for which every member returns:

1. the full integral Cartan order packet of Section 1;
2. a class \(H\in N=\operatorname {NS}(X)\) with \(H^2\ge s_0\); and
3. a constant \(C\) such that, for every large affine coordinate
   \(\theta_C\) in a field-generating selection of pair/chordless cycles,
   \[
                  H\cdot\tau_{\theta_C}(H)\le C.
   \tag{4.3}
   \]

Then the ground fields occurring throughout the class form an explicitly
finite set.

More precisely, put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D&=\left\lfloor{20\over n+1}\right\rfloor,\\
 L&=n+3,\qquad
 U=2^L,\\
 r&=\lfloor\log_2D\rfloor,\qquad
 M_D={D(D-1)\over2}+1,\\
 R_0&=\max\{2U,C/s_0\},\qquad
 R=rM_DR_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

For \(D\ge2\),

\[
             \boxed{
             |\operatorname {Disc}k|
             \le R^{D(D-1)}.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Proof

The K3 Picard bound and (4.1) give \(d\le D\).  Lemma 1.2 and Theorem 2.1
show that pair and chordless cycles of length at most \(L\) generate \(k\).
Replace each selected cycle coordinate by its affine coordinate (3.3).

For a small coordinate, Lemma 3.1 bounds every conjugate by
\(2U_C\le2U\).  For a large coordinate, (4.2)--(4.3) bound its
distinguished conjugate by \(C/s_0\), and dominance bounds all the other
conjugates by the same number.  Thus every selected affine coordinate has
all conjugates in \((0,R_0]\).

Select generators successively outside the current subfield.  Each strict
extension has degree at least two, so at most
\(r=\lfloor\log_2D\rfloor\) are needed.  The positive-grid primitive-element
lemma supplies coefficients at most \(M_D\) whose linear combination
generates \(k\).  Every conjugate of that primitive element lies in
\((0,R]\).

The squared Vandermonde has exponent \(d(d-1)\le D(D-1)\), and \(R\ge1\),
giving (4.5).  Equivalently, the elementary symmetric functions bound every
coefficient of its monic minimal polynomial:

\[
               |c_{d-j}|\le {d\choose j}R^j.
\tag{4.6}
\]

These finite integer boxes prove a finite field set directly.  \(\square\)

### The \(n=2\) constant

For hyperbolic dimension two,

\[
 D=6,\qquad L=5,\qquad U=32,\qquad r=2,\qquad M_D=16.
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 |\operatorname {Disc}k|
 \le
 \left(32\max\{64,C/s_0\}\right)^{30}.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

On a K3, \(H^2\) is positive even, so \(s_0=2\) is always available once
the carrier returns an integral big-and-nef \(H\).

## 5. Project-native interpretation

If a correspondence
\(Z_{\theta_C}\in CH^2(X\times X)_{\mathbf Q}\) acts as
\(\tau_{\theta_C}\), the projection formula identifies the only new height:

\[
 H\cdot\tau_{\theta_C}(H)
 =
 \deg\bigl(
 Z_{\theta_C}\cdot p_1^*H\cdot p_2^*H
 \bigr).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus Theorem 4.1 needs neither a bounded full Gram matrix nor a bounded
matrix for the field action.  It needs:

- one canonical positive divisor in the full trace lattice; and
- one bounded scalar bidegree for each selected large signed-cycle
  correspondence.

For \(n\ge2\) at most two such selected coordinates are needed on a K3.
Small coordinates cost no correspondence-height hypothesis at all.

The rank-four exact maximal family shows the height premise is substantive:
its fixed square-\(14\) polarization has

\[
                       H\cdot\tau_t(H)=32a-10.
\]

Any higher-dimensional coupled infinite-field family must likewise make at
least one large field-generating affine-cycle bidegree escape, or fail to
return a positive class in the full trace lattice.

## 6. Scope and remaining theorem

The result removes two apparent obstructions:

1. the number of facets need not be bounded, because chordless generator
   cycles have length at most \(n+3\); and
2. the field need not be generated by pair products, because signed longer
   cycles are made totally positive by (3.3).

It still assumes:

- exact full-Picard trace return;
- a finite projective returned order-module with integral reflections, or
  an explicit cyclic-integrality and multiplication-action certificate;
- a returned big-and-nef \(H\in N\); and
- the uniform mixed-degree bound (4.3).

Reflection maximality has not yet been proved to imply the last two items.
Consequently this is a conditional AG field theorem, not a replacement for
raw essential surjectivity.  The sharpened global target is now the scalar
implication

\[
\begin{split}
 &\text{maximal exact Coxeter eigen-nef carrier}\\
 &\qquad\Longrightarrow
 \text{canonical }H\in N\text{ and bounded degrees (5.1)}
 \text{ for the large chordless generator cycles}.
\end{split}
\tag{6.1}
\]
