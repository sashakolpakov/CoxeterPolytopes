# Limited primary-source check of the dimension-29 scope

**Finding.** The primary sources checked explicitly state Vinberg's dimension-29 theorem for compact hyperbolic Coxeter polytopes. This check did not locate an explicit published proof of the same conclusion for the larger class with both finite vertices and simple ideal vertices. That absence is not a novelty determination.

Vinberg's original §6.1 begins with a bounded fundamental polytope, and the finite-vertex weighting argument follows under that hypothesis; §6.5–11 is its quadrilateral payment proof. See printed pp.80–91 of [the original 1984 paper](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=mmo&option_lang=rus&paperid=444&what=fullt). The present project's mixed finite-volume argument therefore needs its separate affine-corner analysis and compact-face transfer; the original statement alone does not supply it.

Felikson and Tumarkin's [*On simple ideal hyperbolic Coxeter polytopes*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0502413), introduction, explicitly distinguishes the compact dimension-29 theorem from the general finite-volume estimate. Their own Theorem 1 concerns polytopes whose vertices are **all ideal** and gives dimension at most eight. It is not a theorem for arbitrary mixtures of finite and simple ideal vertices. Section 1 does supply the primary geometric facts used here: a simple ideal vertex has a connected affine diagram, and every affine subdiagram lies in the diagram of an ideal vertex.

A potentially misleading phrase occurs at the start of §9 of Burcroff's [*Near Classification of Compact Hyperbolic Coxeter d-Polytopes with d+4 Facets and Related Dimension Bounds*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.03437): its introductory description refers to simple Coxeter polytopes. The same paragraph states the compact scope, Proposition 9.2 explicitly assumes a compact Coxeter polytope, and the subsequent star diagrams have elliptic vertex diagrams. That phrase does not independently verify a mixed finite-volume extension. The [published article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0195669824000428) likewise describes the inherited theorem as compact.

This is a targeted scope check, completed on 2026-10-17, rather than an exhaustive literature or priority search. The appropriate wording for the project's result is an internally checked extension with its hypotheses stated explicitly; whether the extension was previously known remains unestablished by this check. No current-best or novelty claim is justified from it.
