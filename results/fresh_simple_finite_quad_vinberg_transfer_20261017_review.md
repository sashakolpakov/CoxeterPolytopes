# Independent review of the finite-corner quadrilateral transfer

**PASS — mathematical/source review.** No remaining compactness or dimension-scope gap was identified in the [transfer proof](fresh_simple_finite_quad_vinberg_transfer_20261017.md). The reviewed claim is restricted to an all-simple, finite-sided, finite-volume hyperbolic Coxeter polytope of dimension \(n\ge18\), and a quadrilateral face whose four vertices are finite. At least one corner receives positive weight under Vinberg's original finite-vertex rule.

The published finite diagram classifications, displayed configurations, and determinant calculations remain explicit inputs. This review checks their transfer hypotheses; it is not a new independent census or a rederivation of every published table.

The following are the substantive checks, rather than just agreement of manifests.

1. **Actual face geometry, §6.5, pp.82–83.** The four corner diagrams are positive definite. A positive-semidefinite opposite-pair block would produce a nonzero feasible future null vector, hence an ideal point of the compact quadrilateral; positive definiteness would instead give an ordinary intersection of its opposite sides. Both are impossible. The full star has corank one, and its unique dependence has nonzero alternating black coefficients. These statements concern this actual face, not arbitrary root subsets.
2. **Preliminary lemmas, §6.6, pp.83–85.** The private diagrams \(U,V\) have at most \(n-1\) nodes; their indefinite complements have at most \(n\). Their necessary affine exclusions therefore follow from the separately proved simple-cusp exclusion. Lemma 7 supplies connectedness before the high-bond finite diagrams are bounded by order four. The corresponding all-rank classification statement is about finite labels at least five; it does not accidentally include the infinite bond of affine \(A_1\).
3. **The first three branches, §6.7–9, pp.85–89.** The exceptional carriers have bounds 13, 13, and 16. The long classical branch has a separate justification: any actual diagram orthogonal to an indefinite diagram is positive definite. The strict center prevents a positive singular-kernel combination from vanishing. This handles arbitrarily large end pieces without excluding an order-\(n\) affine block merely by assertion. The neighbor enlargements \(O(U),O(V)\) have order at most ten, since every relevant black root has at most three white neighbors in a finite corner.
4. **The fourth branch, §6.10, pp.89–90.** Its exceptional carrier has at most 17 nodes. The determinant polynomial really has a nonzero quadratic coefficient: the unique star dependence is nonzero on each private \(U\) root, so deleting the high-bond endpoints leaves independent normals whose span still contains an indefinite block. Their Gram determinant is strictly negative. This closes the potential degeneracy in applying the published algebraic angle argument.
5. **The cyclic-core branch, §6.11, pp.90–91.** The affine tests on the two induced cyclic Lannér cores and their connections have at most ten nodes. This is not a claim that the core union alone accounts for every extra shared white branch. The later complete shared paths are controlled by the already positive-definite \(T_{ij}\), Lemma 8, and the finite end-piece Lemmas 10–11. The final contradictions use ellipticity of \(U,V\), rather than an unproved global prohibition of affine diagrams.

All finite carriers just listed have order below \(n\). The argument thus allows full order-\(n\) affine diagrams elsewhere in the ambient polytope. It does not assume an induced face is itself a Coxeter polytope, and it does not treat the compact ambient theorem as already applying to noncompact polytopes.

This is a completed narrow internal source/proof review. It does not duplicate any numerical replay, establish the mixed-corner branches, or by itself give an ambient dimension bound. No novelty or current-best claim is made.
