## Exact radical obstruction at height zero

Modulo three, the section Gram has rank 22.  In the displayed basis its
radical is generated by the last coordinate, whose raw vector is

```text
(-23,1^23) = (1^24) mod 3.
```

Thus all 24 raw coordinates of a radical vector are congruent modulo three.
The radical has no nonzero vector of norm at most six:

- norm three has raw square sum 24.  Residue zero is impossible; residue
  `+/-1` forces all coordinates to have one common sign, contradicting raw
  coordinate sum zero;
- norms four and five have raw square sums 32 and 40.  In a nonzero residue
  class, common odd parity permits only the least representative `+/-1` at
  these bounds (the next odd representative raises the square sum to at
  least 48), contradicting coordinate sum zero; common even parity already
  has square sum at least 96.  Residue zero is impossible modulo nine;
- norm six has raw square sum 48.  In residue `1`, write every coordinate as
  `1+3k`.  The zero-sum and square-sum equations give `sum(k)=-8` and
  `sum(k^2)=8`, hence eight coordinates `-2` and sixteen coordinates `1`.
  Their parities differ, impossible in the odd-Leech construction.  Residue
  `-1` is the negative case.

The ambient coefficient-Gram census has one formal type at each of norms
three and six, namely a constant vector of that norm.  The radical
obstruction removes both; norms one and two have no ambient type.  Therefore
the height-zero wall count is exactly zero.
