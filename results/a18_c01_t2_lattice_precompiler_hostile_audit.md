## Complete crystallographic scaling enumeration

The four diagonal-minus-two nodes are `W1,W5,W9,W14`.  The ten
diagonal-minus-four nodes form two connected five-node `m3` components.
Every short node meets each long component once by an `m4` edge.

Along an `m3` edge the two normal scales must agree.  Along an `m4` edge,
crystallographic integrality permits the long/short scale ratio `1` or `1/2`.
After fixing one common scale, exhaustive propagation gives exactly 18 scale
rays.  For each ray the verifier independently chooses the smallest common
squared factor making the generated Gram integral and even; it then checks
both Cartan entries on every edge.

Interpreting a folded facet as either

```text

```

leaves exactly two of the 18 rays:

```text

```

No other scale ray has every primitive normal of square `-2` or `-4`.

## Independent obstruction gates

Three finite gates leave one candidate.

First, all four long classes in the reversed root lattice—`W1,W5,W9,W14`—
are literal radical rows in the marked long-class discriminant pairing.
The property persists down its stable-overlattice lane, so the fully reversed
normal assignment fails the required long-class sheet-glue gate.

Second, every repeated long discriminant class found anywhere in the two
lanes occurs on a folded pair of pairing zero.  To keep the two sheet-pairings
acute, matching anti-invariant square-minus-four representatives would have
to be orthogonal.  Their half-difference would then be a `(-2)`-root in the
anti-lattice.  Choosing a nonorthogonal pair instead makes one of the two
sheet-pairings negative.  Hence repetition is incompatible with the required
rootless acute fold-back.

Third, the displayed root lattice has discriminant length ten.  It cannot be
matched primitively to a rank-eight anti-invariant K3 complement.  Exactly
seven stable index-two extensions lower the length to eight.  Six have the
repeated-class obstruction; the seventh has none.
