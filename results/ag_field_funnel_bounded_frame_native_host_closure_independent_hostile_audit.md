## 2. Precise statement audited

Fix a totally real number field (k) and (m\geq3).  In the standard
Vinberg arithmetic model, let (W(P)) be the side-reflection group of a
finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chamber on an admissible (m)-dimensional
quadratic (k)-space.  If (W(P)) is reflection-maximal, then only finitely
many unmarked normalized chambers occur for fixed ((k,m)).

Consequently, if the possible ground fields form a finite set and an
independent universal dimension ceiling is available, only finitely many
reflection-maximal chambers occur uniformly in dimension.

Two standard conventions used by the proof should be kept visible:

1. “Vinberg arithmetic over (k)” includes the (k)-rational reflection
   representation, so (W(P)\subset O(q)(k)).  This makes each translate of
   an (mathcal O_k)-lattice in the core/sum construction another
   (mathcal O_k)-lattice in the same (k)-space.
2. Before applying an integral-lattice formulation of the bounded-frame
   lemma, multiply (q) by one common element of (k^\times) to clear the
   pairing ideal of the chosen projective lattice.  This changes no
   reflection, normalized Cartan product, group, or chamber.

These are not extra finiteness hypotheses.  The first is part of the stated
arithmetic model, and the second is an invariant preparatory normalization.

## 3. Invariant full host: the normal-core translate sum

Let (L') be a full projective (mathcal O_k)-lattice such that

\[
 H=W\cap O^+(q,L')
\]

has finite index in (W=W(P)).  Let

\[
 \Gamma=\bigcap_{w\in W}wHw^{-1}
\]

be the finite-index normal core, and choose representatives
(g_1,\ldots,g_t) for (W/\Gamma).  Set

\[
                         L=\sum_{a=1}^t g_aL'.          \tag{3.1}
\]

This step survives all four natural attacks.

* Each (g_a) is (k)-linear in the Vinberg model, so (g_aL') is an
  (mathcal O_k)-module in (V(k)).
* The sum is finite and torsion-free; over the Dedekind domain
  (mathcal O_k), it is projective.
* If (w g_a=g_b\gamma) with (gamma\in\Gamma), then
  (w g_aL'=g_b\gamma L'=g_bL').  Hence (wL=L).
* No freeness or principal-ideal assumption is used.

The independent certificate includes the concrete model

\[
 L'=2\mathbf Ze_1+\mathbf Ze_2,qquad
 \tau L'=\mathbf Ze_1+2\mathbf Ze_2,qquad
 L'+\tau L'=\mathbf Z^2,
\]

where (	au) swaps the coordinates.  It verifies the translate-sum
stability directly.

Define (R(L)) to be the group generated by all hyperbolic (q)-reflections
preserving (L).  Then

\[
                    W\subseteq R(L)\subseteq O^+(q,L). \tag{3.2}
\]

After clearing the pairing ideal, (O^+(q,L)) is an arithmetic lattice.
Since (W) already has finite covolume, the covolume/index formula makes
both inclusions in (3.2) finite-index where appropriate.  In particular,
(R(L)) is a discrete finite-covolume arithmetic reflection group.  This
uses no reflective-lattice finiteness theorem.

## 5. Projective trace gives exactly the required cyclic integrality

Put

\[
 \alpha_i(x)={2q(x,\delta_i)\over q(\delta_i,\delta_i)},
 \qquad T_i=1-s_i=\delta_i\otimes\alpha_i,
 \qquad a_{ij}=\alpha_i(\delta_j).
\]

Although (delta_i) need not be an integral generator of its normal line,
(s_iL=L) implies

\[
                         T_i\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_k}(L).
\]

For a directed cycle (C=(i_1,\ldots,i_r,i_1)), right-to-left composition
gives

\[
 T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_r}
 =a_{i_1i_2}\cdots a_{i_{r-1}i_r}
   \delta_{i_1}\otimes\alpha_{i_r}.
\]

The trace on a finite projective module is defined after localization and
glues over (mathcal O_k).  Therefore

\[
 \operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal O_k}(T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_r})
 =a_{i_1i_2}\cdots a_{i_ri_1}=:b_C\in\mathcal O_k.     \tag{5.1}
\]

This includes the essential length-two backtrack:

\[
              b_{iji}=a_{ij}a_{ji}
              ={4q(\delta_i,\delta_j)^2\over
                q(\delta_i,\delta_i)q(\delta_j,\delta_j)}=c_{ij}.
                                                               \tag{5.2}
\]

Thus the proof never asserts the generally false statement
(a_{ij}\in\mathcal O_k).  It uses precisely the scale-invariant closed
Cartan products.

For a simple cycle of length at least three, symmetry of (q) gives

\[
 b_C=b_{C^{-1}},\qquad b_C^2=\prod_{e\in C}c_e.         \tag{5.3}
\]

The independent certificate checks (5.1)--(5.3) on the symmetric
Lorentzian matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 2&-1&-1&0\\
 -1&2&-1&-1\\
 -1&-1&2&-1\\
 0&-1&-1&2
 \end{pmatrix},
\]

whose signature is ((3,1)).  This closes the only material gap in the
owner certificate's sample coverage.

## 6. Minkowski finiteness of complete cycle packets

At the distinguished real place, the frame lemma and (5.3) give

\[
                         |b_C|<62^{|C|}.                \tag{6.1}
\]

At every nondistinguished real embedding, admissibility makes the form
definite.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives (0\leq\sigma(c_{ij})\leq4), hence

\[
                         |\sigma(b_C)|\leq2^{|C|}.      \tag{6.2}
\]

For fixed (k) and (m), every cycle has length at most (m).  The
Minkowski image of (mathcal O_k) is a lattice in
(mathbf R^{[k:\mathbf Q]}), so the box defined by (6.1)--(6.2) contains

This step is qualitative but logically complete; neither a unit bound nor a

## 7. A cycle packet recovers the reflection representation

Suppose two Cartan matrices (A=(a_{ij})) and (A'=(a'_{ij})) have the same
connected zero graph and all the same simple directed-cycle products,
including every two-cycle.  Choose a spanning tree, put
(lambda_{\rm root}=1), and propagate

\[
                         {\lambda_j\over\lambda_i}
                         ={a'_{ij}\over a_{ij}}          \tag{7.1}
\]

along oriented tree edges.  Equality of the two-cycle checks the reverse
orientation.  For a non-tree edge, that edge plus the unique tree path is a
simple fundamental cycle; equality of its product proves (7.1) on the
non-tree edge.  Hence

\[
                         a'_{ij}={\lambda_j\over\lambda_i}a_{ij}
                                                               \tag{7.2}
\]

for every entry.  This is exactly the diagonal change of root basis, so the
ordered reflection representations are (k)-conjugate.  The independent
certificate uses a spanning tree omitting several non-tree edges and checks
that (7.2) recovers the entire matrix, not only the tree.

The representation is absolutely irreducible.  Over an algebraic closure,
if (0\ne U\subset V) is invariant and (0\ne u\in U), nondegeneracy and
the spanning normals give an (i) with
(q(u,\delta_i)\ne0).  Thus (T_i u) puts (delta_i) in (U).
Connectedness of (G) then propagates all (delta_j) into (U), so
(U=V).

Burnside over the algebraic closure and scalar-extension dimension
comparison give

\[
                         k[s_1,\ldots,s_m]=\operatorname{End}_k(V).   \tag{7.3}
\]

The independent rank-four sample generates all (16) dimensions of
(M_4(\mathbf Q)).  Finally, any two invariant nondegenerate symmetric
forms differ by an element of the commutant, which is (k); hence the form
is unique up to (k^\times)-scale.  Scaling the form changes neither the
reflections nor the normalized chamber.

## 8. Full-order conductor sandwich counts every projective host

Fix one representation type admitting an invariant full projective lattice
(L_0).  Set

\[
 B=\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_k}(L_0),\qquad
 \Lambda=\mathcal O_k[s_1,\ldots,s_m]\subset B.         \tag{8.1}
\]

Equation (7.3) makes (Lambda) a full order in
(operatorname{End}_k(V)).  Since (B/\Lambda) is torsion, a nonzero
integral ideal (mathfrak c) satisfies

\[
                         \mathfrak cB\subseteq\Lambda.  \tag{8.2}
\]

For any full projective (Lambda)-stable lattice (L), put (M=BL).
Then

\[
                         \mathfrak cM\subseteq L\subseteq M.         \tag{8.3}
\]

The classification of (B)-stable (M) is exact.  At every prime
(mathfrak p), choose a basis of ((L_0)_{mathfrak p}); then

\[
 B_{mathfrak p}=M_m(\mathcal O_{k,mathfrak p}).
\]

Diagonal matrix units project coordinates and off-diagonal matrix units
transfer them.  Consequently every (B_{mathfrak p})-stable full lattice
has the form

\[
 M_{mathfrak p}=I_{mathfrak p}^{,m}
 =\mathfrak p^{e_{mathfrak p}}(L_0)_{mathfrak p}.
\]

Only finitely many (e_{mathfrak p}) are nonzero, so globally

\[
                              M=\mathfrak aL_0          \tag{8.4}
\]

for a fractional ideal (mathfrak a).  Up to (k^\times)-homothety,
(mathfrak a) ranges over the finite ideal class group.  For each chosen
(M), (8.3) embeds (L/\mathfrak cM) into the finite module
(M/\mathfrak cM), which has only finitely many submodules.  Projectivity
and (Lambda)-stability merely filter this finite set.

This argument handles nonfree modules, ideal classes, arbitrary
denominators, and units.  It does not appeal to Jordan--Zassenhaus or to
Nikulin's divisor classification.

and verifies

## 10. Arithmetic-only maximality is sufficient

For every original chamber the construction gives

\[
                              W(P)\subseteq R(L).        \tag{10.1}
\]

If (W(P)) is absolutely reflection-maximal, equality is immediate.  If it
is maximal only among arithmetic reflection groups, equality still follows:
(R(L)) is a finite-index overgroup of the arithmetic lattice (W(P)), so
it is commensurable with (W(P)), hence arithmetic, and it is generated by
reflections.

In fact the two maximality notions agree in this finite-volume arithmetic
setting.  Any discrete reflection overgroup (W'\supset W(P)) has finite
index by the covolume formula.  It is therefore commensurable with the
arithmetic group (W(P)), hence arithmetic.  No overgroup can escape the
arithmetic-only maximality hypothesis.

Finally, the complete locally finite mirror arrangement of (R(L)) has one
chamber orbit.  A generic compact path between chamber interiors crosses
only finitely many mirrors; reflecting successively across the crossed
walls gives a finite gallery from one chamber to the other.  Thus each full
native host contributes at most one unmarked normalized chamber.

Combining Sections 6--10 proves fixed-((k,m)) finiteness without importing
the fixed-data theorem being reconstructed.

## 11. The dimension quantifier is noncircular

The native-host theorem is proved for every fixed (m\geq3).  Uniformity in
dimension is obtained only afterward from the independent geometric facts:

* compact Coxeter chambers do not occur in dimensions (n\geq30); and
* finite-volume noncompact reflection chambers do not occur for (n>995).

Hence

\[
                     2\leq n\leq995,qquad 3\leq m=n+1\leq996.
\]

There are exactly

\[
                              996-3+1=994
\]

possible ranks.  The Vinberg--Prokhorov dimension input is universal
hyperbolic Coxeter geometry: it assumes neither a finite arithmetic field
list nor fixed-data reflective-lattice finiteness.  Conversely, the order
argument never assumes a facet bound or an arithmetic dimension theorem.
The quantifier closure is therefore noncircular.

## 13. Scope of the conclusion

The native-host implication is global:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{finite ground-field set}
 +\text{ universal geometric dimension ceiling}
 +\text{ constant-62 frame}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{finitely many reflection-maximal chambers}.}
\]

When composed only with the already-audited (n\geq6) arrangement field
funnel, it proves the owner report's stated (n\geq6) corollary.  It does
not by itself manufacture the missing low-dimensional field set, and the
owner report explicitly says so.  Once the separate (n=2,3,4,5) field
branches are supplied, the same native-host theorem gives the genuinely
global result.

The route is accurately described as **AG-mediated, not AG-only**.  AG
arrangement integral points supply the essential finite-field funnel;
universal Coxeter geometry supplies the frame and dimension inputs; the
reconstructed order argument supplies finite native hosts; and reflection
maximality collapses each host to one chamber orbit.  Direct `AG-DEG` and a
direct AG facet-degree proof remain open and are not smuggled into this
theorem.
