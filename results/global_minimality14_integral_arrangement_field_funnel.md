# A global minimality-14 integral-arrangement field funnel

**Status (2026-08-12).**  This note proves, without using Nikulin's
fixed-field/fixed-rank reflective-lattice finiteness theorem, Vinberg's
arithmetic dimension cutoff, or Agol--Belolipetsky--Storm--Whyte, that the
ground fields of finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chambers in
\(\mathbb H^n\), \(n\geq 6\), form a finite set.  Reflection
maximality is not needed.

The proof is native to the exact quadric-arrangement carrier.  Its invariant
coordinates are integral Cartan cycles.  Away from the rational, Lanner,
triangle, and quadrilateral branches, a minimal-aperture facet produces a
four-wall arrangement chart of one of five transition types.  Every such
chart contains a three-wall determinant curve of one of two forms.  On these
curves, conjugate definiteness gives intervals of integer Chebyshev capacity
strictly below one.  Fekete's theorem then makes the integral points finite.

This is a genuine global field theorem, not global Coxeter finiteness.  It
does not bound the dimension, total number of facets, dotted heights outside
the selected star, native lattice, or number of maximal chambers.  Those are
recorded precisely in Section 8.

## 1. Statement

Let \(P\subset\mathbb H^n\) be a finite-sided finite-volume arithmetic
Coxeter chamber of simplest type, with minimal Vinberg cycle field \(k(P)\).

> **Theorem A (dimension-at-least-six finite field set).**  There is a
> finite set \(\mathscr K_{\geq6}\) of totally real number fields such that
> \[
>                 k(P)\in\mathscr K_{\geq6}
> \]
> for every such \(P\) with \(n\geq6\).
>
> The set is obtained from:
>
> 1. \(\mathbf Q\);
> 2. the finite Lanner packet of rank at least four;
> 3. the fields of arithmetic Coxeter triangles, finite by the exact
>    genus-zero real-DM curve theorem in dimension two; and
> 4. the integral points on the five finite-type transition arrangement
>    charts of minimality \(14\); and
> 5. the fields of arithmetic Coxeter quadrilaterals, finite by the same
>    exact genus-zero real-DM curve theorem.

No maximality hypothesis occurs in the proof.  The transition part is
effective in principle and has the explicit label cutoff below.  The present
certificate invokes the Long--Maclachlan--Reid finiteness theorem for the
polygon branches but does not implement an enumeration of their finite
lists; consequently effectivity of the displayed union is not asserted here.
In particular, Theorem A is
strictly upstream of the desired finiteness of reflection-maximal chambers.

The only small Coxeter classification input is the elementary Lanner lemma:
a connected Coxeter diagram all of whose proper principal diagrams are
elliptic has at most five vertices, and the rank-at-least-four diagrams form
a finite explicit list.  This is a finite spherical-diagram calculation, not
a finiteness theorem for arithmetic reflection groups.  The seven literal
four-node supports and five schematic transition families are independently
reconstructed in
[`nikulin_four_node_transition_families_exact_audit.md`](nikulin_four_node_transition_families_exact_audit.md).

## 2. Exact arrangement coordinates and field inheritance

Scale every facet normal to square \(-2\), and write

\[
                  A=(a_{ij}),\qquad a_{ii}=-2,
                  \qquad a_{ij}\geq0\quad(i\ne j).          \tag{2.1}
\]

Changing the scale of individual normals changes the directed Cartan
entries, but every closed product

\[
       b_C=a_{i_1i_2}a_{i_2i_3}\cdots a_{i_ri_1}             \tag{2.2}
\]

is invariant.  These are the regular invariant monomials for the diagonal
torus acting on the marked quadric-hyperplane arrangement.  Vinberg
arithmeticity says exactly that the \(b_C\) are algebraic integers, the
selected ambient form is Lorentzian, and every nonselected real conjugate
of that form is negative definite.  A Gram matrix of a dependent collection
at a definite place is only negative semidefinite; this distinction is why
the singular clause of Lemma 2.1 includes linear independence.

We use the following already independently proved lemma.

> **Lemma 2.1 (full-field packet, exact scope).**  The field generated by all
> cyclic products of a principal packet \(A_I\) is the full minimal field
> \(k(P)\) in either of the following cases:
>
> 1. \(A_I\) has a positive eigenvalue in the square-\((-2)\) convention; or
> 2. the packet vectors are linearly independent and \(A_I\) is parabolic.

Indeed, a proper cycle subfield would admit another real extension of its
selected embedding to \(k(P)\).  Fixed two-cycles and longer cycles make the
conjugate packet a vertex-sign switch of \(A_I\), so it retains its inertia.
In case 1 this contradicts negative semidefiniteness of a Gram matrix in the
definite conjugate ambient space.  In case 2, linear independence survives
base change, so the conjugate Gram must be negative definite, again a
contradiction.  The argument includes every hyperbolic packet and the
independent affine packets used below.

This lemma is what lets a bounded arrangement chart remember the **whole**
ground field rather than merely a visible subfield.  A dependent singular
negative-semidefinite packet is deliberately excluded: at a definite
conjugate place its oversized Gram can remain singular, so inertia alone
would not prove field inheritance.

## 3. Two contracting three-wall curves

### 3.1 The tail curve

Consider

\[
 A_d(u)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -2&u&0\\
 u&-2&2\cos(\pi/d)\\
 0&2\cos(\pi/d)&-2
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad d\geq3,\quad 2<u<14.                 \tag{3.1}
\]

Put

\[
 \alpha=u^2,\qquad
 \ell_d=4\sin^2(\pi/d),\qquad
 F_d=\mathbf Q(\ell_d),\qquad D=[F_d:\mathbf Q]
       ={\varphi(d)\over2}.                               \tag{3.2}
\]

Both \(\alpha\) and \(\ell_d\) are integral cycle coordinates.  The
packet contains the positive dotted pair, so Lemma 2.1 gives
\[
                         k=\mathbf Q(\alpha,\ell_d)=F_d(\alpha).
\]
In particular \(F_d\subset k\), which justifies the relative degree and
norm calculations below.  The
determinant is, up to a positive constant, \(\alpha-\ell_d\).  Therefore
conjugate definiteness is precisely

\[
 0<\tau(\alpha)<\tau(\ell_d)\quad(\tau\ne\tau_0),
 \qquad 4<\tau_0(\alpha)<196.                              \tag{3.3}
\]

The interval capacities satisfy

\[
 \prod_{\sigma:F_d\hookrightarrow\mathbf R}
 {\sigma(\ell_d)\over4}
 ={\gamma(d)\over4^D}<1,                                  \tag{3.4}
\]

where

\[
 \gamma(d)=|N_{F_d/\mathbf Q}(\ell_d)|=
 \begin{cases}
 p,&d=p^a\text{ is a prime power},\\
 1,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                               \tag{3.5}
\]

Thus, for fixed \(d\), Fekete's integer-Chebyshev theorem over \(F_d\)
bounds \([F_d(\alpha):F_d]\).  If its auxiliary polynomial vanishes at
\(\alpha\), its degree gives the bound; otherwise the nonzero integral norm
does.  All conjugates in (3.3) are bounded, so the coefficients of the
relative minimal polynomial lie in a bounded Minkowski box.  Consequently
only finitely many \(\alpha\), extensions, and cycle fields occur for fixed
\(d\).

The label \(d\) is itself uniformly bounded.  This can be proved without a
transition table.  Let \(e=[k:F_d]\) and set

\[
                         \beta=\alpha(\alpha-\ell_d).       \tag{3.6}
\]

It is a nonzero algebraic integer.  At a nonselected embedding,

\[
             |\tau(\beta)|\leq{\tau(\ell_d)^2\over4};       \tag{3.7}
\]

at the selected embedding it is less than \(196^2\).  Taking the norm gives

\[
 1<196^2{4\over\ell_{d,0}^2}
       \left({\gamma(d)^2\over4^D}\right)^e.               \tag{3.8}
\]

The elementary estimates

\[
 \ell_{d,0}\geq{16\over d^2},\qquad
 \gamma(d)\leq d,\qquad
 \varphi(d)\geq\sqrt{d/2}                                  \tag{3.9}
\]

turn (3.8), for \(d\geq20000\), into

\[
 1<601d^4{d^2\over2^{\varphi(d)}}<1.                        \tag{3.10}
\]

For completeness, the totient inequality follows multiplicatively from

\[
 {\varphi(d)^2\over d}
   =\prod_{p^a\parallel d}p^{a-2}(p-1)^2\geq{1\over2}.      \tag{3.11}
\]

At \(d=20000\), the final strict comparison in (3.10) is the exact integer
inequality

\[
                   2^{100}>601\cdot20000^6.                \tag{3.12}
\]

The logarithm of \(2^{\sqrt{d/2}}/d^6\) is increasing for \(d\geq20000\)
(use \(\log2>2/3\)).  Hence

\[
                              d<20000.                      \tag{3.13}
\]

This cutoff is intentionally crude; it replaces all published numerical
transition extrema by one exact norm argument.

### 3.2 The fork curve

The second packet is

\[
 A_{s,k}(u)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -2&u&2\cos(\pi/s)\\
 u&-2&2\cos(\pi/k)\\
 2\cos(\pi/s)&2\cos(\pi/k)&-2
 \end{pmatrix},                                           \tag{3.14}
\]

where the transition grammar only requires the fork when
\(s,k\in\{3,4,5\}\).  Over the fixed real cyclotomic coefficient field,
the determinant inequality confines every nongeometric value of \(u\) to
an interval of length

\[
                         4|\sin(\pi/s)\sin(\pi/k)|<4.       \tag{3.15}
\]

Put \(E=\mathbf Q(2\cos(\pi/s),2\cos(\pi/k))\); only finitely many
such base fields occur.  The product of the interval capacities over all
embeddings of \(E\) is therefore strictly less than one.  Since \(u^2\)
is an integral two-cycle, \(u\) is an algebraic integer.  If \(L=E(u)\)
and \(K\) is the cycle field, then \([L:K]\) is uniformly bounded:
adjoining the three Gram entries to their squares and triangle product leaves
only the finite vertex-sign ambiguity (the crude bound \(8\) suffices).
Thus only a bounded number of embeddings of \(L\) restrict to the selected
embedding of \(K\); assign those the exceptional interval \([-14,14]\).
Every other embedding lies in its determinant interval (3.15).  Fekete's
theorem with this bounded number of exceptional embeddings bounds
\([L:E]\).  All conjugates of \(u\) are now bounded, so the usual
bounded-degree/bounded-conjugate argument leaves finitely many \(u\), and
hence finitely many cycle fields.

> **Proposition 3.1 (finite transition integral locus).**  The ground fields
> of all four-node V-arithmetic finite-edge packets of minimality \(14\) form
> a finite effective set.

To see this without hiding a classification step, use the exact seven-support
audit.  In every one of its five schematic families, the dotted endpoint
pair together with one auxiliary node is either a tail (3.1), with
\(d<20000\), or a fork (3.14) with both fork labels in \(\{3,4,5\}\).
The selected three-node packet contains the indefinite dotted pair, so Lemma
2.1 says it has the full field.  Sections 3.1--3.2 make its integral locus
finite.  This proves the proposition.  No reflective-lattice or
reflection-group finiteness statement is used.

## 4. The global packet-existence argument

This section reproduces the global step, because a local five-family audit
alone would not show that an arbitrary chamber contains one of those
families.

If \(P\) is noncompact, a cusp makes the defining \(k\)-form isotropic.
Every nonidentity real conjugate is definite, so the usual isotropic-vector
argument forces \(k=\mathbf Q\).  We may therefore assume that \(P\) is
compact.  Its finite spherical vertex links are Coxeter simplices, hence
\(P\) is simple.

Choose an interior observation point and a facet \(E\) of minimal visual
aperture.  If two closed facets meet \(E\), the elementary cone-plane formula
and

\[
 {\sin((a+b)/2)\over\sin(a/2)}<2
 \quad(0\leq b\leq a<\pi)                                  \tag{4.1}
\]

give normalized pairing \(<14\).  Thus every edge contained in \(E\)
has a finite edge chamber of minimality \(14\).

Let \(\mathscr S\) be the union of \(\mathbf Q\), the finite rank-at-least-
four Lanner fields, the arithmetic-triangle fields, and the fields of the
first four transition families.  Suppose for contradiction that
\(k(P)\notin\mathscr S\).

For an edge \(r=v_1v_2\), let \(e_1,e_2\) be the two endpoint-exclusive
walls.  Then

\[
                              e_1e_2>2.                    \tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed, equality makes the two-node packet an independent parabolic packet,
and Lemma 2.1 gives \(k=\mathbf Q\).  If \(e_1e_2<2\), choose an
inclusion-minimal principal packet with a positive direction in the
hyperbolic edge component.  It inherits the full field by Lemma 2.1.  If all
its proper packets are elliptic, it is a triangle or a rank-at-least-four
Lanner packet.  If a proper packet is parabolic, the minimal packet is
quasi-Lanner and defines a finite-volume noncompact arithmetic Coxeter
simplex over the inherited field.  Its cusp makes the defining form
isotropic; definiteness at every nonidentity real place then gives
\(k=\mathbf Q\).  Each alternative contradicts the choice of \(k\).

Fix a vertex \(v\in E\), and let \(C(v)\) be its spherical \(n\)-node
Coxeter graph.  If a connected component of \(C(v)\) had at least three
nodes, choose a node \(e_1\ne E\) in that component and a connected
three-node subgraph containing it.  The other endpoint of the edge defined
by \(C(v)\setminus\{e_1\}\) supplies \(e_2\).  Adjoining \(e_2\) to the
three-node subgraph gives a four-node finite-edge packet with (4.2), of
minimality \(14\).  The independently exhaustive support calculation puts it
in one of the five transition families.  It cannot be family 5: in that
support, after deleting either endpoint-exclusive node, the component
containing the other endpoint-exclusive node has only two vertices.  Our
chosen component has three.  Lemma 2.1 gives the ambient field, contradicting
\(k\notin\mathscr S\).

Consequently every component of every \(C(v)\), \(v\in E\), has at most two
nodes.  The component containing \(E\) is included: if it had size at least
three, any other node in that same component could be used as \(e_1\).

This is the required global existence statement.  It used only compact
simplicity, the local aperture inequality, full-field inheritance, and the
finite four-node support enumeration.

## 5. The global forcing counts

### 5.1 The dimension-ten matching count

Put \(m=n-1=\dim E\), and let \(a\) be the average number of vertices of a
two-face of the simple polytope \(E\).  The \(h\)-vector identity and
Dehn--Sommerville symmetry give the strict upper bounds

\[
 a<\begin{cases}
 4+4/(n-2),&n\text{ even},\\
 4+4/(n-3),&n\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}                                               \tag{5.1}
\]

At a vertex of \(E\), the graph \(C(v)\) is a matching plus isolated nodes.
After deleting \(E\), at most \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) pairs lie in one
component.  These are exactly the nonright two-dimensional corners of
\(E\) at that vertex.

A hyperbolic triangle has at least two nonright angles and a hyperbolic
quadrilateral at least one.  Double-counting nonright corners over all
two-faces, and using

\[
             af_2(E)=\binom m2 f_0(E),                     \tag{5.2}
\]

gives

\[
 a\left(\binom m2+\lfloor m/2\rfloor\right)
             \geq5\binom m2.                              \tag{5.3}
\]

For even \(n=10\), the lower bound is \(4.5\), while (5.1) is strict
\(<4.5\).  For odd \(n=11\) the same is true.  The lower bound increases
and the upper bound decreases thereafter.  Hence the assumption
\(k(P)\notin\mathscr S\) is impossible for every \(n\geq10\).

### 5.2 Triangles and quadrilaterals force the result from dimension six

The same \(h\)-vector bound gives

\[
                              a<5\qquad(n\geq6).             \tag{5.4}
\]

Therefore the narrow facet \(E\) has a two-face with either three or four
vertices.

Suppose first that it has a triangular two-face.  If the ambient field were
outside the Lanner and arithmetic-triangle sets, (4.2) would hold for every
edge.  For an edge of this triangle, however, the two endpoint-exclusive
walls meet at the third vertex, so their normalized pairing is at most two.
This contradicts (4.2).  Hence the ambient field is already in one of those
two finite sets.

Now let \(M_4\subset E\) be a quadrilateral two-face.  Let \(Q\) be the set
of the \(n-2\) walls containing its plane, and let
\(\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_4\) be the four additional walls cutting its
cyclic edges.  Assume the field is outside the Lanner, triangle, and all five
transition sets.

The quadrilateral has a nonright angle, say between \(\delta_1\) and
\(\delta_2\).  At that vertex these two nodes lie in one connected component
of the spherical vertex diagram.  The component-size argument of Section 4
makes them a two-node component, so both are orthogonal to every wall in
\(Q\).

If \(\delta_3\) were nonorthogonal to some \(q\in Q\), inspect the four nodes
\(\delta_1,\delta_3;\delta_2,q\), with the semicolon separating the two
endpoint-exclusive nodes from the auxiliary nodes.  The opposite walls
\(\delta_1,\delta_3\) are precisely the endpoint-exclusive walls of the
one-dimensional edge cut out by \(Q\cup\{\delta_2\}\), so they form the
dotted endpoint pair by (4.2);
\(\delta_2\) meets \(\delta_1\), and may meet or be orthogonal to
\(\delta_3\); while \(q\) is orthogonal to \(\delta_1,\delta_2\) and, by
assumption, meets \(\delta_3\).  Thus the four-node support is transition
family 4 when \(\delta_2\delta_3>0\), and family 5 when
\(\delta_2\delta_3=0\).  The seven-support audit makes this dichotomy
exhaustive.  Minimality \(14\) holds because all four walls meet \(E\), and
the dotted pair gives a positive eigenvalue.  Lemma 2.1 therefore gives the
full ambient field, a contradiction.  Hence \(\delta_3\perp Q\), and the
same cyclic argument gives \(\delta_4\perp Q\).

Choose unnormalized \(k\)-rational representatives of the wall-normal
lines; these exist because each reflection is \(k\)-rational and its
minus-one eigenspace is a \(k\)-line.  Let
\(U=(\operatorname {span}_k Q)^\perp\).  Its distinguished real form has
signature \((1,2)\), and \(\mathbb P(U)\) is the intrinsic hyperbolic plane
of \(M_4\).  In general an intrinsic face normal is the orthogonal projection
of \(\delta_i\) to \(U\).  Since every \(\delta_i\perp Q\), that projection
is literally \(\delta_i\).  Therefore all four intrinsic norms and pairings,
and hence every Coxeter angle and distance, are unchanged.  The restricted
ambient reflections preserve \(U\).  Choose a full \(W\)-stable
\(\mathcal O_k\)-lattice \(L\): starting with a reference arithmetic
lattice, take the normal core of its finite-index intersection with \(W\)
and sum its finitely many \(W\)-translates.  Then they also preserve
\(L\cap U\).  This intersection has rank three and is projective, since it
is a full torsion-free module in the \(k\)-space \(U\) over the Dedekind
domain \(\mathcal O_k\).

The four-root Gram has a positive eigenvalue and rank three, so Lemma 2.1
says its cycle field is exactly the ambient \(k\).  Its cycles are integral;
at every nonselected real place the restricted form is definite; and the
quadrilateral has finite area.  Vinberg's criterion therefore makes its
reflection group an arithmetic Fuchsian group over that same field.  It is
an exact arithmetic Coxeter quadrilateral of minimality \(14\), not merely a
quasi-arithmetic projected face.

The two-dimensional orbifold-curve theorem makes the fields of these
quadrilaterals finite by the bounded-index argument of Section 6.  This
proves Theorem A for every \(n\geq6\).

## 6. Why the polygon branches are genuinely AG-mediated

An arithmetic Coxeter triangle is sent by the exact two-dimensional functor
to its compactified real algebraic DM orbifold curve with cusp divisor,
descent involution, and chosen mirrored half.  The return functor recovers
the triangle.  The genus-zero arithmetic-curve theorem leaves finitely many
maximal arithmetic Fuchsian overgroups.

Every arithmetic triangle group lies in a maximal arithmetic reflection
overgroup: an ascending chain must stop because chamber area drops by the
integer subgroup index while finite-area hyperbolic orbifolds have a positive
universal area lower bound.  Every resulting overgroup is finite over the
original lattice, hence commensurable and arithmetic.  For each of the
finitely many maximal groups, the triangle has area less than \(\pi\), so its
subgroup index is bounded by \(\pi\) divided by the fixed maximal-chamber
area.  A finitely generated group has only finitely many subgroups of bounded
index.  Thus the arithmetic triangle fields form a finite set without
importing Takeuchi's triangle list.

Exactly the same argument applies to arithmetic Coxeter quadrilaterals.  Their
areas are less than \(2\pi\), so their indices in each fixed maximal
reflection group are bounded by \(2\pi\) divided by its chamber area.  Hence
there are only finitely many such quadrilateral reflection groups; already
commensurability shows that their ground fields lie in the finite maximal
field set.  This is the precise terminal input used in Section 5.2.

For the transition branch, the AG object is the four-hyperplane chart of the
exact quadric-arrangement quotient.  Equations (3.3) and (3.14)--(3.15) are
determinantal arrangement-moduli equations in integral cyclic coordinates,
not extra labels attached after the fact.  Fekete supplies the terminal
arithmetic finiteness of integral points on those exact finite-type charts.
Accordingly Theorem A is arrangement-mediated arithmetic, not pure
BAB/KSBA.  No general algebraic-geometric boundedness or classification
theorem supplies the terminal finiteness: that work is done by Fekete and,
for the polygon branches, arithmetic orbifold-curve theory.

## 7. Low dimensions

The face-average proof deliberately stops at \(n=6\).  A separate exact
codimension-one/codimension-two descent shows that compact dimensions four
and five reduce to dimension three or two, or to the finite transition and
small packets; see
[`low_dimensional_minimality14_field_descent_to_h3.md`](low_dimensional_minimality14_field_descent_to_h3.md).
Thus compact dimension three is the sole remaining ground-field obstruction.

The obstruction is exact.  A finite edge graph can have a two-node
hyperbolic component consisting only of the dotted endpoint pair, orthogonal
to its spherical blocks.  Its only primitive coordinate then has

\[
 \alpha_0>4,\qquad 0<\alpha^\sigma<4,                       \tag{7.1}
\]

and \([0,4]\) has integer Chebyshev capacity one.  Cyclotomic values show
that no degree bound follows from this interval alone.  A bounded basis size
does not change the capacity obstruction.

Dimension two is separately closed by the orbifold-curve theorem.  In
dimension three no automatic arithmetic Coxeter two-face or genus-zero
Fuchsian section exists; the compact maximal tetrahedron \(H_6\) is the
explicit control.  Thus extending Theorem A to dimension three requires a
new determinant coupling or a different bounded carrier.  It cannot be
obtained by silently applying the rank-three tail/fork argument to a
disconnected edge packet.

## 8. Why finite fields still do not imply chamber finiteness

Even if one appends a general geometric upper bound for the dimension,
Theorem A leaves three independent quantities uncontrolled.

1. **Only the narrow star has a finite alphabet.**  Once \(k\) and \(n\)
   are fixed, integrality and the \(<14\) box make the normalized pair cycles
   in the chosen star finite.  More explicitly, let \(T\) be the resulting
   finite set of two-cycles and let
   \(A=\{\sqrt t:t\in T\}\subset[0,14)\) be the selected normalized-pairing
   alphabet.  If \(v_1,\ldots,v_N\) are the distinct square-\((-2)\)
   normalized star roots in an \(m\)-dimensional space, then

   \[
    p_i(x)=\prod_{a\in A}{\langle x,v_i\rangle-a\over-2-a}
   \]

   has \(p_i(v_j)=\delta_{ij}\).  Hence these degree-\(|A|\) polynomials are
   linearly independent and

   \[
                   N\leq\binom{m+|A|}{|A|}.               \tag{8.2}
   \]

   Thus finite fields plus a dimension ceiling really do bound the **local
   narrow star**.  Facets not meeting the chosen facet are not in this
   packet, and a simple polytope can have bounded local facet degree but
   arbitrarily many facets globally.
2. **Dotted height can escape away from the star.**  The Farey polygon
   sequence over \(\mathbf Q\) has finite volume and unbounded remote dotted
   cycles, facets, stable-pair volume, and bad primes.  It is nonmaximal, so
   it shows exactly what a still-missing maximality-to-height theorem must
   prohibit.
3. **The native lattice is not determined by the normalized local Gram.**
   Relative root norms, prime-ideal valuations, overlattices, and glue remain.
   Proving that only finitely many such reflective hosts occur is precisely
   the fixed-data reflective-lattice theorem that this note does not import.

Therefore neither

\[
 \text{finite fields}+\text{bounded dimension}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{bounded facets or finite chambers}                  \tag{8.1}
\]

nor its maximal version has been proved here.  The exact remaining global
statement is still `MAX-ARR`: reflection maximality must force bounded global
arrangement complexity and a finite integral Gram/height locus.

The log-Chern/BAB alternative does not supply that implication.  The exact
Lambert pairs

\[
 \left(\mathbf P^2,{1\over2}\sum_{i=1}^4H_i+{1\over4}Q\right)
\]

are \(1/2\)-lc, fixed-volume log Fano, and uniformly K-stable, while their
Grams move and their absolutely maximal locus is cofinite.  See
[`log_chern_wonderful_root_stack_maximal_arrangement_no_go.md`](log_chern_wonderful_root_stack_maximal_arrangement_no_go.md).

## 9. Executable arithmetic checks

The companion certificate verifies:

- \(\varphi(d)^2\geq d/2\) on a large exact regression range and records the
  multiplicative proof;
- the exact comparison \(2^{100}>601\cdot20000^6\);
- monotonicity beyond \(20000\) using \(\log2>2/3\);
- the cyclotomic norm/capacity values for representative prime-power and
  non-prime-power labels; and
- all seven four-node supports have a tail or a bounded-label fork, using
  the frozen transition-family archive; and
- the strict \(h\)-vector average forces a triangle or quadrilateral from
  dimension six onward, while the stronger matching contradiction starts in
  dimension ten.

The certificate does not machine-prove Fekete's theorem, the
minimal-aperture lemma, or the geometric/lattice restriction of the
quadrilateral face in Section 5.2.  Those remain explicit prose-level
dependencies of the theorem rather than consequences of a passing test.

## 10. Dependencies and nondependencies

Used:

- exact invariant theory of Cartan cycles and Vinberg integrality;
- the elementary minimal-aperture \(<14\) inequality;
- the independently reconstructed seven-support/five-family calculation;
- the Lanner finite spherical-diagram lemma;
- Fekete's integer-Chebyshev theorem over totally real fields; and
- the exact genus-zero real-DM curve finiteness theorem for the triangle and
  quadrilateral branches.

Not used:

- Nikulin's fixed-field/fixed-rank reflective-lattice finiteness;
- Nikulin's published transition constant \(N(14)\leq25\);
- Vinberg's arithmetic dimension cutoff;
- ABSW finiteness;
- BAB, KSBA, or K-moduli as a finiteness theorem for the Gram image; or
- reflection maximality.
