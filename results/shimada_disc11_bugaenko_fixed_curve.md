# The Bugaenko fixed curve and a Klein-four stabilizer on the discriminant-11 K3

**Status.**  Exact algebro-geometric and lattice-theoretic theorem for the
known enhanced involution \(A\).  It determines its complete centralizer
inside \(\operatorname{Aut}(X,\tau)\), constructs two additional explicitly
certified enhanced K3 automorphisms, and converts the still-open finiteness
question for \(\operatorname{Aut}(X,\tau)\) into the orbit problem for one algebraic
genus-one curve.  It does **not** yet decide whether the full enhanced
automorphism group is finite.

Let \(X\) be the singular K3 surface of discriminant \(11\), with the
Weyl-framed golden endomorphism \(\tau\) and complete eigen-nef chamber \(C\).
Write

\[
G=\operatorname{Aut}(X,\tau)
=\{g\in\operatorname{Aut}(X):g^*\tau=\tau g^*\}.
\]

The D68/D27 return gives an involution \(A\in G\).  In field row coordinates
its action is

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
1-\phi&-1&0\\
0&0&I_8
\end{pmatrix},                                                     \tag{1}
\]

so its fixed field hyperplane is \(H=\{x_1=0\}\).

## 1. The fixed hyperplane is exactly Bugaenko's lattice

The H11 field form is

\[
h=\begin{pmatrix}-2\phi&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\oplus E_8.
\]

Restriction to \(H\) removes the second coordinate and gives

\[
\boxed{(H,h|_H)\cong\langle-2\phi\rangle\oplus E_8=L_{\mathrm{Bug}}.} \tag{2}
\]

The exact basis permutation is

```text
(0,1,8,2,3,4,5,6,7).
```

In particular, every norm-two root of \(L_{\mathrm{Bug}}\) is also a
norm-two H11 root.  This turns the known Bugaenko polytope into an intrinsic
fixed slice of the divisor lattice; it is not merely an analogy between two
Coxeter diagrams.

Because \(A\) preserves \(C\), the relative interior of \(C\cap H\) is
nonempty.  Indeed, if \(p\in C\), then \(p+Ap\) is a strict fixed point in
the same convex positive-cone chamber.  Since all Bugaenko roots are H11
roots, \(C\cap H\) lies in one chamber \(D\) of the complete
\(L_{\mathrm{Bug}}\) root arrangement.  The archived exact Vinberg
enumeration proves that a Bugaenko chamber has eleven walls and is compact;
all such chambers are root-reflection translates.  Hence

\[
                         \boxed{D\text{ is compact}.}              \tag{3}
\]

This is the promised new interpretation of Bugaenko's example: it is the
compact fixed-divisor slice of a natural involution on the rank-20 K3
carrier.

## 2. The centralizer of the involution is exactly \(V_4\)

Take \(g\in C_G(A)\).  It preserves \(H\) and \(C\cap H\).  The chambers
\(D\) and \(gD\) therefore have overlapping relative interiors, so \(gD=D\).
Restriction gives

\[
C_G(A)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Stab}_{O(L_{\mathrm{Bug}})}(D). \tag{4}
\]

Exact labeled-graph backtracking finds only two automorphisms of the
eleven-node Coxeter diagram: the identity and

```text
(0 8)(1 7)(2 6)(3 5)(9 10).
```

The first nine simple roots are independent, so the action on the diagram
is faithful.  Thus the target in (4) has order two.  The kernel has order at
most two: an element acting identically on \(H\) acts by \(+1\) or \(-1\) on
the orthogonal anti-invariant line, and hence is \(1\) or \(A\).  Therefore

\[
                         |C_G(A)|\leq4.                             \tag{5}
\]

The diagram reversal itself has two integral H11 lifts, according to the
sign on the anti-invariant line generated by

\[
                         (\phi-1,2,0^8).                            \tag{6}
\]

Starting with the image of the fixed ample class, exact folding of each lift
through the genuine K3 root boundaries terminates after precisely 88
norm-two reflections in a strict physical-D68 interior.  Denote the chamber
residuals by \(B_+\) and \(B_-\).  Direct field-matrix multiplication proves

\[
B_+^2=B_-^2=1,\qquad B_+B_-=B_-B_+=A,\qquad
[A,B_+]=[A,B_-]=1.                                                  \tag{7}
\]

All four matrices are distinct.  Consequently the bound (5) is sharp:

\[
\boxed{C_{\operatorname{Aut}(X,\tau)}(A)
=\{1,A,B_+,B_-\}\cong C_2\times C_2.}                              \tag{8}
\]

The exhaustive search in Shimada's 767 published generators found only
\(1,A\) through word length four.  Therefore both newly certified actions
\(B_+,B_-\) have generator length at least five; there is no conflict between
the bounded word theorem and (8).

The rank-20 characteristic polynomials and discriminant actions are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 &\chi(t)&A_{\operatorname{NS}}\cong\mathbf Z/11\\ \hline
A&(t-1)^{18}(t+1)^2&-1\\
B_+&(t-1)^{12}(t+1)^8&+1\\
B_-&(t-1)^{10}(t+1)^{10}&-1.
\end{array}                                                        \tag{9}
\]

Thus \(B_+\) extends by \(+I\) on \(T_{11}\), while \(A\) and \(B_-\)
extend by \(-I\).  Each extension preserves the Hodge line and orientation;
the strict terminal ample check and global Torelli realize all three as K3
automorphisms.  Commutation with \(\tau\), integrality, the trace-lattice
isometry equation, and the discriminant signs are checked entrywise.

## 3. The algebraic fixed locus

On the trace lattice, the fixed and anti-invariant lattices of \(A\) have

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 &\operatorname{rank}&|\det|&\text{Smith invariants}\\ \hline
\operatorname{NS}(X)^A&18&4&1^{16},2,2\\
\operatorname{NS}(X)^{-A}&2&44&2,22.
\end{array}                                                        \tag{10}
\]

The fixed lattice has signature \((1,17)\), and its discriminant form has

\[
(r,a,\delta)=(18,2,1),\qquad
\operatorname{NS}(X)^A\cong
U\oplus E_8(-1)\oplus E_7(-1)\oplus A_1(-1).                       \tag{11}
\]

Since the K3 extension of \(A\) is \(-I\) on \(T_{11}\), this is also the
full invariant lattice in \(H^2(X,\mathbf Z)\).  Nikulin's fixed-locus
formula gives

\[
g=\frac{22-r-a}{2}=1,\qquad k=\frac{r-a}{2}=8.                     \tag{12}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{X^A=E_A\;\sqcup\;R_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup R_8,}              \tag{13}
\]

where \(E_A\) is the unique smooth genus-one component and the \(R_i\) are
smooth rational curves.  By adjunction, \(E_A^2=0\); its divisor class is
nef and defines a genus-one pencil.

This supplies an algebraic object on the surface itself, not merely a
matrix: the involution \(A\), its fixed divisor, and especially its unique
genus-one component.

## 4. Exact orbit criterion for finite volume

Orbit-stabilizer and (8) give, without assuming \(G\) finite,

\[
                         G\cdot A\cong G/V_4.                       \tag{14}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{G\text{ is finite}\iff G\cdot A\text{ is finite}.}         \tag{15}
\]

The conjugacy orbit can be replaced by an orbit of algebraic curves.  The
map

\[
                         gAg^{-1}\longmapsto g(E_A)                \tag{16}
\]

is \(G\)-equivariant and bijective.  Here is the only point needing proof.
Clearly \(C_G(A)\) preserves the unique genus-one fixed component.  Conversely,
suppose \(g(E_A)=E_A\) and put \(A'=gAg^{-1}\).  Both \(A\) and \(A'\) fix
\(E_A\) pointwise and act by \(-1\) on \(H^{2,0}(X)\).  Thus \(h=AA'\) is
symplectic, belongs to \(G\), and fixes \(e=[E_A]\).  It also fixes
\(\tau e\).  The pairing \((e,\tau e)\) cannot vanish: otherwise \(e\) and
\(\tau e\) would be two orthogonal isotropic vectors in a Lorentzian space,
so they would be proportional, contradicting the irreducibility of
\(x^2-x-1\) over \(\mathbf Q\).  Therefore
\(\langle e,\tau e\rangle\) is a nondegenerate hyperbolic plane.  Its
orthogonal complement is negative definite, so the integral subgroup fixing
that plane pointwise is finite.  Shimada's faithful representation
\(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\hookrightarrow O(\operatorname{NS}(X))\) then makes
\(h\) finite order.  A nontrivial
finite symplectic K3 automorphism has only isolated fixed points, whereas
\(h\) fixes \(E_A\); consequently \(h=1\), \(A'=A\), and \(g\in C_G(A)\).

Combining this with the enhanced covolume theorem gives the entirely
algebro-geometric formulation

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{hyp}}P_{X,\tau}<\infty
&\iff P_{X,\tau}\text{ is compact}\\
&\iff G\text{ is finite}\\
&\iff \{g(E_A):g\in G\}\text{ is finite}.
\end{aligned}}                                                     \tag{17}
\]

Thus finite covolume is visible in classical surface terms as finiteness of
the enhanced automorphism orbit of one distinguished genus-one divisor.
The invariant hyperbolic measure need not itself be reconstructed from the
Cox ring or classical numerical invariants; its finiteness is detected by
this divisor-orbit condition.

## 5. Entropy obstruction if the orbit is infinite

There is also a useful dynamical sharpening.  An infinite-order
null-entropy automorphism of a projective K3 preserves a nef isotropic class
\(f\), equivalently a genus-one fibration.  If it commutes with \(\tau\), it
also fixes \(\tau f\).  The same Lorentzian argument as above shows that
\((f,\tau f)\ne0\), so these vectors span a hyperbolic plane and their
orthogonal complement is negative definite.  The automorphism would then
have finite order by the same faithful Néron--Severi representation, a
contradiction.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{G\text{ infinite}\Longrightarrow
G\text{ contains a positive-entropy automorphism}.}                \tag{18}
\]

For such an automorphism, its irreducible Salem primary space is preserved
by \(\tau\).  The commutant on that space is the Salem field
\(K=\mathbf Q(\lambda)\), and the restriction of \(\tau\) still satisfies
\(x^2-x-1\).  Moreover, adjunction for the intersection form induces the
involution \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{-1}\) on \(K\).  Since \(\tau\) is
self-adjoint, its field element is fixed by this involution.  Hence the
quadratic field lies in the maximal totally real subfield
\(K^+=\mathbf Q(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\), not merely somewhere in \(K\):

\[
\boxed{\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)\hookrightarrow K^+.}                      \tag{19}
\]

If the Salem polynomial has degree \(2d\), then \([K^+:\mathbf Q]=d\).
The tower through \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)\) forces \(2\mid d\), so

\[
\boxed{4\mid\deg S.}                                               \tag{20}
\]

This is a sharp spectral filter for any attempted infinite-order generator,
restricting its Salem degree in rank 20 to \(4,8,12,16\), or \(20\), although
it does not by itself decide whether one exists.
