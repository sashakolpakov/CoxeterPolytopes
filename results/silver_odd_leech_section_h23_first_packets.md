# Silver odd-Leech section in H23: two exact first-packet screens

## Outcome

Neither of the two screened prefixes is compact or finite-volume.  For the
rank-23 odd-Leech section

\[
S_{23}=O_{24}\cap(1^{24})^\perp
\]

over `Z[sqrt(2)]`, both admissible coefficients

\[
a=1+\sqrt2,\qquad a=\sqrt2
\]

have no height-zero wall.  Their complete chronological prefixes through the
first nonempty packet each contain an explicit strict spacelike interior
point.  This proves infinite volume for those prefixes.  Later mirrors were
not enumerated and can cut the displayed points, so this is not a global
nonreflectivity result.

The first packets are:

| coefficient | first height | primitive wall counts | total |
|:---|:---|:---|---:|
| `1+sqrt(2)` | `2+3sqrt(2)/2` | 2,576 of norm `2+sqrt(2)` | 2,576 |
| `sqrt(2)` | `8+6sqrt(2)` | 2,576 of norm 1; 3,740,352 of norm 2 | 3,742,928 |

## Exact section model and complete norm alphabet

The inherited certified basis of `S23` has

```text
rank = 23
determinant = 3
Smith invariants = 1^22,3
minimum = 3
complete norm-three shell = 2,576 vectors
```

The shell consists exactly of the weight-12 Golay sign vectors and spans rank
23.  Modulo three the Gram has rank 22; its radical is generated by a vector
whose raw residue is `(1^24)`.  The existing raw-coordinate argument proves
that this pairing radical has no nonzero vector of norm at most six.

Put `pi=sqrt(2)`.  For a primitive root `r=(x,y)`, reflection integrality is

\[
(q(r))\mid 2I(r),\qquad I(r)=(ax,(y,S_{23})).
\]

Local unimodularity excludes primes away from `(pi)` and `(3)`.  The dyadic
valuation is at most two.  This remains true for `a=pi`: if the spatial
pairing ideal is not a unit, then `y` is in `pi*S23`, primitivity makes `x` a
unit, and `q=-pi*x^2 mod pi^2` has valuation exactly one.  At three, a
positive valuation forces `x` into `(3)` and both coefficients of `y` into
the one-dimensional mod-three radical; the discriminant exponent bounds the
valuation by one.  Since totally positive units in `Z[sqrt(2)]` are squares,
the complete canonical alphabet for both coefficients is

```text
1, 2+sqrt(2), 2, 3, 6+3sqrt(2), 6.
```

For the last three norms, both vectors in `y=u+sqrt(2)*v` lie in the
mod-three radical.

At height zero,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(y^2)=2(u^2+2v^2).
\]

The first three canonical norms have trace below the section minimum bound.
The last three are removed by the radical minimum-seven obstruction.  Thus
the exact height-zero wall count is zero for both forms.

## Unit coefficient: complete first prefix

The endpoint is below physical height five.  The complete Minkowski box,
followed by exact crystallographic, conjugate-positivity, and endpoint tests,
contains six controls:

| height | norm | control `x` | target `y^2` | necessary types |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|---:|
| `sqrt(2)/2` | `2+sqrt(2)` | 1 | `3+2sqrt(2)` | 0 |
| `(1+sqrt(2))/2` | 2 | 1 | `3+sqrt(2)` | 0 |
| `1+sqrt(2)` | 1 | 1 | `2+sqrt(2)` | 0 |
| `1+sqrt(2)` | 2 | `sqrt(2)` | `4+2sqrt(2)` | 0 |
| `3/2+3sqrt(2)/2` | 6 | 3 | `15+9sqrt(2)` | 0 after the 3-gate |
| `2+3sqrt(2)/2` | `2+sqrt(2)` | `1+sqrt(2)` | `9+6sqrt(2)` | 1 |

At the endpoint the sole binary Gram triple is

```text
(u^2,v^2,u.v)=(3,3,3).
```

It forces `u=v=b`, `b^2=3`, so all roots are

\[
r_b=((1+\sqrt2),(1+\sqrt2)b),\qquad b\in S_{23},\quad b^2=3.
\]

They have norm `2+sqrt(2)`, are primitive because the time coordinate is a
unit, and have integral reflections.  For distinct shell vectors `b,c`,
minimum three gives `b.c<=1`; hence every off-diagonal root pairing is at
most

\[
(1+\sqrt2)^2(1-(1+\sqrt2))=-4-3\sqrt2<0.
\]

The entire packet of 2,576 walls is therefore accepted simultaneously.

## Dyadic coefficient: complete simultaneous packet

The endpoint `8+6sqrt(2)` is below physical height 17.  There are exactly ten
controls in the complete box; eight have no necessary Gram type after the
minimum/radical gates.  The two surviving controls occur simultaneously:

| norm | control `x` | target `y^2` | necessary triples |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| 1 | `2+sqrt(2)` | `9+6sqrt(2)` | `(3,3,3)` |
| 2 | `2+2sqrt(2)` | `18+12sqrt(2)` | `(12,3,6)`, `(6,6,6)` |

The norm-one roots are

\[
s_b=(\sqrt2(1+\sqrt2),(1+\sqrt2)b),\qquad b^2=3,
\]

giving 2,576 walls.  For norm two, the triple `(12,3,6)` forces `u=2v`
with `v^2=3`; every such candidate is exactly `sqrt(2)*s_b` and is rejected
as imprimitive.  The triple `(6,6,6)` forces `u=v=c`, producing

\[
t_c=(2(1+\sqrt2),(1+\sqrt2)c),\qquad c\in S_{23},\quad c^2=6.
\]

These roots are primitive: divisibility of the rational vector `c` by
`sqrt(2)` would force `c` to be twice a section vector, impossible because
`c^2=6` is not divisible by four.  Their reflections are integral.

### Complete norm-six shell

On the raw `sqrt(8)` scale a norm-six section vector has square sum 48 and
coordinate sum zero.  The odd Construction-B coset contributes none: three
coordinates would have absolute value three, and the zero-sum equation is
incompatible with all Golay weights.

In the even coset write the raw vector as `2z`, so `z^2=12` and `z mod 2` is
a Golay word.  Only weights 8 and 12 survive:

```text
weight 12: 2576*C(12,6)       = 2,380,224
weight  8: 759*16*2*C(8,3)    = 1,360,128
total                           = 3,740,352
```

The Construction-B residual parity is even in both cases, so this is an
exact shell census, not merely an upper bound.

Minimum three gives `b.c<=3` for norms three and six, and `c.d<=4` for
distinct norm-six vectors.  Consequently all three kinds of simultaneous
off-diagonal root pairing are strictly negative.  The packet contains
exactly 3,742,928 primitive walls after rejecting the 2,576 scalar
duplicates.

## Strict hyperideal witnesses

Let

\[
e=(4,-4,0^{22})/\sqrt8,\qquad e^2=4.
\]

Its pairing maximum on the norm-three shell is one.  Its exact pairing
histogram on the complete norm-six shell is

```text
-3: 12,320; -2: 229,152; -1: 924,000; 0: 1,409,408;
 1: 924,000;  2: 229,152;  3: 12,320.
```

For `a=1+sqrt(2)`, take `z=(1,e)`.  Then

```text
z^2 = 3-sqrt(2) > 0
closest wall pairing = -2-sqrt(2) < 0.
```

For `a=sqrt(2)`, take `z=(1,(2/3)e)`.  Then

```text
z^2 = 16/9-sqrt(2) > 0
closest wall pairing = -2 < 0.
```

Each point lies strictly inside its complete prefix cone and is spacelike.
The segment from the timelike basepoint crosses the light cone away from all
walls, leaving an open ideal-boundary patch.  Hence both exact first-prefix
chambers have infinite volume.
