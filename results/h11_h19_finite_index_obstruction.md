# Finite-index obstruction and exact decision plan for H11 and H19

Status: **both reflection-index questions remain undecided**. This note gives
an exact, finitely checkable certificate for the infinite-index branch, two
arithmetic isometries with which to start that search, and a source audit
showing why modular-form nonexistence is not such a certificate.

## 1. Setup

Let $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, $\mathcal O_F=\mathbb Z[\phi]$, and let $(M,h)$ be
either

\[
H_{11}=\begin{pmatrix}-2\phi&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\oplus E_8,
\qquad
H_{19}=\begin{pmatrix}-2\phi&\phi\\\phi&2\end{pmatrix}\oplus E_8.
\]

At the identity embedding $h_0$ has signature $(9,1)$; at the conjugate
embedding $h_1$ it is positive definite. Put

\[
 \Gamma=\operatorname{PO}^{+}(M,h),\qquad
 W_2=\langle s_r:h(r,r)=2\rangle.
\]

The even-mirror theorem in docs/divisor_lattice_factory.md identifies the
complete restricted K3 arrangement with these norm-two mirrors. If $C$ is
one chamber, then

\[
       \Gamma/W_2\simeq\Gamma_C,
       \qquad
       \Gamma_C=\{g\in\Gamma:gC=C\}.
\]

Thus $W_2$ has infinite index exactly when the chamber stabilizer is infinite.

## 2. A finite non-reflectivity certificate

### Proposition (same-chamber infinite-order obstruction)

Let a discrete group $\Gamma\leq\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb H^n)$ preserve a
locally finite mirror arrangement, and let $W\triangleleft\Gamma$ be the
group generated by its reflections. Assume, as for the complete reflection
arrangement above, that $W$ acts simply transitively on its chambers. Suppose
$p$ lies in a chamber $C$. If there is $g\in\Gamma$ such that

1. $g$ has infinite projective order; and
2. no mirror separates $p$ from $gp$,

then $g\in\Gamma_C$, $\Gamma_C$ is infinite, and
$[\Gamma:W]=\infty$.

**Proof.** The image $gC$ is a chamber and contains $gp$. The second condition
says that $p$ and $gp$ lie in the same component of the mirror complement,
namely $C$. Hence $gC=C$. The infinite cyclic projective subgroup generated
by $g$ lies in $\Gamma_C$. Finally the chamber action identifies $\Gamma/W$
with $\Gamma_C$. $\square$

This proposition is useful because condition 2 has a **finite exact test**.
It does not require a globally completed Vinberg enumeration.

### Finite separator bound

Take $p\in M\otimes F$ in the chosen time cone and put $q=gp$. Rescale is
unnecessary. Write

\[
 c=-h_0(p,p)>0,
 \qquad
 \cosh L=\frac{-h_0(p,q)}{c},
\]

where $L=d(p,q)$ and $h_0(q,q)=h_0(p,p)$. For a norm-two root $r$, the
distance from $p$ to $r^\perp$ satisfies

\[
 \sinh^2 d(p,r^\perp)
   =\frac{h_0(p,r)^2}{(-h_0(p,p))h_0(r,r)}
   =\frac{h_0(p,r)^2}{2c}.
\]

Consequently, if $r^\perp$ meets the geodesic segment $[p,q]$, then

\[
             h_0(p,r)^2\leq 2c\sinh^2 L.                 \tag{2.1}
\]

The Lorentzian majorant based at $p$,

\[
 q_p(x)=h_0(x,x)-2\frac{h_0(p,x)^2}{h_0(p,p)},
\]

is positive definite. For a root satisfying (2.1),

\[
 q_p(r)\leq2+4\sinh^2L.
\]

At the other embedding $h_1(r',r')=2$. Therefore the positive-definite
Minkowski majorant

\[
 Q_p(r)=q_p(r_0)+h_1(r_1)
\]

satisfies the exact bound

\[
                 Q_p(r)\leq4+4\sinh^2L=4\cosh^2L.       \tag{2.2}
\]

Choose an $\mathcal O_F$-basis $e_1,\ldots,e_{10}$ and write
$r=\sum_i(a_i+b_i\phi)e_i$. In the integral coordinate vector
$z=(a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_{10},b_{10})\in\mathbb Z^{20}$, $Q_p$ is an explicit
positive-definite $20\times20$ real algebraic matrix. Choose rational
$\epsilon>0$ and rational $B_+$ such that exact inertia/sign computations
prove

\[
 Q_p-\epsilon I>0,
 \qquad
 B_+\geq4\cosh^2L.
\]

Every possible separator then lies in the finite box

\[
        |z_i|\leq\left\lfloor\sqrt{B_+/\epsilon}\right\rfloor.
\]

Enumerate this box, retain exactly the vectors with $h(r,r)=2$, quotient by
$r\sim-r$, and evaluate the two signs

\[
       \operatorname{sgn}h_0(p,r),\qquad
       \operatorname{sgn}h_0(q,r).
\]

The endpoints must have no zero sign. A mirror separates them exactly when
the signs are opposite. Completeness follows from (2.2): every mirror meeting
the compact segment is inside the enumerated box.

### Certificate payload

A machine-checkable infinite-index certificate should archive:

1. the field Gram matrix, $p$, and the integral matrix $g$;
2. exact checks $g^T H g=H$, time-component preservation, and an
   infinite-projective-order spectral certificate;
3. the exact matrix of $Q_p$, rational $\epsilon,B_+$, and the positive
   inertia of $Q_p-\epsilon I$;
4. the resulting integral coordinate bound;
5. the canonical exhaustive norm-two root list in that box, or its data file
   plus a verifier and digest; and
6. the nonzero equal-sign check at both endpoints for every retained root.

For a general $g$, infinite projective order can be certified by an exact real
eigenvalue interval disjoint from $[-1,1]$. For the binary seeds below,
determinant one and trace $>2$ give this immediately.

## 3. Exact H11 and H19 symmetry seeds

Both binary blocks have Pell-type determinant-one isometries. Extending each
matrix by $I_8$ on $E_8$ gives an element of the full field-lattice orthogonal
group.

For $H_{11}$, set

\[
 A_{11}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1-\phi\\-1&\phi\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
 A_{11}^{T}
 \begin{pmatrix}-2\phi&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}A_{11}
 =\begin{pmatrix}-2\phi&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
 \quad \det A_{11}=1,
 \quad \operatorname{tr}A_{11}=1+\phi>2.
\]

The underlying Pell identity is

\[
 (1+\phi)^2-(1+4\phi)(\phi-1)^2=4.
\]

For $H_{19}$, set

\[
 A_{19}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-\phi&1+\phi\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
 A_{19}^{T}
 \begin{pmatrix}-2\phi&\phi\\\phi&2\end{pmatrix}A_{19}
 =\begin{pmatrix}-2\phi&\phi\\\phi&2\end{pmatrix},
 \quad \det A_{19}=1,
 \quad \operatorname{tr}A_{19}=2+\phi>2,
\]

with Pell identity

\[
 (2+\phi)^2-(1+5\phi)=4.
\]

These identities and the deliberately false same_chamber_certified flag are
checked by scripts/h11_h19_symmetry_seeds.py and
tests/test_h11_h19_symmetry_seeds.py. The matrices are **search seeds, not
index obstructions**: composing a loxodromic seed with wall reflections to
return its endpoint to a chosen chamber can produce a finite-order element,
so the final folded element must be tested again.

### Exact folding search

Starting with an interior point $p\in C$ and a candidate $\gamma\in\Gamma$:

1. use the separator bound to list every wall crossed by $[p,\gamma p]$;
2. reflect the endpoint chamber back across those walls, obtaining
   $w\in W_2$ with $w\gamma p\in C$;
3. verify the same-chamber condition independently for $g=w\gamma$; and
4. test the projective order of $g$.

Success at step 4 proves infinite index. A finite-order folded element, or
failure for any bounded collection of candidates, proves nothing. Products
of the two Pell seeds with exact lattice automorphisms and already discovered
wall reflections provide a systematic candidate stream.

## 4. Primary-source audit

| Source | Verified scope | Consequence for H11/H19 |
|---|---|---|
| Bugaenko 1984 | The paper is explicitly about **unimodular** hyperbolic forms over $\mathbb Z[(1+\sqrt5)/2]$. | It does not classify the determinant-norm $11$ or $19$ forms. |
| Bugaenko 1992 | Primary source for arithmetic reflection constructions, including the known compact dimension-eight example. No theorem covering H11/H19 was verified in the locally accessible material. | No non-reflectivity inference is available. |
| Agol--Belolipetsky--Storm--Whyte 2008 | The main theorem gives finiteness of conjugacy classes of maximal arithmetic hyperbolic reflection groups. | This is ineffective for deciding membership of these two explicit lattices. |
| Borcherds 2000, Theorem 4.2 | A suitable reflective vector-valued modular form gives a semidirect-product description involving a reflection subgroup and a Weyl-vector stabilizer. | It is a construction theorem, not a converse. The introduction explicitly says no such converse is known; Problems 13.1--13.2 ask for the relation with Bugaenko groups and for a converse. |
| Bruinier 2012, Theorem 1.3 and Proposition 4.5 | In signature $((n,2),(n+2,0),\ldots)$, weakly holomorphic Whittaker forms lift to meromorphic orthogonal modular forms with prescribed special divisors; cusp forms obstruct prescribed principal parts. | This can decide existence of specified automorphic products, not finite index of $W_2$ in a signature-$(9,1)$ hyperbolic group. |
| Wang 2022, Theorem 1.1 | Complete list of reflective prime-level lattices of signature $(n,2)$. For $p=11$ the list has only $n=4,6$, and $p=19$ is absent. | Up to the overall sign convention, $U\oplus N_{11}$ and $U\oplus N_{19}$ have type-IV signature $(20,2)$, cyclic discriminant of order $11,19$, and prime level $11,19$; neither admits a reflective modular form. |
| Wang 2024, Theorem 7.2 | There are 18 hyperbolic 2-reflective lattices of rank $>5$ with no automorphic correction; for example $S=U\oplus E_8\oplus E_7$ is hyperbolically 2-reflective while $U\oplus S$ is not 2-reflective modular. | This published counterexample blocks the converse needed to turn Wang's modular nonexistence into non-reflectivity of H11/H19. |

The modular calculation is therefore a rigorous negative result about the
**Borcherds realization route**, not about the hyperbolic reflection index.
Borcherds himself treats reflective modular forms as a way to suggest examples
whose reflection properties are then checked directly.

## 5. Assessment of other possible obstructions

| Method | What would prove infinite index | Why a bounded computation is insufficient |
|---|---|---|
| Discriminant-form action | An infinite quotient detected beyond the finite discriminant module. | $O(A_M)$ is finite, so it can give only a finite lower bound on the index. |
| Reduction modulo primes | A rigorously unbounded family -- for example, quotients $|\Gamma\bmod\mathfrak p^k|/|W_2\bmod\mathfrak p^k|$ tending to infinity. | Any one reduction is finite. Equality in many tested reductions still does not exclude a thin, even Zariski-dense, subgroup. |
| Determinant/spinor norm | An infinite image on which all norm-two reflections vanish. | For these arithmetic groups the readily available square-class targets are finite; they usually yield only finite-index divisibility. |
| Growth/critical exponent | A proof that $\delta(W_2)<9=\delta(\Gamma)$. | This needs a complete automatic/Markov coding or an equivalent global chamber description; a Vinberg prefix does not control growth. |
| Chamber symmetry | One certified infinite-order element of $\Gamma_C$. | This is exactly the finite separator certificate above and is the most direct current obstruction. |
| Full arithmetic cell quotient | Compute the finite labelled quotient complex and its loop-label stabilizer $\Gamma_C$. | Finiteness of the unlabelled quotient alone says nothing about whether $\Gamma_C$ is finite. |

Congruence and discriminant calculations are still useful for rejecting
candidate finite-index values or organizing searches, but none of their
finite outputs can be relabeled as non-reflectivity.

## 6. A genuine decision procedure after the cell quotient

The finite arithmetic quotient described in docs/divisor_lattice_factory.md,
Section 5, gives generators for $\Gamma_C$ as loop labels in a finite cell
groupoid. Once that exhaustive quotient and its side pairings have been
certified, finiteness of $\Gamma_C$ is decidable in parallel:

1. enumerate the matrix group generated by the loop labels; termination with
   a closed finite multiplication table proves finite index;
2. enumerate words and test their exact projective orders; an infinite-order
   word proves infinite index.

A finitely generated torsion linear group in characteristic zero is finite
(Schur), so one of these two branches terminates. Thus the arithmetic cell
quotient is a true decision method, whereas a bounded Vinberg run is not.

For the immediate theorem-side search, the less expensive branch is the
same-chamber certificate:

1. implement the 20-coordinate Minkowski majorant and exact separator
   enumerator;
2. feed it the existing exact H11 interior point and the Pell candidate
   stream;
3. archive any folding word and recheck the final matrix from scratch;
4. repeat for H19; and
5. pursue the full cell quotient only if the symmetry search does not find a
   certificate.

No finite failure in steps 1--4 changes the status. At present there is
neither a complete compact chamber certificate nor an infinite-order
same-chamber certificate for H11 or H19.
