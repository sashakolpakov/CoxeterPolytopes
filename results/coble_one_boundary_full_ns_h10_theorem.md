## Why the full lattice is `H10`, not the usual `H9` slice

Let `S` be a one-boundary Coble surface and write its unique
anti-bicanonical divisor as

\[
 B\in |-2K_S|,\qquad b:=\frac B2=-K_S.
\]

Kondō's boundary is a smooth rational `(-4)`-curve.  Hence

\[
 K_S^2=-1,\qquad \rho(S)=10-K_S^2=11,\qquad b^2=-1.
\]

Kondō's Proposition `CME10` identifies the Coble--Mukai lattice with the
even unimodular Lorentzian lattice `E10`.  For one boundary it is exactly
`K_S^perp=b^perp`.  Since `b` has norm `-1`, the splitting is integral:

\[
 \operatorname{NS}(S)=\operatorname{CM}(S)\mathbin\perp\mathbf Zb
 \cong E_{10}\mathbin\perp\langle-1\rangle\cong I_{1,10}.
\]

The traditional Coble--Mukai chamber is therefore a codimension-one
totally geodesic `H9` face.  It does not exhaust the full divisor geometry.

## Complete cusp-to-curve lift

This is the key step that prevents the full-NS construction from being an
uncertified wall guess.

Let `f` be a primitive isotropic cusp ray of the complete Coble--Mukai
chamber.  Kondō's Lemma `fibration-isotropic` identifies primitive isotropic
vectors in that chamber bijectively with genus-one fibrations on `S`.
Kondō's Proposition `Halphen` says that such a fibration is obtained either
from an index-two Halphen surface by blowing up the singularities of one
reduced singular fibre, or from an index-one Halphen surface by doing this
to two reduced singular fibres.

When there is only one boundary component, the index-one alternative is
impossible: its two blown fibres contribute at least two boundary
components.  In the index-two alternative, one boundary forces the blown
fibre to be of type `I1` or `II`.  Blowing its node or cusp produces exactly
one exceptional `(-1)`-curve `E`, and the total fibre is

\[
 B+2E=2(b+E).
\]

Kondō uses the primitive half-fibre, so

\[
 f=b+E,\qquad E=f-b.
\tag{1}
\]

This also fixes the scaling: `f` is primitive and the scheme-theoretic fibre
is `2f`.

Conversely, if `E` is any `(-1)`-curve, adjunction gives `K_S.E=-1`, hence

\[
 f:=E+b=E-K_S\in b^\perp,qquad f^2=0.
\]

For every effective Coble--Mukai root `R`, which in the one-boundary case is
a `(-2)`-curve, `f.R=E.R>=0`.  Kondō's Proposition `NefCone` therefore makes
`f` nef.  The Halphen index is at most two, and the preceding fibre
description makes `f` primitive.  Thus (1) is a bijection

```text
CM ideal cusp rays  <---->  all (-1)-curves on S.
```

The exact Type-I and Type-II CM chambers have 9 and 17 cusps.  Their complete
full-NS wall lists are consequently

\[
 \{b\}\ \cup\ \{\text{12 CM }(-2)\text{-roots}\}\ \cup
 \ \{f-b:f\text{ a CM cusp}\},
\]

giving `1+12+9=22` and `1+12+17=30` walls.

## Why these are the complete nef cones

Kondō records that the only negative curves on a Coble surface are the
boundary curves, `(-2)`-curves, and `(-1)`-curves.  For one boundary, his
no-hidden-root proposition gives the complete 12-element `(-2)` list, and
the cusp lift above gives the complete `(-1)` list.  An irreducible negative
curve spans an extremal effective ray and hence supplies an irredundant nef
facet.  An irreducible curve of nonnegative square lies in the closure of the
positive cone and cannot cut an additional hyperbolic wall.  The displayed
list is therefore the full nef wall list.

There is also an independent characteristic-zero closure.  The pair

\[
 (S,\tfrac12B)
\]

is klt and `K_S+B/2=0`.  Every automorphism preserves the unique divisor
`B`, so `Aut(S,B/2)=Aut(S)`.  Kondō proves this group finite for Types I and
II.  Totaro's Corollary 5.1 for complex klt Calabi--Yau pairs then implies
that the complete nef cone is rational polyhedral; since `b_1(S)=0`, it also
implies finite generation of the Cox ring.  This second proof is structural
and does not replace the exact curve enumeration.

Primary sources:

- Shigeyuki Kondō, [*Coble surfaces with finite automorphism
  group*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00591), Main Theorem/Table 2 and
  Propositions/Lemmas `CME10`, `NefCone`, `Vinberg2`, `Halphen`, and
  `fibration-isotropic`.
- Burt Totaro, [*The cone conjecture for Calabi-Yau pairs in dimension
  two*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.3361), Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 5.1.

The terminology here is **anti-bicanonical**: `B` is the unique divisor in
`|-2K_S|`.

## Coxeter and arithmetic gates

Use `b=-K_S` rather than the nonprimitive divisor `B=2b` as the first wall
normal.  The complete primitive normal set has norms `-1` and `-2`:

- `b^2=-1`;
- every exceptional wall has `E^2=-1`;
- every Coble--Mukai root has `R^2=-2`.

Adjunction and positivity of intersections give

\[
 b.R=0,\quad b.E=1,
\]

and, for distinct curves, nonnegative integral intersections.  Therefore
the only intersecting dihedral angles are

```text
pi/2, pi/3, pi/4;
```

equality in the Lorentzian Cauchy bound gives parallel walls, and larger
pairing gives ultraparallel walls.  The reflection formulas are integral:

\[
 s_v(x)=x+2(x,v)v\quad(v^2=-1),\qquad
 s_v(x)=x+(x,v)v\quad(v^2=-2).
\]

The Smith diagonal of each `11 x m` wall matrix is `1^11`, so both wall
sets span `I_(1,10)` integrally.  Their reflection groups are finite-index
subgroups of Vinberg's full root-reflection group `W(P10)`.  Complete
Solomon sums give

\[
 \chi(W_{\rm I})=-\frac{527}{21772800},\qquad
 \chi(W_{\rm II})=-\frac{527}{483840},
\]

and comparison with `chi(W(P10))=-1/11147673600` gives the indices in the
table.

## The polytope-first recognition theorem

The result above began with surfaces.  The following converse is genuinely
polytope-first, but deliberately finite in scope.

Take only an oriented integral rank-11 Coxeter datum `(L;v_1,...,v_m)`.  Run:

1. orient its primitive normals so all off-diagonal inner products are
   nonnegative;
2. enumerate facets `b` of norm `-1` that are characteristic vectors of
   `L`;
3. require `b^perp` to be even unimodular of signature `(1,9)`, hence `E10`;
4. require every other wall to be either a norm-`-2` vector orthogonal to
   `b`, or a norm-`-1` vector `E` with `b.E=1`;
5. form `f=E+b` and require the resulting primitive isotropic vectors to be
   **exactly all** ideal extreme rays of the chamber cut out by the extracted
   norm-`-2` roots in `b^perp`;
6. compare that complete root chamber with Kondō Type I and Type II.

Within the class of complex finite-automorphism one-boundary Coble carriers,
this is necessary and sufficient:

\[
 \text{the carrier moduli is nonempty}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{the exact recognizer returns Type I or Type II}.
\]

Failure is only a **Coble-carrier obstruction**.  It says nothing about
existence of the input polytope.

## Immediate `H11` corollary, exact ledger pending

Kondō's complex Type-I surface with two boundary components gives a further
existential result.  Here

\[
 K_S^2=-2,\qquad \rho(S)=12,
\]

so the full nef chamber lies in `H11`.  Again `(S,B/2)` is a complex klt
Calabi--Yau pair and `Aut(S)` is finite, so Totaro proves the full nef cone
rational polyhedral.  Its negative walls are boundary `(-4)`-curves,
`(-1)`-curves, and `(-2)`-curves.  Scaling their normals as

```text
B_i,  2E,  R
```

makes every Cartan integer integral.  Indeed, boundaries are disjoint,
`B_i.R=0`, and `sum_i B_i.E=(-2K).E=2`; the remaining pairings reduce to
the integral `(-1)/(-2)` cases above.  Thus the complete nef chamber is a
finite-volume noncompact arithmetic Coxeter polytope in `H11`.

This is presently an **existence theorem, not an exact ledger row**.  The
complete `(-1)`-curve enumeration, facet count, Gram, double description,
and literal Vinberg audit remain to be computed.  Positive-characteristic
cases with 5 or 8 boundaries do not follow from Totaro's complex theorem and
remain separate `H14/H17` audits.
