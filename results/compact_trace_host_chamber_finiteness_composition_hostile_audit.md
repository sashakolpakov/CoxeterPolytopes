# Compact trace-host composition: when finite hosts give finitely many chambers

**Verdict (2026-08-11): PASS under full native-host maximality.**  A finite
list of complete oriented integral order-module hosts, exact compact return,
and reflection maximality inside the same native host gives a finite list of
unmarked normalized Coxeter chambers.  In fact, each host contributes at most
one chamber isometry class.

Two qualifications are essential.  A bare restriction-of-scalars
\(\mathbf Z\)-lattice is not a complete host when \([k:\mathbf Q]>1\), and
maximality only among isometries which extend across a selected K3 complement
or fix a selected period is weaker than maximality in the native host.

## 1. The complete native host

An oriented admissible integral reflection host is the packet

\[
 \mathcal H=(k,\sigma _0,\mathcal O,M,h,\mathcal C^+),
\]

where:

- \(k\) is totally real with distinguished embedding \(\sigma _0\);
- \(\mathcal O\subset k\) is the declared order and \(M\) is a finite
  projective rank-\(m\) \(\mathcal O\)-module, including its saturation;
- \(h\) is Lorentzian at \(\sigma _0\) and definite at every other real
  embedding; and
- \(\mathcal C^+\) is the selected time-cone component at \(\sigma _0\).

Put

\[
 \Gamma_{\mathcal H}=
 \{g\in\operatorname {Aut}_{\mathcal O}(M):
       h(gx,gy)=h(x,y),\ g_{\sigma _0}(\mathcal C^+)=\mathcal C^+\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Here the superscript `+` means preservation of the time-cone component, not
determinant one: hyperplane reflections must be allowed.  Let
\(\operatorname {Ref}(\mathcal H)\) be all elements of \(\Gamma_{\mathcal H}\)
whose action at \(\sigma _0\) is a hyperplane reflection, and set

\[
                 R(\mathcal H)=
                 \langle\operatorname {Ref}(\mathcal H)\rangle.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The distinguished action of \(\Gamma_{\mathcal H}\) is discrete.  Indeed, its
diagonal action lies in

\[
 O(m-1,1)\times\prod_{\sigma\ne\sigma _0}O(m),
\]

as an integral arithmetic subgroup, and all nondistinguished factors are
compact.  If distinct distinguished components approached the identity, a
convergent subsequence in the compact factors would make pairwise quotients
approach the identity in every factor, contradicting discreteness of the
diagonal integral group.

## 2. Conditional composition theorem

### Theorem 2.1

Let \(\mathfrak H\) be a finite set of isometry classes of complete oriented
hosts \(\mathcal H\).  Let \(\mathfrak C\) be a class of compact finite-sided
Coxeter chambers such that every \(P\in\mathfrak C\) is assigned a host
\(\mathcal H(P)\in\mathfrak H\) satisfying:

1. **Exact native return.**  The returned chamber lies in the distinguished
   \(\mathbf H^{m-1}\), every facet reflection belongs to
   \(\operatorname {Ref}(\mathcal H)\), and \(P\) is a fundamental chamber for
   its facet group \(W(P)\).
2. **Same-host reflection maximality.**  There is no proper subgroup
   \(G\) with
   \[
       W(P)<G\le\Gamma_{\mathcal H}
   \]
   which is generated by hyperplane reflections.  Equivalently, it suffices
   to require
   \(\langle W(P),s\rangle=W(P)\) for every
   \(s\in\operatorname {Ref}(\mathcal H)\).

Then

\[
                         W(P)=R(\mathcal H(P)),             \tag{2.1}
\]

and all chambers belonging to the same oriented host are isometric.  Hence

\[
 \#\{\text{unmarked normalized chamber classes in }\mathfrak C\}
 \le |\mathfrak H|.                                     \tag{2.2}
\]

If the finite host list forgets the time-cone component, adjoining that
choice gives at worst two oriented packets per underlying host.  In the
usual projective hyperbolic interpretation the opposite choices are often
already isometric, but no such identification is needed for finiteness.

### Proof

Facet preservation gives \(W(P)\le R(\mathcal H)\).  For any
\(s\in\operatorname {Ref}(\mathcal H)\), the group

\[
                         \langle W(P),s\rangle
                         \le\Gamma_{\mathcal H}
\]

is discrete because \(\Gamma_{\mathcal H}\) is discrete, and it is generated by
reflections.  Same-host maximality forces \(s\in W(P)\).  Doing this for
every native integral reflection proves (2.1).

The complete mirror arrangement of \(R(\mathcal H)\) is locally finite.  If
infinitely many distinct mirrors met a compact set \(K\), their reflections
would all satisfy \(sK\cap K\ne\varnothing\), contradicting proper
discontinuity of the discrete group.

Now choose regular points in two components of the mirror complement and a
generic compact path between them.  Local finiteness makes the number of
crossed mirrors finite, and the path can avoid all multiple intersections.
Reflection successively across the crossed mirrors walks through adjacent
components and carries the first chamber to the second.  Therefore the
complete arrangement has one \(R(\mathcal H)\)-orbit of chambers.

Finally, (2.1) makes the supplied \(P\) a chamber of that complete
arrangement: an additional native mirror through its interior would be a
\(W(P)\)-reflection mirror through the interior of a fundamental chamber.
Thus two returns on the same oriented host are conjugate by a host isometry
and have the same normalized Coxeter Gram matrix, up to facet permutation.
This proves (2.2). \(\square\)

## 3. No finite-index hypothesis is needed in the relative version

The proof above uses only host discreteness and same-host reflection
maximality.  It does not require that \(W(P)\) have finite index in
\(\Gamma_{\mathcal H}\), that \(R(\mathcal H)\) was already known reflective, or
that either group was already known to have finite covolume.  Equality with
the supplied compact chamber group proves reflectivity and cocompactness of
\(R(\mathcal H)\) afterwards.

Two alternative maximality hypotheses also suffice:

1. absolute maximality among discrete hyperbolic reflection groups; or
2. maximality among arithmetic reflection groups in the same arithmetic
   representation.

For the second alternative, finite volume matters.  The chamber group is a
lattice, and every discrete overgroup \(\langle W(P),s\rangle\) has finite
index over \(W(P)\), hence is commensurable and arithmetic.  No separate
finite-index assumption is necessary.  Maximality of the order, of the
integral lattice, of a known root list, or among effective walls is not a
substitute for reflection-group maximality.

## 4. Why a bare trace lattice is insufficient

Let

\[
                       T=\operatorname {Res}_{k/\mathbf Q}M,
 \qquad d=[k:\mathbf Q].
\]

A \(k\)-linear facet reflection has a one-dimensional \((-1)\)-eigenspace
over \(k\), but after restriction of scalars its \((-1)\)-eigenspace has
\(\mathbf Q\)-dimension \(d\).  For \(d>1\) it is therefore **not** an
ordinary rational pseudoreflection of the trace form.  The relevant mirror
is a hypersurface in the native distinguished \(\mathbf H^{m-1}\), not a
hypersurface in the full rational trace hyperbolic space.

Consequently the group generated by ordinary integral pseudoreflections of
the bare \(\mathbf Z\)-lattice \(T\) is generally the wrong group.  The
following data must remain in a finite trace-host packet:

- the field and order action;
- the distinguished embedding and time component;
- the saturated order-module and form; and
- the native integral \(k\)-reflection species.

A finite list of local star Gram matrices, unsaturated star spans, or
unmarked trace lattices does not yet satisfy Theorem 2.1.  If the physical
\((-2)\)-star argument initially returns only a full-rank submodule, every
compatible integral saturation/overlattice must first be shown finite and
included in the host list.

## 5. K3 complements and periods

Complement, glue, and period data affect **realizability**, not the abstract
one-chamber conclusion after the theorem's hypotheses hold.  Exact return
must already prove simultaneous effectivity and exclude every hidden
projected wall.  Different primitive embeddings or period points may then
produce no carrier, one carrier, or a positive-dimensional family of
carriers for the same host chamber; they do not create a second unmarked
native chamber once (2.1) holds.

There is, however, a genuine weaker-maximality trap.  For a primitive K3
embedding \(j\) and period \(\omega\), let

\[
 \Gamma_{\mathcal H,j,\omega}
 =\{g\in\Gamma_{\mathcal H}:g\text{ extends across the glue and preserves the
 period/declared monodromy data}\}.
\]

Maximality only inside \(\Gamma_{\mathcal H,j,\omega}\) proves saturation only
to the reflections of that smaller group.  This subgroup can depend on the
complement, glue, and period, so a finite list of native hosts alone no
longer proves a finite decorated or extendable-reflection chamber list.  One
must instead either:

1. impose maximality in the full \(\Gamma_{\mathcal H}\) as in Theorem 2.1;
2. prove that every native host reflection extends; or
3. include a finite list of the relevant complement/glue/extension-group
   packets.

An individual \((-2)\)-root reflection acts trivially on the discriminant
group of its lattice and therefore often extends by the identity across a
primitive K3 glue.  The same is true for a field reflection explicitly
unfolded as a product of mutually orthogonal such root reflections.  But the
existence of a physical \((-2)\)-star does not by itself prove that **every**
native integral host reflection has this form.

Finally, Theorem 2.1 counts unmarked normalized abstract chambers.  It does
not count marked physical-facet packets, K3 surfaces, primitive embeddings,
or period points.  Those sets can be infinite even when the abstract chamber
set has one element.

## 6. Exact payoff and remaining upstream gate

Thus the proposed compact composition is valid in the form

\[
 \text{finite complete native hosts}
 +\text{ exact return and same-host reflection maximality}
 \Longrightarrow\text{ finitely many normalized compact chambers}.
\]

This is qualitative finiteness.  It does not supply an effective
reflectivity decision or enumerate the facets of every candidate host.  The
remaining mathematical gate is upstream: derive the finite list of complete
saturated order-module hosts from the physical \((-2)\)-star, and prove that
every raw compact input receives an exact return with maximality in precisely
that same native host.
