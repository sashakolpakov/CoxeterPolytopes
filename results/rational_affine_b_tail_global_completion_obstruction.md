# The affine \(B_m\) blowup packet does not have a reflection-maximal \(\mathbf P^1\!\times\!\mathbf P^1\) completion

**Status (2026-08-10): uniform obstruction proved; no classification or
census is used.**  This note answers the global-completion question left
open by
[`rational_global_exceptional_tail_bridge_no_go.md`](rational_global_exceptional_tail_bridge_no_go.md)
for its explicit ruled-surface family.

Let \(S_m\to S_0=\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) be the iterated blowup used
there.  The first centre lies on a fibre \(c\), the second is the
intersection of its strict transform with the first exceptional curve, and
every later centre is a free point of the newest exceptional curve.  Let
\(d\) be the fibre of the other ruling through the first centre.  On the
standard full Neron--Severi host

\[
 L=\operatorname {NS}(S_m)
   =U\mathbin\perp\langle-1\rangle^m,
 \qquad q_M=2q_L,
\]

the conclusion is stronger than a tail bound:

> **Uniform completion obstruction.**  For every \(m\ge2\), for every
> allowed choice of the free centres, and independently of all hidden
> negative curves, the complete nef cone of \(S_m\) cannot be an
> absolutely reflection-maximal Coxeter chamber on the standard full host
> \(M=L\) (with either \(q_L\) or its harmless scaling \(2q_L\)).

The obstruction is the elementary ruling-swap root \(c-d\).  Its integral
mirror crosses the ample cone, so it is not in the nef facet group, while
adjoining it always gives a proper discrete reflection overgroup.

There is a second exact result.  The curves which are present on every
member of the family -- the affine \(B_m\) fibre and one horizontal
\((-1)\)-section -- bound a hyperbolic simplex of finite volume exactly for
\(m\le4\).  Starting at \(m=5\), any finite-volume completion therefore
requires genuinely hidden horizontal facets.  The first possible integral
truncators and their adjunction genera are computed below.  This identifies
the special incidence which a **nonmaximal** or deliberately relative-host
completion would still have to supply.

The result does not say that these surfaces cannot have rational-polyhedral
or finite-volume nef cones.  It does not apply after replacing the full
Neron--Severi host by a proper sublattice chosen to omit \(c-d\), after
passing to a proper invariant slice, or automatically to an adapted
index-two overhost generated by half-\((-4)\) classes.  On such an integral
overhost the ruling-swap formula on a half-boundary is

\[
 s_{c-d}(B/2)=B/2+{B.(c-d)\over2}(c-d).                 \tag{0.1}
\]

Thus preservation requires the parity test \(B.(c-d)\equiv0\pmod2\) for
every half-boundary generator.  There is no hidden odd escape through
\((c-d)/2\): if that half-root belonged to the integral overhost, its
pairing with every \(B/2\) would already force the same evenness.  These are
different carrier problems, and maximality relative to a restricted or
adapted host is not the full-NS reflection maximality audited here.

## 1. The curves which are present for every choice of centres

Use orthogonal total-transform coordinates

\[
 c^2=d^2=0,\qquad c.d=1,\qquad e_i^2=-1,
 \qquad e_i.e_j=c.e_i=d.e_i=0.
\]

Besides the affine fibre components

\[
 \delta _0=c-e_1-e_2,\qquad
 \delta_i=e_i-e_{i+1}\ (1\le i<m),\qquad
 \delta_m=e_m,                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

there is always the strict transform

\[
                         D=d-e_1.                    \tag{1.2}
\]

Indeed, the second centre is the tangent direction of \(c\), whereas the
strict transform of the other ruling fibre meets \(e_1\) at the other
point.  Thus later blowups do not meet \(D\), and \(D^2=-1\).

The classes \(D,\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_m\) form an integral basis of
\(L\).  One recovers

\[
 e_i=\delta_i+\cdots+\delta_m,\qquad
 d=D+e_1,
\]

and the affine null relation recovers \(c\):

\[
 c=\delta_0+\delta_1+2\delta_2+\cdots+2\delta_m.     \tag{1.3}
\]

Their only additional nonzero intersection is

\[
                         D.\delta_1=1.                \tag{1.4}
\]

Every displayed class is represented by an actual smooth rational curve,
and its reflection is integral: the squares are \(-2\), except for the two
short roots \(D\) and \(\delta_m\), whose squares are \(-1\).

## 2. Exact finite-volume threshold for the visible simplex

Let \(G_m\) be the intersection Gram matrix in the ordered basis

\[
                     (D,\delta_0,\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_m).
\]

Since this is a basis of \(U\perp\langle-1\rangle^m\), \(|\det G_m|=1\)
and its inertia is \((1,m+1)\).  The rays dual to its walls are especially
simple:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{deleted wall}&\text{dual ray}&\text{square}\\ \hline
D&c&0\\
\delta_0&d&0\\
\delta_1&c+d-e_1&1\\
\delta_j\ (2\le j\le m)
  &c+2d-e_1-\cdots-e_j&4-j.
\end{array}                                                \tag{2.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \operatorname {diag}(G_m^{-1})
       =(0,0,1,2,1,0,-1,-2,\ldots,4-m).               \tag{2.2}
\]

For a hyperbolic simplex, a deleted-wall link is finite or ideal exactly
when the corresponding dual ray has positive or zero square.  A negative
dual square is a hyperideal escape and gives infinite volume unless another
wall truncates it.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\text{the visible fibre--section simplex has finite volume}
        \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\le4.}          \tag{2.3}
\]

At \(m=5\) the first escaping ray is

\[
                  v_5=c+2d-e_1-\cdots-e_5,
                  \qquad v_5^2=-1.                    \tag{2.4}
\]

This calculation uses the complete vertex links, not a wall count or a
numerical volume approximation.

More generally, write a proposed hidden irreducible curve as

\[
                C=a c+b d-\sum_{i=1}^m\mu_i e_i.
\]

If every added wall has nonnegative pairing with \(v_5\), the hyperideal
escape survives.  Thus every finite-volume completion with \(m\ge5\) must
contain at least one hidden wall satisfying

\[
 C.v_5=2a+b-\sum_{i=1}^5\mu_i<0,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
 \boxed{\sum_{i=1}^5\mu_i>2a+b.}                       \tag{2.5}
\]

This is the first exact global incidence debt concealed by the local
affine packet.

## 3. The ruling-swap root kills absolute maximality uniformly

Put

\[
                              r=c-d.                    \tag{3.1}
\]

Then \(r^2=-2\), and on the doubled host \(q_M(r,r)=-4\).  In either
normalization its reflection is

\[
                    s_r(x)=x+(x.r)r.                    \tag{3.2}
\]

It is integral, fixes every exceptional coordinate, and swaps the two
ruling classes:

\[
                         s_r(c)=d,\qquad s_r(d)=c.       \tag{3.3}
\]

Its mirror does not merely touch the nef cone.  It crosses its interior.
Start with the ample class \(c+d\) on \(S_0\).  At each blowup
\(f_j:S_j\to S_{j-1}\), if \(A_{j-1}\) is ample then

\[
                       f_j^*A_{j-1}-\epsilon_jE_j       \tag{3.4}
\]

is ample for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon_j>0\).  Iterating gives an
ample real class

\[
                  A=\pi^*(c+d)-\sum_i\lambda_i e_i
\]

with \(\lambda_i>0\).  Every exceptional class is orthogonal to \(r\), so

\[
                              A.r=0.                    \tag{3.5}
\]

Now suppose the projectivized complete nef cone \(P\) were a finite-volume
Coxeter chamber, and let \(W(P)\) be its facet-reflection group.  A mirror
of a nonidentity reflection in \(W(P)\) cannot cross the interior of its
fundamental chamber.  Equation (3.5) therefore gives

\[
                              s_r\notin W(P).            \tag{3.6}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
                    W'=\langle W(P),s_r\rangle
                         \subset O^+(L)                  \tag{3.7}
\]

is a discrete hyperbolic reflection group because the integral orthogonal
group is discrete.  It properly contains \(W(P)\) by (3.6).  If maximality
is imposed only among finite-covolume or arithmetic reflection groups, the
same conclusion holds: a discrete overgroup of the supplied lattice
\(W(P)\) has finite index and finite covolume.

This proves the uniform obstruction.  Notice what does the work: the
global pullback frame remembers both rulings.  The local \(B_m\) cusp
calculation cannot see the mirror \(c-d\).

## 4. First truncating mirrors and their geometric cost

Although Section 3 already rules out absolute maximality, the hidden-wall
calculation remains useful for any deliberately weaker relative-host
problem.

For \(m=5\), the escaping ray itself is an integral root:

\[
                    T_5=c+2d-e_1-\cdots-e_5,
 \qquad T_5^2=-1,\quad K.T_5=-1,\quad p_a(T_5)=0.       \tag{4.1}
\]

The half-space with normal \(T_5\) truncates the first hyperideal vertex.
Moreover \(T_5\) is an effective divisor class for every allowed five-point
cluster: \(h^0(c+2d)=6\), while following the length-five cluster imposes at
most five linear conditions.  When those conditions are independent this
is the expected unique projective member of \(|c+2d|\).  This dimension
argument does **not** assert that the member is irreducible, smooth, or an
actual facet of the complete nef cone; those are additional geometric
conditions.

Starting at \(m=6\), the nearest tail-compatible integral mirrors after
the section wall \(D\) include the uniform family

\[
 R_a=a c+2d-e_1-\cdots-e_{4a+2},
 \qquad 1\le a\le\left\lfloor{m-2\over4}\right\rfloor. \tag{4.2}
\]

They satisfy

\[
 R_a^2=-2,qquad K.R_a=2a-2,qquad p_a(R_a)=a-1.         \tag{4.3}
\]

The first one,

\[
                  R_1=c+2d-e_1-\cdots-e_6,             \tag{4.4}
\]

has \(R_1.v_5=-1\), so the first square-minus-two mirror at the minimal
normalized height can cut the escape when six centres are present.  This
does not exclude the higher-height square-minus-one candidate \(T_5\),
which remains an integral class after further blowups.  The whole family is
compatible with the affine walls:

\[
 R_a.\delta_0=0,qquad
 R_a.\delta_i=0\ (i\ne4a+2),\qquad
 R_a.\delta_{4a+2}=1,                                   \tag{4.5}
\]

with the final formula interpreted as \(R_a.\delta_m=1\) when
\(4a+2=m\).  Also

\[
 R_a.D=a-1,qquad
 R_a.R_b=2(b-a-1)\quad(a<b).                             \tag{4.6}
\]

These formulas are obtained directly from the lattice.  Conversely, the
elementary cusp-height conditions classify (4.2) at the first positive
normalized height without a table.  A primitive integral mirror in the
unimodular lattice \(L\) can be normalized to have square \(-1\) or \(-2\).
A root

\[
 r=\alpha c+\beta d-\sum x_i e_i
\]

which pairs nonnegatively with the affine simple roots has

\[
 x_1\ge\cdots\ge x_m\ge0,
 \qquad \beta\ge x_1+x_2.                               \tag{4.7}
\]

Compatibility with \(D\) adds \(\alpha\ge x_1\).  A horizontal negative
root cannot have \(\beta=1\): (4.7) and \(\alpha\ge x_1\) would give
\(r^2\ge0\).  At the first available normalized height
\(\beta^2/(-r^2)=2\), one therefore has
\(\beta=2\), \(r^2=-2\), every nonzero \(x_i=1\), and the number of ones
to be \(4\alpha+2\).  This is exactly (4.2).

Physical irreducible realization is a genuine incidence condition.  The
class downstairs is \(a c+2d\), with

\[
 h^0\bigl(S_0,\mathcal O(a c+2d)\bigr)=3a+3.            \tag{4.8}
\]

Following \(4a+2\) prescribed infinitely-near centres gives a
\((4a+2)\)-row jet-evaluation map.  A nonzero kernel requires at least
\(a\) dependent conditions.  For an irreducible nonexceptional
representative of the exact class \(R_a\), all displayed virtual
multiplicities are one.  After the fixed first two centres the cluster has
\(4a\) free parameters, whereas the curves already satisfying the first
two conditions have projective dimension \(3a\); each such curve has only
finitely many continuing branch chains.  Hence the locus on which \(R_a\)
has an irreducible exact-multiplicity representative is a proper
special-incidence locus of codimension at least \(a\).

This codimension statement is not asserted for arbitrary reducible
effective divisors in the class.  Excess multiplicity can create
exceptional fixed components and moving later cluster directions.  The
row-dependency condition remains necessary for any effective divisor, but
irreducibility and exact multiplicities are what justify the incidence
dimension bound relevant to a physical facet.

There is also an immediate physical-color obstruction.  Only \(R_1\) has
the adjunction data of a possible smooth-rational \((-2)\)-wall.  For every
\(a\ge2\), (4.3) has positive arithmetic genus.  Thus, if one asks for the
whole first-height mirror family while insisting on the project's physical
smooth-rational wall alphabet, that family leaves the alphabet once
\(m\ge10\).  This is not a claim that every \(R_a\) is effective or that
reflection maximality turns every lattice mirror into a nef facet.
Allowing positive-genus negative curves avoids the color obstruction, but
any member actually used as a wall must still satisfy its growing special
jet incidence; no such unbounded complete family is constructed here.

## 5. Companion affine-H3 gate: semiampleness has index one

The same global-completion audit exposes and repairs a separate issue in
the affine H3 star.  Work over \(\mathbf C\), and let

\[
 F=4E+B_1+B_2+B_3+B_4                              \tag{5.1}
\]

be the nef isotropic divisor with \(E^2=-1\), \(B_i^2=-4\), the \(B_i\)
pairwise disjoint, and \(E.B_i=1\).  Thus

\[
 F.E=F.B_i=0,\qquad F^2=0,\qquad K_S.F=4.           \tag{5.2}
\]

The earlier numerical argument must not write a base-point-free multiple as
a primitive fibre.  Halphen fibrations show why: a connected fibre class
can be divisible in the Neron--Severi lattice.  If the least free multiple
were \(qF\), its general member would have genus \(2q+1\), so the old
genus-two/genus-three dichotomy did not follow from numerical primitivity.

For this particular nonreduced star, however, scheme-theoretic geometry
forces \(q=1\).

### Proposition 5.1 (index-one affine-H3 abundance)

If \(F\) is semiample, then \(|F|\) itself is base-point-free.  Its
connected-fibre morphism has general fibre of genus three.

### Proof

Put

\[
                         C=F_{\mathrm {red}}
                           =E+B_1+B_2+B_3+B_4.
\]

The reduced curve \(C\) is an SNC tree of five copies of \(\mathbf P^1\).
Consequently \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(C)=0\).  Let
\(\mathcal I\) be the nilradical of \(C\subset F\).  Since
\(\mathcal I\) is nilpotent and the ground field has characteristic zero,
the finite logarithm gives an isomorphism of abelian sheaves

\[
                         1+\mathcal I\simeq(\mathcal I,+).  \tag{5.3}
\]

The unit sequence

\[
 1\longrightarrow1+\mathcal I\longrightarrow\mathcal O_F^*
  \longrightarrow\mathcal O_C^*\longrightarrow1             \tag{5.4}
\]

therefore identifies the kernel of
\(\operatorname {Pic}(F)\to\operatorname {Pic}(C)\) with an additive
complex vector group.  Constants lift, and \(H^2(C,\mathcal I)=0\), so no
multiplicative quotient is hidden in this statement.  In particular the
multidegree-zero group \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(F)\) has no nontrivial
finite torsion.

The normal line bundle

\[
                             N=\mathcal O_F(F)             \tag{5.5}
\]

has degree zero on every reduced component by (5.2), hence lies in
\(\operatorname {Pic}^0(F)\).  If \(|qF|\) is base-point-free, choose a
member \(G\sim qF\) distinct from \(qF\).  Since \(G.F=0\), a general such
member has no common component with, and is therefore disjoint from,
\(F\).  Its defining section is a unit in every local ring of the whole
nonreduced divisor \(F\), and gives

\[
                             N^{\otimes q}\simeq\mathcal O_F. \tag{5.6}
\]

Torsion-freeness then gives \(N\simeq\mathcal O_F\).

Finally use

\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S(F)
   \longrightarrow\mathcal O_F(F)\longrightarrow0.          \tag{5.7}
\]

Because \(S\) is rational, \(H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0\).  The constant section
\(1\in H^0(F,\mathcal O_F)\) lifts to a section of \(\mathcal O_S(F)\)
which is nowhere zero along \(F\).  Together with the canonical section
whose zero divisor is \(F\), it generates \(\mathcal O_S(F)\) everywhere.
Thus \(|F|\) is base-point-free.  The component coefficients of the member
\(F\) have gcd one, so its pencil has Stein degree one rather than a hidden
multiple-fibre degree.  Adjunction gives

\[
                    p_a(F)=1+{F^2+K.F\over2}=3.             \tag{5.8}
\]

This proves the proposition. \(\square\)

After contracting vertical \((-1)\)-curves to a relatively minimal model,
the relative canonical divisor of this genus-three fibration is nef.  Hence

\[
 0\le K_f^2=10-\rho(S_{\min})+16,
 \qquad\boxed{\rho(S_{\min})\le26}.                         \tag{5.9}
\]

There is no genus-two/\(18\) branch.  The result remains conditional on
semiampleness, and (5.9) still does not bound the number of vertical
exceptional blowups on \(S\).

The tempting replacement by a lattice-divisibility argument is false.  For
an exact calibration, take

\[
                         M=U\mathbin\perp D_4(-1),           \tag{5.10}
\]

whose discriminant group has exponent two.  Let \(u\) be a primitive
isotropic vector of \(U\), let \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_4\) be finite
\(D_4\) simple roots with highest root \(\theta\), and for any
\(N\ge1\) put

\[
                         \alpha_0=Nu-\theta.                 \tag{5.11}
\]

The five roots have the same affine \(D_4\) Gram matrix for every \(N\),
but their positive mark relation is

\[
                         \alpha_0+\theta=Nu.                 \tag{5.12}
\]

Thus an exponent-two ambient discriminant does not bound the saturation
index of an affine null relation.  The index-one conclusion above comes
from the torsion-free Picard group of the actual nonreduced rational tree,
not from the numerical cusp lattice.

## 6. Claim boundary

The conclusions, in descending order of strength, are:

1. On the standard full Neron--Severi host of the explicit
   \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) blowup family, absolute reflection
   maximality is impossible for **every** \(m\), because of \(c-d\).
2. Ignoring maximality, the universally visible fibre--section chamber has
   finite volume exactly for \(m\le4\).
3. From \(m=5\) onward a complete finite-volume nef cone must contain
   hidden curves satisfying (2.5).  The first integral truncators are
   represented by the candidates (4.1)--(4.2); irreducible
   exact-multiplicity realization of \(R_a\) is a growing special-jet
   condition.
4. This does not rule out specially tuned finite-volume **nonmaximal** nef
   chambers, positive-genus wall systems, or maximality relative to a
   smaller host from which the ruling-swap reflection has been deleted.  An
   index-two half-boundary overhost is likewise outside the theorem unless
   every half-boundary passes the ruling-swap parity test.
5. In affine H3, semiampleness (if supplied) has index one and gives only
   the genus-three core bound \(\rho(S_{\min})\le26\).  Semiampleness itself
   and the vertical-tail length remain separate open inputs.

Accordingly, the affine packet remains a sharp local no-go for naive tail
bounds, but it is not the seed of an unbounded reflection-maximal rational
surface counterfamily.  Its natural global host contains an elementary
missing reflection before any appeal to a classification theorem.
