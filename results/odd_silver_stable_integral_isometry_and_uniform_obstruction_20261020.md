# Odd silver: stable integral isometry and a uniform class obstruction

Paper proof, 20 October 2026; no scientific execution. This is a successor
to the unchanged [two-edge note](compact_h9_odd_silver_diagonal_two_edges_20261020.md),
SHA256 `85095d203aea614961425bf356cfbad21529cd9f6f01d32b77f73cc5b2ce234c`.
Put `s=√2`, `α=1+s`, `O=Z[s]`. The F4 lattice below has diagonal 2 and
successive path products `−1,−s,−1`.

## 1. The exact module statement

There is an integral O-isometry

    <-α> ⊥ I5  ≅  <-α> ⊥ F4 ⊥ <1>.

Consequently, applying this twice,

    L9=<-α> ⊥ I9  ≅  <-α> ⊥ F4 ⊥ F4 ⊥ <1>
                  ≅  <-α> ⊥ E8(O) ⊥ <1>.

The last isometry is the already preserved explicit matrix proof in
[fresh_silver_e8_f4_identification_20261016.md](fresh_silver_e8_f4_identification_20261016.md).
The new first isometry is proved below by explicit local bases and
Eichler's strong approximation theorem. It is an existence proof of an
integral basis, not a claimed newly computed integral matrix.

### 1.1 Explicit bases establish the genus, including the dyadic place

In Euclidean I5 set

    c  = (α,1−s,1,1,1)/3,
    f1 = e3−e4,
    f2 = e4−e5,
    f3 = (α^(-1),α,−s,−s,2s)/3,
    f4 = (1−2s,−(2+s),1,1,1)/3.

Their exact products are

    c²=1,       c.fi=0,
    fi²=2,      f1.f2=−1, f2.f3=−s, f3.f4=−1,
    f1.f3=f1.f4=f2.f4=0.

The F4 Gram determinant is 1 (successive path determinants are 2,3,2,1).
Thus the displayed five-column matrix has determinant ±1 and inverse
over `O[1/3]`. It proves the positive lattices `I5` and `F4+<1>` are
locally isometric at every prime not over 3, in particular at the
ramified dyadic prime `(s)`.

At primes over 3, use the familiar F4 basis in I4

    g1=e2−e3,       g2=e3−e4,       g3=s e4,
    g4=(e1−e2−e3−e4)/s,

and the fifth unit basis vector. Its Gram is the same F4 Gram, determinant
1, and its only denominators are powers of s, a unit at 3. These are
literal local integral bases; no classification of dyadic lattices has
been assumed. Adding the identical time coordinate shows the two
rank-six lattices in the assertion belong to the same genus.

### 1.2 Every finite local proper-spinor group contains all units

Let `Λ=<-α>+I5` and `θ` be the spinor norm, with
`θ(τ_v)=v²` modulo squares. At each odd finite prime, I2 represents
every local unit: solve the sum-of-two-squares equation over the finite
residue field and lift a solution with a nonzero derivative. Reflection
in a vector of unit norm preserves Λ locally. Composing it with a
norm-1 coordinate reflection makes determinant +1 without changing
the spinor norm. Therefore

    θ(SO(Λ_p)) ⊇ O_p^× modulo squares      (p not dyadic).

For the dyadic completion `K_s=Q2(s)` it is enough to generate the unit
squareclasses by `−1,5,α`. Here is a direct verification of sufficiency.
A local unit has the form `u=a+b s`, with `a∈Z2^×`, `b∈Z2`, hence
`N(u)=a²−2b²≡1 or7 (mod8)`. Multiplying u by α if necessary makes
its norm a square in Q2. Divide by the rational square root of that
norm and apply the elementary quadratic Hilbert-90 identity: a norm-one
element w is `v/v'`, with `v=1+w` unless `w=−1`, when `v=s` works.
Modulo squares `v/v'=v²/N(v)` is a rational class. The rational
squareclasses in K_s are generated by `−1,5`, since 2=s². Thus every
local unit class lies in the group generated by `−1,5,α`.

All three classes occur in the local proper-spinor group. The norm 5 is
represented by `(1,2,0,0,0)` in I5. The norm −1 is represented in I4 by
`(√−7,1,1,2)`; the root `√−7∈Z2` exists since `−7≡1 (mod8)`.
Finally the time basis vector has norm `−α`. Each norm is a local
unit, so each symmetry is integral; composition with a norm-1
reflection gives the required proper isometry. The classes `−1` and
`−α` supply α as well. Hence

    θ(SO(Λ_s)) ⊇ O_s^× modulo squares.

This checks the ramified prime explicitly; unimodularity by itself was
not used as an unexplained spinor-norm criterion.

### 1.3 The global step and its sign boundary

The ring O is a PID and its units −1 and α realize all real sign
patterns. Thus every finite fractional ideal has a principal generator
which is positive at the nonphysical embedding. The local unit result
in Section 1.2 removes every residual finite unit spinor class.

For completeness, the global spinor-norm condition introduces no extra
obstruction. Every `a∈K^×` positive at the nonphysical embedding is
represented by the rational quadratic space `V=KΛ`. Indeed `V⊥<-a>`
has dimension 7, is indefinite at both real places, and is isotropic at
every finite place (local quadratic spaces of dimension >4 are
isotropic). Hasse–Minkowski makes it isotropic over K. The coefficient
in the last summand cannot vanish because V is anisotropic over K,
being positive definite at the other real place. It follows that V
represents a. Composing the corresponding symmetry with a norm-1
symmetry gives a global proper isometry of spinor norm a.

One can now perform the standard genus-to-spinor-genus reduction
explicitly. For an adelic proper isometry taking Λ to a genus mate,
choose a principal generator a of its finite spinor valuations, with
the stated positive conjugate sign. Modify on the right by local
lattice stabilizers to remove the residual unit classes, and on the
left by the global isometry of spinor norm a. The resulting local
maps have trivial spinor norm and lift to Spin(V).

**External theorem used:** strong approximation for Spin(V) away from
the physical real place, equivalently Eichler's theorem that a proper
spinor genus has one proper class when the quadratic space is indefinite
at a real place and has rank at least 3. It applies here: rank 6,
signature `(5,1)` at the physical embedding, and a noncompact physical
spin group. It does not require V to be isotropic over K. Consequently
the spinor-genus reduction just obtained yields an integral isometry
between Λ and its displayed genus mate.

The precise strong-approximation statement is Kneser–Platonov, as stated
in the primary [Cao–Huang, *Arithmetic purity of strong approximation
for semisimple simply connected groups*, Theorem 1.1, v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.06967v4):
strong approximation off a finite set S holds exactly when each
K-simple factor has a noncompact group over S. Here Spin(V) in rank 6
is absolutely almost simple, semisimple and simply connected, and we
take S to consist of the physical real place. Its physical group is
noncompact. These verify the exact criterion used, even though V is
anisotropic over K. If the resulting integral isometry exchanges the
two physical time cones, multiply it by −I to make it future-preserving;
in ranks 6 and 10 this also preserves determinant +1.

References for the precise number-field framework are
[O'Meara, *Introduction to Quadratic Forms*, §§102–104, especially
104:5](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-662-41922-9), and the
primary application of the same idelic genus count/strong-approximation
principle in
[He–Hu–Xu, *On indefinite k-universal integral quadratic forms over
number fields*, Example 5.2 and Section 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.10730).
The explicit local bases and local spinor computations above are our
inputs to that theorem, rather than borrowed classification assertions.

## 2. Consequence for the whole diagonal class

The existing full-arrangement proof establishes nonreflectivity of
`S=<-α>+E8(O)+<1>`; see the unchanged
[silver E8 proof](fresh_silver_e8_h9_continuation_20261016.md) and
[scope audit](fresh_silver_e8_chamber_scope_20261016.md).
The integral isometry in Section 1 conjugates *all* integral reflections,
so the full L9 chamber also has infinite volume. This is stronger than
mere commensurability, which would not have justified the transfer.

The full coordinate-short-facet identity in Section 3.2 of the two-edge
note is uniform: an actual norm-1 coordinate facet of L_n has exactly
the full L_(n−1) mirror arrangement. If the full L_n chamber were
finite-volume, that actual facet and its iterated coordinate facets
would have finite volume. (Facets of finite-volume acute Coxeter
polytopes inherit finite volume.) For `n≥9` this would produce a
finite-volume full L9 chamber, a contradiction. Therefore

> For every n≥9, the full integral reflection group of
> `L_n=<-1−√2>+I_n` has infinite index in its arithmetic isometry group.
> No finite-volume Coxeter subgroup with all reflections preserving
> this literal lattice can exist.

This is a uniform obstruction for one diagonal lattice family, not a
dimension bound over `Q(√2)` and not an exclusion of every lattice in
the rational commensurability class. No further L9 edge work is needed.

## 3. Low-rank status and limits

The primary [Bogachev thesis summary, Table 5](https://www.hse.ru/data/2018/12/29/1142187645/Bogachev-Summary.pdf)
explicitly reports `M4=<-√2>+I4` reflective with eight facets.
Accordingly this particular lower facet does not supply an obstruction.
The rank-four (hyperbolic dimension-three) classification, Theorem C
and Table 4 in
[Bogachev's paper](https://nvbogachev.netlify.app/Bogachev-GeomArithmCoxeter-2020.pdf),
also confirms L3 and M3 reflective. These statements are low-rank
inputs only. The present argument does not decide reflectivity of L5;
it identifies L5 with the one-F4-plus-line host. It avoids an unnecessary
low-rank or higher-dimensional census entirely by settling the integral
duplicate needed for the uniform n≥9 obstruction.
