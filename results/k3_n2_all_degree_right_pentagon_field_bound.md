# Compact K3 right pentagons have a finite field set in every allowed degree

**Status (2026-08-11): proved for all full-trace complex-K3 degrees.**
Let a rank-three module over a totally real field return an exact compact
right-angled Coxeter pentagon in \(\mathbf H^2\).  The full trace has rank
\(3d\), so the K3 Picard ceiling gives

\[
                              d=[k:\mathbf Q]\le6.
\tag{0.1}
\]

The five four-normal Gram identities form the cyclic \(A_2\) recurrence.
Integral pair reflections make four times every shifted squared wall ratio
an algebraic integer.  The signed-place norm therefore gives

\[
              4^{-d}<x_i<(4^d+1)^2-1
              \qquad(0\le i<5).
\tag{0.2}
\]

In particular, for every K3-allowed degree,

\[
                 x_i<16,785,408,
                 \qquad x_i+1<4097^2=16,785,409.
\tag{0.3}
\]

The five pair cycles and the directed length-five cycle are consequently
bounded at every embedding.  If \(k\) is the ground field generated by this
cyclic Cartan packet, a bounded primitive integral combination gives

\[
 |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|
 \le
 \bigl(1,182,033,476,457,431,303,168\bigr)^{30}.
\tag{0.4}
\]

Hermite--Minkowski therefore gives a finite field set.  No imposed
correspondence height, power relation between the free pentagon parameters,
angle classification, or reflective-lattice classification is used.
Exact same-chamber return, complete reflection saturation, and relative
reflection maximality are retained, but the bound is proved before
maximality enters.

The previously frozen quadratic theorem remains sharper:

\[
                    |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|\le1,343,277
                    \qquad(d=2).
\tag{0.5}
\]

## 1. Exact carrier package

Let \(k\) be totally real of degree \(d\), let \(\mathcal O\subset k\) be a
returned order, and let \(M\) be a finite projective rank-three
\(\mathcal O\)-module with an admissible symmetric \(k\)-form \(h\).  At one
distinguished embedding \(\sigma_0\), the form has signature \((2,1)\); at
all other embeddings it is positive definite.  Assume:

1. a minimal complex projective K3 surface \(X\) has Picard lattice equal
   to the primitive full trace of \((M,h)\), with the returned order acting
   self-adjointly by divisor-product correspondences;
2. the prescribed projectivized eigen-nef chamber is exactly a compact
   right-angled Coxeter pentagon in \(\mathbf H^2\);
3. its five facet reflections preserve \(M\), the restricted K3 mirror
   packet is complete and saturated, and its group is reflection-maximal
   among integral \(k\)-linear reflections of the declared order host; and
4. \(k\) is the ground field generated by the cyclic Cartan products of
   these five facet reflections.

The primitive full trace has rank \(3d=\rho(X)\le20\), proving (0.1).  The
rational case is already finite, so below \(2\le d\le6\).  The projective
nef dimension is \(\operatorname {rank}_k(M)-1=2\).

## 2. The five shifted pair coordinates

Choose cyclic facet normals \(e_0,\ldots,e_4\).  Adjacent normals are
orthogonal.  For the five nonadjacent pairs, let \(A_i\in k\) be their
invariant squared normalized correlations, indexed cyclically around the
nonadjacency five-cycle, and put

\[
                              x_i=A_i-1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

No normalized square root is asserted to lie in \(k\).  At the
distinguished place, nonadjacent sides of a compact right pentagon are
ultraparallel, hence

\[
                              (x_i)_0>0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

At every definite embedding \(\sigma\), strict Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
                              -1<\sigma(x_i)<0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The endpoints are excluded over every embedding: the relevant pairing and
wedge are nonzero field tensors at \(\sigma_0\), and field embeddings are
injective.

## 3. Exact five-equation closure

Every four of the five normals lie in a three-dimensional field space.
Their five principal Gram determinants vanish.  Because the adjacent
pairings are zero, the identities become

\[
                   x_{i-1}x_{i+1}=x_i+1
                   \qquad(i\bmod5).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently, if \(r=x_0\) and \(s=x_2\), then

\[
 x_1=rs-1,
 \qquad x_3={s+1\over rs-1},
 \qquad x_4={r+1\over rs-1}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

All five identities, not just the displayed parametrization, are used when
the bound is rotated around the pentagon.

## 4. The all-place norm floor

For each nonadjacent pair, the pair cyclic Cartan product is \(4A_i\).  It
belongs to \(\mathcal O\): it differs by an integer from the module trace
of the product of the two integral facet reflections.  Therefore

\[
                         y_i:=4x_i=4A_i-4\in\mathcal O.
\tag{4.1}
\]

At \(\sigma_0\), \((y_i)_0>0\), while at each of the \(d-1\) definite
places, \(-4<\sigma(y_i)<0\).  Its nonzero norm is an integer, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
1
&\le\left|N_{k/\mathbf Q}(y_i)\right|\\
&=(y_i)_0\prod_{\sigma\ne\sigma_0}|\sigma(y_i)|
 <(y_i)_0\,4^{d-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

It follows that

\[
                              (x_i)_0>4^{-d}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the exact all-degree replacement for the quadratic lower bound
\(1/16\).  One cannot retain \(1/16\) after increasing \(d\) without a new
divisibility theorem.

## 5. The corrected cyclic upper bound

Put \(\varepsilon=4^{-d}\) and fix an index \(i\).  Write
\(z=x_{i+2}\).  Two instances of (3.1) give

\[
 x_{i+1}=x_i z-1,
 \qquad
 x_{i+3}={z+1\over x_i z-1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

All distinguished coordinates are positive.  Using both lower bounds
\(x_{i+3}>\varepsilon\) and \(z>\varepsilon\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \varepsilon
 &<{z+1\over x_i z-1},\\
 x_i
 &<\varepsilon^{-1}+{1+\varepsilon\over\varepsilon z}\\
 &<\varepsilon^{-2}+2\varepsilon^{-1}
   =(4^d+1)^2-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This proves (0.2).  At \(d=6\), \(4^d=4096\), and (0.3) follows.

The tempting value \(16+17\cdot4^6=69,648\) is not automatic: it keeps
the quadratic bound \(x_{i+3}>1/16\) while weakening only the bound on
\(z\).  In degree greater than two, (4.3) supplies \(4^{-d}\) for both
variables.  The corrected bound remains uniform and is all that field
finiteness needs.

## 6. Bounded cyclic packet and field finiteness

The nonzero Cartan graph of a right pentagon is itself a five-cycle.
Consequently, its simple cyclic packet consists of the five pair products
and the directed length-five product \(c_5\).  Reflection-word trace
induction on the finite projective order-module puts all of them in
\(\mathcal O\).

At \(\sigma_0\), (0.3) gives

\[
                    0<4A_i<4\cdot4097^2.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Symmetrizability of the Cartan matrix gives

\[
                  c_5^2=4^5\prod_{i=0}^4 A_i.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus

\[
                    |(c_5)_0|<32\cdot4097^5.
\tag{6.3}
\]

At every definite embedding, pair cycles have magnitude below four and
the length-five cycle has magnitude below \(32\).  Hence every generator
of the cyclic ground field has every conjugate bounded by

\[
 H:=32\cdot4097^5
   =36,938,546,139,294,728,224.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Choose a field-generating subpacket minimally.  Each strict adjunction has
degree at least two, so \(d\le6\) requires at most two generators
\(\theta_1,\theta_2\).  Among

\[
                       \theta_1+c\theta_2,
                       \qquad 0\le c\le15,
\tag{6.5}
\]

some element is primitive: each unordered pair of the at most six
embeddings forbids at most one value of \(c\), and there are at most
\(\binom62=15\) pairs.  The chosen primitive algebraic integer \(\theta\)
satisfies \(|\sigma(\theta)|\le16H\), so

\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|
 &\le |\operatorname {Disc}(\mathbf Z[\theta])|\\
 &\le(32H)^{d(d-1)}\\
 &\le
 \bigl(1,182,033,476,457,431,303,168\bigr)^{30}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Hermite--Minkowski proves the finite field set.

### Theorem 6.1 (all-degree compact-right-pentagon field theorem)

Every exact full-trace complex-K3 carrier satisfying Section 1 and having
a compact right-angled pentagon as its prescribed eigen-nef chamber belongs
to a finite set of totally real cyclic ground fields.  The explicit bound
is (0.4).  No bounded-height hypothesis is added.

### Quadratic compatibility

For \(d=2\), (4.3)--(5.2) recover
\(1/16<x_i<288\).  Moreover, any pair cycle has distinguished value in
\((4,1156)\) and conjugate value in \((0,4)\), hence is nonrational and
generates the quadratic field.  The frozen quadratic argument then gives
(0.5), much sharper than (0.4).

## 7. Scope

The theorem proves a finite field set, not merely bounded field degree.  It
does not:

- prove that every exact \(n=2\) chamber is a right pentagon;
- treat non-right or noncompact pentagons;
- allow a gratuitous declared field extension larger than the cyclic
  Cartan ground field;
- construct a K3 carrier satisfying the hypotheses; or
- show that reflection maximality alone bounds a canonical polarization
  correspondence height in arbitrary incidence.

The all-degree norm floor and the full five-equation closure are the
decisive ingredients.  The remaining incidence-independent problem is to
obtain comparable bounds for every compact or finite-volume \(n=2\)
Coxeter polygon.
