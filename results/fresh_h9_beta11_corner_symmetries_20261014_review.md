## Why these are complete corner sectors

Let v be a finite vertex of an acute Vinberg prefix, with nine incident
outward normals n_i and positive-definite Gram matrix B. Because B has
nonpositive off-diagonal entries, B inverse is entrywise nonnegative. For the
future timelike Vinberg basepoint H, write

`H = a v + sum b_i n_i`, where `a = (H,v)/(v,v) > 0` and
`b = B inverse * ((H,n_i)) <= 0`.

Every later simple wall r has `(H,r)<0` and `(n_i,r)<=0`. Therefore

`a(v,r) <= (H,r) < 0`.

No later wall cuts or touches v. The verifier checks this decomposition
explicitly at the target corner and checks the required Gram signs and
positive definiteness. The complete zero-height sector has already been
enumerated. Local finiteness follows from the exact two-embedding bounds:
only finitely many mirrors meet a bounded ball, since their heights are
bounded and the primitive norm alphabet is finite. Thus a neighborhood of
each finite vertex has the complete chamber sector, without a distance
restriction on the vertex.

## Why the isometry proves infinite volume

An integral lattice isometry preserves the entire arrangement of integral
reflection mirrors, including both norm classes. Here T maps all incident
outward normals of one complete sector to those of the other, with a positive
time scale. It consequently sends the full connected reflection chamber to
itself. An abstract diagram isomorphism alone would not suffice; the literal
integral oriented map is essential.

A finite-order real 10-by-10 matrix has all eigenvalues on the unit circle,
so its trace has absolute value at most 10. The checked trace proves T has
infinite order. The integral isometry group is discrete: at the conjugate
embedding it preserves a positive-definite form, and bounded matrix entries
at both embeddings contain only finitely many algebraic integers. Its
infinite chamber stabilizer therefore produces infinitely many disjoint
translates of a small interior ball within the chamber. The chamber has
infinite volume.

Any reflection subgroup generated by lattice-preserving walls lies in this
full reflection group. It cannot have finite covolume when the full group has
infinite covolume. This gives the stated host-wide consequence.
