## Why the optimistic test is rigorous

For one forest let G be the current conservative compatibility graph, let d be its actual orbit demand, and let t(G,d) be the minimum mass of a nonnegative cover by graph cliques. Its dual has nonnegative orbit charges whose sum on every compatible clique is at most1. The frozen full-demand optimum and attaining cover are checked first.

Deleting edges makes the graph-clique covering problem stronger, so t cannot decrease. If E is one proposed unowned domain and U is the set of all currently unowned pairs, then

\[
t(G,d)\le t(G-E,d)\le t(G-U,d).
\]

An explicit cover for G−U is therefore also an upper-bound certificate for every individual G−E problem. The code first tests G−U in every forest, then tests individual domains only where the all-unowned test permits an increase above the existing vertex multiplier.

Two exact certificate forms are used:

* **Feasible split cover.** Each old attaining literal clique is partitioned into cliques of the graph after the hypothetical deletion. Its original rational mass is placed on every part. All parts and demand coverage are checked. For a single orbit-pair domain, separating one of its orbits from the remaining vertices gives at most two valid parts. If the resulting total mass is at most the existing vertex multiplier, this proves zero possible witness gain without a local optimizer. The old nonnegative charge gives the unchanged lower bound under edge deletion.
* **Matching charge and cover.** Where that shortcut does not settle the question, complete maximal literal cliques and maximal orbit-count vectors are enumerated. An exact nonnegative charge and an equal-value rational covering mixture certify the hypothetical optimum. All recorded literal representatives are checked in the changed conservative graph. No partial clique enumeration is accepted as an exact result.

All enumeration/recovery guards were resolved in this run. Some individual local optima need only a verified interval; every individual possible **vertex-multiplier increase** is nevertheless decided exactly. In forests with enough existing multiplier slack, the local optimum can rise while the required witness multiplier stays unchanged.
