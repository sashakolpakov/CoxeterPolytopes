## From the twenty folded roots to all 36 curves

Write

\[
 B=B_1+B_2\in|-2K_S|,\qquad h_i=B_i/2.
\]

list of effective irreducible Coble--Mukai roots, and that its chamber has
29 primitive isotropic cusp rays.  Sixteen roots are actual `(-2)`-curves.
The other four have the form

\[
             \alpha_j=2E_j+h_1+h_2,
\]

and give four `(-1)`-curves meeting both boundaries once.

This species marking is recovered from the Gram matrix without importing a
diagram drawing: the four circle vertices are the unique roots pairing
evenly with every other root.  Equivalently, they form the unique four-set
whose six internal pairings all equal `2`.

The full Picard glue is likewise forced.  The lattice generated by the two
boundaries, sixteen `(-2)`-curves, four crossing `(-1)`-curves, and `K_S`
has determinant `-4` and Smith diagonal `1^10 2^2`.  Direct discriminant
enumeration finds exactly one integral index-two overclosure.  It is the
odd unimodular rational-surface lattice

\[
                   \operatorname{NS}(S)\cong I_{1,11}.
\]

Adjunction gives `B.E=2` for every `(-1)`-curve.  Apart from the four
crossing curves, it must therefore meet one boundary twice and the other
not at all.  Such a curve is

\[
                         E=f-h_i
\]

for a primitive nef isotropic CM vector `f`.  Conversely, Kondō's
genus-one-fibration correspondence and Halphen blow-up theorem produce the
exceptional curve whenever the cusp is boundary-split.  In the unique full


negative curve.  This exhausts the full nef wall set; exact double

\[

\[


14 walls.  The 36-wall group has finite index, although odd-dimensional
Coxeter Euler characteristics do not yield its numerical index.
