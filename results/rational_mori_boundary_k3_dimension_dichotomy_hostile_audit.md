## 1. Mori generators and the weak-anticanonical branch

The spanning statement is automatic from the hypothesis, not a separate
assumption.  Curve classes span \(N_1(S)_{\mathbf Q}\) by definition, and
the complete closed Mori cone is assumed to equal the cone generated by the
displayed curves.  Therefore those generators span the ambient numerical
curve space.  A finite list of wall classes known only to span a lattice
would not suffice.

Adjunction gives \(K_S C=-2-C^2\).  On the weak alphabet, \(-K_S\) has
degree one on every \((-1)\)-generator and degree zero on every
\((-2)\)-generator.  Since the list generates the whole closed Mori cone,
\(-K_S\) is nef.  Hence \(K_S^2\geq0\), and the rational-surface identity
\(K_S^2+\rho(S)=10\) gives \(\rho(S)\leq10\).  This part is valid in every
characteristic.

## 2. From numerical orthogonality to a linear branch divisor

For the index-two alphabet, \(L=K_S+B/2\) pairs to zero with every Mori
generator:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{boundary}&(-4,-4)\Longrightarrow 2L.B_i=0\\
\text{root}&(-2,0)\Longrightarrow 2L.C=0\\
\text{crossing}&(-1,2)\Longrightarrow 2L.C=0.
\end{array}
\]

Spanning gives \(L\equiv0\), but the integral object used for descent to
Picard theory is \(2L=2K_S+B\).  On a smooth rational surface,
\(\operatorname{Pic}^0(S)=0\) and \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)\) is torsion-free;
thus an integral numerically trivial divisor is linearly trivial.  Therefore
\(B=-2K_S\) in \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)\).  The revised proof no longer
silently treats the rational class \(B/2\) as an integral line bundle.

## 3. Smoothness, connectedness, and the characteristic boundary

When the characteristic is not two, the disjoint union of the smooth curves
\(B_i\) is a smooth effective divisor.  The section cutting out \(B\) and
\(\mathcal O_S(B)\simeq\mathcal O_S(-2K_S)\) define the finite flat cover
with algebra \(\mathcal O_S\oplus\mathcal O_S(K_S)\).

Near the branch it has local equation \(z^2=t\), so it is smooth because
\(2\neq0\) and \(dt\neq0\); away from the branch it is etale.  A disconnected
degree-two cover of connected normal \(S\) would be two degree-one copies of
\(S\) and hence unbranched, so the nonempty branch forces connectedness.
The canonical-cover formula gives \(K_X=0\), while the displayed pushforward,
rationality of \(S\), and Serre duality give \(H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0\) and
\(\chi(\mathcal O_X)=2\).  Thus \(X\) is K3.

No separable-double-cover or K3 conclusion is made in characteristic two.
The Picard ceiling is \(20\) in characteristic zero and \(22\) in odd
positive characteristic.  Pullback is injective on numerical divisor
classes and doubles the pairing, so
\(\operatorname{NS}(S)(2)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{NS}(X)\).

## 4. Literal controls and their limit

The original certifier only checked that five known ranks happened to lie
below the proposed ceilings.  It now pins the five underlying full-nef
archives and replays their physical wall Grams.

| control | physical curve alphabet | Gram rank |
|---|---|---:|
| toric H6/9 | \(6(-2)+3(-1)\) | 7 |
| Coble II | \(1(-4)+12(-2)+17(-1)\) | 11 |
| Coble I | \(2(-4)+10(-2)+14(-1)\) | 12 |
| Coble VI, characteristic 3 | \(5(-4)+10(-2)+45(-1)\) | 15 |
| Coble MII, characteristic 3 | \(8(-4)+24(-2)+16(-1)\) | 18 |

For every Coble row, the replay checks pairwise-disjoint square-minus-four
boundaries and verifies that each other generator has
\((C^2,B.C)=(-2,0)\) or \((-1,2)\).  The Type-II source stores the first
normal as \(b=-K_S=B/2\); its row and column are doubled before the physical
alphabet is tested.  The wall-Gram ranks equal the respective Picard ranks.

These computations verify the alphabet and spanning data literally.
Generation of the **entire Mori cone** is still imported from the pinned
full-nef source theorems; this small replay does not independently repeat
their curve-exhaustion or no-hidden-wall arguments.  The two
characteristic-three rows are ceiling-22 controls and are not evidence for
the complex ceiling 20.
