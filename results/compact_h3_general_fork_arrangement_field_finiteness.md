# Compact hyperbolic three-space: a general-fork arrangement field theorem

**Status (2026-08-12): theorem, pending independent hostile audit.** This
note closes the compact dimension-three ground-field gap left by the global
minimality-\(14\) funnel. The proof uses the exact real-framed
quadric--hyperplane arrangement carrier, integral Cartan-cycle coordinates,
one new three-wall determinant contraction, and the already proved
two-dimensional real-DM orbifold-curve theorem. It does **not** invoke ABSW,
Nikulin's finiteness theorem for reflective lattices, a pre-existing global
reflection-field theorem, a volume formula, or a classification of compact
hyperbolic Coxeter polyhedra.

## 1. The theorem

Let \(P\subset\mathbb H^3\) be a compact arithmetic Coxeter chamber of
simplest type, and let \(k(P)\) be its minimal Vinberg cycle field.

> **Theorem A (compact \(H^3\) field finiteness).** The fields \(k(P)\)
> occurring among all such \(P\) form a fixed finite set.
>
> More precisely, every \(k(P)\) is either in the already finite
> rational/Lanner/triangle/transition packet, is the field of an exact
> arithmetic Coxeter polygon, or is the cycle field of one of the
> three-wall general-fork arrangement points described in Section 3. The
> latter locus is finite.

Reflection maximality is not used. Combined with the audited theorem in
[`global_minimality14_integral_arrangement_field_funnel.md`](global_minimality14_integral_arrangement_field_funnel.md)
and the exact descent in
[`low_dimensional_minimality14_field_descent_to_h3.md`](low_dimensional_minimality14_field_descent_to_h3.md),
this gives a finite ground-field set in every hyperbolic dimension, subject
only to the scopes already stated in those reports.

Theorem A is a field theorem, not yet a finiteness theorem for chambers or
reflection groups. Facet and dimension bounds are separate downstream gates.

## 2. Invariant arithmetic coordinates

For a \(k\)-rational facet-root line choose a nonzero representative \(r_i\)
and put

\[
             c_{ij}={2q(r_i,r_j)\over q(r_i,r_i)}.       \tag{2.1}
\]

Rescaling \(r_i\mapsto\lambda_i r_i\) sends
\(c_{ij}\mapsto\lambda_j\lambda_i^{-1}c_{ij}\). Therefore every closed
Cartan product

\[
 c_C=c_{i_1i_2}c_{i_2i_3}\cdots c_{i_si_1}             \tag{2.2}
\]

is invariant under arbitrary root rescaling. It is also an algebraic
integer. Intrinsically, if \(s_i\) is the integral reflection and
\(T_i=1-s_i\), then

\[
 c_C=\operatorname {Tr}(T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_s}),         \tag{2.3}
\]

the canonical trace of an endomorphism of the finite projective
\(\mathcal O_k\)-module. Thus no individual directed Cartan entry is being
assumed integral, and no square-\((-2)\) normalization is being assumed over
\(k\).

Vinberg arithmeticity says that the field generated by all coordinates
(2.2) is the minimal field \(k(P)\), and that every nonselected real
conjugate of the ambient form is definite.

We use the following audited inheritance principle. If a principal root
packet has a positive Gram direction, its cycle field is the full \(k(P)\).
Indeed, an embedding fixing the packet cycles changes its entries only by a
vertex-sign switch, hence preserves inertia; it cannot take a positive
packet into the Gram space at a definite nonselected place.

## 3. The general-fork arrangement chart

Take three walls \(1,2,3\) such that \(1,2\) are ultraparallel at the
selected place and wall \(3\) meets both with Coxeter angles
\(\pi/m,\pi/n\), where \(m,n\ge3\). For determinant calculations only,
pass to the symmetric square-\((-2)\) normalization over a real algebraic
closure:

\[
 G(u,a,b)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -2&u&a\\u&-2&b\\a&b&-2
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 a=2\cos(\pi/m),\quad b=2\cos(\pi/n),\quad2<u<14.       \tag{3.1}
\]

Define

\[
 \beta={1\over2}\det G
       =u^2+a^2+b^2+uab-4.                               \tag{3.2}
\]

This is not a normalization-dependent expression. If
\(t_{ij}=c_{ij}c_{ji}\) and \(z=c_{12}c_{23}c_{31}\), then the signs in
(2.1) and the symmetric convention (3.1) give

\[
             \boxed{\beta=t_{12}+t_{13}+t_{23}-z-4}.     \tag{3.3}
\]

Every summand is a closed Cartan-cycle coordinate, so

\[
                         \beta\in\mathcal O_k             \tag{3.4}
\]

under arbitrary facet-root rescaling and on arbitrary finite projective
order modules. Formula (3.3), not the auxiliary symmetric entries in
(3.1), is the arithmetic definition of \(\beta\).

The dotted pair makes \(G\) have a positive direction. Its packet cycle
field is therefore the whole \(k(P)\). In fact the single coordinate
\(\beta\) is already primitive. At the selected place the dotted pair has a
nondegenerate two-span of signature \((1,1)\). Its orthogonal complement in
the ambient space of signature \((1,3)\) is negative definite. The third
wall is independent from the pair, so its orthogonal component is nonzero
and negative. Thus the three-span has signature \((1,2)\), and

\[
                         0<\beta_0<256.                   \tag{3.5}
\]

The upper bound follows from \(u<14\), \(0\le a,b<2\):
\(\beta_0<196+4+4+56-4=256\). At every nonselected embedding the
three roots remain independent, because their wedge is a nonzero
\(k\)-rational vector and scalar extension is injective. The conjugate form
is definite, hence \(G^\sigma\) is negative definite and

\[
                         \beta^\sigma<0.                 \tag{3.6}
\]

If \(F=\mathbf Q(\beta)\subsetneq k\), total reality and separability give a
nonidentity \(F\)-embedding \(k\hookrightarrow\mathbf R\). It fixes the
positive number \(\beta_0\), whereas every nonselected embedding sends
\(\beta\) to a negative number. This is impossible. Thus

\[
                         \boxed{k=\mathbf Q(\beta)}.      \tag{3.7}
\]

This sign argument is stronger than merely saying that the three-wall packet
has the full field: it proves full-field inheritance for the single integral
coordinate on which Fekete will be applied.

### 3.1 The contracting conjugate intervals

Let

\[
 s_m=4\sin^2(\pi/m),\qquad s_n=4\sin^2(\pi/n),\qquad
 E=\mathbf Q(s_m,s_n)\subset k.                          \tag{3.8}
\]

The inclusions follow because \(t_{13}=a^2=4-s_m\) and
\(t_{23}=b^2=4-s_n\) are packet cycles in \(k\). Treating (3.2) as a
quadratic polynomial in \(u\), its discriminant is

\[
              (4-a^2)(4-b^2)=s_ms_n.                    \tag{3.9}
\]

At a nonselected embedding \(\tau:k\hookrightarrow\mathbf R\), pass to a
real extension containing a symmetric normalization and choose signed
entries \(u^\tau,a^\tau,b^\tau\) after a diagonal vertex-sign switch. The
cycle expression (3.3), and hence \(\beta^\tau\), is independent of these
choices. Negative definiteness puts the chosen \(u^\tau\) strictly between
the two roots of the corresponding quadratic. Its minimum is
\(-s_m^\tau s_n^\tau/4\), so

\[
       -{s_m^\tau s_n^\tau\over4}\le\beta^\tau<0,
       \qquad \tau\ne\tau_0.                            \tag{3.10}
\]

Each interval has length \(s_m^\rho s_n^\rho/4<4\). If
\(D=[E:\mathbf Q]\), its product of integer-Chebyshev capacities is

\[
 \prod_{\rho:E\hookrightarrow\mathbf R}
 {s_m^\rho s_n^\rho\over16}
 ={|N_E(s_ms_n)|\over16^D}<1.                            \tag{3.11}
\]

The strict inequality also follows pointwise because every conjugate of
each \(s_d\) lies strictly between \(0\) and \(4\).

For fixed \(m,n\), Fekete's theorem over \(E\), with the single selected
exceptional interval \(0<\beta_0<256\), bounds
\([E(\beta):E]=[k:E]\). All conjugates are bounded, so the relative
minimal-polynomial coefficients occupy a bounded Minkowski box. Hence only
finitely many \(\beta\) and \(k\) occur for fixed \(m,n\).

### 3.2 Uniformly bounding the two Coxeter labels

Put \(M=\max\{m,n\}\), \(e=[k:E]\). Since \(\beta\) is a nonzero algebraic
integer, (3.5) and (3.10) give

\[
 1\le |N_{k/\mathbf Q}(\beta)|
 <256\,{4\over s_{m,0}s_{n,0}}
       \left({|N_E(s_ms_n)|\over4^D}\right)^e.           \tag{3.12}
\]

The factor \(4/(s_{m,0}s_{n,0})\) is essential: the selected embedding
replaces one contracting factor \(s_{m,0}s_{n,0}/4\) by the exceptional
bound \(256\).

The cyclotomic norm identity is

\[
 |N_{\mathbf Q(s_d)/\mathbf Q}(s_d)|=\gamma(d),\qquad
 \gamma(d)=
 \begin{cases}
 p,&d=p^r,\\
 1,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{3.13}
\]

The tower formula gives

\[
 |N_E(s_ms_n)|
 =\gamma(m)^{D/[\mathbf Q(s_m):\mathbf Q]}
  \gamma(n)^{D/[\mathbf Q(s_n):\mathbf Q]}.              \tag{3.14}
\]

Since \([\mathbf Q(s_d):\mathbf Q]=\varphi(d)/2\), put
\(h(d)=\gamma(d)^{2/\varphi(d)}\). Equation (3.14) becomes

\[
 { |N_E(s_ms_n)|\over4^D}
 =\left({h(m)h(n)\over4}\right)^D.                       \tag{3.15}
\]

The elementary bounds

\[
 s_{d,0}\ge16/d^2,\qquad
 \gamma(d)\le d,\qquad
 \varphi(d)^2\ge d/2                                   \tag{3.16}
\]

bound the exceptional prefactor in (3.12) by \(4M^4\). For
\(M\ge10^6\), they also give

\[
 h(M)\le M^{2/\varphi(M)}
      \le M^{2/\sqrt{M/2}}<13/12.                        \tag{3.17}
\]

The final inequality holds at \(10^6\), and
\(\log x/\sqrt{x}\) decreases for \(x>e^2\). For every other label
\(d\ge3\), \(h(d)\le3\), with equality only at \(d=3\). Hence

\[
 { |N_E(s_ms_n)|\over4^D}<\left({13\over16}\right)^D.   \tag{3.18}
\]

Also

\[
 D\ge{\max\{\varphi(m),\varphi(n)\}\over2}
  \ge{\sqrt{M/2}\over2}\ge353.                          \tag{3.19}
\]

Since \(e\ge1\), (3.12) now gives

\[
 1<4M^4\left({13\over16}\right)^D.                      \tag{3.20}
\]

At \(M=10^6\) the reverse strict inequality is the exact integer comparison

\[
             16^{353}>4(10^6)^4 13^{353}.                \tag{3.21}
\]

For real \(x\ge10^6\), the continuous envelope
\[
        4x^4(13/16)^{\sqrt{x/2}/2}
\]
decreases: its logarithmic derivative is negative once
\(\sqrt{x/2}>80\), using \(\log(16/13)>1/5\). Thus the endpoint comparison
(3.21) makes (3.20) impossible for every \(M\ge10^6\), and

\[
                         \boxed{m,n<10^6}.                \tag{3.22}
\]

There are only finitely many base fields \(E\); Section 3.1 now proves:

> **Proposition 3.1 (general-fork integral locus).** The cycle fields of
> all three-wall arithmetic fork packets (3.1) of minimality \(14\), with
> arbitrary Coxeter labels \(m,n\ge3\), form a finite set.

The numeric cutoff is intentionally crude. Its purpose is logical
finiteness, not a competitive degree estimate.

## 4. Every compact \(H^3\) chamber enters a finite branch

Choose a facet \(E\) of minimal visual aperture. The elementary cone-plane
calculation gives

\[
 b(F,G)<14.                                               \tag{4.1}
\]

for every two closed facets \(F,G\) meeting \(E\). Write the sides of the
compact polygon \(E\) cyclically as

\[
             E\cap\delta_1,\ldots,E\cap\delta_N.         \tag{4.2}
\]

All roots below lie in this closed star, so (4.1) applies.

If every \(\delta_i\perp E\), the intrinsic side normals of \(E\) are
literally the ambient \(\delta_i\). Their reflections preserve
\(E^\perp\) and the intersection with a full stable arithmetic lattice.
The polygon packet spans the Lorentzian plane and therefore inherits the
full field \(k(P)\). Hence \(E\) is an exact compact arithmetic Coxeter
polygon over \(k(P)\). Its reflection group ascends through a finite chain
to a maximal arithmetic reflection group: the chamber coarea drops by the
integer subgroup index, while hyperbolic orbifold coarea has a positive
universal lower bound. The minimal Vinberg/adjoint field is invariant under
finite index. The exact real-DM orbifold-curve theorem therefore puts this
field in the finite maximal-\(H^2\) list.

Otherwise choose \(j\) with \(E\cdot\delta_j>0\) and consider the polyhedron
of the edge

\[
                         r=E\cap\delta_{j+1}.             \tag{4.3}
\]

Its endpoint-exclusive walls are \(\delta_j,\delta_{j+2}\). The audited
edge lemma gives the following exhaustive alternatives:

1. \(u=\delta_j\cdot\delta_{j+2}\le2\), in which case the full field lies
   in the already finite rational, Lanner, triangle, or noncompact packet;
2. \(2<u<14\).

The three wall normals \(\delta_j,\delta_{j+2},E\) are independent. Indeed,
if the normal of \(E\) lay in the span of the other two, their restrictions
to \(E^\perp\) would be proportional. The side hyperplanes
\(E\cap H_{\delta_j}\) and \(E\cap H_{\delta_{j+2}}\) inside \(E\) would then
coincide, whereas they are distinct sides containing the opposite endpoints
of the edge \(r\). In the second case, if
\(E\cdot\delta_{j+2}=0\), the packet
\(\{\delta_j,\delta_{j+2},E\}\) is the already certified tail chart of the
global funnel. If \(E\cdot\delta_{j+2}>0\), it is exactly the general fork
of Section 3, with labels given by the two Coxeter angles at \(E\).
Proposition 3.1 makes its full fields finite. This exhausts all compact
\(H^3\) chambers and proves Theorem A.

Notice what was *not* claimed: a two-face obtained by arbitrary orthogonal
projection need not be a Coxeter polygon and need not inherit the full
ambient field. Only the all-perpendicular branch descends to \(H^2\); the
remaining transverse information is retained by the exact three-wall
arrangement determinant.

## 5. AG interpretation and exact scope

The carrier sends the three marked mirrors to a point of the diagonal-torus
quotient of the quadric--hyperplane arrangement space. Its regular
coordinates are precisely the integral cycles \(t_{12},t_{13},t_{23},z\).
Equation (3.3) is a regular function on that quotient, (3.9) is its Gram
determinant discriminant, and (3.10) cuts out contracting real tubes at all
nonselected embeddings. Thus Proposition 3.1 is a finiteness theorem for
integral points on an exact finite-type arrangement chart. The other branch
uses the exact algebraic orbifold curve of the perpendicular facet.

The terminal finiteness tools are arithmetic Fekete theory and the
arithmetic genus-zero orbifold-curve theorem. This is therefore
AG-arrangement-mediated arithmetic, not a consequence of BAB or KSBA alone.

What is now proved:

- finite Vinberg ground fields for compact arithmetic Coxeter polyhedra in
  \(\mathbb H^3\);
- no bounded facet/frame assumption and no reflection maximality assumption;
- exact full-field inheritance, \(k=\mathbf Q(\beta)\), in the new chart.

What is not proved here:

- a dimension bound;
- a uniform facet bound;
- finiteness of chambers or groups;
- any statement for non-simplest-type arithmetic lattices not governed by
  the Vinberg cycle criterion used above.
