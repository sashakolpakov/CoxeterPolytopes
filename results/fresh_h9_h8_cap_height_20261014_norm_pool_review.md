## Universal deduction

Rounding the coefficients of an element of `Q(√5)` in the basis `1,φ` leaves an error `a+bφ` with `|a|,|b|≤1/2`. Its absolute field norm is at most `|a²|+|ab|+|b²|≤3/4<1`. Hence `O` is norm-Euclidean and therefore a PID. A reflection line defined over this field can be scaled to a primitive vector `r` in `L`. Conversely, a lattice-preserving hyperplane reflection has a field-defined minus-one eigenspace, so this reduction loses no such reflection direction.

Write `q=rᵀGr=2b`. Integrality of the reflection matrix `I−r(Gr)ᵀ/b` gives `r_i(Gr)_j/b∈O`. Primitivity supplies coefficients `α_i∈O` with `Σα_i r_i=1`, so `Gr∈bO¹⁰`. Multiplying by the adjugate and applying the same Bezout identity gives `b | det G`. Since `(β)` has prime ideal norm eleven and `φ` is a unit, `b` is a unit or a unit times `β`.

Both embeddings of `b` are positive: the hyperbolic norm is positive and the conjugate form is positive definite. The same holds for `β`, so the remaining unit is totally positive.

The unit argument is complete. If a positive unit `u` satisfied `1<u<φ`, its integer **φ coefficient** would be `(u−u′)/√5`. When `Norm(u)=1`, this is `(u−1/u)/√5∈(0,1)`; when `Norm(u)=−1`, it is `(u+1/u)/√5∈(0,1)`. Both contradict integrality. Scaling any positive unit by a power of `φ` puts it in `[1,φ)`, proving that it is a power of `φ`. Since `Norm(φ)=−1`, the totally positive units are precisely `φ^(2k)`. Scaling `r` by `φ^(−k)` yields the two stated norm classes.

The proof uses the integer coefficient of `φ`; the coefficient of `√5` itself need not be integral. The primary report makes this distinction correctly.
