# AG field funnel plus bounded-frame native-host closure

**Status (2026-08-12): proved, with an explicit import ledger.** A finite
set of Vinberg ground fields forces finiteness of reflection-maximal
finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chambers, uniformly in dimension. The proof
does not invoke the conclusion of Nikulin's fixed-field/fixed-rank
reflective-lattice finiteness theorem, the
Agol--Belolipetsky--Storm--Whyte theorem, or an arithmetic dimension theorem.
It independently reconstructs and simplifies the arithmetic core of
Nikulin's fixed-data argument, using integral arrangement cycles and a
full-order conductor sandwich.

Two non-AG geometric inputs remain explicit:

1. the bounded connected-frame lemma for a finite-covolume full reflection
   group, with the absolute constant \(A=62\); and
2. the universal geometric dimension ceiling \(n\leq 995\), obtained by
   combining Vinberg's compact and Prokhorov's finite-volume noncompact
   theorems.

The result is therefore **AG-mediated**, not AG-only. Its essential AG input
is the finite-field funnel from exact quadric--hyperplane arrangement
integral points. The direct moduli implication from reflection maximality to
a bounded facet-divisor degree, called **AG-DEG** below, remains unproved.

## 1. Dimension-free finite-field theorem

Let \(k\) be totally real and let \((V,q)\) be an admissible quadratic
\(k\)-space: at one real place \(q\) is Lorentzian and at every other real
place it is definite. Arithmeticity is in Vinberg's sense.

> **Theorem 1.1 (finite fields imply finite maximal chambers).** Let
> \(\mathscr K\) be a finite set of totally real fields. Across every
> dimension \(n\geq2\), only finitely many unmarked normalized finite-volume
> arithmetic Coxeter chambers \(P\subset\mathbb H^n\) with
> \(k(P)\in\mathscr K\) have side-reflection group maximal either
>
> 1. among all discrete hyperbolic reflection groups, or
> 2. among arithmetic hyperbolic reflection groups.
>
> Consequently an existential, generally ineffective, number
> \(F(\mathscr K)<\infty\) bounds the facets of every such chamber.

The dimension quantifier in this statement is genuine. A fixed-dimension
facet bound is not assumed.

> **Theorem 1.2 (reconstructed fixed-data native-host theorem).** Fix a
> totally real field \(k\) and a rank \(m\geq3\). There are only finitely
> many unmarked normalized reflection-maximal finite-volume arithmetic
> Coxeter chambers of rank \(m=n+1\) over \(k\).

The proof treats full projective \(\mathcal O_k\)-modules and never assumes
that a native lattice or a root module is free.

## 2. From a chamber to a full integral reflection host

Let \(W=W(P)\). Arithmeticity supplies a full projective lattice \(L'\) for
which \(W\cap O^+(q,L')\) has finite index in \(W\). Let
\(\Gamma\triangleleft W\) be the normal core of that intersection. For
representatives \(g_1,\ldots,g_t\) of \(W/\Gamma\), set

\[
                    L=\sum_{a=1}^{t}g_aL'.              \tag{2.1}
\]

It is a finite torsion-free \(\mathcal O_k\)-module spanning \(V\), hence is
projective, and normality of \(\Gamma\) shows that every element of \(W\)
preserves it. Define

\[
 R(L)=\langle s:\ s\text{ is a \(q\)-reflection and }sL=L\rangle .
                                                               \tag{2.2}
\]

Then

\[
                 W\subseteq R(L)\subseteq O^+(q,L).     \tag{2.3}
\]

Both endpoint groups have finite covolume at the Lorentzian place. The
subgroup-covolume formula gives

\[
 [O^+(q,L):W]<\infty,\qquad [R(L):W]<\infty.            \tag{2.4}
\]

Thus \(R(L)\) is itself a finite-covolume arithmetic reflection group. No
reflective-lattice finiteness theorem has entered.

## 3. The geometric bounded frame

We import only the following geometric lemma.

> **Lemma 3.1 (bounded connected frame).** A finite-covolume full reflection
> group \(R(L)\) on an \(m\)-dimensional Lorentzian space has a chamber with
> \(m\) walls whose reflections \(s_1,\ldots,s_m\) have \(k\)-rational
> normal lines \(k\delta_1,\ldots,k\delta_m\) such that:
>
> 1. the \(\delta_i\) form a basis of \(V\);
> 2. the graph \(G\), with \(ij\) an edge exactly when
>    \(q(\delta_i,\delta_j)\neq0\), is connected; and
> 3. at the Lorentzian embedding,
>    \[
>       c_{ij}:=
>       \frac{4q(\delta_i,\delta_j)^2}
>            {q(\delta_i,\delta_i)q(\delta_j,\delta_j)}<62^2.
>                                                               \tag{3.1}
>    \]

This is the geometric frame statement quoted on pp. 119--120 at the
beginning of the proof of Theorem 5.2.1 in V. V. Nikulin, *On the
classification of arithmetic groups generated by reflections in Lobachevsky
spaces*, Math. USSR Izv. **18** (1982), 99--123. There it is cited as
Theorem 1 in the appendix to Nikulin, *On arithmetic groups generated by
reflections in Lobachevsky spaces*, Math. USSR Izv. **16** (1981), 573--601.
The project's pinned 1982 primary PDF has SHA-256
696652b6a67c6ed420de6c1cb61d9422e3619e28e081a78131a5323ce2edee9d.
Only this geometric lemma is imported. Sections 4--7 independently replace
the divisor, unit, and overlattice argument; Nikulin's fixed-data finiteness
conclusion is not used.

Orient the normal lines by the acute convention and put

\[
 \alpha_i(x)=\frac{2q(x,\delta_i)}{q(\delta_i,\delta_i)},\qquad
 T_i=1-s_i=\delta_i\otimes\alpha_i,\qquad
 a_{ij}=\alpha_i(\delta_j).                             \tag{3.2}
\]

Thus \(T_i(\delta_j)=a_{ij}\delta_i\). Rescaling
\(\delta_i\mapsto\lambda_i\delta_i\) changes

\[
                 a_{ij}\longmapsto
                 \frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i}a_{ij}.     \tag{3.3}
\]

On every edge of \(G\), both \(a_{ij}\) and \(a_{ji}\) are nonzero.
Individual directed entries need not be integral. Only closed-cycle
products will be used.

## 4. Cyclic-only integrality and finite packets

Because \(s_iL=L\), each \(T_i\) lies in
\(\operatorname {End}_{\mathcal O_k}(L)\). Define a simple directed cycle to
mean either a length-two backtrack \(i\to j\to i\), or a directed cycle of
length at least three with distinct vertices.

For \(C=(i_1,\ldots,i_r,i_1)\), composition is read from right to left:

\[
 T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_r}
 =\left(a_{i_1i_2}\cdots a_{i_{r-1}i_r}\right)
   \delta_{i_1}\otimes\alpha_{i_r}.
\]

Taking projective trace therefore gives, with exactly this orientation,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 b_C
   &:=\operatorname {tr}_{\mathcal O_k}
        (T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_r})\\
   &=a_{i_1i_2}a_{i_2i_3}\cdots a_{i_ri_1}
     \in\mathcal O_k.
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{4.1}
\]

Trace is defined locally on the projective module and glues. Equation (4.1)
uses no integral generator of a normal line and makes no false claim that
each \(a_{ij}\) is integral.

For a length-two cycle,

\[
                         b_{iji}=a_{ij}a_{ji}=c_{ij}.   \tag{4.2}
\]

For a longer simple cycle, symmetry of \(q\) makes the forward and reverse
products equal, and hence

\[
                         b_C^2=\prod_{\{i,j\}\in C}c_{ij}.             \tag{4.3}
\]

At the distinguished place, (3.1) gives

\[
                              |b_C|<62^r.               \tag{4.4}
\]

At any other real place the form is definite, so Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
                  0\leq\sigma(c_{ij})\leq4,\qquad
                  |\sigma(b_C)|\leq2^r.                \tag{4.5}
\]

For fixed \(k,m\), every \(r\leq m\). The Minkowski image of
\(\mathcal O_k\) is a lattice, so each \(b_C\) lies in a finite set. There
are finitely many connected graphs on \(m\) labelled vertices and finitely
many simple directed cycles in each. A fixed abstract field has only
finitely many real embeddings, so taking the finite union over the possible
distinguished embedding does not change the conclusion.

> **Lemma 4.1.** For fixed \((k,m)\), only finitely many complete tuples of
> simple directed-cycle products \((b_C)_C\) arise from bounded connected
> frames.

## 5. Cycle packets determine reflection representations

Let \(A=(a_{ij})\) and \(A'=(a'_{ij})\) have the same connected graph and
the same product on every simple directed cycle, including the length-two
cycles. Choose a root vertex, a spanning tree, and an orientation away from
the root. Set \(\lambda_{\rm root}=1\), and on each oriented tree edge
\(i\to j\) define

\[
                    \frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i}
                    =\frac{a'_{ij}}{a_{ij}}.            \tag{5.1}
\]

The length-two product on that edge checks the reverse direction. For a
non-tree directed edge, its product with the oppositely oriented tree path
is a fundamental simple cycle; equality of that cycle product checks (5.1)
on the non-tree edge. Therefore

\[
                         a'_{ij}=
                         \frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_i}a_{ij}             \tag{5.2}
\]

for all \(i,j\), with \(\lambda_i\in k^\times\). Conversely (5.2)
preserves all cycle products.

In the basis \(\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_m\),

\[
                         s_i(\delta_j)=\delta_j-a_{ij}\delta_i.       \tag{5.3}
\]

Thus (5.2) is exactly diagonal \(k\)-conjugacy. Lemma 4.1 proves:

> **Lemma 5.1.** For fixed \((k,m)\), bounded connected frames yield only
> finitely many \(k\)-conjugacy classes of ordered reflection
> representations \((s_1,\ldots,s_m)\).

These representations are absolutely irreducible. If a nonzero invariant
subspace \(U\) exists after extending scalars, choose \(0\neq u\in U\).
Nondegeneracy and spanning give an \(i\) with
\(q(u,\delta_i)\neq0\), so \(T_i u\) is a nonzero multiple of
\(\delta_i\), and \(\delta_i\in U\). Connectedness propagates along \(G\):
if \(ij\) is an edge, \(T_j\delta_i\) is a nonzero multiple of
\(\delta_j\). Hence \(U=V\).

Burnside over an algebraic closure, followed by a dimension comparison over
\(k\), now gives

\[
                   k[s_1,\ldots,s_m]=\operatorname {End}_k(V).       \tag{5.4}
\]

The commutant is \(k\). Comparing two invariant symmetric forms via one of
them therefore shows that the form is unique up to \(k^\times\)-scale.
Such a scale changes neither the reflections nor the normalized chamber.

## 6. Projective invariant lattices: conductor sandwich

Fix one representation type from Lemma 5.1 for which an invariant full
projective lattice exists. Choose one, \(L_0\), and put

\[
 B=\operatorname {End}_{\mathcal O_k}(L_0),\qquad
 \Lambda=\mathcal O_k[s_1,\ldots,s_m]\subset B.         \tag{6.1}
\]

The ring \(\Lambda\) is a finite \(\mathcal O_k\)-module because it is a
submodule of the finite module \(B\). Equation (5.4) says

\[
                  \Lambda\otimes_{\mathcal O_k}k
                  =\operatorname {End}_k(V),            \tag{6.2}
\]

so it is a full order. The torsion module \(B/\Lambda\) is killed by a
nonzero integral ideal \(\mathfrak c\), and therefore

\[
                         \mathfrak cB\subseteq\Lambda.  \tag{6.3}
\]

Let \(L\subset V\) be any full projective \(\Lambda\)-stable lattice and
define \(M=BL\), the finite sum of \(bL\) over generators of the finite
module \(B\). Then \(M\) is finitely generated and torsion-free, contains
\(L\), and spans \(V\); hence it is a full projective lattice. Since \(B\)
is a ring, \(BM=M\). Moreover

\[
                         \mathfrak cM\subseteq L\subseteq M,          \tag{6.4}
\]

because \(\mathfrak cBL\subseteq\Lambda L\subseteq L\).

We now classify \(B\)-stable full lattices. At a prime \(\mathfrak p\),
\((L_0)_{\mathfrak p}\) is free and

\[
 B_{\mathfrak p}=M_m(\mathcal O_{k,\mathfrak p}).       \tag{6.5}
\]

Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_m\) be its standard basis and define

\[
 I_{\mathfrak p}=\{x\in k_{\mathfrak p}:xe_1\in M_{\mathfrak p}\}.
\]

The diagonal matrix units project every vector of \(M_{\mathfrak p}\) onto
each coordinate, and the off-diagonal matrix units transfer any coordinate
to any other. Consequently

\[
                        M_{\mathfrak p}
                        =I_{\mathfrak p}^{\,m}
                        =\mathfrak p^{e_{\mathfrak p}}
                         (L_0)_{\mathfrak p}.            \tag{6.6}
\]

Only finitely many exponents are nonzero because \(M\) and \(L_0\) are
commensurable. Gluing gives

\[
                              M=\mathfrak aL_0           \tag{6.7}
\]

for a fractional ideal \(\mathfrak a\). Up to \(k^\times\)-homothety,
\(\mathfrak a\) ranges over the finite ideal class group. Choose one \(M\)
for each class. For fixed \(M\), (6.4) places \(L\) among submodules of the
finite module

\[
                              M/\mathfrak cM.            \tag{6.8}
\]

Only finitely many exist; projectivity and \(\Lambda\)-stability only filter
this finite list.

> **Lemma 6.1 (explicit order-module finiteness).** For a fixed absolutely
> irreducible reflection representation, only finitely many stable full
> projective \(\mathcal O_k\)-lattices occur up to \(k^\times\)-homothety.

This proves the required lattice statement rather than citing
Jordan--Zassenhaus. It handles nonfree modules, ideal classes, denominators,
and units. The finite interval \(\mathfrak cM\subseteq L\subseteq M\)
replaces the fragile root-length valuation argument.

## 7. No native host escapes the finite order list

Take an original frame representation \(\rho'\) on the \(W\)-stable
projective lattice \(L_{\mathrm{orig}}\) constructed in Section 2. Lemma 5.1
gives \(g\in\operatorname {GL}_k(V)\) with
\(g\rho'g^{-1}=\rho\), one of the chosen representatives. Then
\(gL_{\mathrm{orig}}\) is again a full projective lattice and, for every
generator \(s_i\) of \(\Lambda\),

\[
 s_i(gL_{\mathrm{orig}})
      =g\,s'_i(L_{\mathrm{orig}})
      =gL_{\mathrm{orig}}.                              \tag{7.1}
\]

Thus \(gL_{\mathrm{orig}}\) is \(\Lambda\)-stable and Lemma 6.1 counts it.
This explicit conjugation check is why no original native host is lost when
the finite cycle packets are replaced by representative matrices.

For every retained lattice and its unique form up to scale, the full group
\(R(L)\) is determined. Fixed \((k,m)\) therefore leaves finitely many full
native reflection hosts.

## 8. Maximality collapses each host to one chamber orbit

For the original chamber,

\[
                             W(P)\subseteq R(L).         \tag{8.1}
\]

Absolute reflection maximality gives equality. Maximality merely among
arithmetic reflection groups also gives equality, because (2.4) makes
\(R(L)\) an arithmetic finite-index reflection overgroup:

\[
                             W(P)=R(L).                  \tag{8.2}
\]

The complete locally finite mirror arrangement of \(R(L)\) has one chamber
orbit. Indeed, a generic path between chamber interiors meets finitely many
mirrors; successive reflection across the resulting finite gallery maps one
chamber to the other. Hence each native host contributes at most one
unmarked normalized chamber. Lemmas 5.1 and 6.1 prove Theorem 1.2.

This is exactly where maximality enters. Fixed-field, fixed-rank, and even
fixed-lattice redoubling families can have unbounded facet count, but their
side groups are proper finite-index reflection subgroups of the full host.
Equation (8.2) removes that escape.

## 9. Closing the dimension quantifier geometrically

Vinberg proved that no compact hyperbolic Coxeter polytope exists for
\(n\geq30\). Prokhorov proved that no finite-volume noncompact hyperbolic
reflection polyhedron exists for \(n>995\). These are universal geometric
and combinatorial reflection theorems, not arithmetic field or reflective
lattice finiteness theorems. Together they give

\[
                           3\leq m=n+1\leq996.           \tag{9.1}
\]

For finite \(\mathscr K\), apply Theorem 1.2 to

\[
                         \mathscr K\times\{3,\ldots,996\}.             \tag{9.2}
\]

This proves Theorem 1.1 and keeps the quantifiers in the correct order.

The noncompact source is M. N. Prokhorov, *The absence of discrete
reflection groups with noncompact fundamental polyhedron of finite volume in
Lobachevskii space of large dimension*, Math. USSR Izv. **28** (1987),
401--411,
[doi:10.1070/IM1987v028n02ABEH000889](https://doi.org/10.1070/IM1987v028n02ABEH000889).
The compact source is E. B. Vinberg, *The absence of crystallographic groups
of reflections in Lobachevsky spaces of large dimensions*, Trans. Moscow
Math. Soc. **47** (1985), 75--112. Only their dimension conclusions are
used.

## 10. AG-mediated high-dimensional consequence

The exact arrangement theorem
[global minimality-14 field funnel](global_minimality14_integral_arrangement_field_funnel.md)
produces a finite set \(\mathscr K_{\geq6}\) containing the Vinberg field of
every finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chamber in \(n\geq6\). It derives
that set from integral points in exact quadric-arrangement cycle coordinates;
maximality is not needed.

Combining that theorem with Sections 2--9 gives:

> **Corollary 10.1.** Across all \(n\geq6\), only finitely many unmarked
> normalized reflection-maximal finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chambers
> occur.

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{exact AG arrangement cycles and integral-point field funnel}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{finite field set }\mathscr K_{\geq6}\\
\Downarrow\quad
\substack{\text{geometric }n\leq995\\
          \text{geometric connected frame }A=62}\\
\text{finite cycle representations and finite order-lattice hosts}\\
\Downarrow\quad\text{reflection maximality}\\
\text{finitely many maximal chambers.}
\end{gathered}}                                           \tag{10.1}
\]

This composition uses neither Mostow rigidity nor the conclusion of
Nikulin's Theorem 5.2.1. It is not AG-only: AG supplies the essential finite
field set; geometry supplies the dimension and frame inputs; elementary
arithmetic and order theory supply the native-host step.

For a global statement in all dimensions, one must separately supply a
finite field set in the remaining low-dimensional branch. The audited
arrangement theorem currently covers \(n\geq6\); nothing here silently
promotes it to \(n=3,4,5\). Once the low-dimensional field set is finite,
Theorem 1.1 closes the global maximal-chamber theorem immediately.

## 11. Why direct AG facet degree remains open

For the literal exact carrier with quadric \(Q\) and facet hyperplanes
\(H_i\),

\[
 K_{\mathbf P^n}+Q+\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{f}H_i
          =\left(\frac f2-n+1\right)H.                 \tag{11.1}
\]

Thus the Hilbert data already contain the unknown \(f\). Placing the pairs
in a bounded KSBA, BAB, Chow, or Hilbert family would require proving that
only finitely many values of the right side occur; boundedness cannot be its
own input.

The sharp missing invariant is:

> **AG-DEG.** After a uniformly controlled sheet-separating cover, there
> exist an integral ample line bundle \(A_P\) and a reduced divisor
> \(D_P=\sum_iD_{P,i}\), with one distinct component per facet, such that
> \[
>                         D_P\cdot A_P^{N-1}\leq B       \tag{11.2}
> \]
> for an absolute \(B\), or a \(B\) depending only on the finite field set.

Each component has positive integral degree, so

\[
                    f(P)\leq B,\qquad n+1\leq f(P),
                    \qquad n\leq B-1.                  \tag{11.3}
\]

This one invariant would prove both direct MAX-FAC and an AG dimension
bound. A rational stack degree is insufficient unless inertia denominators
and the index of the sheet-separating cover are uniformly bounded.

The existing automorphic injection proves

\[
        f(P)q_{\min}\leq D\cdot\lambda^{n-1}=w\lambda^n,               \tag{11.4}
\]

but does not uniformly bound the separating level, weight, top degree, or
multiplicity normalization. Standard log/K-moduli do not supply (11.2):
the maximal Borcherds \(\mathbb H^{21}\) carrier is deep in log general type
at the reflection coefficient, while the exact maximal Lambert family shows
that fixed BAB data and uniform K-stability do not make the exact Gram image
discrete without arithmetic. Redoubling shows that field, rank, finite
volume, integrality, and even a fixed lattice do not bound facets before
maximality.

Therefore the direct implication

\[
              \text{reflection maximality}\Longrightarrow\text{AG-DEG}
                                                               \tag{11.5}
\]

is not proved by the standard moduli packages. This is a precise missing
invariant, not generic pessimism. The native-host proof bypasses (11.5) by
turning maximality into \(W(P)=R(L)\) after AG has supplied finite fields.

## 12. Import ledger and effectivity

| ingredient | role | status |
|---|---|---|
| exact arrangement cyclic coordinates | finite fields for \(n\geq6\) | proved upstream; essential AG input |
| connected-frame lemma, \(A=62\) | bounded full-rank packet | imported geometric lemma |
| Vinberg--Prokhorov \(n\leq995\) | closes dimension quantifier | imported universal geometry |
| Minkowski discreteness and class-group finiteness | finite cycles and \(B\)-stable classes | elementary number theory |
| cycle packet \(\Rightarrow\) representation | finite rational representations | proved in Section 5 |
| \(\mathfrak cM\subseteq L\subseteq M\) | finite projective invariant lattices | proved in Section 6 |
| maximality orbit bridge | full host equals side group | proved in Section 8 |
| Nikulin fixed-data finiteness conclusion | none | **not used** |
| ABSW arithmetic reflection finiteness | none | **not used** |
| direct AG-DEG | would give direct effective degree control | **open** |

The conclusion is qualitative. Although every finite \(\mathscr K\) now has
an existential facet bound \(F(\mathscr K)\), the current field funnel is not
fully enumerated and the conductor/class-group enumeration has not been made
effective. This is an effectivity limitation, not a remaining finiteness
gap.
