# Fixed-dimension full-host cover: theorem, effectivity, and terminal certificates

**Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Verdict:** A finite full-host cover exists as a mathematical theorem for
every fixed hyperbolic dimension, and it covers nonmaximal finite-volume
arithmetic Coxeter chambers as well as maximal ones.  This is presently an
**existential finite cover**, not an implemented exhaustive host list.  The
exact AGCat functor faithfully transports an empty exhaustive list back to
polytopes, but does not make that list empty.

## 1. Exact theorem that is justified

Fix $n\geq2$.  Consider finite-sided finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter
chambers $P\subset\mathbf H^n$, up to unmarked normalized isometry, and put
$W=W(P)$.

> **Fixed-dimension full-host cover theorem.** There is a finite set
> \(\mathscr H_n\) of similarity classes of oriented admissible integral
> quadratic hosts
> \[
>       (k,\sigma _0,V,q,L,C^+),
> \]
> with native rank $n+1$, such that every $P$ admits a host in
> \(\mathscr H_n\) for which
> \[
>        W\subseteq R(L)\subseteq O^+(q,L),
>        \qquad [R(L):W]<\infty.                         \tag{1.1}
> \]
> Here $R(L)$ is generated by every $k$-hyperplane reflection preserving
> the full projective \(\mathcal O_k\)-lattice $L$.  In particular, $R(L)$
> has a finite-volume chamber.  If (P) is compact, that complete host chamber
> is compact as well.

This statement does **not** assert finitely many finite-index reflection
subgroups or finitely many nonmaximal chambers.  Those can be infinite even
inside one fixed host.  It says exactly what is needed for nonexistence: if
none of the finitely many complete hosts is compact reflective, then no compact
arithmetic Coxeter chamber exists in that dimension.

There are two unconditional proofs of finiteness.  The shortest imports the
Agol--Belolipetsky--Storm--Whyte theorem: every arithmetic reflection group
lies in a maximal arithmetic reflection group, and there are only finitely
many conjugacy classes of the latter.  Applying the invariant-lattice
construction below to a maximal group makes it equal to its full integral
reflection host.  Thus the finite maximal-group list is already a finite
full-host cover of all finite-index descendants.  This route is existential
and does not publish the list.

The repository's factorized proof exposes what must be enumerated.  It has
three independent parts.

1. Vinberg's structural reflection lemma places every arithmetic lattice
   containing an arithmetic reflection subgroup in the admissible
   quadratic-form (simplest-type) model.  Starting with a lattice preserved
   by a finite-index subgroup of $W$, take the normal core and the finite sum
   of its $W$-translates.  This gives a full projective $W$-stable lattice
   $L$, without assuming a principal ideal domain or a free module.  Then
   $W\subseteq R(L)\subseteq O^+(L)$.  Since $W$ and $O^+(L)$ have finite
   covolume, the inclusions relevant to (1.1) have finite index.  Cocompactness
   is invariant under finite-index extension.
2. For fixed totally real $k$ and rank $m=n+1$, Nikulin's bounded
   connected-frame theorem plus arithmetic of orders gives finitely many
   host similarity classes.  A full $m$-wall frame has connected
   nonorthogonality graph and two-cycle products less than $62^2$ at the
   selected embedding.  All simple closed Cartan products are integral and
   lie in bounded boxes at every real embedding.  Minkowski discreteness gives
   finitely many cycle packets; spanning-tree gauge reconstructs finitely many
   representations; Burnside and a full-order conductor sandwich leave
   finitely many projective invariant lattices up to homothety.  Equivalently,
   one may import Nikulin's fixed-field/fixed-rank reflective-lattice theorem.
3. The possible ground fields in a fixed dimension form a finite set.  This
   follows classically from the arithmetic-reflection field theorems, or from
   the separately audited AG-mediated field funnel in this repository.  Taking
   the finite union of Part 2 over these fields proves the theorem.

Reflection maximality is required only if one wants the original side group
itself to equal $R(L)$, and hence at most one original maximal chamber orbit
per host.  It is not required for the existence/nonexistence cover (1.1).

The quantifier variants are therefore all finite in the arithmetic setting:

- fixed \((k,n)\): the bounded-frame/order theorem gives finitely many full
  hosts directly;
- fixed (n): the finite ground-field theorem followed by the fixed-data
  theorem gives finitely many hosts;
- fixed (k): Vinberg's arithmetic ceiling (n\leq29) leaves a finite union
  of the fixed-\((k,n)\) host sets; and
- any finite set of fields or dimensions is covered by the corresponding
  finite union.

None of these quantifier manipulations emits the actual host records.

## 2. The especially useful dimensions 22--29

For a compact arithmetic Coxeter chamber with $22\leq n\leq29$, Vinberg's
high-dimensional field restriction and Meyer's theorem reduce the cover to
the three fields

\[
 \mathbf Q(\sqrt2),\qquad
 \mathbf Q(\sqrt5),\qquad
 \mathbf Q(\cos(2\pi/7)).                              \tag{2.1}
\]

Vinberg's list before compactness also contains \(\mathbf Q\).  A rational
Lorentz form in rank at least five is isotropic, so its arithmetic lattice is
noncocompact; hence it cannot contain a compact finite-index reflection
subgroup.  The native ranks in this range are $23,\ldots,30$.

Therefore a finite terminal computation covering the 24 pairs

\[
 \{23,\ldots,30\}\times
 \{\mathbf Q(\sqrt2),\mathbf Q(\sqrt5),
                  \mathbf Q(\cos(2\pi/7))\}             \tag{2.2}
\]

would settle compact arithmetic existence in every dimension $22,\ldots,29$.
If every row were empty, then Esselmann's theorem on the noncompact rational
branch would lower the overall arithmetic-reflection ceiling from $29$ to
$21$.  This is a genuine dimension result, not merely a field result.

## 3. Exceptional arithmetic types do not escape the cover

The ambient classification of arithmetic lattices in real hyperbolic space
has non-simplest-type families in odd dimensions and a trialitarian family in
dimension seven.  They do not create missing hosts here.

Vinberg's lemma, quoted as Lemma 5.2 in Agol--Belolipetsky--Storm--Whyte,
states that if an arithmetic lattice contains an arithmetic subgroup generated
by real hyperplane reflections, then it is defined by a quadratic form.  Thus
an arithmetic Coxeter lattice is of simplest type even in the odd-dimensional
ambient groups where quaternionic forms exist.  A genuine trialitarian
arithmetic lattice is confined to $\mathbf H^7$, and in any event cannot
contain the finite-index arithmetic reflection lattice under consideration.
The $D_4$-triality appearing in the repository's K3 fold examples is a
diagram/lattice automorphism and is unrelated to this exceptional arithmetic
commensurability type.

## 4. Orders, conductors, and nonmaximal groups

The host record may be normalized over the maximal ring of integers
\(\mathcal O_k\), but
this does not assume that the original root module was free or already an
\(\mathcal O_k\)-lattice.  Starting from a coefficient suborder, take its
\(\mathcal O_k\)-span and clear the one common pairing ideal.  At the
representation stage one must still enumerate:

- ideal-valued diagonal gauges, including local valuations and ideal classes;
- the full reflection order
  \(\Lambda=\mathcal O_k[s_1,\ldots,s_m]\);
- a conductor ideal \(\mathfrak c\) with
  \(\mathfrak c\operatorname {End}_{\mathcal O_k}(L_0)\subseteq\Lambda\);
- every stable projective lattice in
  \(\mathfrak cM\subseteq L\subseteq M\), modulo homothety and normalizers.

The finite class group and the finite quotient $M/\mathfrak cM$ prove that
this stage is finite.  They do not materialize it.  Original coefficient
suborders do not require a separate host branch after this
\(\mathcal O_k\)-saturation: their information is absorbed by the resulting
stable projective lattice.  But omitting the saturation, nonprincipal ideal
classes, the reflection order/conductor, or any stable quotient module would
make a purported host cover incomplete.

Nonmaximal side groups need not be enumerated.  If a hypothetical compact
$W$ occurs, (1.1) produces a compact reflective full host $R(L)$.  Thus a
certificate excluding every compact full host excludes all finite-index
reflection subgroups automatically.  Conversely, a surviving full host does
not classify all its finite-index Coxeter subgroups.

## 5. Existential finiteness versus an effective terminating census

The current state supports three different claims, only the first two of
which are proved.

1. **Existential finiteness:** proved.  For fixed $n$, only finitely many
   reflective full-host similarity classes are needed.
2. **An effective reduction in principle:** largely justified.  The
   Minkowski boxes, graph/cycle constraints, trace order, conductors, ideal
   classes, and finite submodule intervals all admit exact finite
   descriptions.
3. **A presently implemented terminating census:** false.  No complete
   rank-23--30 cycle-packet, ideal-gauge, order/conductor, and full-host
   manifests exist for (2.2).

There is also a published-effectivity qualification.  Belolipetsky--Kapovich
give a facet-count stopping bound which turns Vinberg's process into an
algorithm once the candidate integral mirrors can be enumerated.  Their
discussion is simplest for principal-ideal settings, but explicitly says that
the general systems of quadratic equations over algebraic integers require
details to be checked.  All three fields in (2.1) have class number one, which
removes projective ideal-class complications from this particular high-rank
slice; it does not by itself supply the missing certified global packet join
or the full algebraic-integer root-enumeration interface.  The repository's
projective order/conductor argument repairs finiteness of the *input host
list*; it has not yet supplied and verified that last general-field interface.
Accordingly, the 24-pair problem is a finite exact program with strong
effectivity evidence, but the current repository is not yet a completed
terminating decision procedure.

A bounded Vinberg prefix with a surviving hyperideal ray is not terminal.
For each enumerated host a finite terminal record must contain one of:

- a complete compact chamber transcript;
- a complete finite-volume but noncompact chamber transcript;
- a certified facet-count termination overflow after exhaustive root ordering;
- an exact proof that there are no integral mirrors; or
- an independent nonreflectivity certificate, such as an infinite-order
  chamber stabilizer with a proved exhaustive separator bound.

The last alternative is exactly what closes the silver
\(\langle-(\sqrt2-1)\rangle\oplus\Lambda_{22}\) host.  It is one terminal row,
not a substitute for enumerating all rows in (2.2).

## 6. AGCat's precise role

Let \(\mathsf C_n\) be the category of marked compact arithmetic Coxeter
chambers in dimension $n$.  The real-framed arrangement functor has exact
return on its image:

\[
       R_{\rm arr}F_{\rm arr}(P)\cong P.                \tag{6.1}
\]

Hence an exhaustive empty certificate for
\(F_{\rm arr}(\mathsf C_n)\) proves \(\mathsf C_n=\varnothing\).  This is an
important losslessness theorem: no hypothetical chamber is discarded during
the translation.  It is not an independent emptiness theorem.  The target is
the original quadratic form and polar wall arrangement with enough real
framing to recover the chamber, so faithfulness, exact return, or finiteness
of its integral points cannot replace the missing exhaustive enumeration.

In short,

\[
 \boxed{\text{finite full-host cover}+
        \text{terminal verdict for every host}
        \Longrightarrow\text{dimension nonexistence},}
\]

whereas

\[
 \boxed{\text{AGCat exact embedding alone}
        \not\Longrightarrow\text{target emptiness}.}
\]

## 7. What a terminal dimension certificate must freeze

For a chosen dimension $n$, a defensible certificate must include:

1. the complete allowed-field manifest, with primary-source scope;
2. every connected $62$-frame support and complete closed-cycle packet,
   modulo graph automorphisms and diagonal gauge;
3. exact selected Lorentzian and all-conjugate definite sign checks;
4. every symmetrizable representation and its minimal field;
5. every coefficient order, full reflection order, conductor, ideal class,
   and stable projective module;
6. canonical host deduplication under field automorphisms, similarity, and
   lattice isometries;
7. a terminal full-mirror verdict for every host in the sense of Section 5;
8. a coverage digest showing that every branch ends in one explicit rejection,
   duplicate, noncompact survivor, compact survivor, or nonreflectivity record;
9. an independent implementation replaying both coverage and every terminal
   witness.

Only an empty **compact-reflective survivor** list after these nine gates proves
nonexistence in dimension $n$.  The raw host list need not be empty: most rows
may be admissible but nonreflective.
