# Hostile audit: rational-polyhedral nef cones and hyperbolic volume

**Audit target:**
[`projective_surface_nef_hyperbolic_volume_theorem.md`](projective_surface_nef_hyperbolic_volume_theorem.md).  
**Audit date:** 2026-08-08.  
**Verdict:** **PASS**, with the stated `rho >= 3`, full-nef, and non-Coxeter
scope essential.

This audit gives an independent convex-Hodge proof, tests the sharp
one-dimensional failure, and separates the theorem from claims about divisor
subsystems, invariant sections, Mori cones, Coxeter angles, or Vinberg
parabolic data.

## 1. Precise statement audited

Let `X` be a smooth projective surface over an algebraically closed field of
arbitrary characteristic.  Work in

```text
V = N^1(X)_R,
rho = dim V,
q(D) = D^2,
n = rho - 1.
```

If `rho >= 3` and `Nef(X)` is finitely generated by real rays (in particular,
if it is rational polyhedral), then its projectivization in the positive cone
is an `n`-dimensional finite-volume hyperbolic polytope.  It is compact if and
only if no nonzero extremal nef ray is isotropic.

Rationality is not used in the volume estimate; finite polyhedral generation
is enough.  Rationality remains relevant to algebraic and integral marking.

## 2. The nef cone has the required Lorentz position

Fix an ample numerical class `H`.  Hodge index gives `q` signature `(1,n)`.
The ample cone is open in `V`, so its closure `Nef(X)` is full-dimensional.
For every nef class `D`, continuity from ample classes gives

```text
D^2 >= 0.
```

If `D` is nonzero and nef, then `H.D>0`.  Indeed, `H.D=0` would put `D` in
`H^perp`, where Hodge index is negative definite, contradicting `D^2>=0`
unless `D=0`.  It follows in particular that the nef cone is pointed: if both
`D` and `-D` were nef, positivity against `H` would force `D=0`.

Thus every nonzero nef ray lies in the closure of the same future Lorentz
cone, and the nef cone satisfies all three hypotheses that can fail for an
arbitrary divisor cone: it is full-dimensional, pointed, and has no spacelike
ray.

## 3. Independent finite-volume proof

Use the affine slice

```text
A_H = {D in V : H.D=1}.
```

In this slice, `{q>0}` is a Klein ball `B` and `{q=0}` is its boundary sphere.
Write the nef cone using its finitely many extremal generators:

```text
Nef(X) = cone(v_1,...,v_s),
p_i = v_i/(H.v_i).
```

The normalized base is the compact Euclidean polytope

```text
K = Nef(X) intersect A_H = conv(p_1,...,p_s).
```

Section 2 proves `K` is contained in the closed Klein ball.  Hence all its
vertices are finite or ideal and none is hyperideal.  Its hyperbolic part is

```text
P = K intersect B.
```

Triangulate `K` into finitely many full-dimensional Euclidean simplices, using
vertices in `K`.  Every resulting hyperbolic simplex has vertices only in the
open or closed Klein ball.  To check rather than quote its volume behavior,
consider one ideal vertex `p` and let `t` be affine distance from `p` inside a
simplex.  Its Euclidean shell has size

```text
O(t^(n-1) dt).
```

The Klein volume density is

```text
O((1-|x|^2)^(-(n+1)/2)) = O(t^(-(n+1)/2)),
```

because every edge from `p` enters the ball transversely.  The local volume is
therefore bounded by

```text
integral_0^epsilon t^((n-3)/2) dt.
```

This converges exactly for `n>1`, equivalently `rho>=3`.  There are finitely
many simplices and ideal vertices, so `P` has finite total hyperbolic volume.

## 4. Compactness and non-simple cusps

The future light cone is strictly convex projectively: a positive combination
of two distinct future causal rays is timelike.  Consequently a null point of
`K` cannot be a nontrivial convex combination of other points of `K`; it is a
vertex and corresponds to an isotropic extremal nef ray.  Therefore

```text
P compact
  <=> K avoids the Klein sphere
  <=> every extremal nef generator has positive square.
```

This also disposes of the non-simplicial case.  An ideal vertex may be
non-simple and incident to more than `n` facets, but its tangent cone admits a
finite simplicial subdivision and the estimate above applies to every piece.
There is no positive-dimensional projective face contained entirely in the
light sphere: strict convexity forbids a segment between distinct null points
from remaining null.  A face can have several ideal vertices, but its edges
and relative interior then enter hyperbolic space; this creates no additional
infinite-volume end.

## 5. Sharp failure in hyperbolic dimension one

Take

```text
X = P^1 x P^1,
N^1(X) = Z F_1 + Z F_2,
F_1^2=F_2^2=0,
F_1.F_2=1.
```

Here `rho=2` and

```text
Nef(X) = R_{>=0} F_1 + R_{>=0} F_2
```

is rational polyhedral.  Its two extremal rays are isotropic.  On the unit
hyperbola, set

```text
D(t) = (e^t F_1 + e^(-t) F_2)/sqrt(2),    -infinity < t < infinity.
```

Then `D(t)^2=1` and `D'(t)^2=-1`, so its projectivized nef cone is the whole
hyperbolic line with length

```text
integral_{-infinity}^{infinity} dt = infinity.
```

Thus `rho>=3` is sharp.  In rank one the hyperbolic section is
zero-dimensional and requires a separate volume convention; it is not part
of the audited theorem.

## 6. No automatic Coxeter or parabolic conclusion

The proof uses only Hodge signature, completeness of the nef cone, and finite
polyhedral generation.  It does **not** show that a nef facet normal:

- has square `-2` or any fixed norm;
- defines an integral lattice reflection;
- meets the other facets at angles `pi/m`;
- gives an acute-angled polytope; or
- belongs to a complete Coxeter wall list.

Accordingly rational-polyhedral nefness alone supplies no Coxeter diagram and
no elliptic-edge, affine-subdiagram, or full parabolic-zero-neighborhood
statement.  If reflection integrality, Coxeter angles, and exact identification
of all nef facets are proved independently, this audit supplies a structural
finite-volume proof for that Coxeter polytope.  Vinberg's endpoint and full
parabolic-neighborhood replay is then an independent literal certificate of
the extracted wall data, not a consequence of rational polyhedrality alone.

Likewise, an isotropic extremal nef ray is an ideal vertex, but it need not
come from a fibration on an arbitrary surface without a separate semiampleness
theorem.  Finite automorphism group, finite Cox ring, and Mori-dreamness are
also not consequences of this convex-Hodge argument.

## 7. Exact scope for subsystem, invariant, and Mori constructions

### Divisor or root subsystems

A cone cut by only selected divisor inequalities is normally **larger** than
the complete nef cone.  It may contain spacelike extremal rays, hence
hyperideal ends, even when the selected list is finite and rational.  The
theorem applies only after proving equality with the full nef cone, or after
separately proving the three convex hypotheses—full relative dimension,
pointedness, and containment in the closed positive cone—and handling the
dimension-one exception.  Hidden nef walls remain an exact-realization
failure even when a larger subsystem cone independently has finite volume.

### Invariant and eigenspace sections

For a rational subspace `F`, the section `Nef(X) intersect F` inherits the
argument provided that:

1. the restricted intersection form has signature `(1,k)`;
2. `k>=2`;
3. the section contains a relative-interior positive, preferably ample,
   class; and
4. the displayed finite wall cone equals the complete nef section.

For the fixed subspace of a genuine finite automorphism group, averaging an
ample class supplies the positive interior class.  This does not prove that a
proposed orbit-wall or quotient-wall list is complete, reflective, or
Coxeter.  A nontrivial eigenspace may contain no ample class or no positive
direction at all.  One-dimensional invariant sections retain the infinite-
length exception.

### Mori cones and Mori chambers

The cone of curves is not contained in the Lorentz positive cone: its
extremal curve classes may have negative square.  Projectivizing a rational-
polyhedral Mori cone therefore does not invoke this theorem.  If the complete
closed Mori cone is rational polyhedral, its dual nef cone is rational
polyhedral and the theorem applies to that **dual full nef cone**.  A selected
Mori chamber, a partial negative-curve list, or a cone in the effective divisor
space still requires effectivity, extremality, dual-cone completeness, and
positive-cone checks before any hyperbolic-volume conclusion.

## 8. Audit conclusion

The target theorem survives the hostile audit with no counterexample in its
stated range:

```text
rho(X) >= 3 + complete finitely generated Nef(X)
    => finite hyperbolic volume;

compact
    <=> no isotropic extremal nef ray.
```

The `rho=2` counterexample, exact-nef equality, and separation from Coxeter
and parabolic claims are indispensable.  Thus this document independently
confirms
[`projective_surface_nef_hyperbolic_volume_theorem.md`](projective_surface_nef_hyperbolic_volume_theorem.md)
at precisely its advertised scope, while preserving every existing
subsystem, invariant-section, and Mori-cone promotion gate.
