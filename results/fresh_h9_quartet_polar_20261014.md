The exact [census](fresh_h9_quartet_polar_20261014.json), generated by the [checker](../scripts/fresh_h9_quartet_polar_20261014.py), finds 53 finite vertices, no ideal vertices and 285 hyperideal extreme rays of the literal nineteen-wall cone. Four of the 285 polar hyperplanes have Coxeter-compatible relations with every retained wall. The other 281 each have at least one forbidden finite angle. Every compatible wall retains the strict center and misses the existing strict spacelike escape.

The compatible ray indices are 37, 38, 95 and 186. Their complete coordinates, norms, incidence, pairings and angle invariants are saved. All six mutual pairs are ultraparallel, so the four can be added together; the resulting chamber is verified separately in the [extension report](fresh_h9_quartet_polar_extension_20261014.md).

## Complete finite-angle test

The coefficient field is `K = Q(√5)(√R)`, with `R=6+(16/5)√5`. Its relative radicand norm is `−76/5`: one real embedding of `Q(√5)` extends to two real embeddings, the other to a conjugate pair of complex embeddings. Hence K has signature `(2,1)` and is not totally real.

Its only quadratic subfield is `Q(√5)`. Two different quadratic subfields would make K biquadratic and Galois; a Galois number field cannot have mixed real and complex embeddings. If `cos²(π/m)` belongs to K, its totally real field has degree at most four and dividing four. Degree four would make it equal to K, impossible. The rational cases give m=2,3,4,6. The quadratic cases are m=5,8,10,12; only m=5,10 have quadratic field `Q(√5)`. Thus the exhaustive finite-label alphabet is **2,3,4,5,6,10**, with no arbitrary label cutoff.

For a candidate polar normal r and retained normal n, the exact test uses `(r,n)²/(r² n²)`: zero gives a right angle, one gives a parallel pair, values greater than one give an ultraparallel pair, and values strictly between zero and one must equal one of the five allowed nonright cosine squares. The normal orientation is fixed by the original chamber's inequalities.
