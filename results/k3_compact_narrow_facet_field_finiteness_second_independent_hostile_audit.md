# Second independent hostile audit: compact narrow facets force finite K3 fields

**Verdict (2026-08-11): PASS in the exact stated scope.**  Let
\(P\subset\mathbf H^{m-1}\) be compact with \(m\ge3\), let its facet
reflections act linearly on a finite projective module over an order in a
totally real field \(k\), and let the underlying \(k\)-form be Lorentzian at
one place and positive definite at every other place.  If the primitive full
trace embeds in the Neron--Severi lattice of a complex projective K3 surface,
then

\[
 dm\le20,
 \qquad
 |\operatorname {Disc}k|
 \le (32\cdot14^{10})^{30}.
\]

The proof really does remove the earlier dotted-edge and imposed-height gates
for compact exact K3 carriers.  It does not construct a carrier, prove
Property 4, or imply finiteness of hosts or chambers.

## 1. The star-spanning step is valid

Choose a facet \(E\) of minimal visual aperture.  The elementary cone-plane
calculation gives

\[
 {2|h(f,g)|\over\sqrt{h(f,f)h(g,g)}}<14
\]

whenever the closed facets \(F,G\) both meet \(E\); pairs involving \(E\)
have the sharper Coxeter bound at most \(2\).

Compactness is used in a second, logically separate way.  A finite vertex of
a Coxeter chamber has a spherical simplex as link, so the compact chamber is
simple and its facet \(E\) is a simple compact convex polytope.  At a vertex
\(x\in E\), the \(m-2\) projected side normals through \(x\) span
\(x^\perp\cap e^\perp\).  Since \(\dim E=m-2\ge1\), compactness supplies a
side of \(E\) not containing \(x\); its projected normal has nonzero pairing
with \(x\), and therefore adds the missing direction in \(e^\perp\).  Adding
the normal \(e\) of \(E\) spans the full \(m\)-dimensional Lorentz space.

This is exactly where \(m\ge3\) is indispensable.  In hyperbolic dimension
one, a facet is a point, there is no nonincident side of that facet, and the
endpoint star need not span the rank-two field space.  The owner theorem
states and preserves this exclusion.

Choose \(m\) star normals forming a \(k\)-basis.  Their nonsingular Gram
matrix decomposes according to the connected components of its nonzero-entry
graph.  Signature additivity leaves exactly one nondegenerate Lorentz
component \(I\); all other components are positive definite.  Thus \(I\) is
a genuine hyperbolic principal Cartan block on at most \(m\) nodes.

## 2. The Lorentz block cannot lose the coefficient field

Put

\[
 a_{ij}={2h(r_i,r_j)\over h(r_i,r_i)}
\]

on \(I\), and let \(k_I\) be the field generated by all directed cyclic
products.  Suppose \(k_I\subsetneq k\).  Total reality and separability give
a nonidentity real embedding \(\sigma:k\hookrightarrow\mathbf R\) fixing
\(k_I\) pointwise.  For every nonzero directed edge set

\[
 q_{ij}={\sigma(a_{ij})\over a_{ij}}.
\]

Fixed two-cycles give \(q_{ij}q_{ji}=1\), while fixed longer cycles give
trivial multiplicative holonomy.  Connectivity therefore makes this edge
cochain a vertex coboundary:

\[
 q_{ij}=\lambda_j/\lambda_i,
 \qquad
 A_I^\sigma=D^{-1}A_ID.
\]

Hence the two Cartan matrices have identical eigenvalues.  At the
distinguished place, positive symmetrization identifies the signs of those
eigenvalues with the Lorentz Gram inertia, so one eigenvalue is negative.  At
the nonidentity place, admissibility and independence of the conjugate roots
positively symmetrize \(A_I^\sigma\) to a positive-definite Gram matrix, so
all eigenvalues are positive.  This contradiction proves \(k_I=k\).

Thus no separate minimal-field assumption is needed: under the exact
\(k\)-linear admissible-form hypotheses, a gratuitous proper coefficient
extension is already impossible.

## 3. Integrality and the height calculation survive rescaling

For a facet reflection \(s_i\), the rank-one operator \(T_i=1-s_i\) is an
endomorphism of the finite projective order-module.  Its canonical module
trace is order-valued, and

\[
 \operatorname {tr}(T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_\ell})
 =a_{i_1i_2}\cdots a_{i_\ell i_1}.
\]

Consequently every cyclic Cartan product is an algebraic integer even when
individual directed entries are not integral and the displayed normals are
not primitive.  Closed-walk splitting and chord splitting reduce the field
generators to pair cycles and chordless simple cycles on at most \(|I|\le m\)
nodes.

Symmetry identifies the two orientations of a cycle and gives

\[
 c(C)^2=\prod_{(i,j)\in C}a_{ij}a_{ji}.
\]

At the distinguished place each normalized factor is below \(14\); at every
definite place strict Cauchy--Schwarz bounds it by \(2\).  Therefore every
selected integral generator \(c\) satisfies

\[
                         |\tau(c)|<14^m
\]

at every real embedding \(\tau\).

## 4. The constant is safe

For \(d>1\), the K3 rank inequality and \(m\ge3\) imply

\[
                         d\le6,\qquad m\le10.
\]

A strict field tower generated from the bounded cycle packet has at most two
steps, since three proper adjunctions would force degree at least eight.  If
two elements \(x,y\) are needed, each unordered pair of the at most six real
embeddings forbids at most one coefficient in \(x+qy\).  There are at most
\(\binom{6}{2}=15\) pairs, so one of \(q=0,\ldots,15\) is primitive.  With
\(H=14^{10}\), its conjugates have magnitude below \(16H\), and any two
conjugates differ by less than \(32H\).  The integral primitive element then
gives

\[
 |\operatorname {Disc}k|
 \mid |\operatorname {Disc}(\mathbf Z[\theta])|
 <(32H)^{d(d-1)}
 \le(32\cdot14^{10})^{30}.
\]

The one-generator branch only improves this estimate.  The exponent \(30\)
is the maximum of \(d(d-1)\) for \(d\le6\).

## 5. Exact boundary

The compact theorem requires all of the following: \(m\ge3\), compactness,
an exact admissible \(k\)-form, \(k\)-linear integral facet reflections on a
finite projective order-module, and the primitive full trace inside complex
K3 Neron--Severi.  It uses neither reflection maximality nor a facet-count or
correspondence-height bound.  The separately audited cusp fixed-line theorem
