## 2. The physical normalization census is genuinely finite

Let \(m=n+1\), choose a compact facet star, and choose \(m\) star lines that
form a \(k\)-basis of \(V\). Compactness supplies such a basis: the neighboring
facet normals project to the side normals of the compact facet and span its
orthogonal hyperplane; adjoining the chosen facet normal spans \(V\).

There is a subtle field point here, but it passes. The nonzero-pairing graph
of the chosen basis may have definite components as well as one Lorentz
component \(I\). Let \(k_I\) be the field generated by the directed Cartan
cycles of \(I\). If \(k_I\subsetneq k\), the distinguished embedding of
\(k_I\) has another real extension to \(k\). Equality of all cycles makes the
two Cartan blocks diagonally similar: the ratios on directed edges form a
multiplicative cocycle with trivial products on cycles, hence a coboundary.
The conjugate block would then have the same Lorentz inertia as the
distinguished block. This contradicts definiteness at every other embedding.
Thus


\[
                              k_I=k.                  \tag{2.1}
\]

Because every raw facet reflection preserves a successful projective order
module, the traces of products of its rank-one operators are in the
coefficient order. Pair cycles and chordless cycles therefore give algebraic
integers generating \(k\). For

\[
             \mathcal O_0=\mathbf Z[\text{these cycles}],
\]

every successful coefficient order satisfies

\[
              \mathcal O_0\subseteq\mathcal O
                    \subseteq\mathcal O_k.            \tag{2.2}
\]

The quotient \(\mathcal O_k/\mathcal O_0\) is finite, so enumerating additive
subgroups and retaining the multiplicatively closed ones lists every possible
order. In particular, an unbounded conductor cannot escape this interval.

Now let the literal physical roots on the basis lines be
\(\delta_i=a_i r_i\) and let \(g=\lambda g_0\). Put

\[
              u_i=g(\delta_i,\delta_i)
                  =\lambda a_i^2h_i.
\]

At the distinguished place a wall normal is negative and at every other
place the form is negative definite. Since the trace lattice square is
\(-2\),

\[
 u_i\in\mathcal O_0^\vee,\qquad
 \tau(u_i)<0\ \text{for all }\tau,qquad
 \operatorname {Tr}_{k/\mathbf Q}(u_i)=-2.            \tag{2.3}
\]

The codifferent assertion follows from
\(\operatorname {Tr}(a u_i)=B(a\delta_i,\delta_i)\in\mathbf Z\) for every
\(a\in\mathcal O_0\). The positive place energies \(-\tau(u_i)\) sum to two,
so (2.3) lies in a compact Minkowski box. Its intersection with the
codifferent lattice is finite.

For each finite tuple in that shell, simultaneous normalization is equivalent
to the exact common-square-class conditions

\[
        \frac{u_i/h_i}{u_1/h_1}\in k^{\times2}
        \quad (2\le i\le m).                           \tag{2.4}
\]

Gauge \(a_1=1\). Then \(\lambda=u_1/h_1\), the distinguished orientations
choose the signs of all square roots \(a_i\), and every entry of the field
Gram is forced. The gauge is exactly multiplication of the module by
\(a_1^{-1}\) together with multiplication of the form by \(a_1^2\); it is a
marked raw-similarity, not a loss of hosts.

The golden exact replay independently recovers the five points in the
\(\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)\) trace-\(-2\) shell. It also checks a golden Lorentz
Cartan block whose pair cycle has minimal polynomial
\(X^2-3X+1\), hence generates the full field.

## 3. The order/module census has no ideal or saturation escape

For one surviving physical basis put

\[
                   F_0=\bigoplus_i\mathcal O_0\delta_i.
\]

Every successful module contains \(F_0\). Integrality against \(F_0\) gives

\[
                         F_0\subseteq M\subseteq F_0^\#. \tag{3.1}
\]

The finite discriminant group \(F_0^\#/F_0\) therefore contains every
possible additive module as the inverse image of one of its finitely many
subgroups. For each candidate the multiplier order

\[
             \mathcal O_M=\{a\in k:aM\subseteq M\}
\]

is exact and finite to compute. Enumerating every
\(\mathcal O_0\subseteq\mathcal O\subseteq\mathcal O_M\), then filtering for
evenness, integrality, projectivity over that particular order, and stability
under all raw reflections is exhaustive. Projectivity over
\(\mathcal O_0\) or over the maximal multiplier order is not silently
assumed.

For a nonbasis facet line \(\ell_a\), the trace form on the underlying
rational space of that totally negative \(k\)-line is negative definite.
Consequently

\[
 \{\delta\in M\cap\ell_a:B(\delta,\delta)=-2\}/\{\pm1\}
\]

is finite. Requiring this set to be nonempty for every facet finishes a
simultaneous same-module physical normalization of all walls. Merely having
ambient Picard roots project to the same real lines would not justify (2.3)
or (3.1), which is exactly why the projected-root scaling family lies outside
the theorem.

## 4. Primitive K3 embeddability is the exact discriminant gate

For \(N=M_{\mathbf Z}\), the necessary rank/signature test is

\[
             \operatorname {rk}N=[k:\mathbf Q]m\le20,
             \qquad \operatorname {sig}N=(1,\operatorname {rk}N-1).
                                                               \tag{4.1}
\]

A primitive embedding in the K3 lattice exists precisely when an even
complement \(T\) of signature \((2,20-\operatorname {rk}N)\) exists with

\[
                             q_T\simeq-q_N.             \tag{4.2}
\]

Necessity is unimodular anti-gluing. Conversely, gluing along an
anti-isometry of the two discriminant forms produces the unique even

## 5. Compactness turns all relevant hidden roots into one finite ellipsoid

Fix an exact interior point \(p\in P^\circ\). On the full Lorentz space
define

\[
 Q_p(z)=\frac{2B(p,z)^2}{B(p,p)}-B(z,z).               \tag{5.1}
\]

Writing \(z=ap+z_\perp\) proves directly that \(Q_p\) is positive definite.
Let \(R\) be the maximum hyperbolic distance from \([p]\) to \(P\); compactness
makes it finite and it is computable from the exact vertices. If a root
\(\delta^2=-2\) has a proper mirror meeting \(P\), the point-to-hyperplane
formula gives

\[
 \frac{|B(p,\delta)|^2}{B(p,p)}\le2\sinh^2R,
 \qquad Q_p(\delta)\le2\cosh(2R).                     \tag{5.2}
\]

Thus every root that can cut the interior or witness a facet belongs to one
terminating lattice-point enumeration. Roots with zero distinguished
projection satisfy \(Q_p(\delta)=2\); if one occurs, its mirror contains all
of \(E\), so no point of \(E\) can be ample. It is correctly treated as a
terminal rejection rather than ignored.

For each enumerated root, exact algebraic feasibility decides whether its
restricted mirror meets \(P^\circ\) or equals a facet. The three tests

1. no zero distinguished projection;
2. no proper root mirror meets \(P^\circ\); and
3. every facet is witnessed by a root mirror

are necessary and sufficient. The first two put \(P^\circ\) in one connected
component of the locally finite restricted arrangement. The third prevents
that component from extending through any facet. No limiting wall can occur
because the integral root arrangement is locally finite.

## 6. Period, Weyl framing, and codimension-greater-than-one facets

Choose a period in the complement \(T\) away from all extra
Noether--Lefschetz hyperplanes. Period surjectivity gives a marked projective
K3 surface with \(\operatorname {NS}(S)=N\). At Picard rank twenty the
positive rank-two complement is already the whole period plane, so equality
is automatic.

Let \(D\) be the ambient complete root chamber containing \(p\). Section 5
gives

\[
                             D\cap E=P^\circ.           \tag{6.1}
\]

The K3 Weyl group is transitive on positive root chambers. Weyl re-marking
takes \(D\) to the ample chamber and conjugates the self-adjoint order action,
so the closure of (6.1) becomes exactly the claimed eigen-nef section. This
does not assert that the Weyl element is a surface automorphism.

The most delicate geometric return is a facet of \(D\cap E\) that lies in an
ambient face of codimension greater than one. Let \(y\) be generic in that
facet. Local finiteness leaves finitely many ambient simple-root inequalities
near \(y\). At least one is active, or \(y\) would be interior to \(D\cap E\).
Its restriction is nonzero because zero-projection roots were rejected. Any
nonzero active inequality is nonnegative on \(D\cap E\) and vanishes at a
relative-interior point of a facet; hence its restricted hyperplane is the
unique supporting hyperplane of that facet. Therefore at least one ambient
simple root supports every restricted facet, even when several independent
ambient walls are simultaneously active.

After Weyl framing, such a simple root is the class of a smooth rational
square-\((-2)\) curve. Full Picard equality places the very same vector in the
trace module. If its distinguished projection is proportional to a raw
\(k\)-normal, equality of coordinate ratios at the injective embedding
\(k\hookrightarrow\mathbf R\) descends the proportionality to \(k\). Thus
the physical same-vector return used by the compact host theorem is an output
of every accepted package.

The executable models the codimension issue with the first orthant in
\(\mathbf R^3\) sliced by \(x=y\): the slice facet \(x=y=0\) is an ambient
codimension-two face, while both active inequalities restrict to the same
nonzero supporting functional on the slice.

## 7. Maximality does not replace the compact root gate

For \(d=[k:\mathbf Q]>1\), an ordinary Picard root reflection has rational
minus-eigenspace dimension one and generally does not preserve \(E\). The
native \(k\)-reflection has rational minus-eigenspace dimension \(d\), but it
need not preserve the candidate module. Raw maximality therefore does not by
itself exclude a hidden restricted K3 wall.

There is a valid finite shortcut only after checking that every relevant
native reflection in the ellipsoid preserves the same marked module and that
maximality is relative to that full native host. A hidden root would then
produce a proper discrete reflection overgroup. Facet witnesses and the
zero-projection rejection remain necessary.

In the exact finite-volume cusped full-trace lane, the ground-field theorem
forces \(k=\mathbf Q\), so \(E=N_{\mathbf R}\) and ordinary/native reflection
species coincide. The bounded closed-star theorem supplies a physical root
basis with

\[
                    0\le E_i.E_j\le13.                \tag{7.1}
\]

There are finitely many possible basis Grams, and for each root frame
\(R\subseteq N\subseteq R^*\) leaves finitely many rational Picard hosts.
Full-host reflection maximality then excludes hidden root cuts. This proves
the stated \(B=13\) cusped compiler corollary, but neither the rational nor
the compact compiler proves that a raw packet has any carrier.

Necessity follows by choosing physical simple roots from a spanning compact
