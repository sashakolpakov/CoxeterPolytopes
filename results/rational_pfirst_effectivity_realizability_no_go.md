# A P-first effectivity obstruction before the incidence stack

**Status (2026-08-10): exact classification-free no-go and field-of-definition
lemma.**  The rank-three calculation is replayed by
[`certify_rational_pfirst_effectivity_realizability_no_go.py`](../scripts/certify_rational_pfirst_effectivity_realizability_no_go.py).
No Nikulin--Vinberg classification, ABSW theorem, arithmetic-volume formula,
or chamber census is used.

This note answers a deliberately narrow inverse question.  Start with an odd
unimodular Lorentzian lattice, a characteristic vector having the numerical
square of a rational-surface canonical class, a complete finite-volume
Coxeter chamber, and the integral-reflection/adjunction alphabet

\[
 (-1),\qquad (-2),\qquad C=2r,\ C^2=-4.
\]

Those data do **not** force a rational surface whose whole nef cone is the
given chamber.  There is a missing canonical orientation gate even before
one reaches proximity or incidence geometry.  After that gate, simultaneous
effectivity remains a genuine Hilbert/incidence-stack condition rather than
a consequence of Riemann--Roch.

## 1. The canonical-escape condition

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective rational surface over an algebraically
closed field.  Then

\[
                         K_S\notin\operatorname {Nef}(S).       \tag{1.1}
\]

Indeed, run the elementary rational-surface MMP.  If \(S\) is nonminimal,
a contracted \((-1)\)-curve \(E\) has \(K_S.E=-1\).  On a minimal rational
surface, a line on \(\mathbb P^2\) or a ruling fibre has canonical degree
\(-3\) or \(-2\).  This argument works in every characteristic.

Consequently a full-nef physical chamber must satisfy the **canonical escape
gate**

\[
                              K\notin P.                        \tag{1.2}
\]

When the complete Mori cone is generated by smooth rational physical
facets of square \(-1,-2,-4\), (1.2) is equivalent to the existence of a
\((-1)\)-facet: adjunction gives the canonical pairings \(-1,0,2\),
respectively.  In particular, a complete chamber containing only physical
\((-2)\)- and twice-primitive \((-4)\)-walls cannot be a rational nef cone.

## 2. A reflection-maximal finite-area counterexample

Put

\[
 L=\mathbb ZH\oplus\mathbb ZE_1\oplus\mathbb ZE_2\simeq I_{1,2},
 \qquad H^2=1,\quad E_i^2=-1,
\]

and

\[
                         K=-3H+E_1+E_2.                        \tag{2.1}
\]

Thus \(K\) is characteristic and

\[
                         K^2=7=10-\operatorname {rk}L.         \tag{2.2}
\]

Take the oriented physical wall classes

\[
 R=E_1-E_2,qquad B_1=-2E_1,qquad
 B_2=-2(H-E_1-E_2).                                           \tag{2.3}
\]

They obey

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 C&R&B_1&B_2\\ \hline
 C^2&-2&-4&-4\\
 K.C&0&2&2
\end{array}                                                    \tag{2.4}
\]

and hence pass smooth-rational adjunction numerically.  The two long walls
are twice primitive.  Their primitive mirror roots are

\[
       R,\qquad -E_1,\qquad -H+E_1+E_2,                        \tag{2.5}
\]

which form a determinant-one basis of \(L\); all three reflections are
integral on \(L\).

The physical Gram matrix is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -2&2&0\\
 2&-4&4\\
 0&4&-4
 \end{pmatrix}.                                                \tag{2.6}
\]

Thus the angles are

\[
                         (\pi/4,\pi/2,0).                      \tag{2.7}
\]

The component cut out by \(x.R,x.B_1,x.B_2\ge0\) contains

\[
                         h=-4H+E_1+2E_2,qquad h^2=11,          \tag{2.8}
\]

strictly.  Its three vertices are represented by

\[
 -H,\qquad -2H+E_1+E_2,qquad -H+E_2,                          \tag{2.9}
\]

of squares \(1,2,0\).  Hence this is a complete finite-area hyperbolic
Coxeter triangle, with one cusp and area

\[
                             \pi/4.                            \tag{2.10}
\]

It is also reflection-maximal, even without restricting the overgroup to
\(O(L)\).  A proper discrete reflection supergroup would have index at
least two and therefore a noncompact Coxeter fundamental polygon of area at
most \(\pi/8\).  Every positive-area noncompact hyperbolic Coxeter polygon
has area at least \(\pi/6\): for at least four sides the bound is \(\pi/2\),
and for a triangle it is attained by \((2,3,\infty)\).  This is impossible.

Nevertheless

\[
                         K.R=0,\qquad K.B_1=K.B_2=2,            \tag{2.11}
\]

and \(K^2>0\), so \([K]\) lies on the finite side \(R^\perp\) of this
very chamber.  If a marked rational surface had this chamber as its entire
nef cone and (2.1) as its canonical class, then \(K_S\) would be nef,
contradicting (1.1).

> **Canonical-orientation no-go.**  Odd unimodularity, the rational canonical
> square, characteristic parity, the exact physical adjunction alphabet,
> integral spanning reflections, finite volume, and even absolute reflection
> maximality do not force a rational full-nef realization.

The counterexample is not a claim that its three mirror hyperplanes are new.
It is an inverse-realizability theorem: the oriented physical scaling and
canonical marking cannot be forgotten.

## 3. What Riemann--Roch does and does not orient

For a proposed smooth rational curve \(C\) with \(C^2=-q\), adjunction gives
\(K.C=q-2\).  On a rational surface,

\[
 \chi(\mathcal O_S(C))
 =1+\frac{C^2-K.C}{2}=2-q.                                   \tag{3.1}
\]

Thus the three physical colors give

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
q&1&2&4\\ \hline
\chi(\mathcal O(C))&1&0&-2.
\end{array}                                                    \tag{3.2}
\]

Only the \((-1)\) row can be forced by the elementary argument, and even
there one needs an ample orientation which kills
\(H^0(K-C)=H^2(C)\); for example it suffices that
\(A.(K-C)<0\).  Riemann--Roch supplies no effectivity at all for the
\((-2)\) and \((-4)\) rows.

This failure is geometric, not a loose inequality.  Blow up three marked
points of \(\mathbb P^2\).  The noncollinear and collinear configurations
have the same \((L,K)\), but

\[
                         H-E_1-E_2-E_3                         \tag{3.3}
\]

is ineffective in the first and is the strict-transform \((-2)\)-curve in
the second.  Simultaneous wall effectivity therefore records incidence and
infinitely-near cluster data absent from \((L,K)\).

An exact rational P-first criterion must consequently quantify over a
**geometric blowdown frame**.  In a plane or ruled frame it must require:

1. a realizable cluster of proper and infinitely-near centres satisfying
   proximity;
2. an irreducible effective representative of every physical wall class;
3. all forced Bezout/incidence conditions simultaneously; and
4. equality of the generated curve cone with the complete Mori cone, so
   that no additional effective curve cuts the proposed nef chamber.

Conversely, such a frame and cluster construct the required blow-up, and
the fourth condition gives \(P=\mathbb P(\operatorname {Nef}S)\).  This is
the non-tautological content of the finite Hilbert/incidence stacks used by
the inverse compiler.  Their nonemptiness is not a lattice theorem.

## 4. A full ordinary nef chamber has minimal field \(\mathbb Q\)

There is a second, independent correction to a universal direct-full-nef
claim.

> **Rational-field lemma.**  Let \(S\) be an ordinary projective surface and
> suppose a finite-sided Coxeter polytope is literally the projectivized
> full nef cone in \(\operatorname {NS}(S)_\mathbb R\).  Then its minimal
> Vinberg field is \(\mathbb Q\).

Every facet is supported by a rational curve ray and hence has a primitive
normal \(c_i\in\operatorname {NS}(S)\).  Put \(q_i=-c_i^2\).  In normalized
Coxeter Gram coordinates, an edge entry is

\[
                    g_{ij}=\frac{c_i.c_j}{\sqrt{q_iq_j}}       \tag{4.1}
\]

up to the harmless common convention factor two.  Around any closed cycle,
every square root occurs twice, and therefore

\[
 g_{i_1i_2}g_{i_2i_3}\cdots g_{i_ki_1}
 =\frac{(c_{i_1}.c_{i_2})\cdots(c_{i_k}.c_{i_1})}
        {q_{i_1}\cdots q_{i_k}}\in\mathbb Q.                  \tag{4.2}
\]

Vinberg's minimal field is generated by these cyclic products, so it is
\(\mathbb Q\).  Equivalently, all facet hyperplanes and reflection matrices
are already defined over the rational Neron--Severi space.

This does **not** forbid non-rational arithmetic polytopes from having an
algebraic carrier.  It says that they cannot be the entire nef cone of an
ordinary surface.  They require a proper invariant/eigen-nef slice (or a
restriction-of-scalars construction), where projected normals need not be
rational in the slice.

## 5. Consequence for canonical chamber rigidity

The direct functor cannot start with only

```text
(L, K, finite-volume Coxeter walls, adjunction colors).
```

Before any dimension or field bound is pushed back, it must prove at least

```text
canonical escape K notin P
+ a geometric blowdown/proximity frame
+ simultaneous irreducible effectivity
+ no hidden Mori rays
+ (for non-Q fields) an exact invariant/eigen-nef slice, not full Nef(S).
```

Reflection maximality does not supply the first item, as Section 2 proves.
Riemann--Roch does not supply the middle two, as Section 3 proves.  The
rational-field lemma makes the last distinction formal.  These are precise
obligations for property 4 of a polytope-to-surface functor, not reasons to
replace the AG program by a Coxeter classification.
