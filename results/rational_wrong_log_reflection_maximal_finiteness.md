# Reflection-maximal weak-incidence carriers: an AG dimension and finiteness theorem

**Status (2026-08-10): proved, carrier-relative, and
reflective-classification-free.**
This note combines the project-native wrong-log rank theorem with an exact
adapted-lattice saturation argument.  It gives an ABSW-type finiteness
conclusion for the whole weak-incidence rational full-nef lane, not merely
for its direct and Coble subloci.  It uses no Nikulin--Vinberg reflective
classification, ABSW theorem, Prasad formula, or arithmetic-volume bound.

The theorem does **not** assert that every raw reflection-maximal arithmetic
Coxeter chamber has such a rational-surface carrier.  Carrier construction,
complete-Mori equality, and the hostile-incidence complement remain separate
gates.

## 1. Geometric input and the AG rank ceiling

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective rational surface over \(\mathbf C\).  Assume
that its complete Mori cone is generated by smooth rational curves of square
\(-1,-2,-4\).  Write

\[
                         B=\sum_{i=1}^b B_i
\]

for the reduced square-minus-four part, and impose the weak-incidence gates

\[
 B_i.B_j=0\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
 B.R=0\quad(R^2=-2),\qquad
 B.E\leq2\quad(E^2=-1).                                  \tag{1.1}
\]

The wrong-log theorem proves that

\[
                         D=-\left(K_S+{B\over2}\right)
\]

is nef.  Contracting the \(B_i\) gives a projective klt surface with
\(1/4(1,1)\) points, and the exact identities

\[
 D^2=10-\rho+b,\qquad
 \widehat c_2=\rho+2-{7b\over4}                            \tag{1.2}
\]

together with orbifold \(c_2\)-semipositivity give

\[
 7b\leq4\rho+8,\qquad \rho\leq10+b,qquad
                         \boxed{\rho\leq26}.               \tag{1.3}
\]

Thus the associated literal full-nef hyperbolic chamber has

\[
                              \boxed{n\leq25}.              \tag{1.4}
\]

The rest of this note turns this AG dimension bound into a finite chamber
theorem under reflection maximality.

## 2. The adapted host is integral and contains every facet reflection

Let \(L=\operatorname {NS}(S)\), and use the doubled intersection form.  Put

\[
 M=\left\langle L,{B_1\over2},\ldots,{B_b\over2}\right\rangle,
 \qquad q_M=2q_L.                                          \tag{2.1}
\]

The lattice is integral.  Indeed,

\[
 q_M(B_i/2,B_j/2)=-2\delta_{ij},\qquad
 q_M(B_i/2,x)=B_i.x\in\mathbf Z\quad(x\in L).             \tag{2.2}
\]

Every physical facet reflection preserves \(M\):

* \(B_i/2\) and every square-minus-one curve have host norm \(-2\), so
  integrality of \(M\) is enough;
* a square-minus-two curve \(R\) has host norm \(-4\), while
  \(q_M(x,R)=2x.R\) is even for \(x\in L\), and
  \(q_M(B_i/2,R)=B_i.R=0\) by (1.1).

No log equation \(B=-2K_S\) is used here.  In particular, this construction
strictly includes the wrong-log locus \(D\ne0\).

## 3. Reflection maximality saturates the host

Let \(P=\mathbf P(\operatorname {Nef}S)\) be the supplied complete
finite-sided finite-volume Coxeter chamber and \(W(P)\) its facet-reflection
group.  Let \(R(M)\) be the group generated by all integral hyperbolic
reflections of \(M\).

Assume either that \(W(P)\) has no proper discrete hyperbolic reflection
overgroup, or only the weaker relative condition that it has no proper
reflection overgroup inside \(O^+(M)\).  Then

\[
                              \boxed{W(P)=R(M)}.             \tag{3.1}
\]

For if \(s\in O^+(M)\) is any integral reflection, then
\(\langle W(P),s\rangle\) is a discrete reflection group inside the discrete
integral orthogonal group.  Maximality forces \(s\in W(P)\).  The same proof
works for maximality among arithmetic reflection groups: a discrete
overgroup of the finite-covolume lattice \(W(P)\) has finite index and is
again arithmetic.

The complete mirror arrangement of \(R(M)\) is locally finite.  A generic
path between two chambers crosses finitely many mirrors, and the product of
the crossed reflections carries one chamber to the other.  Hence all
complete chambers are \(R(M)\)-conjugate: there is at most one unmarked
chamber isometry class for each host \(M\).

## 4. Only finitely many adapted hosts occur

The rational Picard lattice is unimodular.  In positive hyperbolic dimension
its possibilities are

\[
 L\simeq U\text{ or }I_{1,1}\quad(\rho=2),\qquad
 L\simeq I_{1,\rho-1}\quad(3\leq\rho\leq26).              \tag{4.1}
\]

Regard \(L(2)\) as \(L\) with doubled form.  Unimodularity gives

\[
             L(2)\subseteq M\subseteq L(2)^*={1\over2}L.   \tag{4.2}
\]

If \(H\leq L/2L\simeq\mathbf F_2^\rho\) is the span of the classes
\(B_i\bmod2L\), then

\[
 M=M_H=L+{1\over2}\widetilde H.                           \tag{4.3}
\]

It is integral exactly when \(x.y\equiv0\pmod2\) for all \(x,y\in H\).
The geometric boundary span automatically satisfies this condition by
(2.2).  Forgetting integrality and isometric duplication gives the explicit
coarse upper bound

\[
 2S_2(2)+\sum_{\rho=3}^{26}S_2(\rho)
 =5{,}515{,}561{,}289{,}142{,}524{,}504{,}797{,}715{,}176{,}506{,}813{,}442{,}521{,}589{,}215{,}285{,}286, \tag{4.4}
\]

where

\[
 S_2(\rho)=\sum_{h=0}^{\rho}{\rho\brack h}_2,qquad
 {\rho\brack h}_2=
 \prod_{i=0}^{h-1}{2^{\rho-i}-1\over2^{h-i}-1}.            \tag{4.5}
\]

The deliberately enormous number (4.4) counts every binary subspace,
including nonintegral ones and isometric repetitions.  It is a transparent
proof-of-finiteness bound, not a predicted census size.

## 5. The theorem

> **Theorem 5.1 (weak-incidence AG/ABSW theorem).**  Up to ambient
> hyperbolic isometry, only finitely many Coxeter chambers which are
> reflection-maximal in any of the three senses of Section 3
> admit a literal complete full-nef complex rational carrier satisfying
> (1.1).  Every such chamber has
> 
> \[
>                         n\leq25,
> \qquad k(P)=\mathbf Q,                                  \tag{5.1}
> \]
> 
> and the number of unmarked chamber classes is at most (4.4).

The dimension bound is genuinely algebro-geometric, coming from the klt
\(1/4(1,1)\) contraction and orbifold \(c_2\).  Finiteness then comes from
the finite adapted-host interval and reflection saturation.  Finally,
\(k(P)=\mathbf Q\) because every wall normal lies in the rational quadratic
space \(M_{\mathbf Q}\), so every Cartan entry and every cyclic Gram product
is rational.

This is stronger than a collection of examples: it is a class-wide
dimension, field, and finiteness theorem on the declared carrier class.  Its
two remaining universality gates are explicit.

1. A raw reflection-maximal arithmetic chamber is not yet proved to admit
   such a literal rational full-nef carrier.
2. Intersecting square-minus-four curves, positive \((-4)/(-2)\) incidence,
   and aggregate \(B.E\geq3\) lie outside (1.1).  Their audited core/tail
   reduction does not yet give an absolute rank bound.

The host bound does not itself provide a terminating reflectivity census for
candidate hosts.  No such census is required for the existential finiteness
statement: only finitely many hosts can contribute, and every contributing
host has one complete chamber orbit.
