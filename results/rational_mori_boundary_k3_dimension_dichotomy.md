# A rational Mori-boundary dichotomy with an AG dimension bound

**Status (2026-08-10): proved carrier-relative class theorem.**  This is an
algebraic-geometric rank bound, not a census and not an invocation of
Nikulin--Vinberg, ABSW, Prasad, or an arithmetic-volume formula.  Its remaining
global gap is stated explicitly in Section 5.

## 1. The geometric input

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective rational surface over an algebraically
closed field.  Assume that the closed cone of curves is rational polyhedral
and generated by finitely many smooth rational negative curves.  These
generators automatically span \(N_1(S)_{\mathbf Q}\): by definition,
numerical curve classes span \(N_1(S)_{\mathbf Q}\), every such class lies in
the linear span of the closed effective cone, and that cone is assumed to be
the cone on the displayed finite generators.  Thus spanning is a consequence
of the complete-Mori-cone hypothesis, not an extra empirical assertion about
a partial wall list.

We consider two intrinsic alternatives.

1. **Weak-anticanonical alphabet.**  Every extremal generator has square
   \(-1\) or \(-2\).
2. **Index-two boundary alphabet.**  A nonempty distinguished set
   \(B_1,\ldots,B_b\) consists of pairwise disjoint curves of square \(-4\).
   Put \(B=\sum_i B_i\).  Every other extremal generator \(C\) satisfies

   \[
   (C^2,B\mathbin\cdot C)=(-2,0)\quad\hbox{or}\quad(-1,2).       \tag{1.1}
   \]

The second alphabet is exactly the numerical Coble boundary/root/crossing
alphabet used by the raw inverse compiler.  No assertion that \(S\) belongs
to a classified list is made.

## 2. The dichotomy theorem

### Theorem 2.1

Under the weak-anticanonical alphabet,

\[
                         -K_S\ \text{is nef},\qquad
                         \rho(S)\leq 10.                         \tag{2.1}
\]

Under the index-two boundary alphabet,

\[
                         B=-2K_S\quad\text{in }\operatorname {Pic}(S). 
                                                                    \tag{2.2}
\]

If the characteristic is not two, the double cover branched over \(B\) is
smooth, connected, and K3.  Consequently

\[
 \rho(S)\leq
 \begin{cases}
 20,&\operatorname {char}=0,\\
 22,&\operatorname {char}>2.
 \end{cases}                                                       \tag{2.3}
\]

If the full restriction-of-scalars trace module of an arithmetic Coxeter
datum injects into \(\operatorname {NS}(S)_{\mathbf Q}\), then the same proof
gives

\[
 [k:\mathbf Q](n+1)\leq
 \begin{cases}
 10,&\text{weak-anticanonical branch},\\
 20,&\text{index-two branch in characteristic zero},\\
 22,&\text{index-two branch in odd positive characteristic}.
 \end{cases}                                                       \tag{2.4}
\]

In particular, when the original chamber is literally the projectivized
full nef cone of \(S\), its dimension is \(n=\rho(S)-1\).  The respective
dimension ceilings are therefore \(9,19,21\).

### Proof

For a smooth rational curve \(C\), adjunction gives

\[
                         K_S\mathbin\cdot C=-2-C^2.                \tag{2.5}
\]

In the first branch, \(-K_S\mathbin\cdot C\) is respectively \(1\) or
\(0\) on every generator of the Mori cone.  Thus \(-K_S\) is nef, so
\(K_S^2\geq0\).  A smooth rational surface satisfies

\[
                         K_S^2+\rho(S)=10,                         \tag{2.6}
\]

which proves (2.1).

For the second branch put \(L=K_S+B/2\).  Pairwise disjointness and
adjunction give

\[
 L\mathbin\cdot B_i=2+\tfrac12(-4)=0.
\]

Equation (1.1) gives \(L\mathbin\cdot C=0\) for every remaining extremal
generator: it is \(0+0\) for a \((-2)\)-curve and
\(-1+2/2\) for a \((-1)\)-curve.  The generators span numerical curve
space, so \(L\equiv0\) as a rational divisor class.  Equivalently, the
integral divisor \(2K_S+B\) is numerically trivial.  A smooth rational
surface has \(\operatorname {Pic}^0(S)=0\), and its Picard group is a free
lattice (for example by a blowup presentation from a minimal rational
surface).  Hence an integral numerically trivial divisor is linearly trivial,
so \(B=-2K_S\) in \(\operatorname {Pic}(S)\), proving (2.2).  This argument
works in every characteristic.

Now assume the characteristic is not two.  The curves \(B_i\) are smooth and
pairwise disjoint, so their union \(B\) is a smooth, though generally
disconnected, effective divisor.  Its canonical section and the isomorphism
\(\mathcal O_S(B)\simeq\mathcal O_S(-2K_S)\) define the finite flat double
cover \(\pi:X\to S\) with cover algebra
\(\mathcal O_S\oplus\mathcal O_S(K_S)\).  Locally along \(B\) it has equation
\(z^2=t\), where \(t=0\) is a local equation for the smooth branch divisor;
because \(2\ne0\), this proves smoothness.  Away from \(B\) the cover is
étale and hence smooth.  Thus

\[
 K_X=\pi^*(K_S-K_S)=0,
 \qquad
 \pi_*\mathcal O_X=\mathcal O_S\oplus\mathcal O_S(K_S).          \tag{2.7}
\]

The cover is connected: if a finite flat degree-two cover of the connected
normal surface \(S\) were disconnected, both degree-one components would be
isomorphic to \(S\), leaving no branch divisor.  Nonemptiness of \(B\) rules
this out.  Moreover rationality of \(S\), Serre duality, and (2.7) give

\[
 H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)
       \oplus H^1(S,\mathcal O_S(K_S))=0,
 \qquad \chi(\mathcal O_X)=1+1=2.                         \tag{2.7a}
\]

Thus the smooth connected surface \(X\) has trivial canonical bundle and
\(H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0\), so it is K3.  Pullback is
injective on numerical divisor classes and multiplies their intersection
form by two, so

\[
                 \operatorname {NS}(S)(2)\hookrightarrow
                 \operatorname {NS}(X).                           \tag{2.8}
\]

The K3 Picard ceilings \(20\) in characteristic zero and \(22\) in positive
characteristic prove (2.3).  The first follows after descent to a finitely
generated characteristic-zero subfield and embedding in \(\mathbf C\); the
geometric Neron--Severi rank is unchanged by the resulting algebraically
closed base extension.  No K3-cover conclusion is asserted in characteristic
two, where the local separable-double-cover argument fails.  Injecting the
full trace block of rank
\([k:\mathbf Q](n+1)\) proves (2.4).  \(\square\)

## 3. Why this is stronger than assuming a Coble label

The argument never assumes that \(|-K_S|\) is empty, that \(|-2K_S|\) is a
single point, that the automorphism group is finite, or that \(S\) occurs in
a classified table.  The anti-bicanonical identity is *forced* from the
complete Mori alphabet and its spanning property.  A raw compiler must still
prove that its displayed curves generate the **entire closed Mori cone**;
incidence checks on a partial list are insufficient.  Once that completeness
statement and the displayed incidences are established, it does not need to
recognize a named Coble type before the rank bound applies.

The theorem also explains the two successful surface species in one line.
The toric full-nef endpoint lies in the weak-anticanonical branch and has
\(\rho=7\).  The frozen rational Coble carriers in dimensions
\(10,11,14,17\) lie in the index-two branch with
\(\rho=11,12,15,18\).  Their canonical covers are the already certified K3
carriers.  Here “control” is literal but source-relative: the executable pins
the five independent full-nef archives and replays their physical wall Grams,
checking the square alphabet, all boundary incidences, and rank spanning.
Completeness of the Mori generators is imported from those full-nef source
theorems rather than reproved by this small certifier.  For the one-boundary
Type-II archive, the stored first normal is \(b=-K_S=B/2\); the replay doubles
that row and column before checking the physical \((-4)\)-boundary alphabet.
The Type-VI and Type-MII controls are in characteristic three and use the
ceiling \(22\), not the complex ceiling \(20\).

## 4. Exact relation to the equivariant-minimalization theorem

This theorem and equivariant minimalization solve different failures.
Finite numerical operator orbit of one chosen geometric exceptional lattice
lets a canonical-orthogonal trace block descend to a minimal surface.  The
present theorem instead handles rational carriers whose trace block cannot
descend through the
rational minimal model: the complete boundary alphabet manufactures a K3
cover before contraction.  Consequently the next surface compiler may pass
by either of two checkable routes:

```text
finite operator orbit of chosen E_pi -> minimal canonical carrier -> rank <= 20/22;
complete rational Mori alphabet  -> canonical K3 cover         -> rank <= 10 or 20/22.
```

## 5. The remaining global theorem

This does **not** prove that every reflection-maximal arithmetic chamber has
such a surface.  Reflection maximality alone supplies neither a geometric
Mori cone nor effectivity of the numerical wall classes.  The exact next
P-first obligation is now narrower:

> From raw marked Coxeter data, construct an exact carrier and prove either
> that one chosen geometric exceptional lattice has finite numerical operator
> orbit, or prove the complete rational Mori alphabet above, with literal
> recovery of the original chamber.

For the Coble lane this is precisely the nonemptiness half of the proposed
`PB-Atlas`: the current all-contraction theory describes a finite incidence
stack, but no arbitrary-input executable yet derives all collision,
Jacobian, irreducibility, and Hilbert bad factors from raw walls.  Once that
compiler exists, Theorem 2.1 supplies the dimension pushback without any
Kond\=o/Nikulin classification.
