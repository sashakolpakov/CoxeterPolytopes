# Every compact exact K3 trace chamber in dimension at least two has a finite field set

**Status (2026-08-11): proved for every compact Coxeter chamber in
\(\mathbf H^n\), \(n\ge2\), returned by an exact primitive full-trace
complex-K3 carrier.**  Put \(m=n+1\), let \(k\) be the coefficient field
of the exact admissible carrier, and let \(d=[k:\mathbf Q]\).  The K3 trace
embedding gives

\[
                              dm\le20.                    \tag{0.1}
\]

A facet of minimal visual aperture has a star whose absolute normalized
symmetric pairings are all smaller than \(14\).  Compactness makes that star
span the complete Lorentz space.  A basis extracted from it has one connected
Lorentz Cartan block, and admissibility forces the cyclic field of that block
to be the full coefficient field \(k\).  Hence \(k\) is generated by integral
cycles on at most \(m\) nodes, all of whose real conjugates have absolute
value below \(14^m\).

If \(d>1\), then (0.1) and \(m\ge3\) give \(d\le6\) and \(m\le10\).  A
two-generator primitive-element argument therefore yields the explicit safe
bound

\[
       |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|
       \le \bigl(32\cdot14^{10}\bigr)^{30}.               \tag{0.2}
\]

Consequently the field set is finite and effectively enumerable.  No
reflective-lattice classification, arithmetic volume formula, imposed
correspondence height, facet-count bound, saturation, or reflection
maximality is used.

Together with the separate
[noncompact ground-field collapse](exact_trace_noncompact_ground_field_collapse.md),
this closes the field-set problem for **every finite-volume exact full-trace
K3 carrier in hyperbolic dimension at least two**.  It does not construct the
carrier or prove Property 4.

## 1. Exact carrier package

Let \(k\subset\mathbf R\) be a totally real field with distinguished
embedding \(\sigma _0\), let \(\mathcal O\subset k\) be an order, and let
\(M\) be a finite projective rank-\(m\) \(\mathcal O\)-module.  Put

\[
                         V=M\otimes_{\mathcal O}k.
\]

Let \(h\) be a nondegenerate symmetric \(k\)-form which has signature
\((m-1,1)\) at \(\sigma _0\) and is positive definite at every other real
embedding.  Assume:

1. \(m=n+1\ge3\), and finitely many \(\mathcal O\)-linear \(h\)-reflections
   return exactly the facets of a compact Coxeter chamber
   \(P\subset\mathbf H^{m-1}\);
2. a complex projective K3 surface has Neron--Severi lattice containing the
   primitive full trace of \(M\).

The second condition gives (0.1), because \(\rho(X)\le20\).  Individual wall
normals need not be primitive and \(M\) need not be free.  After tensoring
with \(k\), however, every reflection has a one-dimensional \(k\)-rational
minus eigenspace, so one may choose a nonzero \(k\)-normal for each wall.

No separate minimal-ground-field hypothesis is imposed.  Lemma 4.1 proves
that the coefficient field of an exact admissible \(k\)-form with these
\(k\)-linear reflections is already the cyclic Cartan field.  Thus a
gratuitous proper scalar extension is incompatible with admissibility.

## 2. The narrow-facet bound (14)

Fix an interior point \(O\in P\).  For each facet take the angular opening of
its visual cone from \(O\), and choose a facet \(E\) with minimal opening
\(\theta_E\).  Let \(F,G\) be any two facets whose closed faces meet \(E\).
Write \(\theta_F,\theta_G\) for their openings and
\(\theta_{FG}\) for their mutual visual separation.  The two-dimensional
cone-plane calculation, after scaling all three normals to square \(2\), is

\[
 b(F,G)=
 4\frac{
 \sin((\theta_F+\theta_{FG})/2)
 \sin((\theta_G+\theta_{FG})/2)}
 {\sin(\theta_F/2)\sin(\theta_G/2)}-2.                  \tag{2.1}
\]

Minimality gives

\[
 0\le\theta_{FG}\le\theta_E
 \le\min\{\theta_F,\theta_G\}<\pi.                     \tag{2.2}
\]

For \(0\le b\le a<\pi\),

\[
              \frac{\sin((a+b)/2)}{\sin(a/2)}<2.        \tag{2.3}
\]

Indeed, for \(a\le\pi/3\) the ratio is at most
\(\sin(a)/\sin(a/2)=2\cos(a/2)<2\), while for
\(a>\pi/3\) it is at most \(1/\sin(a/2)<2\).  Applying (2.3) twice in
(2.1) gives

\[
                              0\le b(F,G)<14.             \tag{2.4}
\]

For outward spacelike normals \(f,g\), set

\[
 b(F,G)=\frac{2|h(f,g)|}{\sqrt{h(f,f)h(g,g)}}.
\]

Thus intersecting Coxeter walls have value \(2\cos(\pi/q)\) and
ultraparallel walls have value \(2\cosh(\delta)\).  If one of the walls is
\(E\) itself, the Coxeter angle
gives the sharper bound \(b\le2\).  Thus every absolute normalized
off-diagonal entry in the star

\[
 \mathcal S(E)=\{E\}\cup\{F:F\cap E\ne\varnothing\}     \tag{2.5}
\]

has value below \(14\).  This is the elementary minimality-\(14\)
lemma, reproduced here rather than imported from a reflective-lattice
classification.

## 3. The compact star contains a Lorentz basis block

Let \(e\) be a \(k\)-normal to \(E\).  Project every neighboring wall normal
orthogonally to \(e^\perp\).  These are precisely the supporting side normals
of the compact convex facet \(E\subset\mathbf H^{m-2}\).  Its intrinsic side
angles need not themselves be Coxeter angles.

### Lemma 3.1 (star spanning)

The projected neighboring normals span \(e^\perp\); consequently the normals
in \(\mathcal S(E)\) span \(V\).

### Proof

A compact acute Coxeter chamber has finite spherical vertex links and is
simple; consequently every face, including \(E\), is simple.
Choose a vertex \(x\) of \(E\).  The \(m-2\) side facets of \(E\) incident at
\(x\) have projected normals spanning \(x^\perp\) inside \(e^\perp\).  Since
\(E\) is compact and positive-dimensional, it has a side not containing
\(x\); that side normal pairs nontrivially with \(x\), so it is not in
\(x^\perp\).  The projected side normals therefore span all of \(e^\perp\).
Adding \(e\) spans \(V\).  The same argument for the one-dimensional facet
case says that the two endpoints of a compact segment supply the two
required projected directions. \(\square\)

Choose \(m\) actual star normals which form a \(k\)-basis of \(V\).  Their
Gram matrix is nonsingular and Lorentzian.  Split its nonzero-pairing graph
into connected components.  Orthogonal block decomposition and Lorentz
signature show that exactly one component \(I\) has Lorentzian Gram matrix;
every other component is positive definite.  The roots in \(I\) are still
linearly independent, and

\[
                              |I|\le m.                    \tag{3.1}
\]

## 4. The Lorentz block inherits the full field

For roots \(r_i\), put

\[
 a_{ij}=\frac{2h(r_i,r_j)}{h(r_i,r_i)}.                  \tag{4.1}
\]

Let \(k_I\) be the field generated by all directed cyclic products of the
connected Cartan block \(A_I=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}\).

### Lemma 4.1 (cyclic-field inheritance)

One has \(k_I=k\).

### Proof

Suppose \(k_I\subsetneq k\).  Because \(k/k_I\) is separable and totally
real, the distinguished embedding of \(k_I\) has a second real extension
\(\sigma:k\hookrightarrow\mathbf R\), distinct from \(\sigma_0\).

For every directed nonzero edge define

\[
             q_{ij}=\frac{\sigma(a_{ij})}{a_{ij}}.
\]

Every block cycle is fixed by \(\sigma\).  In particular
\(q_{ij}q_{ji}=1\), and the product of the \(q_{ij}\) around every directed
cycle is one.  On a connected graph this multiplicative one-cocycle is a
coboundary: there are nonzero real numbers \(\lambda_i\) with

\[
                       q_{ij}=\lambda_j/\lambda_i.
\]

Hence

\[
                    A_I^\sigma=D^{-1}A_ID,
                    \qquad D=\operatorname {diag}(\lambda_i).            \tag{4.2}
\]

At the distinguished place \(A_I\) is positively symmetrizable to a
Lorentzian matrix, so it has one negative eigenvalue.  More explicitly, if
\(D_0=\operatorname{diag}(h(r_i,r_i)/2)\) and \(G_I\) is the Gram matrix,
then

\[
 D_0^{1/2}A_ID_0^{-1/2}=D_0^{-1/2}G_ID_0^{-1/2},
\]

so the Cartan eigenvalue signs equal the Gram inertia.  Equation (4.2)
preserves all eigenvalues.  At the nonidentity place, however, \(h^\sigma\)
is positive definite and the independent conjugate roots positively
symmetrize \(A_I^\sigma\) to a positive-definite Gram matrix.  All its
eigenvalues are positive, a contradiction.  Thus \(k_I=k\). \(\square\)

This step explains why mere spanning was not enough: admissibility and the
complete cycle identities are the descent mechanism.  It also proves that
the coefficient field is the minimal cyclic/Vinberg field rather than taking
that equality as an additional hypothesis.

## 5. Integral bounded generators

Let \(s_i\) be a facet reflection and put \(T_i=1-s_i\).  Since every
\(s_i\) preserves the finite projective order-module, so does \(T_i\), and
the module trace of every product \(T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_\ell}\) belongs to
\(\mathcal O\).  The rank-one trace identity identifies this trace with the
corresponding directed cyclic Cartan product.  Thus every such product is
integral.  This does not require primitive normals, a free module, or
integral individual Cartan entries.

Closed walks split at repeated vertices, and simple cycles with chords split
into shorter cycles after adjoining their pair cycles.  Therefore \(k_I=k\)
is generated by integral pair products and simple chordless cycles on at
most \(|I|\le m\) vertices.

For a cycle \(C=(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)\), symmetry of \(h\) gives equality of
the two orientations and hence

\[
        c(C)^2=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}
        \bigl(a_{i_ji_{j+1}}a_{i_{j+1}i_j}\bigr).        \tag{5.1}
\]

At \(\sigma_0\), (2.4) bounds each square root of a pair product by \(14\).
At every definite embedding strict Cauchy--Schwarz bounds it by \(2\).  Thus
every selected integral generator \(c\) satisfies

\[
                    |\tau(c)|<14^m                         \tag{5.2}
\]

for every real embedding \(\tau:k\hookrightarrow\mathbf R\).

## 6. K3 rank turns the height bound into a finite field set

The case \(d=1\) is \(k=\mathbf Q\).  Assume \(d\ge2\).  From \(dm\le20\)
and \(m\ge3\),

\[
                         d\le6,\qquad m\le10.             \tag{6.1}
\]

Consequently every integral cycle generator has all conjugates bounded by

\[
                              H=14^{10}.                   \tag{6.2}
\]

Choose a minimal field-generating list.  Every proper step in its field tower
has degree at least two, so \(2^r\le d\le6\); hence the list has at most two
elements.  If there is one generator \(x\), take \(\theta=x\).  If there are
two generators \(x,y\), consider

\[
                              x+qy,\qquad 0\le q\le15,     \tag{6.3}
\]

at least one is primitive: each pair of the at most six real embeddings
excludes at most one \(q\), and there are at most \(\binom62=15\) pairs.
For that primitive element \(\theta\),

\[
                    |\tau(\theta)|\le16H,
 \qquad
                    |\tau(\theta)-\tau'(\theta)|\le32H.  \tag{6.4}
\]

The field discriminant divides the polynomial discriminant of \(\theta\),
so

\[
 |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|
 \le (32H)^{d(d-1)}
 \le\bigl(32\cdot14^{10}\bigr)^{30}.                    \tag{6.5}
\]

This proves (0.2).  Equivalently, the bounded conjugates give finite boxes
for the coefficients of the possible monic minimal polynomials, so the field
set is effectively enumerable without invoking Hermite--Minkowski as a black
box.

## 7. Exact scope and global corollary

### Theorem 7.1

Every package satisfying Section 1 has a ground field in the finite effective
set defined by (6.5).

### Corollary 7.2

Let an exact primitive full-trace complex-K3 carrier return any finite-volume
Coxeter chamber in hyperbolic dimension \(n\ge2\).  If the chamber is compact,
Theorem 7.1 applies.  If it is noncompact, the exact cusp fixed-line theorem
forces \(k=\mathbf Q\).  Hence the field set is finite in every case, with no
separate dotted-height or correspondence-height hypothesis.

The theorem does **not** apply to hyperbolic dimension one.  The frozen
rank-four exact eigen-nef interval family has infinitely many real quadratic
fields and is the sharp countercontrol.  Nor does this theorem construct a
K3 carrier, prove essential surjectivity/Property 4, bound the number of
chambers or hosts, or classify compact Coxeter polytopes.  It proves field
finiteness after the exact carrier has been supplied.
