# Smooth toric surfaces: an exact Coxeter factory and its sharp `H6` ceiling

**Status (2026-08-09): proved scoped classification/return theorem and one
exact endpoint realization.**  This replaces the earlier nonsharp toric
`H9` estimate.  It does not bound arbitrary Coxeter polytopes; it completely
settles the direct **smooth-toric full-nef integral-reflection** carrier.

## 1. Outcome

There is a genuinely automatic algebraic factory, but it is much smaller
than the higher-dimensional search needs.

> **Toric full-nef Coxeter theorem.**  Let \(X\) be a smooth projective toric
> surface with \(\rho(X)\ge3\).  Projectivize its complete nef cone inside the
> positive cone of \(\operatorname{NS}(X)_\mathbf R\).  The following are
> equivalent:
>
> 1. every hyperbolic nef facet is the mirror of an integral reflection of
>    \(\operatorname{NS}(X)\);
> 2. every torus-invariant boundary curve has square at least \(-2\);
> 3. \(-K_X\) is nef.
>
> Under these conditions the complete nef section is automatically a
> finite-volume integral hyperbolic Coxeter polytope.  It is noncompact, and
>
> \[
>   \rho(X)\le7,\qquad \dim P_X=\rho(X)-1\le6.
> \]

There are exactly sixteen equivariant isomorphism classes before the low-rank
cut, and twelve with \(\rho\ge3\).  Their hyperbolic dimensions occur with
multiplicities

```text
H2: 2,   H3: 4,   H4: 2,   H5: 3,   H6: 1.
```

These are twelve marked toric carriers; no claim is made that their abstract
polytopes or arithmetic commensurability classes are new.

The unique endpoint is an exact full-nef `H6/9` chamber.  Its Coxeter diagram
is a nine-cycle with cyclic labels

```text
(4,4,3) (4,4,3) (4,4,3).
```

Its complete exact ray profile is

```text
18 finite + 3 ideal + 0 hyperideal.
```

The abstract chamber is **not new**: the same labelled nine-cycle is the
dimension-six bottom-right diagram in Im Hof's twice-truncated-simplex/Napier
cycle list.  What is new to this project is the complete toric full-nef
realization, the sharp theorem classifying the whole smooth-toric species,
and an exact \(216\)-chamber degeneration/gluing interpretation relating it
to the classical smooth-cubic chamber \(P^6\).  Worldwide priority for that
marked interpretation is not claimed.

Thus the toric lane supplies a clean positive realization and a sharp
negative stopping theorem at the same time.  It cannot reach the desired
dimensions above six without leaving at least one of smoothness, the full
nef cone, primitive integral facet reflections, or the toric category.

## 2. Why nef walls are not automatically reflection mirrors

Write the invariant boundary in cyclic order as

\[
  D_1+\cdots+D_r=-K_X,\qquad D_iD_{i+1}=1,\qquad
  D_iD_j=0\quad(j\ne i,i\pm1).
\]

The toric cone theorem says that \(\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\) is
generated by the \(D_i\).  A boundary curve of negative square spans an
extremal ray: removing its fan ray gives the corresponding toric contraction,
possibly to a singular surface.  Consequently its orthogonal hyperplane is
an actual irredundant nef wall.  Curves of square zero or greater do not give
hyperbolic facets: their orthogonal hyperplanes do not cut a codimension-one
face in the open positive cone.  Such an inequality can still be part of the
linear nef-cone presentation on its light boundary; it becomes invisible or
redundant only after restriction to the chosen open hyperbolic sheet.

Every \(D_i\) is primitive, since it meets either adjacent boundary curve
once.  The numerical lattice of a smooth rational surface is unimodular, so
reflection in \(D_i^\perp\),

\[
  s_i(x)=x-\frac{2(x,D_i)}{D_i^2}D_i,
\]

is integral exactly when

\[
  D_i^2\in\{-1,-2\}.
\]

This is the first indispensable gate: toric polyhedrality alone gives a nef
polyhedron, not a Coxeter or arithmetic one.

## 3. Equivalence with the weak-del-Pezzo condition

For an invariant boundary curve, adjunction is the elementary toric identity

\[
  (-K_X,D_i)=2+D_i^2.                       \tag{3.1}
\]

Since the invariant curves generate the Mori cone,

\[
  -K_X\text{ nef}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  D_i^2\ge-2\text{ for every }i.            \tag{3.2}
\]

If every hyperbolic facet reflection is integral, a hypothetical
\(D_i^2\le-3\) would itself be a negative extremal invariant curve and hence
an integral facet mirror, contradicting primitivity.  This proves
`(1) => (2)`.  Equation (3.2) proves `(2) <=> (3)`, while primitivity proves
`(2) => (1)`.

The anticanonical divisor on a complete toric variety is big.  Therefore
nefness makes \(X\) a smooth toric weak del Pezzo surface, not merely a
surface satisfying a numerical lower bound.

## 4. The Coxeter-angle gate is then automatic

Two distinct invariant prime curves have intersection zero or one.  For the
negative curves that actually define nef facets, put

\[
 c_{ij}=\frac{(D_i,D_j)}{\sqrt{D_i^2D_j^2}}.
\]

There are only four cases:

| curve pair | \(c_{ij}\) | hyperbolic relation |
|---|---:|---|
| disjoint | \(0\) | \(m_{ij}=2\) |
| adjacent \((-2),(-2)\) | \(1/2\) | \(m_{ij}=3\) |
| adjacent \((-1),(-2)\) | \(1/\sqrt2\) | \(m_{ij}=4\) |
| adjacent \((-1),(-1)\) | \(1\) | parallel mirrors, \(m_{ij}=\infty\) |

Thus the exact Coxeter gate follows from the divisor intersection algebra.
It is important that the last row is **not** a zero-angle ridge: the two
facets are asymptotic and do not meet inside hyperbolic space.

## 5. Finite volume and noncompactness are separate gates

Toric nef cones are rational polyhedral.  Hodge index puts
\(\operatorname{NS}(X)\) in signature \((1,\rho-1)\), and the complete nef
cone has no hyperideal extremal rays.  The convex--Hodge theorem therefore
gives finite hyperbolic volume for \(\rho\ge3\).  This conclusion uses
**complete nef equality**; a selected list of invariant divisors would not
be enough.

Compactness fails independently.  A smooth projective toric surface is
rational.  After equivariant contractions it maps to a Hirzebruch surface
(or passes through \(\mathbb F_1\) on its way to \(\mathbf P^2\)); pulling back
a ruling fibre gives a nonzero nef isotropic class.  Hence every
positive-dimensional full-nef toric chamber has an ideal point.

In surface language the volume statement is therefore

```text
complete toric nef cone + rho>=3  => finite volume;
toric ruling fibre                => noncompactness.
```

Neither statement follows merely from seeing a finite Coxeter-labelled
subdiagram.

## 6. The sharp dimension bound from reflexive polygons

For a smooth toric weak del Pezzo surface, the anticanonical contraction is a
Gorenstein toric del Pezzo surface.  If \(\Delta\subset N_\mathbf R\) is its
reflexive fan polygon, every ray of the fan of \(X\) is a boundary lattice
point of \(\Delta\).  In dimension two, smoothness forces the maximal crepant
subdivision: skipping a boundary lattice point would make the determinant of
the adjacent fan rays exceed one.  Thus in this precisely defined maximal
fan

\[
  r=\#(\partial\Delta\cap N).                \tag{6.1}
\]

The twelve-point theorem for a reflexive polygon and its polar says

\[
  \#(\partial\Delta\cap N)+
  \#(\partial\Delta^\vee\cap M)=12.          \tag{6.2}
\]

The polar has at least three boundary lattice points.  Hence \(r\le9\), and
the standard toric divisor sequence gives

\[
  \rho(X)=r-2\le7,\qquad \dim P_X=r-3\le6.  \tag{6.3}
\]

This is the missing sharp step in the earlier toric `H9` estimate.  The
Noether identity alone gives only

\[
  \sum_iD_i^2=12-3r
\]

and cannot see the reflexive-polygon dual perimeter.

Equality in (6.3) forces the polar polygon to have precisely three boundary
lattice points.  Up to \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf Z)\) it is the primitive
reflexive triangle, so the nine-ray endpoint is unique.  Equivalently, its
boundary self-intersection cycle is

\[
  (-2,-1,-2,\,-2,-1,-2,\,-2,-1,-2).         \tag{6.4}
\]

## 7. Independent finite fan classification

The bound and uniqueness do not depend on a database.  The frozen certifier
also replays the full classification by equivariant blowups.

Every nonminimal smooth complete toric surface has an invariant \((-1)\)-curve.
Blowing it down preserves \(D_i^2\ge-2\).  The only minimal endpoints allowed
by that inequality are

```text
P2: (1,1,1),
F0: (0,0,0,0),
F2: (-2,0,2,0).
```

The inverse torus-fixed blowup rule is

```text
(...,a,b,...) -> (...,a-1,-1,b-1,...),
```

and it preserves the inequality exactly when \(a,b\ge-1\).  Closing these
three seeds under that rule and quotienting by cyclic reversal/rotation gives

| fan rays \(r\) | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | total |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| classes | 1 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 1 | **16** |

The twelve rows with \(r\ge5\) are exactly the hyperbolic factory stated in
Section 1.  The archive records every cyclic word, its wall Gram rank and
inertia, and its Coxeter-label histogram.

## 8. Exact `H6/9` endpoint

Start with the toric degree-six del Pezzo surface
\(\operatorname{Bl}_{p_1,p_2,p_3}\mathbf P^2\), whose boundary is a six-cycle
of \((-1)\)-curves.  Blow up the three torus fixed points corresponding to a
perfect matching of that six-cycle.  The centers are pairwise disjoint on the
degree-six surface.  In the basis

\[
  (H,E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4,E_5,E_6),\qquad
  q=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1),
\]

the nine boundary walls are

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
 E_1-E_4,&E_4,&H-E_1-E_2-E_4,\\
 E_2-E_5,&E_5,&H-E_2-E_3-E_5,\\
 E_3-E_6,&E_6,&H-E_3-E_1-E_6.
\end{array}                                      \tag{8.1}
\]

Their squares are `(-2,-1,-2)^3`; adjacent intersections are one and all
other intersections vanish.  Hence the diagram is the nine-cycle
`(4,4,3)^3`.  The wall matrix has Smith factors

```text
1,1,1,1,1,1,1,
```

so the curves span the whole \(I_{1,6}\), not a proper divisor sublattice.
The \(9\times9\) Gram has

```text
rank 7, inertia (1,6,2).
```

Every reflection is an integral involutive \(q\)-isometry.  The canonical
class

\[
  K_X=-3H+E_1+\cdots+E_6,\qquad K_X^2=3,
\]

satisfies adjunction against all nine walls.  The timelike class

\[
  A=7H-2(E_1+E_2+E_3)-(E_4+E_5+E_6)
\]

pairs strictly positively with every wall.

For a direct volume check, an extreme ray in the seven-dimensional cone has
six independent active walls.  The certifier exhausts all
\(\binom96=84\) subsets over \(\mathbf Q\), deduplicates primitive rays, and
finds exactly

```text
21 rays = 18 timelike + 3 lightlike + 0 spacelike.
```

The three cusps are the ruling classes

\[
  H-E_1,\qquad H-E_2,\qquad H-E_3.            \tag{8.2}
\]

Projection of \(A\) to each wall gives an exact positive witness for all nine
facet inequalities, so none of the nine boundary curves is redundant.  This
proves that (8.1) is the **complete** full-nef `H6/9` chamber, rather than a
finite wall subsystem with an unchecked end.

There is also a rigid equation-level description.  One exact fan recurrence
gives the nine primitive rays

```text
(1,0), (0,1), (-1,1), (-2,1), (-3,1),
(-1,0), (1,-1), (3,-2), (2,-1).
```

The anticanonical polygon has exactly four lattice points

```text
X=(-1,-1),  Y=(0,-1),  W=(0,0),  Z=(1,2).
```

They satisfy the sole affine exponent relation

\[
  X+Y+Z=3W.
\]

Consequently the anticanonical image is the toric cubic

\[
  XYZ=W^3\subset\mathbf P^3.                 \tag{8.3}
\]

The six \((-2)\)-curves form three disjoint \(A_2\) chains and are precisely
the exceptional locus of the crepant anticanonical contraction.  Over
characteristic different from three, (8.3) has the familiar three \(A_2\)
singularities.  The integral toric equation and fan contraction remain
valid in characteristic three, but quotient/inertia and Jacobian
descriptions there require the usual bad-characteristic care.

Finally, the three lightlike rays pass the literal Vinberg cusp gate, not
only the no-hyperideal test.  Each has seven active walls whose Gram inertia
is

```text
(positive,negative,zero)=(0,5,2).
```

The active diagram splits into affine components of Coxeter ranks \(3+2=5\)
(types \(\widetilde C_3+\widetilde C_2\)); every outside wall pairs strictly
positively with the cusp ray.  Thus all three are complete maximal
parabolic cusp supports.

## 9. The exact \(3A_2\)-smoothing and \(216\)-chamber theorem

The equation (8.3) supplies more than a convenient model.  Let
\(G_{\mathrm{tor}}\) be the reflection group generated by the nine walls in
(8.1), and let \(F\) be the subgroup generated by its six
\((-2)\)-reflections.  Their diagram is three disjoint \(A_2\)'s, and exact
matrix closure gives

\[
 F\cong W(A_2)^3,\qquad |F|=6^3=216.         \tag{9.1}
\]

Reduction of the seven-dimensional integral reflection matrices modulo two
is faithful on \(F\): the archive finds 216 distinct matrices.  Each of the
three \((-1)\)-reflections is congruent to the identity modulo two.  More
strikingly, the \(F\)-orbits of the three \((-1)\)-wall classes
\(E_4,E_5,E_6\) are disjoint nine-element sets, and their union is exactly
the standard \(6+15+6\) set

\[
 \{E_i\}_{i=1}^6\ \cup\
 \{H-E_i-E_j\}_{i<j}\ \cup\
 \{2H-\!\sum_{j\ne i}E_j\}_{i=1}^6          \tag{9.2}
\]

of the 27 \((-1)\)-classes on a marked smooth cubic surface.  Conjugating a
reflection conjugates its root, so \(G_{\mathrm{tor}}\) contains all 27 line
reflections.

Here one external identification enters.  Everitt--Ratcliffe--Tschantz
identify the group generated by the 27 reflections in (9.2) with the
principal level-two reflection group \(\Gamma_2^6\), whose chamber is the
classical Potyagailo--Vinberg right-angled polytope \(P^6\).  Since
\(\Gamma_2^6\) is the mod-two kernel, the preceding exact calculation gives

\[
  1\longrightarrow\Gamma_2^6\longrightarrow G_{\mathrm{tor}}
   \longrightarrow W(A_2)^3\longrightarrow1,              \tag{9.3}
\]

with the displayed copy of \(W(A_2)^3\) as a complement.  Equivalently,

\[
 [G_{\mathrm{tor}}:\Gamma_2^6]=216,
 \qquad P^6=\bigcup_{f\in W(A_2)^3} fP_{\mathrm{tor}}.    \tag{9.4}
\]

The [independent congruence
audit](toric_h6_congruence_smoothing_audit.md) verifies coefficientwise that
every one of the 27 classes in (9.2) is a \(0/1\) sum of the nine toric
boundary classes.
Therefore the chosen toric nef chamber lies inside the chosen \(P^6\)
chamber; together with the index, this proves (9.4) without an orientation
ambiguity.

Thus the right-angled smooth-cubic nef chamber is obtained by deleting the
six mirrors in the \(3A_2\) configuration and gluing 216 copies of the
toric endpoint chamber.
Under a simultaneous-resolution marking, this is precisely the Weyl-chamber
gluing associated with smoothing the three \(A_2\) singularities of
\(XYZ=W^3\).  The statement is exact at the lattice and chamber level; it
does not assert that the abstract Im Hof or Potyagailo--Vinberg polytopes are
new.  Here \(W(A_2)^3\) is the deck group of the product local
simultaneous-resolution marking cover; no claim is made that a single
one-parameter smoothing pencil has the full product as its monodromy image.

Two independent Euler calculations check the index numerically.  Solomon's
formula over all \(2^9=512\) endpoint special subsets finds 322 spherical
subsets and

\[
 \chi(G_{\mathrm{tor}})=-\frac1{1728},\qquad
 \operatorname{Vol}(P_{\mathrm{tor}})=\frac{\pi^3}{3240}.
\]

For the right-angled 27-wall chamber, the exact mutually-disjoint-line
clique counts are

```text
1, 27, 216, 720, 1080, 648, 72
```

in sizes zero through six.  Hence

\[
 \chi(\Gamma_2^6)=-\frac18,\qquad
 \operatorname{Vol}(P^6)=\frac{\pi^3}{15},
\]

and both the Euler and volume ratios are 216.

## 10. Exact scoped `P -> surface` recognizer

The classification gives a terminating inverse theorem, not merely a
surface-first list.

Let \(P\) be a marked integral Coxeter polytope with primitive wall normals in
an integral Lorentz lattice \(N\).  Then \(P\) is the entire nef chamber of a
smooth projective toric surface, with every facet reflection integral, if and
only if the following finite procedure accepts.

1. Select one of the twelve cyclic boundary words with \(r\ge5\).
2. Reconstruct its fan from two consecutive primitive rays and the recurrence

   \[
     u_{i-1}+u_{i+1}=-D_i^2u_i,
   \]

   checking cyclic closure, determinant one for adjacent rays, and winding
   number one.
3. Form the exact toric divisor presentation

   \[
     0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow\mathbf Z^r
      \longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(X_\Sigma)\longrightarrow0.
   \]

4. Extract every negative invariant boundary class.  These, and only these,
   are the hyperbolic nef facets.
5. Test an integral lattice isometry

   \[
     (N,\{\text{walls of }P\})\cong
     (\operatorname{Pic}(X_\Sigma),\{D_i:D_i^2<0\})
   \]

   including saturation, root lengths, cyclic intersection Gram, and the
   positive-cone orientation.

Acceptance constructs the surface \(X_\Sigma\), and the toric cone theorem
returns exactly the input \(P\).  Rejection is exhaustive because there are
only twelve rows.  A match of the unweighted Coxeter graph alone is
insufficient: the integral lattice and primitive root lengths are part of the
criterion.  The algorithm retains all twelve candidates; it does not assume
that a Gram matrix by itself selects a unique fan.  Once a cyclic
self-intersection word is selected, that word determines the smooth fan up
to \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf Z)\), cyclic shift, and reversal.

This is a real but **scoped** same-\(P\) return inside the declared
smooth-toric completion species:

```text
P + strong smooth-toric full-nef marking
  -> finite exhaustive fan list
  -> reflexive-polygon theorem
  -> dim(P)<=6 and P noncompact.
```

It is not a theorem that every arithmetic Coxeter polytope possesses such a
marking.  More pointedly, the accepted denominator is the toric-completable
locus itself, obtained by comparison with the twelve surface-classified fan
presentations.  It is not an independently broad surface-free Coxeter class,
and no pre/post surface survivor delta has been proved on such a class.
Therefore this is a strong exact inverse recognizer/classification, **not**
the flagship `L4` polytope-to-surface universality theorem.  It contributes no
universal upper bound for compact arithmetic Coxeter polytopes.

## 11. Design verdict

Smooth toric surfaces are therefore useful in three sharply delimited ways.

1. **Automatic positive factory:** twelve exact finite-volume noncompact
   full-nef Coxeter carriers in dimensions two through six.
2. **Exact inverse test:** a bounded fan/divisor reconstruction decides the
   strong toric carrier locus from marked Coxeter data.
3. **Structural no-go:** the twelve-point theorem forbids this species above
   `H6`, and rational rulings forbid compact members altogether.

The surface-side leverage is real only after the smooth-toric full-nef
completion species has been declared: within that species,
reflexive-polygon classification forces the sharp \(H^6\) ceiling and a
ruling forces noncompactness.  Under the strict inverse contract, however,
the pre/post AG survivor delta on an independently defined broad P-only class
is zero.  Arbitrary arithmetic polytopes and every compact target remain
outside the inference.

They do not offer a route to new compact examples above dimension nine.  A
higher-dimensional toric-inspired search must change the carrier species:
singular/root-stack surfaces, a proper invariant/eigencone, orbit-sum walls,
or a non-full divisor subsystem.  Each such escape reopens the reflection,
hidden-wall, and volume gates and is not covered by the automatic theorem.
