## 2. Universal contact and completion lemmas behind the finite records

### 2.1 One-end projection bounds

Let H be the physical square-two core with nonsingular Gram G of signature
(n-1,1), and J=G^{-1}. Its span is nondegenerate Lorentzian, so its
orthogonal complement is positive definite. An additional white root with
contact column -q has projection square q^t J q and residual square
2-q^t J q >=0. In every assessed record the necessary contact domain is
reconstructed from the actual preferred corner, not guessed from a target
subgraph. It is partitioned into two end sets. A root meeting both ends
gives the independent marked path of length at most8. In the unpaid branch
its contacts lie in one end, whose inverse block is entrywise positive.
Every extra contact therefore increases the projection bound. A required
contact of magnitude a at i already gives a^2 J_ii <=2, without setting
unknown contacts to zero.

`fresh_b14_b15_singleton_completion_transfer_20261017.py:constraints`
checks these hypotheses and the old corner's orthogonal complement.
`fresh_d16_partial_weight_transfer_20261017.py:core_constraints` additionally
checks that every two distinct same-end unit contacts have projection >2.
Hence every remaining root has at most one old-core contact. When J_ii>1,
all nonsimple native magnitudes are excluded by 2 J_ii>2; there is no upper
Coxeter-label cutoff. These hypotheses are finite inverse-matrix predicates.

### 2.2 At most one root can contact the old core

In the ordinary Lorentz-residual branch all candidate J_ii lie in (1,2].
For two roots with respective unit contacts i,j their residual squares are
rho_i=2-J_ii and rho_j=2-J_jj. Every ordered pair, including i=j, satisfies

    J_ij^2 > rho_i rho_j.

If J_ij>0, Cauchy--Schwarz forces their actual product to be positive,
contrary to acuteness. If J_ij<0, it forces a nonzero negative product;
the additional bond gives the explicitly bounded S8 path. Thus at most
one further white can contact H in the unpaid branch. Repeated contact at
one coordinate has not been omitted.

This is stronger than classifying each root separately. Once its unique
contacting root has been forced by the target, every other new white is
orthogonal to **all** of H. It justifies the otherwise dangerous zero
entries in a completed principal Gram.

### 2.3 Relevant-component branch and indirect tails

The relevant branch permits exactly one contact position in the old marked
component. Its root G has J_ii>1 and hence a simple contact. The checked
pair tests allow at most one extra old-core contact root C, outside the
marked component, orthogonal to G. Candidate J_jj=1 doubled contacts are
separately excluded by saturation and a positive product or the S8 exit;
larger magnitudes exceed the norm bound. The remaining residual matrices

| J_GG | J_CC | J_GC | Residual determinant | Maximum tail at G |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|4/3|4/3|2/3|0|0|
|5/4|1|1/2|1/2|1|

Multiplicity of C needs its own argument. For J_CC=4/3, two such unit
contact roots have projection product4/3 and residual product of absolute

residuals. Both would have residual pairing-1/2 with G, impossible for
opposite vectors. Thus the single surviving outside **coordinate** really
permits at most one outside root; it is not a multiplicity assumption.

it with a core-orthogonal supporting white and using nonpositive products
forces that white to be orthogonal to both. For determinant1/2 the residual
inverse GG entry is2 and the inverse block is entrywise positive. A root

zero C contact and zero residual outside their span. It is the unique such
root. Its expression as a negative combination of the two residuals makes
a further contact to G or C incompatible with acuteness. Thus no indirect
tail or bridge escapes the bounds0/1.

Equivalently, for one isolated residual of square rho<1, the positive
forest inverse bound gives k/(k+1)<=rho for the total rank k of all
core-orthogonal attached components, hence k<=rho/(1-rho). This applies to
entire components, including indirect tails. The joint argument above is
