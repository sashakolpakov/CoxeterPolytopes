# Hostile rational incidences: bounded AG cores and the global tail gate

**Status (2026-08-10): proved synthesis; no universal rank theorem is
claimed.** This note combines the independently audited project-native
results for finite vertices and for the three failures H1--H3 of the
wrong-log anticanonical construction. It uses rational-surface adjunction,
the canonical exact sequence, log contractions, Hodge index, and exact
reflection lattices. It does not import a Nikulin--Vinberg dimension bound,
ABSW, Prasad, or an arithmetic-volume formula.

The result is a reduction theorem, not a census. Away from H1--H3 every SNC
negative-definite packet admitting the declared normal projective
\(\mathbf Q\)-Gorenstein contraction is uniformly klt. At the two minimal
genus-one failures the log-minimal cores have sharp ranks \(14\) and \(12\).
H3 has an exact \(D_4/\widetilde D_4/\)hyperbolic-star trichotomy. The affine
H3 branch retains a separate semiampleness/abundance gate. Once a relative
minimalization is available, the remaining obstruction is the same in all
three hostile lanes: an arbitrarily long exceptional or vertical \(B_m\)
tail can be contracted without being detected by the core Chern numbers,
while its visible local reflections already form the full
signed-permutation group.

Thus the remaining tail theorem is genuinely global. One must use the way
the tail is attached to the **complete finite-volume reflection-maximal nef
chamber**; in affine H3 this comes after, or must simultaneously prove, the
separate abundance statement. Another finite-vertex discrepancy estimate,
another list of small diagrams, or the assertion that a pure exceptional
reflection is missing cannot finish the proof.

## 1. The four exact lanes

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective rational surface over \(\mathbf C\) whose
complete polyhedral Mori cone is generated by smooth rational curves of squares
\(-1,-2,-4\). Write \(B=\sum B_i\) for the reduced sum of the
square-minus-four curves. The already proved weak-incidence theorem starts
from

\[
 B_i.B_j=0,\qquad B.R=0,\qquad B.E\leq2.                 \tag{1.1}
\]

The present synthesis has the following exact boundary.

| lane | AG object | proved output | unresolved datum |
|---|---|---|---|
| weak incidence | contract the disjoint \(B_i\) to \(1/4(1,1)\) points | \(\rho(S)\leq26\), hence \(n\leq25\) | raw carrier existence only |
| finite vertex satisfying (1.1) | contract an arbitrary SNC negative packet | every log discrepancy is at least \(1/2\) | exceptional-tail length |
| minimal H1 | \(B_1^2=B_2^2=-4,\ B_1.B_2=2\) | transverse log core \(\rho(S_0)\leq14\), sharply | exceptional-tail length |
| minimal H2 | \(B^2=-4,\ R^2=-2,\ B.R=2\) | adjoint core \(\rho(S_0)\leq12\), sharply | exceptional-tail length |
| H3, excluding H1/H2 | \(E^2=-1,\ t_i=B_i.E,\ T\geq3\) | exact \(Q=\sum t_i^2\) trichotomy | abundance and/or exceptional tail |

The H1 and H2 rows are the minimal finite-angle hostile incidences. They do
not claim to settle higher or dotted intersections. In particular, for an
H1 pair with \(I=B_1.B_2>2\), the dualizing degree on each component is
\(I-2>0\), so the disjoint-adjoint argument used below no longer applies.

## 2. Uniform finite-vertex theorem and its sharp limitation

For an SNC negative-definite packet \(\{C_i\}\) with physical squares
\(-4,-2,-1\), let \(Q=(C_i.C_j)\), and assume a normal projective
\(\mathbf Q\)-Gorenstein contraction with precisely this exceptional locus.
The discrepancy vector satisfies

\[
 Qa=k,\qquad k_B=2,\quad k_R=0,\quad k_E=-1.              \tag{2.1}
\]

Because \(-Q\) is a positive-definite M-matrix, its inverse is entrywise
nonnegative. The barrier vector

\[
 u_B=\frac12,\qquad u_R=u_E=0                              \tag{2.2}
\]

gives, under (1.1),

\[
 (-Q)u-k\geq0.
\]

Hence

\[
 a_B\geq-\frac12,\qquad a_R,a_E\geq0.                     \tag{2.3}
\]

All higher surface valuations retain log discrepancy at least \(1/2\).
Thus the contraction is uniformly \(\epsilon\)-klt for every strict
\(0<\epsilon<1/2\). This is a symbolic theorem for packets of arbitrary
rank, not a replay of a finite list.

It does not bound that rank. Successive free blowups produce the actual
nef-vertex chain

\[
 R_1-\cdots-R_{m-1}-E_m,\qquad R_i^2=-2,\quad E_m^2=-1,     \tag{2.4}
\]

with discrepancy vector \((1,2,\ldots,m)\). The pullback of an ample class
from the unblown surface is nef and positive-square, and its null locus is
exactly (2.4). In the doubled host the reflections in

\[
 e_1-e_2,\ldots,e_{m-1}-e_m,e_m                            \tag{2.5}
\]

generate the full signed-permutation group \(W(B_m)\). The example asserts
neither completeness nor reflection maximality of the global nef chamber;
it proves that the missing global hypotheses must do indispensable work.

## 3. The two sharp genus-one cores

### 3.1 Minimal H1

For two transverse square-minus-four curves with \(B_1.B_2=2\), put
\(Z=B_1+B_2\). The reduced nodal cycle has

\[
 p_a(Z)=1,\qquad \omega_Z\simeq\mathcal O_Z.               \tag{3.1}
\]

The canonical exact sequence lifts the unit section to a unique effective
divisor \(A\in|K_S+Z|\) disjoint from \(Z\). Every component on which \(A\)
is negative is, by adjunction, a smooth square-minus-one curve disjoint from
\(Z\). Contracting these curves terminates at a smooth pair
\((S_0,Z_0)\) with \(K_{S_0}+Z_0\) nef. Therefore

\[
 0\leq(K_{S_0}+Z_0)^2=14-\rho(S_0),\qquad
 \boxed{\rho(S_0)\leq14}.                                  \tag{3.2}
\]

The bound is sharp. The \(n=2\) split log-Calabi--Yau construction on
\(\mathbb F_2\), with six private boundary blowups on each component,
has rank \(14\) and complete Mori alphabet

\[
 2\cdot(-4)+12\cdot(-2)+2\cdot(-1).                        \tag{3.3}
\]

Its standard host gives a literal finite-volume Coxeter nef chamber. No
reflection-maximality statement is imported from a table. The transverse
pair contracts analytically with discrepancies \((-1,-1)\); algebraic or
projective \(\mathbf Q\)-Gorenstein statements are conditional on a supplied
such contraction. If the two contacts coalesce to an order-two tangency,
the resolved discrepancy drops to \(-2\), so the singularity is not log
canonical.

### 3.2 Minimal H2

For \(B^2=-4,\ R^2=-2,\ B.R=2\), put \(D=B+R\). The connected Gorenstein
curve has \(p_a(D)=1\), dualizing multidegree \((0,0)\), and
\(\omega_D\simeq\mathcal O_D\), even when the length-two intersection is
nonreduced. The same canonical sequence gives a unique effective
\(A\in|K_S+D|\) disjoint from \(D\). Contracting its negative components
again removes only disjoint square-minus-one curves and yields

\[
 0\leq(K_{S_0}+D_0)^2=12-\rho(S_0),\qquad
 \boxed{\rho(S_0)\leq12}.                                  \tag{3.4}
\]

This is sharp on the blowup of \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) at six
private points of one \((1,1)\)-curve and four private points of another.
The negative-definite pair contracts complex-analytically by Grauert. For a
supplied \(\mathbf Q\)-Gorenstein contraction, the transverse realization
has discrepancies \((-1,-1)\), while the tangential realization is not log
canonical. Unlike the H1 equality example, no complete-Mori or maximality
claim is attached to this sharp surface-pair control.

In both lanes, if \(m\) disjoint-from-boundary blowups are removed, then

\[
 \rho(S)\leq14+m\quad\text{(H1)},\qquad
 \rho(S)\leq12+m\quad\text{(H2)}.                           \tag{3.5}
\]

The exceptional host is \(\langle-2\rangle^m\), and (2.5) already generates
its full integral orthogonal group. A pure-tail hidden-root argument is
therefore false.

There are two honest conditional closures. If an integral ample marking
has an absolute **total** physical-facet degree \(T\), and every contracted
tail component is a physical facet, then \(m\leq T\), giving dimensions at
most \(13+T\) and \(11+T\). A bound on each facet separately is
insufficient. Alternatively, if a full trace lattice independently
descends through the displayed contraction, then

\[
 d(n+1)\leq14\quad\text{(H1)},\qquad
 d(n+1)\leq12\quad\text{(H2)}.                              \tag{3.6}
\]

For a literal full-nef tail the exceptional components are themselves
visible facet roots, so trace orthogonality and descent cannot be assumed.

## 4. H3 is an exact star trichotomy

Assume the \(B_i\) are disjoint and orthogonal to every square-minus-two
generator. Let \(E^2=-1\), \(t_i=B_i.E\), \(T=\sum t_i\geq3\), and
\(Q=\sum t_i^2\). The canonical divisor

\[
 F=4E+\sum_i t_iB_i                                       \tag{4.1}
\]

satisfies

\[
 F.B_i=0,\quad F.E=Q-4,\quad F^2=4(Q-4),\quad K_S.F=-4+2T. \tag{4.2}
\]

Completeness of the Mori alphabet makes \(F\) nef for \(Q\geq4\). In the
standard host, \(F/2\) is the distinguished vector of the star with central
root \(E\) and leaves \(B_i/2\). Exactly three cases remain.

1. **\(Q=3\).** The profile is \((1,1,1)\), the packet is spherical
   \(D_4\), and its fundamental cycle is
   \(3E+B_1+B_2+B_3\), of arithmetic genus one. Grauert gives a
   complex-analytic contraction. Any supplied normal contraction is
   non-rational; if it is also \(\mathbf Q\)-Gorenstein, its discrepancies
   are \((-2,-1,-1,-1)\), so it is not log canonical. The apparent
   highest-root reflection is already in \(W(D_4)\); a leaf-swap extension
   is integral only under the additional congruence
   \(B_i\equiv B_j\pmod{2\operatorname{NS}(S)}\).
2. **\(Q=4\).** The profile is \((1,1,1,1)\), the packet is affine
   \(\widetilde D_4\), and \(F\) is nef isotropic. If \(F=mG\) is
   semiample, adjunction leaves \((m,g)=(1,3)\) or \((2,2)\). The
   relatively minimal fibration core has rank at most \(26\) or \(18\),
   respectively. Semiampleness and the vertical blowup count remain open.
3. **\(Q>4\).** The star is hyperbolic and \(F\) is nef and big. After
   projectively contracting the disjoint \(B_i\), the image \(\bar E\)
   satisfies

   \[
   \bar E^2=Q/4-1>0,\qquad K_Y.\bar E=T/2-1>0.              \tag{4.3}
   \]

   This is the wrong sign for anticanonical orbifold semipositivity. The
   exact coefficient inequality

   \[
   (1-Q/4)a_E\geq T/2-1                                  \tag{4.4}
   \]

   rules out repair by any effective supported boundary. Hodge index gives
   only a lower constraint on \(\rho(S)\), while the negative-definite null
   tail remains uncontrolled.

Thus H3 is not awaiting another local coefficient choice. Its missing
input is affine abundance plus a global exceptional/vertical-tail bound.

## 5. A canonical-coloring obstruction, and why it is not yet the bound

The rational canonical class supplies one useful global test that is
invisible in an uncoloured Coxeter diagram. In the standard doubled host
\(q_M=2q_{\operatorname{NS}}\):

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{physical facet}&q_M(r,r)&q_M(K_S,r)\\ \hline
 R^2=-2&r=R:\ -4&0\\
 E^2=-1&r=E:\ -2&-2\\
 B^2=-4&r=B/2:\ -2&+2.
 \end{array}                                               \tag{5.1}
\]

Since \(K_S\not\equiv0\) on a rational surface, the norm-minus-four
physical normals span rank at most \(\rho(S)-1\). In particular, a proposed
full chamber whose norm-minus-four facets span the carrier has no rational
surface realization, independently of a surface census.

This does not kill the free exceptional tail. Its norm-minus-four roots
\(e_i-e_{i+1}\) span only the sum-zero \(A_{m-1}\) subspace. The remaining
diagonal direction \(e_1+\cdots+e_m\) is precisely the tail component of
the canonical class, and the norm-minus-two end root supplies the missing
facet direction. Hence (5.1) explains why some candidate full chambers
fail, but it also proves why local \(B_m\) saturation evades the simplest
canonical-rank argument.

## 6. Exact remaining theorem

The combined work isolates one global tail target. In affine H3 it is
conditional on proving semiampleness of the null class, or it must be proved
together with that separate abundance statement.

> **Global mixed-root tail lemma.** Let a rational surface carry a literal
> complete finite-volume Coxeter nef chamber on the standard physical host,
> and suppose its facet group is reflection-maximal. Prove an absolute
> bound on every exceptional or vertical \(B_m\) tail removed by the H1,
> H2, or an available H3 relative-minimalization, using the mixed facets
> which attach that tail to the core. Equivalently, derive an absolute total
> marked-facet degree or another global invariant that bounds the tail
> length. In affine H3, also prove semiampleness of \(F\), or replace it by
> an equally bounded algebraic model.

There is a precise but insufficient finite-volume observation. If every
wall split as either a core wall or a root wholly inside a fixed negative
\(B_m\) summand, the generated group would preserve the proper totally
geodesic core subspace. A finite spherical sector in the orthogonal normal
directions still has infinite radial hyperbolic volume, so this split group
has infinite covolume. Finite volume therefore forces genuinely mixed
core/tail walls or group elements. It does **not** bound their number or
their coefficients. Indeed, under the signed-permutation \(W(B_m)\), the
orbit of any one nonzero exceptional projection already spans the whole
tail space. Thus one mixed root orbit can satisfy the elementary span
requirement for every \(m\). No theorem here says that such mixed roots
satisfy the physical adjunction and effectivity constraints uniformly in
\(m\). That is the next proof, not an omitted computation.

The reduction also identifies what does **not** suffice:

* uniform klt singularities at finite vertices;
* a bound on each individual facet degree;
* saturation of the pure exceptional root system;
* contraction to a bounded log or fibration core;
* bare reflection maximality with no geometric control of mixed roots; or
* an assumed full-trace descent through curves which are themselves visible
  trace roots.

Accordingly the present output is real progress toward an AG/ABSW-type
theorem: the broad weak lane already has \(n\leq25\), and, apart from the
explicit affine-H3 abundance gate, the minimal hostile boundary has been
reduced to one global attachment invariant. It is not yet the universal
dimension-bound existence theorem.
