# Published-count corollary for the universal silver Pell theorem

**Audit date:** 2026-08-13  
**Verdict:** **PASS for the published count; CONDITIONAL for host elimination.**

## Exact count being used

Let \(\mathcal U_r^{(3)}\) be the set of isometry classes of positive-definite
integral unimodular \(\mathbb Z\)-lattices of rank \(r\) and **minimum exactly
\(3\)**.  The published classifications give

| rank \(r\) | 23 | 24 | 25 | 26 | 27 | 28 | 29 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(\#\mathcal U_r^{(3)}\) | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 3 | 38 | 10092 |

Thus

\[
\sum_{r=23}^{29}|\mathcal U_r^{(3)}|
=1+1+0+1+3+38+10092=10136.
\]

These are counts of \(\mathbb Z\)-isometry classes, not a claim that scalar
extension produces 10,136 pairwise nonisometric
\(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\)-lattices.

## Primary-source check

1. In [King, *A mass formula for unimodular lattices with no roots*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0012231),
   p. 1, “root” is explicitly defined to mean a vector of norm \(1\) or
   \(2\).  Table 2 on p. 12 gives the odd-unimodular no-root counts in ranks
   \(23,\ldots,28\) as
   \(1,1,0,1,3,38\).  The discussion on p. 14 states that the classes through
   rank 28 had been completely enumerated, attributes ranks 27--28 to
   Bacher--Venkov, and Remark 15 on p. 15 says that in these ranks the odd
   no-root lattices have minimum \(3\).

2. [Bacher--Venkov, *Réseaux entiers unimodulaires sans racines en
   dimensions 27 et 28*](https://www.unige.ch/math/EnsMath/monographies/monographie-no-37),
   Monogr. Enseign. Math. 37 (2001), pp. 212--267, is the underlying complete
   enumeration yielding \(3\) and \(38\).  King's independent mass calculation
   explicitly says it verifies those enumerations.

3. [Borcherds, *Classification of positive definite lattices*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9912236),
   Theorem 3.6 on p. 10, independently proves uniqueness in rank 26.

4. [Allombert--Chenevier, *Unimodular hunting II*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.19569),
   defines \(X_n^\emptyset\) to consist of unimodular integral lattices with no
   nonzero vector of norm \(\leq2\), and Theorem A gives
   \(|X_{29}^\emptyset|=10092\).  It also records the earlier count
   \(|X_{28}^\emptyset|=38\).  In rank 29 every integral unimodular lattice is
   odd.  More decisively for the theorem's hypothesis, Examples 4.1 and 4.3
   state that each of these 10,092 lattices has (indeed, is generated by) a
   basis of norm-3 vectors.  Thus all have minimum exactly \(3\).

There is a terminology trap at rank 24.  The row above contains the **odd
Leech lattice**, whose minimum is \(3\).  The even Leech lattice has minimum
\(4\): it is rootless in the “no norm 1 or 2” sense, but it is not in
\(\mathcal U_{24}^{(3)}\) and is not counted here.  Consequently the count is
one, not two.

## Scope boundary

This is terminal only for the dimension-indexed family \(\{M_L:
L\in\mathcal U_r^{(3)},\ 23\leq r\leq29\}\).  It is **not** a
whole-dimension or field-wide nonexistence theorem.  In particular it does not
cover the rank-24 even Leech input, other time coefficients, nonunimodular or
genuinely \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\)-integral spatial lattices, nonorthogonal forms,
or other fields such as \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\).  A lossless AGCat embedding does
not change this quantifier: whole-dimension nonexistence would additionally
require a proved exhaustive reduction of every relevant compact chamber to
this particular host family.
