# Unconditional finiteness of maximal arithmetic reflection chambers

**Status (2026-08-11): unconditional classical theorem, with two independent
terminal routes and a finite-native-pair factorization.**  No algebraic-
geometric carrier hypothesis is present.  The direct statement is already the
main theorem of Agol--Belolipetsky--Storm--Whyte (ABSW).  A second route,
which does not invoke the ABSW finiteness conclusion, factors the result
through Vinberg's dimension bound, the published degree bound, Nikulin's
bounded root frame, and Nikulin's fixed-field/fixed-rank theorem.

Let \(P\subset\mathbf H^n\), \(n\ge2\), be a finite-sided finite-volume
arithmetic Coxeter chamber.  Write \(W(P)\) for its side-reflection group,
\(k(P)\) for its **minimal Vinberg ground field**, and
\(m(P)=n+1\).  Call \(P\) reflection-maximal if \(W(P)\) is maximal among
discrete hyperbolic reflection groups.  Everything below remains true if
maximality is required only among arithmetic reflection groups.

For these finite-volume inputs the two maximality formulations agree.  Any
discrete reflection overgroup \(W(P)\subseteq W'\) has a fundamental chamber
contained in \(P\), hence finite covolume; the covolume/index formula makes
\([W':W(P)]\) finite.  Arithmeticity is invariant under commensurability, so
\(W'\) is automatically arithmetic.

## 1. Terminal theorem

### Theorem 1.1 (unconditional maximal-chamber finiteness)

For all reflection-maximal finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chambers in
dimensions \(n\ge2\):

1. \(2\le n\le29\);
2. \([k(P):\mathbf Q]\le25\);
3. in the noncompact case, \(k(P)=\mathbf Q\) and in fact
   \(n\le19\) or \(n=21\);
4. the set of native pairs
   \[
                   \{(k(P),m(P))\}
   \]
   is finite; and
5. only finitely many unmarked normalized chamber classes occur.

Equivalently, there are only finitely many conjugacy classes in
\(\operatorname {Isom}(\mathbf H^n)\), over all \(n\ge2\), of arithmetic
maximal hyperbolic reflection groups.

This is an **unconditional global finiteness theorem outright**, not a
finite-pair conditional and not an AG-relative result.

### Direct published proof

ABSW prove that there are only finitely many conjugacy classes of arithmetic
maximal hyperbolic reflection groups.  Their definitions are exactly the
finite-volume and reflection-maximal definitions above.  The statement is in
the abstract and Introduction, and the proof closes in Section 6, printed
pp. 493--494:

> I. Agol, M. Belolipetsky, P. Storm and K. Whyte, *Finiteness of arithmetic
> hyperbolic reflection groups*, Groups Geom. Dyn. **2** (2008), 481--498.

The complete mirror arrangement of a reflection group has one group orbit of
chambers: a generic compact path between regular points crosses only finitely
many mirrors, and successive wall reflections give a gallery from one chamber
to the other.  Thus group conjugacy finiteness gives unmarked normalized
chamber finiteness.

Vinberg's crystallographic nonexistence theorem supplies \(n<30\).  Its two
branches exclude compact reflection groups and noncompact arithmetic
reflection groups in dimensions at least 30:

> E. B. Vinberg, *Absence of crystallographic groups of reflections in
> Lobachevskii spaces of large dimension*, Funct. Anal. Appl. **15** (1981),
> 128--130, and the expanded Trans. Moscow Math. Soc. **47** (1985), 75--112.

Belolipetsky--Linowitz, Corollary 5.1 on printed p. 8, gives the
all-dimensional degree bound \([k:\mathbf Q]\le25\):

> M. Belolipetsky and B. Linowitz, *On fields of definition of arithmetic
> Kleinian reflection groups II*, IMRN (2014), arXiv:1211.4443v2.

That corollary combines their dimension-three bound \(\le9\), Maclachlan's
dimension-two bound \(\le11\), Nikulin's 2007 induction, and Nikulin's
published transition bound \(N(14)\le25\).  These numerical bounds strengthen
the ABSW conclusion; ABSW finiteness itself does not require the sharp number
25.

Nikulin's Theorem 2.2, printed p. 56, is a second already-published direct
terminal theorem.  It combines Long--Maclachlan--Reid in dimension two, Agol
in dimension three, Nikulin's 1980/1982 higher-dimensional results, and
Vinberg's \(n<30\):

> V. V. Nikulin, *Finiteness of the number of arithmetic groups generated by
> reflections in Lobachevsky spaces*, Izv. Math. **71** (2007), 53--56.

For the refinement in item 3, a cusp gives a nonzero isotropic vector over the
ground field.  A nondistinguished real conjugate of an admissible arithmetic
form is definite, so such a vector forces \(k=\mathbf Q\).  Esselmann's main
rank theorem then says that reflective integral Lorentzian lattices exist only
in hyperbolic dimensions \(n\le19\) or \(n=21\), with the Borcherds rank-22
example showing the endpoint is real:

> F. Esselmann, *Ueber die maximale Dimension von Lorentz--Gittern mit
> coendlicher Spiegelungsgruppe*, J. Number Theory **61** (1996), 103--144,
> main theorem (the abstract states maximal lattice rank \(n+1=22\)).

## 2. A noncircular finite-native-pair route

This section proves item 4 without using the ABSW finiteness conclusion or
Nikulin's 2007 Theorem 2.2.  It makes the existing finite-native-pair bridge
unconditional.

### 2.1 Every input gives a reflective native lattice

Choose an integral \(\mathcal O_k\)-lattice \(L\) in the native admissible
\(k\)-space which is preserved by \(W(P)\).  This does not require
\(\mathcal O_k\) to be principal: start with an arithmetic lattice \(L_0\),
replace \(W(P)\cap O(L_0)\) by its finite-index normal core in \(W(P)\), and
take the sum of the finitely many translates of \(L_0\).  The resulting
finite torsion-free \(\mathcal O_k\)-module is \(W(P)\)-stable.  One common
rescaling of the form clears its pairing ideal and changes no reflection.
Then

\[
            W(P)\subseteq R(L)\subseteq O^+(L),
\]

where \(R(L)\) is generated by all integral native reflections.  Arithmetic
admissibility makes \(O^+(L)\) a lattice.  Both \(W(P)\) and \(O^+(L)\) have
finite covolume, hence \([O^+(L):W(P)]<\infty\) and therefore
\([O(L):R(L)]<\infty\).  Thus \(L\) is reflective.  Maximality is not used in
this step.

### 2.2 A uniform integral cycle alphabet

Apply Nikulin's bounded-frame lemma to the complete reflection chamber
\(M(L)\) of \(R(L)\).  The lemma, quoted and used in the proof of Theorem
5.2.1 on printed p. 119 of his 1982 paper, selects linearly independent walls

\[
                       \delta_1,\ldots,\delta_m
\]

which span a finite-index sublattice, have connected nonorthogonality graph,
and satisfy, at the distinguished real place,

\[
 c_{ij}:={4(\delta_i,\delta_j)^2\over
                  (\delta_i,\delta_i)(\delta_j,\delta_j)}<62^2.       \tag{2.1}
\]

At every nondistinguished real embedding, definiteness and Cauchy--Schwarz
give \(0\le\sigma(c_{ij})\le4\).  Put

\[
 a_{ij}:={2(\delta_i,\delta_j)\over(\delta_i,\delta_i)}
 \quad\text{and every cyclic product}\quad
 b_C=\prod_{(i,j)\in C}a_{ij}
\].

Individual Cartan coefficients need not be elements of \(\mathcal O_k\) for
a general projective \(\mathcal O_k\)-lattice/root line.  What Vinberg
arithmeticity gives, and what the argument needs, is that every cyclic product
\(b_C\) is an algebraic integer.  This is also the literal reflection-group
ring criterion in Vinberg 1971, Theorem 5, printed p. 55; in particular the
two-cycle \(c_{ij}=a_{ij}a_{ji}\) is integral, as Nikulin records explicitly on
printed p. 120.  For a simple directed cycle \(C\) of length \(r\le m\),

\[
                         b_C^2=\prod_{(i,j)\in C}c_{ij}.
\]

Consequently, at every embedding \(\sigma:k\hookrightarrow\mathbf R\),

\[
                         |\sigma(b_C)|\le62^m\le62^{30}.              \tag{2.2}
\]

The frame Gram matrix has full Lorentzian rank.  Nikulin's ground-field
inheritance theorem (1982, Theorem 2.1.3(2), printed p. 104) therefore says
that this hyperbolic principal subgraph has the same minimal ground field as
the complete chamber \(M(L)\).  Moreover \(W(P)\subseteq R(L)\) has finite
index.  Vinberg 1971, Theorem 3, printed p. 46, proves that a Zariski-dense
group and a finite-index subgroup have the same classes of rings of definition
in the adjoint representation; Theorem 1 and its corollary on printed p. 45
give the least field/ring, while Theorem 5 on p. 55 identifies it for a
reflection group with the simple cyclic Cartan products.  Consequently the
minimal Vinberg field is a finite-index commensurability invariant here.
Thus \(k(M(L))=k(P)=k\), and hence

\[
                  k=\mathbf Q(b_C:C\text{ a simple frame cycle}).     \tag{2.3}
\]

Repeated closed walks add nothing: they split multiplicatively into simple
cycles and two-cycles.

### 2.3 Bounded degree and bounded conjugates give finitely many fields

Put \(B=62^{30}\), and let

\[
 \mathcal A=\{\alpha:\alpha\text{ is an algebraic integer},
 [\mathbf Q(\alpha):\mathbf Q]\le25,
 |\tau(\alpha)|\le B\text{ for every conjugate }\tau(\alpha)\}.
\]

This is a finite, effectively enumerable set.  Indeed, the minimal polynomial
of \(\alpha\) is monic integral of degree at most 25, and its coefficients,
being elementary symmetric functions of the conjugates, lie in explicit
finite intervals bounded by \(\binom{e}{j}B^j\), \(e\le25\).

Vinberg gives \(m\le30\), and Belolipetsky--Linowitz gives
\([k:\mathbf Q]\le25\).  Equations (2.2)--(2.3) express every ground field as
the field generated by one of finitely many bounded tuples from
\(\mathcal A\), with the tuple length bounded solely by \(m\le30\).  Thus the
set of ground fields is finite.  Since \(3\le m\le30\), the set of native
pairs \((k,m)\) is finite.

This argument uses the **selection lemma** behind Nikulin's fixed-data theorem
but not the conclusion of that theorem, so it is not circular.

### 2.4 Fixed data and maximality finish the chamber count

For every remaining fixed pair \((k,m)\), Nikulin's Theorem 5.2.1, printed
pp. 119--122, gives only finitely many similarity classes of reflective
hyperbolic \(\mathcal O_k\)-lattices.  Common rescaling changes neither the
reflections nor the normalized Coxeter Gram matrix.

For a reflection-maximal input, \(R(L)\) is a reflection overgroup of
\(W(P)\), so maximality gives \(W(P)=R(L)\).  If maximality is assumed only
among arithmetic reflection groups, the same follows because \(R(L)\) has
finite index in \(O(L)\) and is arithmetic.  The generic-gallery argument then
gives one chamber orbit per native similarity host.  The finite union over
the finite native-pair set proves item 5.

This is exactly the chamber-orbit bridge proved in
[the finite-native-pair theorem](projected_k3_maximal_abstract_chamber_finiteness.md),
now supplied with an unconditional upstream finite-pair theorem.

## 3. Dependency and noncircularity ledger

| Input | Exact role | What it does **not** assume |
|---|---|---|
| Vinberg 1981/1985, main nonexistence theorem | \(n<30\), hence \(m\le30\) | no AG carrier; no ABSW finiteness |
| cusp rationality plus Esselmann 1996, main theorem | noncompact \(k=\mathbf Q\) and \(n\le19\) or \(n=21\) | no compact-chamber restriction |
| Belolipetsky--Linowitz, Corollary 5.1, p. 8 | \([k:\mathbf Q]\le25\) in every dimension | not the conclusion that maximal chambers are finite |
| Nikulin 1982, Theorem 2.1.3(2), p. 104 | a full-rank hyperbolic frame retains the minimal ground field | no chamber classification |
| Nikulin 1982, bounded-frame input on p. 119 | connected integral rank-\(m\) frame and constant \(62\) | no fixed-field finiteness conclusion |
| Vinberg 1971, Theorems 1, 3 and 5, pp. 45--46, 55 | cyclic-product integrality and finite-index invariance of the least Vinberg/adjoint definition field | no finiteness conclusion |
| elementary coefficient bound | converts degree \(25\) and conjugate height \(62^{30}\) into a finite field set | no Hermite/discriminant black box |
| Nikulin 1982, Theorem 5.2.1, pp. 119--122 | finitely many reflective lattice similarity classes for fixed \((k,m)\) | no finite global field or rank set |
| reflection maximality plus a finite gallery | \(W=R(L)\) and one chamber orbit | no dimension or field bound |

The sharp degree-25 input has its own explicit dependency chain.  Nikulin's
2011 final journal paper proves \(N(14)\le25\) (Theorem 3.2, printed p. 977;
the proof closes on p. 998).  Belolipetsky--Linowitz obtain the dimension-three
bound from Agol's three-dimensional volume estimate plus Borel's volume
formula and explicit number theory, and combine it with Maclachlan's
dimension-two bound and Nikulin's induction.  This uses spectral/arithmetic
technology related to ABSW, but it does **not** invoke the global ABSW
finiteness conclusion as a premise of Corollary 5.1.

For a route avoiding the sharp later constant, Nikulin 2011 already gives a
global degree bound \(35\): Theorem 5.5, printed pp. 999--1000, handles
dimensions four and five using the then-known bounds \(11\) in dimension two
and \(35\) in dimension three, while its preceding theorems handle higher
dimensions.  Replacing 25 by 35 in Section 2 changes none of the finiteness
logic.  Thus the finite-pair proof does not depend essentially on the sharp
constant.

## 4. Universal weak rational-surface corollary

The unconditional arithmetic bounds also make the repository's universal
weak rational-surface eigencarrier uniformly bounded.

### Corollary 4.1

Every finite-volume arithmetic Coxeter chamber in dimension at least two,
whether reflection-maximal or not, has the weak semialgebraic divisor-
eigenslice carrier of
[rational-surface eigencarrier universality](rational_surface_eigencarrier_universality.md)
on a blow-up of \(\mathbf P^2\) at at most

\[
          4[k:\mathbf Q](n+1)-3
             \le4\cdot25\cdot30-3
             =\boxed{2997}
\]

points.

Maximality is needed only for the finite **chamber-class** conclusion, not for
this carrier or for the dimension, degree, and finite-ground-field results.
The carrier is weak: it returns a rational Chow eigensummand and integral
divisor inequalities, not effective irreducible walls, a nef/Mori chamber, or
algebraic realizations of all reflection actions.  Any weak-action upgrade is a
separate companion theorem and is not used here unless independently frozen
and audited.
