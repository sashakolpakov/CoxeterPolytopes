# Compact K3 Coxeter quadrilaterals have a finite field set

**Status (2026-08-11): proved for every full-trace complex-K3 field
degree.**  Let a rank-three module over a totally real field return an exact
compact Coxeter quadrilateral in \(\mathbf H^2\).  The full trace has rank
\(3d\), so the complex-K3 Picard ceiling forces

\[
                              d=[k:\mathbf Q]\le6.
\tag{0.1}
\]

The single rank-three Gram determinant is enough to bound the apparent free
quadrilateral modulus.  Its leading term is the product of the two shifted
opposite-wall ratios, while every remaining term grows only linearly in
their square roots.  Integral opposite-facet reflections and all definite
places give the uniform norm gap

\[
                         A-1,\ C-1>4^{-d}\ge4^{-6}.
\tag{0.2}
\]

Consequently, if \(A,C>1\) are the squared normalized ratios for the two
ultraparallel opposite pairs, then

\[
                         A,C<32772^2=1,074,003,984.
\tag{0.3}
\]

All simple cyclic Cartan coordinates are now uniformly bounded at every
embedding.  If the returned field is the ground field generated by that
cyclic packet, a bounded primitive integral combination gives

\[
 |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|
 \le
 \bigl(590,584,093,515,710,595,072\bigr)^{30}.
\tag{0.4}
\]

Thus compact quadrilaterals admit only finitely many returned fields.  No
imposed height, power relation, angle classification, or reflective-lattice
classification is used.  Exact same-chamber return, complete reflection
saturation, and relative reflection maximality are retained, but the
determinant and reflection integrality prove the bound before maximality
enters.

For the rank-six case \(d=2\), the sharper bound

\[
                   |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|\le4,857,950,597
\tag{0.5}
\]

holds.

## 1. Exact carrier package

Let \(k\) be a totally real field of degree \(d\), let
\(\mathcal O\subset k\) be a returned order, and let \(M\) be a finite
projective rank-three \(\mathcal O\)-module with an admissible symmetric
\(k\)-form \(h\).  At one distinguished embedding \(\sigma_0\), the form
has signature \((2,1)\); at all other embeddings it is positive definite.
Assume:

1. a minimal complex projective K3 surface \(X\) has Picard lattice equal
   to the primitive full trace of \((M,h)\), with the returned order acting
   self-adjointly by divisor-product correspondences;
2. the prescribed projectivized eigen-nef chamber is exactly a compact
   Coxeter quadrilateral in \(\mathbf H^2\);
3. its four facet reflections preserve \(M\), the restricted K3 mirror
   packet is complete and saturated, and its group is reflection-maximal
   among integral \(k\)-linear reflections of the declared order host; and
4. \(k\) is the ground field generated by the cyclic Cartan products of
   the returned facet reflections.

The full trace has rank \(3d=\rho(X)\le20\), proving (0.1).  The rational
case \(d=1\) is already finite, so the proof below may assume \(2\le d\le6\).
The two opposite facet pairs are ultraparallel.  The four adjacent pairs
meet at Coxeter angles, possibly right angles.

## 2. The normalized distinguished Gram matrix

Formally normalize the four facet normals to unit length at \(\sigma_0\).
Write the magnitudes of the four adjacent correlations as
\(a,b,c,d_0\in[0,1)\), in cyclic order, and the two opposite correlations
as \(U,V>1\).  The normalized Gram matrix is

\[
G=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-a&-U&-d_0\\
-a&1&-b&-V\\
-U&-b&1&-c\\
-d_0&-V&-c&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Only the squared ratios

\[
                         A=U^2,\qquad C=V^2
\tag{2.2}
\]

are asserted to belong to \(k\).  The real square roots \(U,V\) and the
adjacent magnitudes are used only in the distinguished-place inequality;
no square root is silently adjoined to the returned field.

At \(\sigma_0\), \(A,C>1\).  At every definite embedding \(\sigma\),
strict Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
                              0<\sigma(A),\sigma(C)<1.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The endpoints are excluded because the opposite pairings and wedges are
nonzero over \(k\), and field embeddings are injective.

## 3. The all-place norm gap

The opposite pair cyclic Cartan products are \(4A\) and \(4C\).  They lie
in \(\mathcal O\), because each differs by an integer from the module trace
of a product of two integral facet reflections.  Put

\[
                        r=A-1,\qquad s=C-1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then \(4r,4s\in\mathcal O\).  At \(\sigma_0\) they are positive, while at
each of the \(d-1\) definite embeddings they lie in \((-4,0)\).  Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
1
&\le\left|N_{k/\mathbf Q}(4r)\right|
 =(4r_0)\prod_{\sigma\ne\sigma_0}|4\sigma(r)|
 <(4r_0)4^{d-1},\\
1
&\le\left|N_{k/\mathbf Q}(4s)\right|
 <(4s_0)4^{d-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence

\[
                              r,s>4^{-d}\ge4^{-6}={1\over4096}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is the only arithmetic lower bound used in the determinant estimate.

## 4. The sole Gram determinant

The four normals lie in a three-dimensional field space, hence
\(\det G=0\).  Direct expansion gives

\[
          0=rs+K-2PU-2QV-2RUV,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
P&=ab+cd_0,&
Q&=ad_0+bc,&
R&=ac+bd_0,\\
K&=-a^2-b^2-c^2-d_0^2
     +a^2c^2+b^2d_0^2-2abcd_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since \(0\le a,b,c,d_0<1\),

\[
                          0\le P,Q,R<2,
                    \qquad |K|<8.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equations (4.1)--(4.3) imply

\[
             rs<4U+4V+4UV+8
                =4(1+V)U+4V+8.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The estimate is uniform in all four Coxeter angles; no list of possible
angle orders is needed.

## 5. Uniform diagonal bound for \(d\le6\)

Because \(s=V^2-1\), equation (4.4) becomes

\[
 U^2
 <{4\over V-1}U
   +{4V+8\over V^2-1}+1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

By (3.3),

\[
                            V>{\sqrt{4097}\over64}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Both coefficient functions in (5.1) decrease for \(V>1\).  Evaluating at
the lower endpoint gives

\[
 U^2
 <(16384+256\sqrt{4097})U
   +(32769+256\sqrt{4097}).
\tag{5.3}
\]

At \(U=32772\), the left side minus the right side is

\[
                537034767-8389888\sqrt{4097}>0,
\tag{5.4}
\]

because

\[
 537034767^2-4097\cdot8389888^2
 =17,606,953,271,521>0.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus the positive root of the quadratic in (5.3) is below \(32772\).
Rotating the quadrilateral gives the same bound for \(V\), proving (0.3).

### Quadratic sharpening

When \(d=2\), equation (3.3) improves to \(r,s>1/16\).  Repeating the same
calculation gives

\[
 U^2<(64+16\sqrt{17})U+(129+16\sqrt{17}).
\tag{5.6}
\]

At \(U=132\), the remainder is

\[
 8847-2128\sqrt{17}>0,\qquad
 8847^2-17\cdot2128^2=1,286,881>0.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Hence \(U,V<132\) in rank six.

## 6. Bounded cyclic generators give finitely many fields

Every cyclic Cartan product belongs to \(\mathcal O\), by reflection-word
trace induction on the finite projective order-module.  Closed walks reduce
to pair products and simple cycles.  With four facets, it is therefore
enough to use cycles of length at most four.

At a definite embedding, Cauchy--Schwarz bounds a length-\(\ell\) cyclic
product by \(2^\ell\).  At the distinguished embedding, every adjacent
normalized correlation is below one and both opposite magnitudes are below
\(32772\).  Thus every simple cyclic generator has every conjugate bounded
in absolute value by

\[
 H:=16\cdot32772^4
   =18,455,752,922,365,956,096.
\tag{6.1}
\]

By hypothesis, these cyclic coordinates generate \(k\).  Select a
field-generating subpacket minimally.  Each strict adjunction at least
doubles the intermediate degree, so \(d\le6\) requires at most two selected
generators, say \(\theta_1,\theta_2\).

If two are needed, among

\[
                     \theta_1+c\theta_2,\qquad 0\le c\le15,
\tag{6.2}
\]

some element is primitive.  Indeed, for each unordered pair of the at most
six embeddings, at most one value of \(c\) makes their images equal; there
are at most \(\binom62=15\) bad values.  Because
\(\theta_1,\theta_2\) jointly generate \(k\), no pair of embeddings agrees
on both.  The chosen primitive algebraic integer \(\theta\) has

\[
                              |\sigma(\theta)|\le16H
\tag{6.3}
\]

at every embedding.  Its Vandermonde discriminant gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\operatorname {Disc}(k)|
&\le|\operatorname {Disc}(\mathbf Z[\theta])|\\
&\le(32H)^{d(d-1)}
\le(32H)^{30},
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

which is (0.4).
Hermite--Minkowski finiteness now gives only finitely many isomorphism
classes of such totally real fields.

### Quadratic discriminant

For \(d=2\), the opposite pair cycle

\[
                              \alpha=4A=4U^2
\]

has \(4<\alpha_0<69,696\) and \(0<\alpha_1<4\).  It is nonrational and
generates \(k\).  Its trace is at most \(69,699\), its norm at most
\(278,783\), and therefore

\[
 |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|
 \le69,699^2-4
 =4,857,950,597.
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 (all-degree compact-quadrilateral field theorem)

Every exact full-trace complex-K3 carrier satisfying Section 1 and having
a compact Coxeter quadrilateral as its prescribed eigen-nef chamber belongs
to a finite set of totally real ground fields.  The explicit general bound
is (0.4); in rank six, the sharper bound is (0.5).

## 7. Scope

The determinant alone allows the geometric escape
\(U\to\infty,\ V\to1\).  Reflection integrality blocks it by forcing
\(V^2-1>4^{-d}\).  Thus the arithmetic all-place norm gap, not maximality,
is the decisive extra equation.

The theorem does not:

- prove that every exact \(n=2\) chamber is a quadrilateral;
- treat noncompact quadrilaterals, where an endpoint can become isotropic;
- construct a K3 carrier or classify its possible Coxeter angles;
- apply when the declared operator field is a gratuitous extension of the
  cyclic ground field; or
- derive a general mixed-correspondence height from maximality.

Together with the compact right-pentagon calculation, it shows that the
automatic norm gap is not peculiar to one incidence.  The next issue is
whether four-side principal minors propagate comparable bounds through
arbitrary compact Coxeter polygons.
