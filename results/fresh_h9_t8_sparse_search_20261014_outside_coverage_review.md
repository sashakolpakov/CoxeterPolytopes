Consequently each swap changes only its own chosen ordering, and all four reductions can be imposed simultaneously.

Category `7` means every finite integer label at least seven. The Lannér-triangle reciprocal-sum test is exact for this category: a triple containing a high label is hyperbolic unless its other two labels are both two. Isolated two-node components accept every finite label. A high bond in a larger connected component cannot be spherical, since an adjacent bond has label at least three and the resulting three-node Gram is not positive definite. For the other labels the finite-component classifier lists precisely the finite tree types. This is a classification argument, not substitution of angle π/7 throughout the search.

At each DFS node, assigned pairs agree across every incident missing triple; filtering removes only inconsistent rows. Only newly nonright entries can change a spherical test. Unassigned meeting pairs are relaxed to right angles, a necessary positive-definiteness relaxation by the nonnegative-matrix Perron bound. For a negative-block clause, every proposed cross pair meets at an actual vertex. If making an unassigned cross pair label three already fails there, every larger finite label fails. If all cross pairs are right, the two disjoint Lannér blocks contribute two negative directions; arbitrary dotted distances on missing pairs do not avoid that obstruction.

The four early metric families are matched literally to their separately certified conditions. For an unassigned theorem pair, the search intersects its possible categories over all incident remaining triangle domains. A theorem is applied only when **every** remaining category satisfies every hypothesis. This does not identify an unassigned pair with one representative completion. Fixed categories are only two or three; lower bounds are at most seven, so no equality of different high integer labels is presumed.
