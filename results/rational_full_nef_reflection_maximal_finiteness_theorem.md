# A project-native finiteness theorem for reflection-maximal rational full-nef carriers

**Status (2026-08-10): proved and carrier-relative.**
This is an ABSW-type finiteness conclusion on the declared rational
full-nef carrier class, but it is not a proof that every raw maximal
arithmetic Coxeter chamber has such a carrier.  No reflective-group or
Kond\=o classification, ABSW theorem, Prasad formula, or arithmetic-volume
formula is used.  Section 4.1 does use the standard elementary
rational-minimal-model description by \(\mathbf P^2\), Hirzebruch surfaces,
and point blowups.

The rank input is the
[rational Mori/K3 dichotomy](rational_mori_boundary_k3_dimension_dichotomy.md);
the exact normalization and \(\mathbb F_4\) scope gate are in the
[rational reflection-integrality theorem](rational_nef_reflection_integrality_gate.md).

The answer is positive for the literal full-dimensional carrier and the
strong reflection-maximality notion:

\[
 \boxed{\text{AG rank bound}+\text{finite standard host lattices}
       +\text{reflection maximality}
       \Longrightarrow\text{finitely many abstract chambers}.}
\]

The mechanism is short but not formal boundedness.  Maximality saturates the
facet group to **all** integral reflections of one host lattice.  The full
reflection arrangement has one chamber orbit.  Rationality and the new
Mori/K3 rank theorem leave only finitely many possible host lattices.

## 1. Exact scope

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective rational surface over an algebraically
closed field, let \(L=\operatorname {NS}(S)\), and assume that

\[
 P=\mathbf P(\operatorname {Nef}S)\subset \mathbb H^{\rho(S)-1}
\]

is literally the complete finite-sided finite-volume Coxeter chamber.  Thus
this is not a proper eigenslice, an invariant section, or a divisor
subsystem.  Let \(W(P)\) be the group generated by its facet reflections.
We use one of two project-native geometric lanes.

### D. Direct Stage-0 lane

The complete Mori cone is generated by primitive physical smooth rational
curves of square \(-1\) or \(-2\).  Put \(M=L\) with its intersection form.
The facet reflections preserve \(M\) automatically.  Adjunction makes
\(-K_S\) nef, and

\[
                         K_S^2+\rho(S)=10
\]

gives \(\rho(S)\leq10\).

This lane deliberately excludes the twice-primitive square-minus-four
escape.  Such a wall belongs here only after the separate log-boundary gate
below has been proved.

### C. Standard index-two Coble lane

There are nonempty pairwise-disjoint smooth rational curves
\(B_1,\ldots,B_b\) of square \(-4\), with \(B=\sum B_i=-2K_S\), and every
other complete Mori generator \(C\) has

\[
 (C^2,B.C)=(-2,0)\quad\hbox{or}\quad(-1,2).
\]

Use the standard adapted lattice

\[
 M=\left\langle L,{B_1\over2},\ldots,{B_b\over2}\right\rangle,
 \qquad q_M=2q_L.                                             \tag{1.1}
\]

It is integral, and the boundary, root, and crossing reflections preserve
it.  Explicitly,
\[
 (B_i/2,B_j/2)_M=-2\delta_{ij},\qquad (B_i/2,x)_M=B_i.x
 \quad(x\in L).
\]
A square-minus-two root is orthogonal to every \(B_i\), since its
nonnegative boundary intersections sum to zero; a square-minus-one crossing
has integral pairing with every half-boundary.  Substitution in the
reflection formula proves preservation of \(M\), with no classification
input.  In characteristic different from two the canonical double cover is
K3, so

\[
 \rho(S)\leq20\quad(\operatorname {char}=0),\qquad
 \rho(S)\leq22\quad(\operatorname {char}>2).                 \tag{1.2}
\]

Also \(B^2=-4b=4K_S^2\), hence

\[
                         \rho(S)=10+b.                         \tag{1.3}
\]

Thus the possible ranks are \(11,\ldots,20\) over characteristic zero and
\(11,\ldots,22\) in odd positive characteristic.  No cover claim is made in
characteristic two.

In either lane impose the following maximality hypothesis.

> **Reflection maximality.**  \(W(P)\) has no proper discrete hyperbolic
> reflection overgroup.  It is enough, more weakly, to require no proper
> reflection overgroup inside \(O^+(M)\).

Maximality merely of an integral lattice, maximality of an order, the
absence of another *effective* nef wall, and maximality among a preselected
list are not this hypothesis.

## 2. The saturation lemma

For an integral Lorentzian lattice \(M\), let

\[
 \operatorname {Ref}(M)=
 \{s_r\in O^+(M):r^2<0,\ s_r(x)=x-2(x,r)r/r^2\}
\]

and put

\[
                         R(M)=\langle\operatorname {Ref}(M)\rangle. \tag{2.1}
\]

### Lemma 2.1

Under the hypotheses of Section 1,

\[
                              W(P)=R(M).                        \tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

Every facet reflection preserves \(M\), so \(W(P)\subseteq R(M)\).  Take
any \(s\in\operatorname {Ref}(M)\).  Then

\[
                         W'=\langle W(P),s\rangle\leq O^+(M).  \tag{2.3}
\]

The integral orthogonal group is discrete in
\(\operatorname {Isom}(\mathbb H^{\rho-1})\), so \(W'\) is a discrete group
generated by hyperplane reflections.  Reflection maximality forces
\(W'=W(P)\), hence \(s\in W(P)\).  This holds for every integral reflection,
which proves (2.2).  Notice that the argument does **not** assume in advance
that \(R(M)\) is reflective or has finite covolume; those facts follow from
its equality with the already supplied \(W(P)\).  \(\square\)

If maximality is stated only among arithmetic reflection groups, the same
argument still works here.  The chamber \(P\) has finite volume, so \(W(P)\)
is a lattice.  Any discrete overgroup \(W'\) of a lattice has finite index,
by the elementary quotient-volume identity.  Hence the group in (2.3) is
commensurable with \(O(M)\) and is arithmetic.  Thus maximality among
arithmetic **reflection** groups is enough.  This must not be confused with
either of the following:

* \(M\) is maximal integral in \(M_{\mathbf Q}\);
* \(W(P)\) is a maximal arithmetic group, which may fail because of a
  nonreflection normalizer extension.

The first says nothing about whether a finite-index facet group already
contains all reflections.  The second is stronger than needed and concerns
nonreflection elements as well.

## 3. One chamber per integral host

### Lemma 3.1

If \(R(M)\) has a finite-volume Coxeter chamber, all chambers of its complete
reflection arrangement are \(R(M)\)-conjugate.  Consequently one isometry
class of unmarked chamber is attached to \(M\).

### Proof

Because \(R(M)\) is discrete, its mirror arrangement is locally finite.
Choose regular interior points in two chambers and perturb a path between
them so that it crosses mirrors one at a time and avoids mirror
intersections.  Compactness of the path and local finiteness make the number
of crossings finite.  Reflection across each crossed mirror moves the
current chamber to its adjacent chamber.  Their product carries the first
chamber to the second.  \(\square\)

Combining Lemmas 2.1 and 3.1, two reflection-maximal full-nef carriers with
isometric host lattices have isometric abstract Coxeter chambers.  The
surfaces need not be isomorphic, deformation equivalent with markings, or
defined over the same field.

## 4. There are only finitely many hosts

### 4.1 Direct rational hosts

The intersection lattice of a smooth rational surface is unimodular.  The
elementary rational minimal-model/blowup calculation gives the complete
finite list needed here:

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \rho&L\\ \hline
 1&\langle1\rangle,\\
 2&U\ \text{or}\ I_{1,1},\\
 3\leq\rho\leq10&I_{1,\rho-1}.
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed a point blowup adds an orthogonal \(\langle-1\rangle\).  The two
parities of a Hirzebruch lattice give \(U\) and \(I_{1,1}\), and already

\[
                         U\oplus\langle-1\rangle\simeq I_{1,2} \tag{4.2}
\]

by the unimodular basis \(f+g+e,f+e,g+e\).  Further blowups remain odd
diagonal.  Thus there are at most **ten** positive-dimensional host-lattice
types in the rank-\(\leq10\) direct lane: two in rank two and one in every
rank three through ten.  Some cannot actually carry a chamber satisfying
all the geometric hypotheses; retaining them only enlarges the finite
upper bound.

### 4.2 Standard Coble hosts

For \(\rho\geq11\), rationality and a blowup presentation give

\[
                              L\simeq I_{1,\rho-1}.             \tag{4.3}
\]

Regard \(L(2)\) as \(L\) with doubled form.  Unimodularity of \(L\) gives

\[
 L(2)^*={1\over2}L,\qquad A_{L(2)}=L(2)^*/L(2)\simeq L/2L.
                                                                    \tag{4.4}
\]

The adapted lattice (1.1) therefore lies in the finite interval

\[
                    L(2)\subseteq M\subseteq {1\over2}L.       \tag{4.5}
\]

More explicitly, \(M\) depends only on the binary subspace

\[
H=\langle B_1\bmod2L,\ldots,B_b\bmod2L\rangle
       \leq L/2L\simeq\mathbf F_2^\rho.                       \tag{4.6}
\]

The log relation supplies the additional binary relation

\[
                         \sum_i(B_i\bmod2L)=0,                 \tag{4.6a}
\]

because \(B=-2K_S\).  Retaining subspaces and boundary tuples that do not
satisfy (4.6a) only enlarges the upper bound below.

Conversely, an intermediate lattice has the form

\[
 M_H=L+{1\over2}\widetilde H,\qquad q_{M_H}=2q_L,             \tag{4.7}
\]

and is integral exactly when

\[
                         x.y\equiv0\pmod2
             \quad\text{for every }x,y\in H.                 \tag{4.8}
\]

Thus only the totally isotropic subspaces for the mod-two pairing survive.
In particular the number of candidates is finite without any Coble-surface
classification.  Before imposing (4.8) or quotienting by isometry, a sharp
elementary upper bound is the total number of binary subspaces

\[
 S_2(\rho)=\sum_{h=0}^{\rho}
       {\rho\brack h}_2,\qquad
 {\rho\brack h}_2=
 \prod_{i=0}^{h-1}{2^{\rho-i}-1\over2^{h-i}-1}.               \tag{4.9}
\]

The exact coarse bounds frozen by the certificate are

\[
 \sum_{\rho=11}^{20}S_2(\rho)
 =9{,}334{,}239{,}813{,}471{,}146{,}416{,}714{,}351{,}279{,}847
                                                                    \tag{4.10}
\]

in characteristic zero, and

\[
 \sum_{\rho=11}^{22}S_2(\rho)
 =19{,}586{,}680{,}934{,}637{,}969{,}160{,}979{,}021{,}208{,}209{,}691{,}846
                                                                    \tag{4.11}
\]

in odd positive characteristic.  These deliberately enormous numbers count
all binary subspaces, including nonintegral ones and many isometric repeats.
They are proof-of-finiteness bounds, not predicted census sizes.  If
\(h=\dim H\), then

\[
 |\det M_H|=2^{\rho-2h},                                      \tag{4.12}
\]

so the discriminant is already bounded in this standard lane.

## 5. Finiteness and field theorem

### Theorem 5.1

Up to ambient hyperbolic isometry, there are only finitely many
reflection-maximal Coxeter chambers admitting a literal full-nef rational
carrier in lane D or lane C.

More precisely:

1. lane D has at most ten positive-dimensional abstract chambers;
2. lane C in characteristic zero has at most the number in (4.10);
3. lane C in odd positive characteristic has at most the number in (4.11).

All three bounds are before removing empty hosts and isometric duplicates.
Every chamber in these literal full-dimensional lanes has minimal Vinberg
field

\[
                                  k(P)=\mathbf Q.              \tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

Sections 1 and 4 leave finitely many integral host lattices \(M\).  Lemma
2.1 identifies a reflection-maximal facet group with \(R(M)\), and Lemma 3.1
allows at most one chamber isometry class per \(M\).  This proves the three
finite bounds.

For (5.1), all wall normals live in the rational quadratic space
\(M_{\mathbf Q}\).  Every Cartan entry \(2(r_i,r_j)/(r_i,r_i)\), and hence every cyclic
product defining the minimal Coxeter/Vinberg field, is rational.  Equivalently,
if one starts with a faithful \(k\)-trace module, literal full dimensionality
gives

\[
 [k:\mathbf Q](n+1)\leq\rho(S)=n+1,
\]

so \([k:\mathbf Q]=1\).  \(\square\)

This is a finite list of **polytope fields**, namely \(\{\mathbf Q\}\).  It is
not a finite list of fields of definition of the carrier surfaces.

## 6. What is effective

The surface route gives more effectivity than a bare volume finiteness
argument, but not a completed census.

1. The candidate host list is terminating and explicit.  Enumerate (4.1),
   then enumerate every binary subspace \(H\leq\mathbf F_2^\rho\) in reduced
   row-echelon form, impose (4.8), and construct (4.7).  Keeping isometric
   duplicates still gives a finite terminating list, so no negative
   lattice-isometry decision is needed for the theorem.
2. For a supplied positive host and a supplied finite chamber, every root,
   reflection, lattice index, and equality \(W(P)=R(M)\) can be checked
   exactly once a genuine reflection-saturation certificate is supplied.
   Naive height enumeration by itself is only a semialgorithm: the theorem
   does not prove that it recognizes completion of the full root chamber.
3. The finite host list plus uniqueness proves qualitative finiteness, but
   it does **not** provide a uniform stopping certificate for a candidate
   whose full reflection group has no finite-volume finite-sided chamber.
   A terminating exhaustive census still needs a reflectivity decision
   procedure or a uniform facet/height bound.
4. Nothing here constructs the carrier from raw \(P\).  PB-Atlas
   nonemptiness/full-Mori recovery, or another exact-return compiler, remains
   the essential-surjectivity gate.

Thus no extra facet degree is needed for the existential finiteness theorem
on this maximal declared class.  A bounded facet degree or Gram height would
still be valuable for a terminating numerical census.  Cox-ring/Hilbert
boundedness and a zero-dimensional Gram image concern finiteness of geometric
carriers, a strictly stronger problem.

## 7. Hostile scope controls

### 7.1 Maximality does not manufacture the AG branch

Blow up \(\mathbb F_4\) at the positive-section torus fixed point.  In the
basis \((B,F,E)\), with \(G=F-E\), the complete physical wall Gram is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-4&0&1\\0&-1&1\\1&1&-1\end{pmatrix}.          \tag{7.1}
\]

After adjoining \(B/2\) and doubling the form, the full-nef chamber is the
reflection-maximal triangle \((2,3,\infty)\).  Nevertheless

\[
 (K+B/2).(B/2,E,G)=(0,-2,-1),                                \tag{7.2}
\]

in the scaled lattice.  It is outside lane D because of its physical
square-minus-four wall and outside lane C because \(K+B/2\ne0\).  This exact
example prevents the circular argument “maximality implies Coble, hence the
K3 rank bound.”  Maximality is used only **after** the independent AG branch
gate and rank theorem.

### 7.2 Fixed lattice and root norm are not enough without maximality

In the fixed odd unimodular lattice \(I_{1,2}\), the exact ideal-polygon
tower has primitive square-minus-one walls

\[
 \ell=(0,-1,0),\quad u_N=(N,1,N),\quad
 a_j=(1+j(j+1),2j+1,j(j+1)-1),\ 0\leq j<N.                  \tag{7.3}
\]

They bound an ideal \((N+2)\)-gon of area \(N\pi\).  For \(N>1\), its
reflection group is a proper index-\(N\) subgroup of the base ideal-triangle
group.  Hence rank, rational field, unimodularity, and primitive norm-one
alphabet permit unbounded facets once reflection maximality is removed.
The all-\(N\) group inclusion and area-ratio proof are independently frozen
in the
[bounded-degree hostile audit](bounded_coxeter_dessin_degree_hostile_audit.md).

This tower is not falsely promoted to a rational-surface counterfamily.  In
rank three a direct rational carrier would require an integral characteristic
canonical vector with \(K^2=7\) and \(K.r=-1\) for every wall.  The exact
linear systems for (7.3) never satisfy that gate: \(N=1\) gives a candidate
of square \(3\), \(N=2\) only a half-integral candidate of square \(1\), and
\(N\geq3\) is inconsistent.

### 7.3 Eigenslices are not literal full-nef hosts

Finitely many ambient lattices do not imply finitely many Lorentzian
sublattices.  In the single odd unimodular lattice \(I_{1,2}\), with
orthogonal basis \(h,e_1,e_2\) of squares \(1,-1,-1\), the saturated
sublattices

\[
 T_m=\langle mh+(m-1)e_1,e_2\rangle,\qquad
 \operatorname {Gram}(T_m)=\operatorname {diag}(2m-1,-1)     \tag{7.4}
\]

have discriminants \(2m-1\) and are pairwise nonisometric.  Saturation
follows from \(\gcd(m,m-1)=1\).  Thus a proper
eigenslice or trace section needs its own discriminant/index/degree bound.
The equality \(n+1=\rho(S)\), used in both host and field arguments, is not
optional.

### 7.4 Abstract chambers are not carrier moduli

The reflection-maximal `Gamma16` K3 lattice has one full-reflection chamber
but a positive-dimensional exact-Picard K3 family.  This is not a rational
counterexample; it is the clean calibration of the logical distinction.
Maximality can make the marked Gram image a point while leaving the carrier
moduli positive-dimensional.  The theorem therefore asserts neither finite
surface isomorphism classes nor a finite list of their fields of definition.
The exact calibration is in the
[Gamma16 maximality theorem](wang_h16_maximal_reflection_no_correction.md).

Arbitrary commensurable modules are excluded for the same reason.  The
ruled-surface modules in the rational reflection-integrality audit have
unbounded denominator or discriminant even in fixed rank.  Finiteness here
uses exactly \(M=L\) or the standard exponent-two interval (4.5).

## 8. The remaining global theorem

The result is genuinely an ABSW-style finiteness theorem for the
**carrier-defined subclass**:

```text
raw P
  -> exact literal rational full-nef carrier in D or C
  -> project-native rank bound and finite host list
  -> reflection maximality saturates W(P)=R(M)
  -> finite abstract chamber list and k(P)=Q.
```

It is not yet global ABSW.  The missing arrow is still the difficult one:
prove from every raw reflection-maximal arithmetic \(P\) that a literal
carrier in D or C exists and returns the same \(P\).  The present theorem
does, however, settle what happens after that arrow: neither a further
surface-classification list nor a volume formula is needed for abstract
finiteness.
