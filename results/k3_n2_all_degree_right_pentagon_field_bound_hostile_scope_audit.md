## 1. Degree and norm-floor audit

The field module has rank three and its primitive full trace is the entire
K3 Picard lattice.  Hence

\[
                         3d=\rho(X)\le20,
                         \qquad d\le6.
\]

For each shifted pair ratio \(x_i=A_i-1\), integral pair reflections give
\(y_i=4x_i\in\mathcal O\).  The distinguished value is positive.  At all
\(d-1\) definite embeddings, strict Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\(-4<\sigma(y_i)<0\).  The nonzero integer norm therefore yields

\[
                         (x_i)_0>4^{-d}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

There is no all-degree justification for the quadratic floor \(1/16\).

## 2. Five-equation recurrence audit

The five principal four-normal Gram determinants have tree-shaped nonzero
graphs because adjacent sides are right-angled.  Their square-only
identities are exactly

\[
                       x_{i-1}x_{i+1}=x_i+1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Put \(\varepsilon=4^{-d}\) and \(z=x_{i+2}\).  Then

\[
 x_{i+3}={z+1\over x_i z-1}>\varepsilon,
 \qquad z>\varepsilon,
\]

so

\[
 x_i<\varepsilon^{-1}+{1+\varepsilon\over\varepsilon z}
     <\varepsilon^{-2}+2\varepsilon^{-1}
     =(4^d+1)^2-1.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For \(d=6\), this is

\[
                 x_i<16,785,408,
                 \qquad A_i<4097^2.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The provisional value \(69,648\) is invalid without an extra theorem: it
uses \(x_{i+3}>1/16\) but \(z>4^{-6}\).  Equation (1.1) supplies the same
degree-dependent floor for both variables.  The owner report correctly
uses (2.2).

## 3. Cyclic-packet audit

The nonzero Cartan graph is a five-cycle.  Its ground-field packet consists
of pair cycles and the directed length-five cycle.  Reflection-word trace
induction makes these algebraic integers.  At the distinguished place,

\[
 |4A_i|<4\cdot4097^2,
 \qquad
 |c_5|<32\cdot4097^5
       =36,938,546,139,294,728,224.
\]

At a definite place, their magnitudes are below \(4\) and \(32\),
respectively.  Thus the displayed length-five bound is a uniform bound
\(H\) for every cyclic generator at every embedding.

Because \(d\le6\), a minimal generating subpacket has at most two elements.
Among \(\theta_1+c\theta_2\), \(0\le c\le15\), at least one is primitive:
the at most \(\binom62=15\) unordered embedding pairs each forbid at most
one coefficient.  Its conjugates have magnitude at most \(16H\), and the
Vandermonde bound is

\[
 |\operatorname {Disc}(k)|
 \le
 \bigl(1,182,033,476,457,431,303,168\bigr)^{30}.
\]

Hermite--Minkowski converts this into a finite field set.  Bounded degree
alone is not being mistaken for field finiteness, and no single pair
coordinate is claimed to generate \(k\) when \(d>2\).

## 4. Quadratic compatibility and scope

For \(d=2\), the same formulas recover \(1/16<x_i<288\).  The distinguished
and definite intervals for any pair cycle are disjoint, so that pair is a
quadratic generator and the frozen sharper discriminant bound

The all-degree theorem does not treat non-right or noncompact pentagons,
arbitrary \(n=2\) incidence, non-ground-field operator extensions, or the
construction of an exact K3 carrier.  Maximality is retained but unused.
