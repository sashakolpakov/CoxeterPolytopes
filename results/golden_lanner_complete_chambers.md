**Status (2026-08-08).**  The eight low-dimensional seeds in
`polytope_first_golden_k3_leverage.md` are no longer merely existence seeds.
For each one, an exact two-place enumeration finds every norm-two
`Z[phi]`-mirror meeting the closed seed simplex.  None cuts its interior.
Consequently, all eight original compact Lanner simplices are already the
complete chambers of their full norm-two reflection arrangements; no seed is
subdivided.

This is a positive inverse-transfer result: beginning with each marked
Coxeter simplex, the computation returns the same polytope as an eigen-nef
section of a family of projective K3 surfaces.  It is not a claim that the
underlying low-dimensional Coxeter simplices are new in the worldwide
literature.

## 2. Why the root enumeration is exhaustive

Write a root as

\[
  r=\sum_i c_i e_i,\qquad c_i\in O,\qquad h(r,r)=2.
\]

The closed simplex cone is generated by the vectors

\[
  v_i=-G^{-1}e_i,
  \qquad h(v_i,e_j)=-\delta_{ij}.
\]

It follows literally that `r^perp` meets the closed simplex if and only if
the real identity-place coefficients `c_i` contain both weak signs.  There
are two cases.

1. If both strict signs occur, put `I={i:c_i>0}` and
   `J={i:c_i<0}`.  Because all off-diagonal entries of `G` are nonpositive,

   \[
     h(c_I,c_I)+h(c_J,c_J)\le 2.
   \]

   Both `I` and `J` are proper subsets, so their principal Grams are positive
   definite.  Their inverse diagonals give exact identity-place bounds on
   each `c_i`.  The full conjugate Gram is positive definite and
   `h^sigma(r,r)=2`, giving the conjugate bounds.
2. If the coefficients have only one weak sign, a contact requires at least
   one zero coefficient.  Its support is therefore proper; its identity and
   conjugate principal Grams are positive definite and give the same kind of
   exact bounds.

For `c_i=a_i+b_i phi`, the two-place bounds become finite integer boxes via

\[
  \operatorname{Tr}(c_i^2)=2a_i^2+2a_i b_i+3b_i^2.
\]

The certifier enumerates every integer point in those derived boxes and then
tests `h(r,r)=2` exactly.  There is no empirical radius or unproved stopping
height.  The mixed-sign workloads, after quotienting by `r -> -r`, are

\[
12,9,12,15,80,104,2463,4992
\]

candidate assignments respectively.  None is a root.  The boundary-support
workloads are

\[
12,9,12,15,63,72,2327,4633,
\]

and yield exactly the contact counts in the table.

Thus every other norm-two root is strictly one-sign.  After orienting it
positively, its inequality is a nonnegative combination of the seed-wall
inequalities and is redundant.  This completes the full-arrangement proof.

## 3. Literal compactness certificates

For every seed, the certificate uses the specified interior point

\[
  x=-G^{-1}(1,\ldots,1)^t,
  \qquad h(x,e_i)=-1,
  \qquad h(x,x)<0.
\]

The vertex opposite wall `i` is represented by `v_i=-G^{-1}e_i`.  The JSON
records its exact coordinates, negative square, all facet pairings, and the
determinant and inertia at both real places of the Gram obtained by deleting
wall `i`.

For each of the eight examples:

- the full identity Gram has inertia `(n,1,0)`;
- the conjugate Gram has inertia `(n+1,0,0)`;
- every deleted-wall Gram has inertia `(n,0,0)` at both places; and
- all `n+1` vertices have negative homogeneous square.

This is the literal simplex form of Vinberg's compactness criterion: all
rank-`n` vertex diagrams are elliptic, so there are `n+1` finite vertices,
zero ideal vertices, and zero hyperideal vertices.  Finite volume is
therefore proved as a consequence of compactness, rather than inferred from
a partial wall list.
