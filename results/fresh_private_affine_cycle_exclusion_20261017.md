# A private affine cycle cannot occur in an unpaid compact quadrilateral star

**Final internally reviewed partial transfer lemma.** In the private/shared decomposition of an actual quadrilateral with four finite corners, suppose every corner has zero Vinberg weight and the ambient diagram contains no connected affine subdiagram of order at most9. Then neither private diagram U nor V can be a connected affine A cycle of order m>=18. This does not yet exclude private affine B/C/D diagrams or transfer the entire finite-corner proof.

The notation and geometric hypotheses are those of the [frozen finite-quadrilateral transfer audit](fresh_simple_finite_quad_vinberg_transfer_20261017.md), but the ambient ideal vertices are not assumed simple here. The four black walls are opposite pairs {u1,u2},{v1,v2}; W is their supporting white diagram. Every W+ui+vj is positive definite. Zero payment forces every native cross contact ui.vj to vanish. A private white component attached to the u-pair has no white path to a v-root. A shared white component has paths to both black pairs.

Assume U is the stated affine cycle, containing u1,u2 and only private whites otherwise. There must be a shared white component attached to U, since the relevant full diagram is connected and native cross-black contacts vanish. Choose a white root z in it with a contact to U. It cannot touch a private white node: such a white edge would make that private component shared. Hence its contacts with U are confined to u1,u2. Also z lies on a white path ending at some vj. The first step of this path is a root outside U, either another white root or that vj itself.

For each i, the diagram (U−ui)+z lies in an actual finite corner and is spherical. The diagram U−ui is A_(m−1). The complete finite one-root extension list for this long A path says that a single nonzero attachment is a bond3 or4 at an endpoint, or a bond3 at a next-to-end node. Exceptional finite types have order at most8 and cannot occur. This is the same finite classification used explicitly in the [cycle payment proof](fresh_simple_cusp_affine_a_quadrilateral_20261017.md); no label cutoff is introduced.

If z touches only one black root, it is a simple or doubled leaf on the full affine cycle. A simple leaf gives the induced affine E8 tree with arms1,2,5, and a doubled leaf with its two cycle neighbors gives the four-node affine doubled star. Both are forbidden. Thus z touches both u1 and u2.

In each deleted cycle the surviving attachment lies at distance one less than the short cycle distance from the nearer endpoint of the resulting A path. The two spherical deletions therefore force the cycle distance between u1,u2 to be one or two. At distance two both contacts are bonds3, giving an induced affine four-cycle on z and the three-node short cycle path. At distance one both contacts are bonds3 or4. If both are3, the triangle z,u1,u2 is affine A2. These cases are forbidden.

The only remaining case has adjacent u1,u2 and at least one bond4 from z, say to u1. In the finite deletion (U−u2)+z, the connected component is the large B_m path, with z its doubled terminal. A connected finite diagram extending this B_m cannot attach any new root to that doubled terminal: at this rank the only finite one-node extension of B_m is at the opposite long endpoint. Finite A/D/E diagrams have no doubled bond, and F4 is too small. Choose that shared path simple. Its first new root is outside U; any attachment to more than one node of the connected B_m would create a cycle, also impossible in a finite diagram. Therefore z cannot have the next contact on its shared white path to vj. That path lies entirely in the actual positive-definite corner W+u1+vj, so its first extension would have to be finite. This is the contradiction.

The proof rules out the large private affine cycle without asserting that every distance-two finite corner is paid. That tempting shortcut would be false for Vinberg's finite weights: a distance-two pair in the middle of a long A path can have zero weight. The needed input here is instead the nonextendible doubled terminal in a spherical diagram.

This is a source-based classification argument, not a numerical enumeration. Its unresolved companions are the private affine-tree cases and the absence of small affine subdiagrams inside the large indefinite diagrams H−U,H−V in the preliminary transfer. The later published carriers of order at most17 do not, by themselves, settle those preliminary issues.
