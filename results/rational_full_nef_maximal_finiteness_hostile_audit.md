## 1. Saturation: exactly what maximality is needed

Let `W=W(P)`.  Every facet reflection preserves the declared host `M`, hence

\[
                         W\subseteq R(M).
\]

If an integral reflection `s` is missing, then

\[
                  W' = \langle W,s\rangle \leq O^+(M)       \tag{1.1}
\]

is a proper overgroup generated by reflections.  Since `O^+(M)` is discrete,
`W'` is discrete.  Therefore either absolute discrete-reflection maximality
or the weaker `M`-relative reflection maximality forbids the missing `s`.
Repeating this for every integral reflection proves

\[
                              W=R(M).                         \tag{1.2}
\]

Arithmetic-reflection maximality also suffices, but for a reason that must
remain explicit.  Both `O^+(M)` and `W` are finite-covolume lattices and
`W<=O^+(M)`, so `W` has finite index in `O^+(M)`.  Any discrete overgroup of
the finite-volume group `W` also has finite index.  Thus the particular
`W'` in (1.1) is commensurable with `O^+(M)` and is arithmetic.  Maximality
among arithmetic **reflection** groups therefore excludes it.

None of the following substitutes for that hypothesis:

- maximality of the integral lattice `M` inside its rational space;
- absence of another known effective curve;
- maximality among an archived list;
- maximality of an order.

The certifier exhausts the corresponding Boolean Horn implications.  A
missing-reflection countermodel exists under “maximal integral lattice
only,” while no countermodel exists under any of the three valid
reflection-maximality formulations.

Maximality as an arbitrary arithmetic group is stronger than necessary and
may introduce irrelevant nonreflection normalizer extensions.  The theorem
correctly does not require it.

## 2. One complete host, one unmarked chamber orbit

Once (1.2) holds, the relevant mirrors are the complete integral-reflection
arrangement.  Discreteness makes this arrangement locally finite: if a
compact set met infinitely many mirrors, a fixed point would have bounded
displacement under infinitely many distinct reflections, contradicting
proper discontinuity.

A generic compact path between regular points of two chambers therefore
crosses finitely many mirrors and no mirror intersections.  Reflecting
successively across those mirrors walks through adjacent chambers and maps
the first chamber onto the second.  Hence every complete-arrangement chamber
is `R(M)`-conjugate.

The hostile certifier models the exact mechanism on the complete three-mirror
`A2` arrangement: all six chambers form one orbit.  Keeping only one mirror
leaves a proper orbit.  This negative control matters—the transitivity
argument belongs to the **complete** reflection arrangement, not to an
arbitrary selected facet set.

Thus one integral host contributes at most one unmarked abstract chamber.
It need not contribute any: its full reflection group may be nonreflective
or lack a finite-volume finite-sided chamber.

## 3. Independent finite-host reconstruction

### Direct lane

The minimal-model/blowup calculation gives

```text
rank 2:       U or I_(1,1)
ranks 3..10: I_(1,rho-1)
```

in positive hyperbolic dimension.  The audit independently verifies the
unimodular change of basis

\[
             U\oplus\langle-1\rangle \simeq I_{1,2}
\]

and recovers the upper bound `2+8=10` hosts.  This uses the rational-surface
minimal-model theorem already declared in the producer; it is not a new
classification-free derivation of that standard input.

### Standard Coble lane

For `L=I_(1,rho-1)`, the doubled lattice has discriminant group

\[
                     A_{L(2)}\simeq L/2L.
\]

An intermediate exponent-two host is determined by a binary subspace `H`.
The half-vector pairing is

\[
            (x/2,y/2)_{2q_L}=x.y/2,
\]

so integrality is exactly the condition `x.y=0 mod 2` for all `x,y in H`.
The hostile audit contains both controls:

- `H=<e_1>` has scaled half-norm `1/2` and is rejected;
- in `I_(1,1)`, `H=<(1,1)>` has scaled half-norm zero and is accepted.

The producer's enormous coarse bounds are rebuilt in two independent ways:

1. Gaussian coefficients through the recurrence

   \[
   {n\brack h}_2={n-1\brack h}_2+2^{n-h}{n-1\brack h-1}_2;
   \]

2. literal unique-RREF enumeration of every subspace through rank five.

The RREF totals are again

```text
rank:                  1   2   3    4    5
all subspaces:         2   5  16   67  374
totally isotropic:     1   2   4   11   31
```

and the independent recurrence recovers exactly

```text
rho=11..20:  9,334,239,813,471,146,416,714,351,279,847
rho=11..22: 19,586,680,934,637,969,160,979,021,208,209,691,846.
```

These count all binary subspaces before integrality, the boundary-sum
relation, and isometry quotient.  That overcount is legitimate.  The
determinant identity

\[
                         |\det M_H|=2^{\rho-2\dim H}
\]

follows from the base determinant `2^rho` and overlattice index
`2^dim(H)`.

No unbounded family of host embeddings survives inside this standard
interval.  Proper eigenslices and arbitrary commensurable modules remain
outside the theorem, as the producer states.

## 4. The missing `F_4` maximality certificate

The adapted half-root Gram of the blowup control is

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}
-2&0&1\\
0&-2&2\\
1&2&-2
\end{pmatrix}.                                               \tag{4.1}
\]

The audit reconstructs all three integral reflection matrices and checks
that they are involutive `G`-isometries.  The normalized pairings give
Coxeter orders `2`, `3`, and `infinity`, so the chamber area is

\[
                  \pi\left(1-\frac12-\frac13\right)=\frac\pi6. \tag{4.2}
\]

This triangle is reflection-maximal.  Indeed every noncompact finite-area
hyperbolic Coxeter polygon has area at least `pi/6`:

- with one ideal vertex, a Coxeter triangle has normalized area
  `1-1/m-1/n`; the largest reciprocal sum below one is `1/2+1/3`, giving
  `1/6`;
- two or three ideal triangle vertices give at least `1/2`;
- a noncompact `k`-gon with `k>=4` has at most `k-1` finite angles, all at
  most `pi/2`, hence normalized area at least
  `(k-3)/2 >= 1/2`.

Any discrete overgroup of the finite-covolume triangle group has finite
index.  It remains noncompact because it contains the triangle's parabolic.
A proper reflection overgroup would consequently have a Coxeter chamber of
area strictly below `pi/6`, contradicting the sharp lower bound.

The script also exhausts one-ideal triangle signatures with finite orders
through `100`, independently finding the unique minimum `(2,3,infinity)`.
The analytic argument, not that finite search, proves the unbounded-order
claim.

So the `F_4` control is valid.  The defect was that the original archive did
not certify this step; it was not a false theorem claim.

## 5. Why maximality cannot be dropped

The audit independently rebuilds the fixed-lattice ideal-polygon tower in
`I_(1,2)`.  For index `N=1,...,24`, every wall is primitive of square `-1`,
every adjacent pair is ideal, the facet count is `N+2`, and the area is
`N*pi`.  For `N>1` these are proper finite-index reflection subgroups of the
base triangle group.

Thus fixed rank, fixed rational field, fixed unimodular host, and fixed wall
norm do not bound facet number.  The saturation theorem genuinely uses
reflection maximality; it is not cosmetic.

This tower is only a group-theoretic hostile control.  It does not pass the
direct rational-surface canonical-class gate, exactly as the producer notes.

## 6. Field statement

On a literal full-dimensional host, every wall normal belongs to the
rational quadratic space `M_Q`.  Hence every Cartan entry

\[
                    \frac{2(r_i,r_j)}{(r_i,r_i)}
\]

and every cyclic product is rational.  This directly proves the minimal
Vinberg field

\[
                              k(P)=\mathbf Q.
\]

No trace-module degree inequality is needed.  The producer's alternative
trace-module sentence is harmless but should be read only under its stated
faithfulness/full-dimensionality hypotheses; the cyclic-product proof is
the unconditional proof inside the declared host lane.

The hostile script includes an irrational-normal control whose three-cycle
is

\[
                         \frac83+\frac43\sqrt2.
\]

It confirms that the rational-host and literal-full-dimensional assumptions
are doing real work.  The conclusion is a field list for the abstract
polytopes, not for fields of definition of carrier surfaces.

## 7. Effectiveness verdict

The following is proved:

- a finite, mathematically terminating list of candidate hosts;
- at most one abstract full-reflection chamber per host;
- qualitative finiteness with the displayed coarse numerical upper bounds;
- the polytope field list `{Q}`.

The following is not proved:

- a uniform reflection-root height or facet bound;
- a terminating decision that an arbitrary candidate host is nonreflective;
- a practical exhaustive chamber census;
- finitely many surface isomorphism classes or surface fields of definition;
- the raw-`P` to literal rational carrier existence arrow.

This scope is already correctly stated in the producer.  In particular, no
extra bounded marked-facet degree is needed for the **existential abstract
finiteness theorem**, but such a bound or a reflectivity decision procedure
would still be needed for a terminating census.
