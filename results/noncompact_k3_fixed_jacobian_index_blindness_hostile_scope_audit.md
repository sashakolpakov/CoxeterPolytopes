# Hostile scope audit: fixed-Jacobian K3 cusp-index blindness

**Verdict (2026-08-11): PASS, with a deliberately local conclusion.**  For
every integer \(d\ge2\), one fixed Jacobian elliptic K3 surface has a
projective torsor \(X_d\) whose fibre divisibility and multisection index are
\(d\).  The two \(II^*\) fibres are literal physical affine \(E_8\) packets,
not formal lattice diagrams.  The exact Picard lattice is

\[
               \operatorname{NS}(X_d)=U(d)\oplus E_8(-1)^2.
\]

The construction proves that the frozen local/fibration invariants are blind
to \(d\).  It does **not** put any \(X_d\) in the finite-volume or
finite-full-automorphism locus, and therefore is not a counterfamily to the
global K3 host theorem.

The separate exact noncompact field theorem forces the trace field to
\(\mathbf Q\).  These integral Picard lattices already live over
\(\mathbf Q\), so the field collapse and the present index escape are
compatible: degree-one ground field does not bound integral cusp
divisibility.

## 1. The fixed Jacobian is genuine

For

\[
 y^2=x^3+a t^4x+t^5(b_0+b_1t+b_2t^2),
\]

the orders of the Weierstrass coefficients and discriminant at \(0\) are
\((4,5,10)\), giving \(II^*\).  After \(s=1/t\) and the standard K3
Weierstrass rescaling, the same orders occur at infinity.  The remaining
discriminant factor is a quartic and is square-free for a general parameter,
so the residual fibres are four \(I_1\)'s.  Their Euler numbers sum to
\(10+10+4=24\).

The two finite fibre-root lattices and the section/fibre plane give
\(U\oplus E_8(-1)^2\).  This has rank 18, and the family has the expected
two moduli; away from the countable Noether--Lefschetz locus there is no
additional Picard class.  Thus the chosen very general Jacobian has exactly
that Neron--Severi lattice and \(T(S)=U^2\).  Shioda--Tate gives Mordell--Weil
rank zero.  The \(II^*\) component group is trivial, excluding nonzero
Mordell--Weil torsion here.

## 2. Projectivity is not smuggled in

There are two Tate--Shafarevich groups in the complex-analytic discussion.
Huybrechts' algebraic group parametrizes **projective** K3 genus-one
fibrations with fixed relative Jacobian and is isomorphic to the algebraic
Brauer group.  The larger analytic group also contains nonprojective K3
surfaces.  The construction uses

\[
              \operatorname{Br}(S)
              \simeq\operatorname{Hom}(T(S),\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z),
\]

not the analytic group.  Hence the exact-order class
\(\alpha_d(ae_1+\cdots)=a/d\) produces a projective K3 torsor for every
\(d\).  Meinsma--Shinder, equation (4.1), Definition 4.2, and Theorem 4.13
give the same surjectivity statement.  Their Lemma 4.4 then gives, without a
period-index guess,

\[
 \operatorname{ind}(X_d)=d,
 \qquad T(X_d)=\ker\alpha_d=U(d)\oplus U.
\]

This closes two common loopholes: the class is neither a merely analytic
torsor nor an abstract Brauer element with geometric existence assumed.

## 3. The fibre schemes really stay fixed

Having the same generic Jacobian would not by itself justify copying a
Kodaira diagram.  The required local theorem is Huybrechts, *Lectures on K3
Surfaces*, Chapter 11, Section 4.1: a genus-one K3 fibration and each of its
relative Jacobians are locally analytically or etale isomorphic over the
base, and every closed fibre is isomorphic.  Absence of multiple fibres on a
K3 is essential to that statement.

Therefore every \(X_d\) has exactly the two \(II^*\) fibres and four
\(I_1\)'s of \(S\).  The eighteen \(II^*\) components are actual irreducible
smooth rational \((-2)\)-curves.  Their two affine relations share the same
fibre class, their combined vector span has rank 17, their Gram rank is 16,
and their common orthogonal in the rank-18 Picard space is \(\mathbf QF_d\).
This is full **local parabolic rank**, not full rank in the host.

## 4. The Neron--Severi computation uses the full discriminant form

Nikulin's unique primitive-embedding criterion applies to
\(T=U(d)\oplus U\): its signature \((2,2)\) is strictly inside \((3,19)\)
and

\[
                    \ell(A_T)+2=4\le18.
\]

An explicit primitive embedding in \(\Lambda_{K3}\) is generated by

\[
              -e_1+df_2,\quad e_2,\quad e_3,\quad f_3.
\]

The indicated four coordinate rows have determinant \(-1\), and direct
orthogonal-complement calculation gives
\(U(d)\oplus E_8(-1)^2\).  Uniqueness of the primitive embedding of the
actual \(T(X_d)\) therefore proves the exact Picard lattice; determinant
equality alone is not being used.

There is a second possible marking loophole: an abstract isometry of Picard
lattices need not send the actual fibre components to the displayed
\(E_8(-1)^2\).  Here the vertical finite-root lattice is unimodular, so it
splits orthogonally.  Its rank-two complement has Gram
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&d\\d&2a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).  The full
discriminant form, not just its order, excludes the sole even-\(d\) residue
\(a=d/2\): in that residue every isotropic discriminant element has even
first coordinate, whereas \(q_{U(d)}\) has two isotropic generators.  Thus
\(a=0\pmod d\), and the marked identification really has
\(F_d=u\) and the actual vertical roots in the two \(E_8(-1)\) summands.

This also calibrates the no-go precisely.  Bounded discriminant **length**
is blind: it stays two.  The complete discriminant form is not blind: its
group has order \(d^2\) and exponent \(d\).

## 5. What is and is not ruled out

Since the multisection index is the positive generator of
\(\{F_d.D:D\in\operatorname{NS}(X_d)\}\),

\[
                     \operatorname{div}(F_d)=d,
 \qquad F_d.D\in d\mathbf Z.
\]

Consequently, if a horizontal physical \((-2)\)-curve exists, its degree is
at least \(d\).  No bound depending only on the following fixed data can
guarantee a bounded-degree transverse physical root:

- the fixed relative Jacobian and its six singular fibres;
- Euler number 24 and Shioda--Tate/Mordell--Weil rank zero;
- Picard rank 18 and the two full-rank local affine packets;
- bounded discriminant length; or
- primitive embeddability in the K3 lattice.

This is a failure of implication, not an existence claim for a particular
horizontal facet.  The formal root \(v-\theta\) in the marked lattice has
square \(-2\) and degree \(d\), but the theorem never labels it irreducible
or a nef-cone facet.

The selected-fibration stabilizer is finite: its vertical kernel is built
from the rank-zero Jacobian Mordell--Weil group and finite generic-fibre
automorphisms, while its base image preserves the six-point colored
discriminant locus.  This says nothing about the full automorphism group,
which can move \(F_d\) to other isotropic classes.

Most importantly, the two affine \(E_8\) packets are not the complete walls
of a finite-volume chamber.  They span only \(F_d^\perp\), one dimension
short of the Picard space.  Neither finite volume, finiteness of
\(\operatorname{Aut}(X_d)\), nor reflection maximality is proved for this
family.  Full finite volume is a global hypothesis on all walls; after the
Nikulin--Vinberg classification it does imply host finiteness, but importing
that reflection-lattice classification is not an independent AG proof.

## 6. Exact interface with a global spanning theorem

If a global theorem supplies one actual transverse physical curve \(C\)
whose intersections with all nine components of one \(II^*\) fibre are at
most \(B\), the highest-root multiplicities sum to 30 and give

\[
                         d\le F_d.C\le30B.
\]

Thus the family is a hostile calibration for the global theorem: any valid
uniform proof must add a transverse/global wall, while a proof using only a
complete packet at one cusp cannot work.

One must not silently identify the fixed \(II^*\) packet with an arbitrary
bounded spanning packet.  The frozen \(B=13\) theorem selects 18 physical
roots in some narrow closed star; it does not assert that the nine chosen
fibre components lie in that star.  Hence \(d\le390\) is only a
**cusp-adapted conditional**, not an automatic corollary of that theorem.

There is nevertheless an automatic, much coarser corollary.  If one of the
surfaces \(X_d\) had a finite-volume complete chamber, the \(B=13\) theorem
would give a rank-18 root lattice \(R\subseteq\operatorname{NS}(X_d)\) with
Gram matrix \(G\).  Therefore

\[
 d^2=|\operatorname{disc}\operatorname{NS}(X_d)|
 \le|\det G|
 \le(4+17\cdot13^2)^9=2877^9.
\]

This determinant bound is the correct hostile compatibility check: global
spanning closes the local escape, while the numerical \(390\) requires the
stronger cusp-adapted wall statement.
