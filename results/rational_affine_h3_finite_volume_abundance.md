# Affine H3 abundance from the finite-volume cusp

**Status (2026-08-10): proved for the literal finite-volume full-nef
carrier; no reflection maximality and no surface census are used.**  This
closes the semiampleness part of the affine H3 gate in the rational
hostile-incidence reduction.  It does not bound the number of vertical
exceptional curves after the fibration is obtained.

The point is global but elementary.  The affine H3 star already fills one
Euclidean factor of the horospherical cusp section.  Every other physical
wall through that cusp is disjoint from the star, so all remaining cusp
walls lie in the orthogonal factor.  Finite volume makes that second
Euclidean factor bounded.  Its wall normals therefore have a positive
rational relation.  On a rational surface this relation is an effective
divisor linearly equivalent to a multiple of the H3 null divisor and
disjoint from it.  The two disjoint representatives give a base-point-free
multiple.  The nonreduced affine star has torsion-free multidegree-zero
Picard group, so that multiple already forces the normal bundle of \(F\)
to be trivial.  Rationality then lifts a trivialization and makes
\(|F|\) itself base-point-free.

This argument uses neither Nikulin--Vinberg, ABSW, Prasad, an arithmetic
volume formula, nor a classification of rational fibrations.

## 1. Exact theorem

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective rational surface over \(\mathbf C\).  Assume
that its closed Mori cone is rational polyhedral and is generated by its
complete set of smooth rational negative curves of squares

\[
                            -1,\qquad -2,\qquad -4.          \tag{1.1}
\]

Assume that the projectivized nef cone is literally a finite-sided
finite-volume hyperbolic polyhedron on the standard physical host

\[
 M=\left\langle\operatorname {NS}(S),B_1/2,\ldots,B_b/2\right\rangle,
 \qquad q_M=2q_{\operatorname {NS}(S)},                     \tag{1.2}
\]

with every facet supplied by the corresponding physical negative curve.
The Coxeter condition may be retained, but only finite volume and the
physical facet description are used below.  Reflection maximality is not
used.

Suppose the H1 and H2 incidences are absent:

\[
 B_i.B_j=0\quad(i\ne j),\qquad B.R=0
 \quad\hbox{for every square-minus-two curve }R.            \tag{1.3}
\]

Let \(E^2=-1\) be a physical Mori generator in the affine H3 case.  Thus,
after relabelling, exactly four square-minus-four curves meet \(E\), each
once, and all remaining ones miss it:

\[
 B_i.E=1\quad(1\le i\le4),\qquad B_j.E=0\quad(j>4).         \tag{1.4}
\]

Put

\[
                  F=4E+B_1+B_2+B_3+B_4.                    \tag{1.5}
\]

The complete-Mori calculation in the H3 trichotomy gives

\[
 F.C\ge0\quad\hbox{for every curve }C,\qquad
 F^2=0,\qquad K_S.F=4.                                     \tag{1.6}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (finite-volume affine-H3 abundance)

Under (1.1)--(1.6), \(F\) is base-point-free.  The finite-volume cusp first
produces a positive multiple \(nF\) with two effective representatives of
disjoint support; the Picard group of the nonreduced affine star then
descends base-point-freeness from \(nF\) to \(F\).

Necessarily

\[
                              \rho(S)\ge11.                 \tag{1.7}
\]

The pencil has connected general-fibre class \(F\) and gives a
genus-three fibration over \(\mathbf P^1\):

\[
                              g(F)=3.                       \tag{1.8}
\]

On a relatively minimal model,

\[
                       \rho(S_{\min})\le26.                 \tag{1.9}
\]

The vertical blowup count \(\rho(S)-\rho(S_{\min})\) is not bounded by this
theorem.  The former genus-two/divisibility-two alternative does not occur:
the coefficient vector \((4,1,1,1,1)\) is primitive as a fibre cycle.

## 2. The affine star and the minimum rank

Use the physical ordered basis \(E,B_1,\ldots,B_4\).  Its intersection
matrix is

\[
 Q_0=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -1&1&1&1&1\\
 1&-4&0&0&0\\
 1&0&-4&0&0\\
 1&0&0&-4&0\\
 1&0&0&0&-4
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 Q_0(4,1,1,1,1)^t=0.                                      \tag{2.1}
\]

The five curve classes are linearly independent.  Indeed, pairing a
putative relation

\[
                         aE+\sum b_iB_i=0
\]

with each \(B_i\) gives \(b_i=a/4\).  The relation would therefore be
\(aF/4=0\).  But \(F\ne0\), since an ample class has positive intersection
with the nonzero effective divisor \(F\).  Hence \(a=b_i=0\).

Their five-dimensional span is degenerate, with radical \(\mathbf RF\).
A degenerate subspace of the nondegenerate space
\(\operatorname {NS}(S)_{\mathbf R}\) has positive codimension.  Therefore

\[
                              \rho(S)\ge6.                  \tag{2.2}
\]

In the doubled host put

\[
                         e=E,\qquad h_i=B_i/2.
\]

Then

\[
 e^2=h_i^2=-2,\qquad e.h_i=1,\qquad h_i.h_j=0,
 \qquad F/2=2e+h_1+h_2+h_3+h_4.                            \tag{2.3}
\]

Modulo the null line, the five star roots span a negative-definite
four-dimensional space \(U_0\).  The last identity is a strictly positive
relation among their Euclidean cusp normals.  Thus these five walls bound
the \(\widetilde D_4\) factor of the horospherical section without any root
classification.

## 3. Every other cusp wall is orthogonal to the star

Let \(C\) be a physical negative curve different from
\(E,B_1,\ldots,B_4\), and suppose its facet contains the ideal point
\([F]\).  Equivalently,

\[
                               F.C=0.                       \tag{3.1}
\]

Distinct irreducible curves on a smooth surface have nonnegative
intersection.  Since every coefficient in (1.5) is positive, (3.1) gives
term by term

\[
             C.E=C.B_1=C.B_2=C.B_3=C.B_4=0.                \tag{3.2}
\]

The physical root of \(C\) is

\[
 r_C=C\quad(C^2=-1,-2),\qquad r_C=C/2\quad(C^2=-4).         \tag{3.3}
\]

Thus every remaining cusp normal is orthogonal to \(U_0\).

Let

\[
             V_F=F^\perp/\mathbf RF,
 \qquad \dim V_F=\rho(S)-2,                                \tag{3.4}
\]

equipped with the induced negative-definite form, and write

\[
                        V_F=U_0\mathbin\perp U_1,
 \qquad \dim U_1=\rho(S)-6.                               \tag{3.5}
\]

By (3.2), every nonstar cusp normal lies in \(U_1\).

## 4. Finite volume produces a second member of \(|nF|\)

A finite-sided finite-volume hyperbolic polyhedron cannot contain a
positive-dimensional piece of the sphere at infinity: such a piece already
has an infinite-volume radial sector.  Hence the isotropic boundary point
\([F]\) is an ideal vertex.  Its horospherical section is compact.  Here is
the only part of that standard fact needed in the proof.  On a sufficiently
deep horosphere, walls not containing \([F]\) disappear.  The remaining
inequalities define a full-dimensional Euclidean polyhedron.  If it had a
nonzero recession direction, its Euclidean volume would be infinite, and so
would the corresponding hyperbolic cusp volume.  Hence its recession cone
is zero and the section is bounded.

Equations (2.3) and (3.2) make this section an orthogonal product

\[
                              P_0\times P_1,                \tag{4.1}
\]

where \(P_0\subset U_0\) is the bounded affine-H3 simplex and
\(P_1\subset U_1\) is cut out by the remaining physical cusp walls.
Therefore \(P_1\) is bounded.

Assume first that \(U_1\ne0\), equivalently \(\rho(S)>6\).  A nonempty
rational Euclidean polyhedron is bounded exactly when its inward normals
positively span the ambient vector space.  Hence the remaining physical
cusp roots \(r_1,\ldots,r_s\) admit a nonzero relation

\[
             \sum_{j=1}^s a_j\overline r_j=0,
             \qquad a_j\in\mathbf Q_{\ge0},                \tag{4.2}
\]

where bars denote images in \(V_F\).  Rational coefficients may be chosen
because all roots and all defining equations are rational.  Explicitly, the
recession cone is the dual of the positive cone of the normals.  Its
vanishing says that the latter cone is all of \(U_1\); expressing the
negative of each generator in that cone and summing gives (4.2), and the
resulting rational linear system has a rational point in its nonempty
relative interior.  Lifting (4.2) to \(F^\perp\) gives

\[
                         \sum_j a_jr_j=\lambda F.           \tag{4.3}
\]

The scalar \(\lambda\) is positive: intersection with any ample divisor is
positive on the nonzero effective left side and on \(F\).  It is rational.
After inserting the physical scalings (3.3) and clearing all denominators,
(4.3) becomes

\[
                G:=\sum_j m_jC_j\equiv nF,
 \qquad m_j\in\mathbf Z_{\ge0},\quad n\in\mathbf Z_{>0}.   \tag{4.4}
\]

Every \(C_j\) is disjoint from the star by (3.2), so

\[
                         \operatorname {Supp}(G)
                         \cap\operatorname {Supp}(F)=\varnothing. \tag{4.5}
\]

For a smooth rational surface,

\[
 H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0,qquad \operatorname {Pic}^0(S)=0,
 \qquad\operatorname {Pic}(S)\text{ is torsion-free}.      \tag{4.6}
\]

Thus integral numerical equivalence is linear equivalence, and (4.4) gives

\[
                               G\sim nF.                    \tag{4.7}
\]

Let \(s_0,s_1\in H^0(S,\mathcal O_S(nF))\) have zero divisors \(nF\) and
\(G\), respectively.  By (4.5), they have no common zero.  The two sections
therefore globally generate \(\mathcal O_S(nF)\).  In particular \(|nF|\)
is base-point-free.

This proves the semiampleness stage whenever \(\rho(S)>6\).  Section 6
descends from the moving multiple to \(|F|\) itself.

## 5. The apparent low-rank endpoint is empty

There is no rank-six endpoint to treat.  In fact the numerical affine-H3
hypotheses themselves force \(\rho(S)\ge11\).

Suppose \(\rho(S)\le10\).  Then

\[
                          K_S^2=10-\rho(S)\ge0.             \tag{5.1}
\]

Rationality gives \(H^0(S,2K_S)=0\).  Riemann--Roch and Serre duality give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \chi(\mathcal O_S(-K_S))&=1+K_S^2\ge1,\\
 h^2(S,\mathcal O_S(-K_S))&=h^0(S,\mathcal O_S(2K_S))=0.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{5.2}
\]

Thus \(h^0(S,\mathcal O_S(-K_S))=1+K_S^2+h^1\ge1\), so an
effective divisor \(D\sim-K_S\) exists.  Nefness of \(F\) would imply
\(D.F\ge0\), whereas (1.6) gives

\[
                              D.F=-K_S.F=-4.                \tag{5.3}
\]

This contradiction proves (1.7).  In particular \(U_1\ne0\), and indeed
\(\dim U_1=\rho(S)-6\ge5\).  Therefore Section 4 covers every surface in
the theorem; no external big-anticanonical or Mori-dream result is needed.

This also diagnoses the tempting rank-six argument.  Testa--Várilly-
Alvarado--Velasco, Lemma 2.5, is correctly stated for rational surfaces
with big anticanonical class, but its preceding Lemma 2.4 forces
\((-K_S).N>0\) for every nonzero nef \(N\).  Taking \(N=F\) immediately
contradicts \((-K_S).F=-4\).  The citation therefore detects an empty
hypothesis range; it does not supply a nonvacuous affine-H3 endpoint.

## 6. The nonreduced Picard group kills the index

Section 4 proves that \(|nF|\) is base-point-free.  It remains to show that
the first multiple already moves.

Put

\[
                         C=F_{\mathrm {red}}
                           =E+B_1+B_2+B_3+B_4.              \tag{6.1}
\]

The curve \(C\) is an SNC tree of five copies of \(\mathbf P^1\): the four
leaves are disjoint, and intersection number one with \(E\) means one
transverse node on each leaf.  Therefore a line bundle on \(C\) is
determined, up to the trivial gluing on a tree, by its five component
degrees.  In particular,

\[
                  \operatorname {Pic}^0(C)=0,\qquad
                  \operatorname {Pic}(C)\simeq\mathbf Z^5.                \tag{6.2}
\]

Let \(\mathcal I\) be the nilradical of \(C\subset F\).  There is an exact
sequence of sheaves of abelian groups

\[
 1\longrightarrow1+\mathcal I\longrightarrow\mathcal O_F^*
  \longrightarrow\mathcal O_C^*\longrightarrow1.           \tag{6.3}
\]

Because the ground field has characteristic zero and \(\mathcal I\) is
nilpotent, the finite logarithm identifies

\[
                         1+\mathcal I\simeq\mathcal I       \tag{6.4}
\]

as sheaves of abelian groups.  The constants on the connected proper curve
\(C\) lift to constants on \(F\).  The cohomology sequence of (6.3)
therefore identifies

\[
 \ker\!\left(\operatorname {Pic}(F)\to\operatorname {Pic}(C)\right)
                         \simeq H^1(F,\mathcal I),           \tag{6.5}
\]

an additive complex vector group.  In particular this kernel has no
nontrivial torsion.

Now set

\[
                              N=\mathcal O_F(F).
\]

Its degree on every component of \(C\) is \(F.E=F.B_i=0\), so (6.2) puts
\(N\) in the kernel (6.5).  The section of
\(\mathcal O_S(nF)\) whose zero divisor is the disjoint divisor \(G\) from
(4.4) is a unit along the whole nonreduced scheme \(F\).  Consequently

\[
                              N^n=\mathcal O_F(nF)\simeq\mathcal O_F.
                                                                    \tag{6.6}
\]

Torsion-freeness of (6.5) forces \(N\simeq\mathcal O_F\).  Finally use

\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S(F)
  \longrightarrow\mathcal O_F(F)\longrightarrow0.          \tag{6.7}
\]

Since \(H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0\), the constant section \(1\) of
\(\mathcal O_F(F)\) lifts to a section \(t\) of \(\mathcal O_S(F)\) that
is nowhere zero along \(F\).  Together with the canonical section whose
zero divisor is \(F\), it globally generates \(\mathcal O_S(F)\).  Thus
\(|F|\) itself is base-point-free.

### 6.1 Why the Picard argument is genuinely geometric

The index cannot be killed from the affine Gram matrix or discriminant
exponent alone.  Let

\[
                         M=U\oplus D_4(-1)
\]

and let \(e\in U\) be primitive isotropic.  If \(\theta\) is the highest
root of \(D_4(-1)\), then for every \(r\ge1\)

\[
                         \alpha_0=re-\theta                \tag{6.8}
\]

together with the four finite \(D_4\) simple roots has the same affine
\(\widetilde D_4\) Gram matrix, while its positive null relation is
\(\alpha_0+\theta=re\).  The lattice and its exponent-two discriminant are
fixed as \(r\) varies.  What forces index one on the surface is instead the
torsion-free multidegree-zero Picard group of the actual nonreduced curve
\(F\).

### 6.2 Fibration and core bound

The two generating sections of \(\mathcal O_S(F)\) define a pencil having
\(F\) as one fibre.  Its Stein base is \(\mathbf P^1\), because a rational
surface has irregularity zero.  The support of \(F\) is connected, and its
coefficient vector \((4,1,1,1,1)\) has gcd one.  Hence the finite map in
the Stein factorization has degree one: otherwise the fibre divisor \(F\)
would be divisible by that degree.  The connected general-fibre class
\(H\) is therefore numerically \(F\).  Adjunction gives

\[
                         2g(H)-2=K_S.F=4,\qquad g(H)=3.     \tag{6.9}
\]

On the relatively minimal smooth model,

\[
                         K_f=K_{S_{\min}}+2H
\]

is nef, and therefore

\[
 0\le K_f^2
   =10-\rho(S_{\min})+8(g(H)-1)
   =26-\rho(S_{\min}).                                    \tag{6.10}
\]

This proves (1.9).  Contracting vertical square-minus-one curves lowers
\(\rho\) without changing the general fibre, so (6.10) still gives no
bound on the vertical tail.

## 7. Exact scope and the repaired project gate

Three logically different assumptions must not be merged.

1. **Numerical effectivity and nefness.**  The complete Mori alphabet and
   the affine H3 incidence prove that the explicit effective divisor \(F\)
   is nef and isotropic.  They also force \(\rho\ge11\); the apparent
   rank-six through rank-ten endpoints are empty by Section 5.  They do not
   themselves produce a second section.
2. **Bare rational polyhedrality.**  This gives finitely many numerical
   walls, but an infinite-volume cusp section may have a nonzero recession
   factor.  The positive relation (4.2) does not follow from polyhedrality
   alone.  No claim about the bare variant is made here.
3. **Finite volume or finite generation.**  Finite volume gives (4.2)
   geometrically.  Independently, finite generation of the Cox ring would
   make every nef divisor semiample by the Mori-dream property, but finite
   generation is not assumed or used in Theorem 1.1.

Accordingly the affine-H3 abundance gate in the actual finite-volume
full-nef program is closed, including the potential fibration index.  The
remaining affine obstruction is the global vertical-tail bound.  This
theorem does not alter the separate higher/dotted H1/H2 gaps and does not
claim an absolute Picard or hyperbolic dimension bound.
