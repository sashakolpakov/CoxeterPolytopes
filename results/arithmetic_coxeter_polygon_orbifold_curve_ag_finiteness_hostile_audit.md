# Hostile audit: arithmetic Coxeter polygons from real orbifold curves

**Verdict (2026-08-12): PASS in the stated geometric dimension-two scope,
with two formalization repairs and one lemma that must be made explicit.**
The core finiteness implication is correct and does not require the
Agol--Belolipetsky--Storm--Whyte finiteness theorem.  Long--Maclachlan--Reid
Theorem 1.1 applies to the orientation-preserving subgroup of a maximal
arithmetic over-lattice once the standard maximal-arithmetic-congruence lemma
is inserted.  It then gives finitely many maximal polygons, not merely
finitely many commensurability classes.

## 1. Exact carrier

Let \(P\subset\mathbb H^2\) be a finite-area Coxeter polygon, let \(W\) be
its full reflection group, and put \(\Gamma=W^+\).  The coarse compactification
of \(X_\Gamma=\mathbb H^2/\Gamma\) is a sphere: doubling the topological disk
\(P\) changes a vertex of angle \(\pi/m\) into a cone point of order \(m\),
and changes an ideal vertex into a puncture.  A side reflection represents
the unique orientation-reversing coset \(W/\Gamma\), and induces an
antiholomorphic involution \(c\) on \(X_\Gamma\).  Uniformization is unique,
so \(c\) is an isometry of the orbifold hyperbolic metric and its fixed real
oval is geodesic.  Quotienting a selected half by this involution recovers
the lengths, angles, cusps, and cyclic side order of \(P\), rather than only
its Coxeter presentation.

This argument passes, but the literal algebraic object should be written as

\[
 (\overline{\mathcal X}_{\mathbf R},D_{\rm cusp},c,
   \mathcal H),                                           \tag{1.1}
\]

or equivalently as a complex smooth DM log curve with a real descent datum
\(c:\mathcal X\to\overline{\mathcal X}\).  Here
\(D_{\rm cusp}=\overline{\mathcal X}\setminus\mathcal X\) is part of the
data, the cone orders are the stack stabilizers, and \(\mathcal H\) consists
of a choice of one of the two complementary halves plus the finite cyclic
labelling of the real stacky/cusp points and intervening facets.

Two repairs follow.

1. An antiholomorphic map is not a morphism in the category of complex
   algebraic stacks.  Calling \(c\) a real structure/descent datum, or working
   over \(\mathbf R\), makes the category literal.
2. The compactified curve alone forgets which ordinary points were cusps.
   The open curve or the boundary divisor \(D_{\rm cusp}\) must remain in the
   object.  The owner text appears to intend this by “with its canonical
   compactification,” but the log pair is the exact formulation.

Algebraization itself is not a problem.  Compact orbifold Riemann existence
algebraizes the analytic stack, punctures give a finite log divisor, and the
antiholomorphic involution gives a real form.  A noncongruence arithmetic
cover need not carry the *canonical Shimura model* claimed informally in
Section 2, but it is still an algebraic orbifold curve (indeed a finite
algebraic cover of a congruence Shimura orbifold after passing to a common
finite-index group).  Thus “Shimura orbifold” should not be used there to
assert a canonical number-field model for every \(\Gamma\); no such model is
needed for exact return or finiteness.

The framing is finite but is not purely complex-algebraic: selection of a
real half and its cyclic orientation is a finite semialgebraic/topological
decoration of a real algebraic DM curve.  The theorem is therefore an exact
**real-framed mixed algebraic** carrier.  It is not a faithful functor into
bare complex curves, which are often just \(\mathbf P^1\).

## 2. Maximal-lattice lemma

Choose a maximal arithmetic lattice
\(\Lambda<\operatorname {Isom}(\mathbb H^2)\) containing \(W\), and let
\(R(\Lambda)\) be the subgroup generated by every reflection in \(\Lambda\).
It is discrete, contains \(W\), and is normal because the set of reflections
is invariant under conjugation by \(\Lambda\).  Reflection-maximality gives

\[
                         R(\Lambda)=W\triangleleft\Lambda. \tag{2.1}
\]

Both groups are lattices, so \([\Lambda:W]<\infty\).  Each coset has a unique
representative preserving the selected Coxeter chamber after multiplication
by an element of \(W\); consequently \(\Lambda/W\) is the finite chamber
symmetry group.  This proves the normality/finite-quotient passage directly
and uses no reflection-group finiteness theorem.

Put \(\Lambda^+=\Lambda\cap\operatorname {Isom}^+(\mathbb H^2)\).  Then
\(\Gamma=W^+\triangleleft\Lambda^+\), and compactified coarse curves have a
finite quotient map

\[
                   \mathbf P^1=X_\Gamma\longrightarrow X_{\Lambda^+}.
                                                               \tag{2.2}
\]

The quotient of \(\mathbf P^1\) by a finite group is \(\mathbf P^1\)
(equivalently use Lüroth or Riemann--Hurwitz).  Hence \(\Lambda^+\) has genus
zero.  Normality is convenient but not needed just for this genus statement;
any finite orbifold quotient of a genus-zero curve has genus zero.

## 3. The missing explicit congruence lemma

The sentence “the orientation-preserving subgroup of a maximal arithmetic
lattice is congruence” is correct, but it is the only nontrivial step in the
owner proof that is asserted without proof or citation.  It must not be
replaced by the false general statement that every arithmetic Fuchsian group
is congruence.

The exact lemma needed is:

> **Maximal-arithmetic congruence lemma.**  If \(\Lambda\) is a maximal
> arithmetic lattice in the full isometry group of \(\mathbb H^2\), then
> \(\Lambda^+\) contains a principal congruence subgroup of the norm-one
> group of an order in its defining quaternion algebra.  In particular
> \(\Lambda^+\) is a congruence arithmetic Fuchsian group in the sense of
> Long--Maclachlan--Reid.

This is a standard arithmetic-lattice fact, not a finiteness theorem.  A
short lattice/order proof is available: realize a maximal arithmetic group
as the normalizer of the stabilizer of an \(\mathcal O_k\)-lattice (or
quaternion order) \(L\).  Relative to a fixed reference lattice \(M\), choose
a nonzero ideal \(\mathfrak a\) clearing the two commensurability
denominators between \(L\) and \(M\).  Every element congruent to the identity
modulo \(\mathfrak a\) on \(M\) stabilizes \(L\).  Thus the corresponding
principal congruence subgroup lies in \(\Lambda\); intersecting with the
positive real component places it in \(\Lambda^+\).  The same statement
follows from the normalizer-of-square-free-Eichler-order description of
maximal arithmetic groups.

Long--Maclachlan--Reid Lemma 4.2 proves the orientation-preserving maximal
version from Borel's Eichler-order description.  For the plus subgroup of a
maximal lattice in the *full* isometry group, the owner theorem should either
state the lemma above or cite the general maximal-arithmetic version.  No
ABSW finiteness conclusion is needed for it.

## 4. Long--Maclachlan--Reid gives the required finiteness

The exact primary statement is Long--Maclachlan--Reid, Theorem 1.1:

\[
 \left\{\begin{array}{c}
   \text{conjugacy classes of congruence arithmetic Fuchsian groups}\\
   \text{whose underlying orbifold surface has genus zero}
 \end{array}\right\}
 \quad\text{is finite}.                                \tag{4.1}
\]

It is stronger than merely saying that maximal genus-zero Fuchsian groups
are finite.  Applying (4.1) directly to \(\Lambda^+\), using Sections 2--3,
gives finitely many conjugacy classes of \(\Lambda^+\).  The paper's proof
uses the congruence spectral gap \(\lambda_1\ge3/16\), Zograf's inequality,
and finiteness of arithmetic Fuchsian groups of bounded coarea; for genus
zero it records

\[
                    \operatorname {Area}(\mathbb H^2/\Lambda^+)
                    \le {128\pi\over3}.                 \tag{4.2}
\]

For each fixed \(\Lambda^+\), its full normalizer has finite quotient by
\(\Lambda^+\), because it is the finite isometry group of a finite-area
hyperbolic orbifold.  Hence only finitely many full \(\Lambda\) occur.  For
each such \(\Lambda\), equation (2.1) uniquely determines \(W\) as the
subgroup generated by all reflections of \(\Lambda\).  Its mirror
arrangement has one chamber orbit, so it determines one polygon up to
isometry; a finite cyclic facet labelling produces only finitely many marked
versions.  Therefore (4.1) really yields

\[
 \boxed{\text{finitely many maximal finite-area arithmetic Coxeter polygons}}
                                                               \tag{4.3}
\]

and not only the weaker LMR Corollary 1.2 about wide commensurability
classes.

## 5. Precise scope of the PASS

The result is exact for the category actually defined in the owner file:
geometric marked polygons and their full reflection groups.  It does not
recover an additional chosen field, order, integral module, scaling of root
vectors, or other presentation data if those are regarded as part of a
richer “admissible arithmetic Coxeter datum.”  Those decorations must either
be forgotten by the source category or added separately to (1.1).

The leverage is also specifically arithmetic-spectral curve leverage.  The
genus-zero invariant is algebraic and the objects are real algebraic
orbifold curves, but the finite list does not follow from
\(\mathcal M_{0,r}\), BAB, or KSBA alone.  Congruence and the automorphic
spectral gap are indispensable.  Subject to that declared scope and the
formal repairs in Section 1, the dimension-two theorem passes independently
of ABSW, Nikulin, or Vinberg finiteness/dimension conclusions.

## 6. Re-audit after owner repair

The live owner file was re-read after its 2026-08-12 repair.  It now makes
the carrier a proper real DM log curve with marked cusp divisor, withdraws a
canonical Shimura-model claim for a possibly noncongruence
\(\Gamma_P\), states the first (congruence) clause of Long--Maclachlan--Reid
Theorem 1.1, and inserts the normal-core/invariant-lattice proof of the
maximal-full-lattice congruence bridge.  These changes close every
substantive defect identified above.

One proof sentence in Lemma 4.0 should still be made literal.  After proving
that a sufficiently deep principal subgroup stabilizes \(L\), the owner text
says that “shrinking the ideal once more puts it in the identity component.”
A congruence condition at finite primes does not, in that general wording,
control a chosen real connected component.  The safe formulation is to
start with the norm-one, orientation-preserving reference Fuchsian group
\(\Gamma_0=P\rho(\mathcal O^1)\) and its principal subgroup
\(\Gamma_0(\mathfrak b)\).  For \(\mathfrak b\subseteq\mathfrak a^2\), the
sandwich

\[
       \mathfrak aL_0\subseteq L\subseteq\mathfrak a^{-1}L_0
\]

gives \((g-1)L\subseteq L\) for every
\(g\in\Gamma_0(\mathfrak b)\), and the same calculation for \(g^{-1}\)
gives \(gL=L\).  Hence
\(\Gamma_0(\mathfrak b)\subseteq\operatorname {Stab}(L)^+=\Lambda^+\)
directly.  This is a one-line local correction, not a new hypothesis or a
finiteness input.

**Updated verdict:** terminal PASS after that wording correction.  No
remaining issue affects exact return, the genus-zero quotient, direct
application of Long--Maclachlan--Reid, or finiteness of maximal polygons.
