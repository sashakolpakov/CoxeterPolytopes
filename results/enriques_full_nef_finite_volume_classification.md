# Full-nef finite-volume Enriques chambers: an exact classification

**Scope.**  Everything below is over the complex numbers and concerns the
projectivized **complete nef cone** of a smooth projective Enriques surface.
It does not concern a proper invariant section, a chosen divisor subsystem,
or an arbitrary abstract Coxeter polytope in `H9`.

## The theorem

Let `Y` be a complex Enriques surface, identify

\[
 \operatorname{Num}(Y)\cong L_{10}=U\oplus E_8(-1),
\]

and let `P(Y)` be the projectivization in `H9` of its complete nef cone.  Let
`W_Y` be generated by the reflections in the numerical classes of **all**
smooth rational curves on `Y`.  The following are equivalent.

1. `P(Y)` has finite hyperbolic volume.
2. `W_Y` has finite index in `O^+(L10)`.
3. `Aut(Y)` is finite.
4. The complete nodal graph of `Y` is one of the Kondō--Martin Types I--VII.
5. The closed effective/Mori cone, equivalently the nef cone, is rational
   polyhedral.
6. The Cox ring `Cox(Y)` is finitely generated.
7. The primitive normalized wall Gram matrix of `P(Y)` is one of the seven
   exact matrices in the Enriques chamber certificate.

Consequently there are exactly seven full-nef finite-volume Enriques
Coxeter-polytopes up to congruence.  Types I--II have 12 facets and Types
III--VII have 20 facets.  Every one is noncompact.  Thus:

\[
 \boxed{\text{there is no compact full-nef Enriques Coxeter polytope,}}
\]

and this entire surface class contains no 14-facet member in `H9`.

This removes the apparently extra hypothesis “assume `Aut(Y)` is finite”
from the earlier seven-image statement: finite hyperbolic volume itself
forces that hypothesis.

## Proof of the new bridge

### 1. The nef cone is the reflection fundamental chamber

Kondō, section 1.3 (internal page 9 of the pinned primary source), defines
the positive component containing the ample classes and the chamber cut by
all nodal classes.  He states that `W_Y` acts properly discontinuously and
that the closure of this chamber is a fundamental domain.  Its
projectivization is precisely `P(Y)`: on an Enriques surface the only
negative irreducible curves are smooth rational `(-2)`-curves, while the
pairing of two classes in the closed positive cone is nonnegative.

Therefore `P(Y)` is a fundamental chamber for `W_Y`; in particular its
hyperbolic volume is the covolume of `W_Y`.  This avoids treating boundary
stabilizers as though the closed polyhedron were literally an orbifold
quotient.

### 2. Hyperbolic volume is exactly reflection index

The integral orthogonal group `Gamma=O^+(L10)` is an arithmetic lattice in
`Isom(H9)`, hence `H9/Gamma` has finite positive volume.  The natural
orbifold covering

\[
 \mathbb H^9/W_Y\longrightarrow\mathbb H^9/\Gamma
\]

has degree `[Gamma:W_Y]`.  Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{vol}P(Y)
   =[\Gamma:W_Y]\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb H^9/\Gamma),
\]

with the left-hand side infinite when the index is infinite.  This proves
`(1) <=> (2)` without substituting a finite wall list or a few observed
cusps for a volume theorem.

### 3. Torelli turns reflection index into an automorphism criterion

Kondō's Theorem 1.5 (internal page 10) gives a finite-index subgroup
`G(Y)` of `O(L10)` containing `W_Y` normally and compares `Aut(Y)` with
`G(Y)/W_Y` by a homomorphism having finite kernel and finite cokernel.
Equivalently, as Martin explicitly records in his introduction,

\[
 [O(L_{10}):W_Y]<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 |\operatorname{Aut}(Y)|<\infty
\]

for complex Enriques surfaces.  This proves `(2) <=> (3)`.  The converse
uses the complex Global Torelli theorem; no positive-characteristic
extension is asserted here.

### 4. Surface classification supplies the complete wall systems

Nikulin and Kondō classify the complex finite-automorphism case.  Martin's
Main Theorem gives the modern iff formulation: `Aut(Y)` is finite exactly
when the graph of **all** smooth rational curves is one of Types I--VII.
Thus `(3) <=> (4)` includes both existence and absence of hidden nodal
walls.

Shimada's exact `L10` vectors reconstruct the seven normalized Gram
matrices.  Their distinct weighted-pair histograms prove that there are
seven, rather than at most seven, congruence types.  This proves
`(4) <=> (7)`.

### 5. The algebra of the surface detects finite volume

Artebani--Hausen--Laface, Theorem 2.10, proves for every complex Enriques
surface that

\[
 \operatorname{Cox}(Y)\text{ is finitely generated}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Eff}(Y)\text{ is rational polyhedral}.
\]

On a surface the nef cone is dual to the closed cone generated by curves, so
polyhedrality passes between the closed effective/Mori and nef cones.  A finite
automorphism Enriques surface has one of the seven finite complete nodal
lists, hence polyhedral cones.  Conversely, if the nef cone is rational
polyhedral, `Aut(Y)` permutes its finitely many primitive extremal rays; an
isometry fixing all of them fixes their full-dimensional span, and the
kernel of `Aut(Y)->O(Num(Y))` is finite.  Hence `Aut(Y)` is finite.  This
proves `(3) <=> (5) <=> (6)`.

In particular, the answer to whether finite covolume is visible in the
classical algebra of the surface is **yes in this full-nef Enriques class**:

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{vol}_{\rm hyp}P(Y)<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Cox}(Y)\text{ is finitely generated}.}
\]

This equivalence is special to the complete nef chamber.  It fails as an
argument for an arbitrary divisor subsystem or a proper fixed/eigencone
section.

## Exact finite-volume and noncompactness audit

The classification theorem supplies complete walls but does not, by itself,
license a casual volume assertion.  Two independent exact replays were
therefore retained.

- GMP double description finds 2,214 finite rays, 294 ideal rays, and zero
  hyperideal rays across the seven chambers.  Every facet is irredundant.
- The literal Vinberg replay checks all 13,182 elliptic rank-eight edges and
  584 full affine zero-neighborhoods.  Every edge has exactly two
  finite/ideal endpoints and every affine neighborhood has the required
  parabolic rank; there are zero failures.

The 294 ideal rays prove that every one of the seven chambers is noncompact.
The detailed type-by-type counts and matrices are in the
[seven-image theorem](enriques_finite_automorphism_nef_transfer_theorem.md),
and the no-import replay is in the
[hostile audit](enriques_finite_aut_nef_chambers_hostile_audit.md).

## What has and has not been transferred

This is a genuine classification-to-polytope transfer for a variable
surface class: starting with an arbitrary complex Enriques surface, finite
generation of its Cox ring, finite automorphism group, finite reflection
index, finite hyperbolic volume, and membership in the seven exact Gram
types are equivalent.  It is stronger than defining a class by listing the
seven matrices first.

It remains scoped.  It does **not** say that every finite-volume integral
`H9` Coxeter polytope has an Enriques carrier, nor that failure of the
Enriques criterion excludes an abstract polytope.  Rather, it proves that
the full-nef Enriques lane is now completely classified and cannot produce a
new eighth finite-volume type.  New examples from Enriques geometry must use
proper invariant/quotient sections, different boundary data, or leave the
full-nef category.
