## 5. Proper-completion exclusions requiring more than one diagonal

Two recurring cases deserve proof beyond a label in the register.
Suppose a missing pair of orthogonal target leaves has the same forced
old contact. Positive same-end blocks make each projected square and
their projected inner product at least r>1. Actual leaf orthogonality
forces residual inner product of magnitude at least r, whereas each
residual square is at most2-r. Cauchy would require r<=2-r, impossible.
The source cases use r=3/2 or4/3. Additional contacts cannot repair the
bound; contacts at the other end give S8 first.

In the sparse D16 fork-center case, the center's sole old contact gives
projection4/3 and residual2/3. Every candidate leaf contact outside the
base is excluded by projection>2, a zero-residual positive product, or
an S8 path using its required bond to the center. Two contacts in the
same end are also excluded by the full quadratic lower bound. Thus both
missing leaves are core-orthogonal, and their residual Gram would be

    [[2/3,-1,-1],[-1,2,0],[-1,0,2]].

Its determinant is-4/3, so it is not positive semidefinite. The positive
orthogonal complement cannot realize it. This excludes the target
without assuming unknown contacts away. The analogous saturated cases
with projection2 have no center residual at all; a required simple bond
then either contradicts the forced product or gives an earlier short path.
