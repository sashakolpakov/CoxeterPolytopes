trace module.”

The fixed polytope is the compact golden Coxeter triangle with labels
\((2,5,5)\).  Its field, order, normalized Cartan cycles, and native field
reflections never change.  Nevertheless, for every integer \(s\ge2\) there
is a primitive full-trace K3 package having:

\[

     \text{ K3 walls}\\

\]

The common trace scale \(s\), glue index \(s^3\), ambient discriminant
\(64s^3(s-1)^3\), and negative-definite Picard complement all escape.
Every physical facet root lies outside the primitive trace block.  Thus
bounded normalized Cartan data, a finite ground field and order, primitive
full trace, physical \((-2)\)-facets, and exact eigen-nef return do **not**
imply finitely many trace hosts unless the physical star is lifted by the
same trace vectors.

## 1. Frozen compact golden seed

Put

\[
 \phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
 k=\mathbf Q(\sqrt5),\qquad \mathcal O=\mathbf Z[\phi].
\]

Use the frozen `triangle_255` package in
[the complete golden-Lanner census](golden_lanner_complete_chambers.md).
Its rank-six even trace lattice \(N_0\) has the following Gram matrix in the
interleaved basis
\((e_1,\phi e_1,e_2,\phi e_2,e_3,\phi e_3)\):

\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}
-2&-2&0&0&1&2\\
-2&-4&0&0&2&3\\
0&0&-2&-2&1&2\\
0&0&-2&-4&2&3\\
1&2&1&2&-2&-2\\
2&3&2&3&-2&-4
\end{pmatrix}.                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

It has signature \((1,5)\), determinant \(-16\), and a self-adjoint
\(\mathcal O\)-action.  Let

\[
 r_1=e_1,\qquad r_2=e_2,\qquad r_3=e_3.
\]

Their trace Gram is

\[
H=(r_i.r_j)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2&0&1\\
0&-2&1\\
1&1&-2
\end{pmatrix}.                                      \tag{1.2}
\]

The matrix \(-H\) has leading principal minors \(2,4,4\), so \(H\) is
negative definite; it is the negative \(A_3\) matrix.  Although their full

three \(r_i\) are the Lorentzian normals of the compact \((2,5,5)\)

eigencomponent is exactly what makes the construction possible.

The frozen golden shell theorem says that every \((-2)\)-mirror of \(N_0\)
whose restriction meets the distinguished positive cone restricts to the
complete native norm-two arrangement.  Its chamber containing the frozen
interior point is exactly the seed triangle \(P\).

## 2. The scaled trace block and glued ambient lattice

Fix an integer \(s\ge2\).  Let

\[
                         T_s=(N_0,sQ).                 \tag{2.1}
\]

This is the same \(\mathcal O\)-module and the same admissible field form,
multiplied by \(s\).  All field reflections and all normalized Cartan
cycles are unchanged.  In particular, the native compact chamber is still
\(P\).  The lattice remains even and integral, but it has no square-
\((-2)\) vector because every square is divisible by \(2s\).

Take a rank-three lattice \(K_s\) with basis \(c_1,c_2,c_3\) and Gram

\[
                         s(s-1)H.                      \tag{2.2}
\]

It is even and negative definite.  Inside
\((T_s\oplus K_s)\otimes\mathbf Q\), put

\[
                 \delta_i={r_i+c_i\over s},qquad
 S_s=\langle T_s\oplus K_s,\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3\rangle.
                                                               \tag{2.3}
\]

The relevant pairings are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \delta_i.x&=Q(r_i,x) &&(x\in N_0),\\
 \delta_i.c_j&=(s-1)H_{ij},\\
 \delta_i.\delta_j&=H_{ij}.
\end{aligned}                                         \tag{2.4}
\]

They prove at once that \(S_s\) is integral and that each \(\delta_i\) is
a primitive square-\((-2)\) vector.  Since its displayed Gram has even
diagonal, \(S_s\) is even.  In the basis consisting of the six trace basis
vectors followed by \(\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3\), its Gram is

\[
 A_s=\begin{pmatrix}sQ&B\\B^t&H\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad B=Q[:,\{1,3,5\}],                              \tag{2.5}
\]

where the displayed column indices are one-based.

The quotient \(S_s/T_s\) is freely generated by the images of the
\(\delta_i\): the relation \(c_i=s\delta_i-r_i\) introduces no torsion.
Thus \(T_s\hookrightarrow S_s\) is primitive.  On the other hand,

\[
             [S_s:T_s\oplus K_s]=s^3,                 \tag{2.6}
\]

which is already an unbounded glue index.

Because \(B^tQ^{-1}B=H\), the Schur complement of \(sQ\) in \(A_s\) is

\[
                     H-{1\over s}H={s-1\over s}H.     \tag{2.7}
\]

It follows that

\[
 \operatorname {sig}(S_s)=(1,8),\qquad
 \det S_s=64s^3(s-1)^3.                               \tag{2.8}
\]

The orthogonal complement of \(T_s\) in \(S_s\) is exactly \(K_s\).
Indeed, in the basis of (2.5), vanishing of the trace projection forces the
three glue coefficients to be divisible by \(s\).  Hence the complement,
its determinant, the glue index and the full ambient discriminant are all
unbounded.

## 3. Every projected K3 root is an old golden mirror

It remains to prove exact chamber return rather than merely exhibit three
root vectors.  Every vector \(\rho\in S_s\) has a unique expression

\[
              \rho=x+\sum_{i=1}^3a_i\delta_i,qquad
              x\in T_s,\quad a=(a_1,a_2,a_3)\in\mathbf Z^3.       \tag{3.1}
\]

Its trace and complement projections are

\[
 t=x+{1\over s}\sum_i a_ir_i,qquad
 u={1\over s}\sum_i a_ic_i.                           \tag{3.2}
\]

Set

\[
                         y=st=sx+\sum_i a_ir_i\in N_0. \tag{3.3}
\]

If \(\rho^2=-2\), multiplying its norm equation by \(s\) gives

\[
                  Q(y,y)=-2s-(s-1)H(a,a).             \tag{3.4}
\]

If \(a=0\), then \(y=sx\), and (3.4) would say
\(sQ(x,x)=-2\), impossible because \(Q(x,x)\) is even and \(s\ge2\).
Thus every ambient root has nonzero complement component.

Because \(H\) is an even negative-definite \(A_3\) lattice, write

\[
                         H(a,a)=-2j,qquad j\ge1.       \tag{3.5}
\]

Suppose the hyperplane of \(\rho\) restricts to a hyperplane meeting the
distinguished positive cone.  Its restricted normal is proportional to the
distinguished component of \(y\), and that component has negative square.
The other real component of the admissible golden trace form is negative
definite.  Therefore the full trace square must satisfy

\[
                              Q(y,y)<0.                 \tag{3.6}
\]

But (3.4)--(3.5) give

\[
             Q(y,y)=2\bigl((j-1)s-j\bigr).             \tag{3.7}
\]

For \(s\ge2\), this is negative only when \(j=1\), in which case

\[
                              Q(y,y)=-2.                \tag{3.8}
\]

The endpoint \((s,j)=(2,2)\) gives zero, not a hyperplane meeting the open
positive cone.  Every remaining case is positive.

Thus every ambient K3 root whose mirror is visible in the eigenslice
projects to the mirror of an old \((-2)\)-root \(y\in N_0\).  By the frozen
golden shell theorem, that is one of the mirrors in the already complete
native arrangement.  Conversely, each \(\delta_i\) is a root and projects
to \(r_i/s\), so all three bounding facets of \(P\) occur.  Hence the
component containing the frozen interior point is exactly

\[
         \boxed{
         \mathbf P\bigl(D_s\cap E_{\tau_0}\bigr)=P,}   \tag{3.9}
\]

where \(D_s\) is the ambient root chamber containing that point.
